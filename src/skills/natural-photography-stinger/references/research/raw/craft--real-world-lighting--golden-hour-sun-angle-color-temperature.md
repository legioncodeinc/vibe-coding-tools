## Sun elevation angles (the actual definitions)
- **Golden hour**: sun between **+6° above** and **-6° below** the horizon.
- **Blue hour**: sun between **-4° and -8°** below the horizon.
- (Civil twilight is 0° to -6°; the overlap is why golden and blue hour bleed into each other.)

## Color temperature by stage
- **Golden hour: ~2500–3500 K** — orange/golden. Note this is *tungsten territory*, which is why an AWB'd golden-hour shot often comes back neutral and disappointing; the warmth must be preserved deliberately.
- **Blue hour: 10,000 K+** — everything lit by sky only.
- Compare: midday sun ~5400 K; overcast ~6800 K; open shade 8000–25,000 K.

## Duration (latitude/season dependent)
- **Summer: 15–20 minutes**
- **Winter: 45–60+ minutes**
- Duration varies significantly with latitude — near the equator golden hour is shortest, at high latitudes it can last hours.

## Light quality
- Low sun angle produces **long, gentle shadows that add depth**; light travels through more atmosphere, scattering blue out (Rayleigh) and leaving warm wavelengths.
- **Reduced contrast** vs midday: "even exposure... easier to capture detail."

## Prompt-usable takeaways (contrast the three canonical daylight conditions)
- **Golden hour**: 2500–3500 K, sun at 0–6° elevation, shadows several times subject height, strong directional rim/kicker, warm skin, cyan/blue shadow fill from the sky (a genuine two-color-temperature scene).
- **Midday**: ~5400 K, sun at 60–90°, short hard shadows directly beneath, raccoon-eye shadows in eye sockets, hot forehead/nose, shadow under nose and chin, high contrast.
- **Overcast**: ~6800 K, entire sky is the source, near-shadowless, very low contrast, faint top-down shading (slight shadow under brow, nose, chin), no catchlight shape except a large soft band in the eye.
