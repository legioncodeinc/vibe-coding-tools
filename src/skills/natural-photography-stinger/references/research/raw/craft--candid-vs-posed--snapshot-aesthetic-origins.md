## The snapshot aesthetic — defining visual conventions
**Framing & composition**
- Unposed, uncomposed; no technical perfection.
- "Dutch-angled, offhand images" — tilted/"drunken" horizons.
- Subjects **cropped by the frame edge**, captured "on the fly."
- Non-hierarchical arrangement — no single dominant subject, no rule-of-thirds discipline.
- Photographs taken "mostly without aiming."

**Technical elements (the literal quote list)**
> "meaningless blur, grain, muddy exposures, drunken horizons, and general sloppiness"
- Blur and grain present and unapologetic.
- Muddy (not optimized) exposures — flat shadows, clipped highlights.
- **Direct flash**, often "dazzling artificial light."

**Conceptual qualities**
- Spontaneous, coincidental appearance; intimate/vulnerable moments.
- "Fragments of gestures and postures" rather than complete poses.
- Emotional register includes boredom and vulnerability, not just joy.
- **Bare, unglamorous interiors replace formal studio settings.**

## Lineage and dates
- Robert Frank — *The Americans* (shot 1955–56)
- Diane Arbus — 1960s
- Lee Friedlander — 1960s
- Mark Cohen — 1970s onward
- Nan Goldin — 1980s–90s
- Wolfgang Tillmans — 1990s
- Juergen Teller — 1990s

## Key insight for prompting
The aesthetic reads "natural, point-and-shoot" but is usually **deliberately constructed** — "staged spontaneity" that mimics amateur vernacular photography. That is exactly the task: reproduce the *signature* of unaimed shooting on purpose.
