# Selfies Distort Your Face by 30% — And Here's the Math (Live Science, reporting Paskhover et al., JAMA Facial Plastic Surgery 2018)

- Fetched: 2026-08-17
- Source type: academic (journalistic summary of a peer-reviewed paper)
- Citation: JAMA Facial Plastic Surgery, March 1, 2018. Lead author Dr. Boris Paskhover (Rutgers New Jersey Medical School) with Stanford. DOI 10.1001/jamafacial.2018.0009

## The headline numbers
- **Selfie distance modeled: 12 inches (30 cm)** — a phone held close, common on the front camera.
- **Standard portrait distance: 5 feet (1.5 m)**.
- **Reference: infinite distance** (orthographic, zero perspective distortion).

### Nasal width increase at 12 in vs 5 ft
- **Men: +30%** perceived nasal width
- **Women: +29%** perceived nasal width

## Method
The researchers "modeled an average male and an average female face as a collection of parallel planes, similar to how an art student might draw a 3D building receding toward the horizon." The nose sits on a plane closer to the lens than the cheeks/ears, so at 12 in it occupies a disproportionately large solid angle.

## Related figure
2017 American Academy of Facial Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery poll: **55% of surveyed plastic surgeons** reported patients requesting changes specifically to improve their appearance **in selfies**.

## Prompt-usable takeaways
- The single most quantified facial-geometry tell of a phone selfie: **nose ~30% wider/larger relative to the face** than in a 5 ft portrait.
- Corollaries of the same geometry: forehead enlarged when the phone is held high, chin enlarged when held low, ears pushed back and partly hidden, jaw narrowed, and the face reading rounder/wider at center and tapering at the edges.
- 12 in (30 cm) = extreme selfie; ~60 cm = arm's length (per Fried et al.); 1.5 m = neutral; 3–4.6 m = flattering telephoto.
