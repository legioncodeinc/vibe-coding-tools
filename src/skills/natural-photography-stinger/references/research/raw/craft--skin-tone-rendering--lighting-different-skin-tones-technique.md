## Physics stated
"Higher melanin content absorbs more light, requiring careful adjustment of light intensity. Darker skin tones may appear flatter under harsh lighting due to increased light absorption." (Pairs with the dichromatic model: the *diffuse* return drops with melanin while the *specular* return does not, so hard light yields hotspots on a dark field — the flatness plus glare problem.)

## Metering and exposure
- **Spot meter on the subject's face**, not the scene — matrix/evaluative metering pulls deep skin toward middle gray and *underexposes* it while blowing the background.
- Apply **positive exposure compensation** to prevent underexposure of darker skin.
- Monitor with **vectorscope and waveform** to keep skin in an acceptable range (the source gives no IRE numbers; the industry rule of thumb elsewhere is skin around 55–70 IRE for light skin, ~35–50 IRE for deep skin, sitting on the vectorscope skin-tone line).

## Fill and contrast
- **"Darker skin tones often require less fill to maintain depth and contrast."**
- **"Lighter skin tones may need more fill to avoid harsh shadows."**
- Counterintuitive but correct: on deep skin the shadow side is already close to black, so heavy fill flattens it; shape comes from a controlled key plus separation, not from lifting the shadow.

## Separation
- **"Use backlight to create separation between subject and background, especially for darker skin tones."** Rim/edge/kicker light is not optional here — without it the subject merges into a dark background.
- A **hair light** "adds separation and helps bring out detail and texture in the hair" that would otherwise absorb all light (SLR Lounge).

## Modifiers named
Softboxes (diffuse), **beauty dish** ("balance between soft and contrasty light"), umbrellas (broad, soft), scrims and diffusion panels.
Reflectors: **gold** adds warmth/radiance to brown and olive skin; **silver** gives bright neutral fill; **unbleached muslin** (warm-toned) "wraps light gently around faces and adds a subtle glow" for medium-to-dark skin, whereas bleached muslin "tends to suit lighter skin better."

## Color temperature and gels
- Daylight **5000–6500 K**; tungsten **3200 K**.
- **CTO** warms a cool source (daylight -> tungsten); **CTB** cools a warm source.
- LED fixtures with adjustable CCT let you match the practical sources in the room rather than fighting them.

## Undertone identification
Wrist-vein test: **blue veins = cool undertone; green veins = warm undertone** (SLR Lounge). Drives background/wardrobe color and grading direction. Standard undertone vocabulary: warm (golden/yellow/peach), cool (pink/red/blue), neutral/olive (green-yellow cast).

## Mixed skin tones in one frame
- **"Place your light source closest to the darkest-skinned subjects"** (imaging-resource) — inverse-square then naturally gives the darker subject more light and the lighter subject less.
- Or run **multiple key lights at different intensities**, and use reflectors to lift only the darker subject.

## Post
"Boosting highlight and shadow sliders can help bring out detail," with the caution to "give a faithful representation of your subject's skin without over-lightening" — do not lift luminance until the skin goes ashy/gray; deep skin should retain saturation and a warm or cool undertone, not become desaturated brown.
