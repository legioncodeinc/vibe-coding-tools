## Notes

Google's automated ranking systems identify a mix of factors demonstrating aspects of Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T). Of these, Trust is most important -- the other three contribute to trust, but content doesn't necessarily need to demonstrate all of them (e.g. some content is helpful based purely on the experience it shows).

E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor, but using a mix of factors that identify content with good E-E-A-T is useful to Google's systems. Google gives even more weight to strong E-E-A-T for topics that could significantly impact health, financial stability, safety, or societal welfare/well-being ("Your Money or Your Life," YMYL topics).

Search Quality Raters (human evaluators) are trained on the Search Quality Rater Guidelines to assess E-E-A-T; their assessments feed back into confirming whether Google's automated ranking changes are working as intended -- they do not directly set rankings for any individual page.

Guidance for content creators: the "why" behind content should be to help people first -- content useful to visitors who come to the site directly. If the primary purpose is attracting search-engine visits, that misaligns with what Google's systems reward. Using automation, including AI generation, to produce content primarily to manipulate rankings is a violation of Google's spam policies.
