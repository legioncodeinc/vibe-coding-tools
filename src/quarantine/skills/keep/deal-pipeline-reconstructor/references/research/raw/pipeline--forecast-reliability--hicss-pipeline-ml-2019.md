## Extracted content, abstract quoted in full

"This study proposes a model designed to help sales representatives in the software
industry to manage the complex sales pipeline. By integrating business analytics in the
form of machine learning into lead and opportunity management, data-driven qualification
support reduces the high degree of arbitrariness caused by professional expertise and
experiences. Through the case study of a software provider, we developed an artifact
consisting of three models to map the end-to-end sales pipeline process using real
business data from the company's CRM system. The results show a superiority of the
CatBoost and Random Forest algorithm over other supervised classifiers such as Support
Vector Machine, XGBoost, and Decision Tree as the baseline. The study also reveals that
the probability of either winning or losing a sales deal in the early lead stage is more
difficult to predict than analyzing the lead and opportunity phases separately.
Furthermore, an explanation functionality for individual predictions is provided."

## Claims this source supports

1. **The strongest vendor-independent statement in the archive on stage reliability.** A
   peer-reviewed paper describes existing human lead and opportunity qualification as
   carrying "a high degree of arbitrariness caused by professional expertise and
   experiences". That is an academic source saying, in its own words, that the stage a
   human assigns is arbitrary to a meaningful degree.
2. Early-stage placement is HARDER than later-stage placement: "the probability of either
   winning or losing a sales deal in the early lead stage is more difficult to predict".
   A board should therefore express lower confidence at Lead and Qualified than at Proposal
   and Negotiation, and should treat an early-stage placement as more provisional.
3. The paper explicitly ships "an explanation functionality for individual predictions".
   Showing the reasoning behind a per-deal placement is established practice in this
   literature, not an optional courtesy.
4. Splitting the lead phase from the opportunity phase produced better results than
   modelling them as one continuum, which supports a board that treats the pre-qualified
   tail differently from the live pipeline.
