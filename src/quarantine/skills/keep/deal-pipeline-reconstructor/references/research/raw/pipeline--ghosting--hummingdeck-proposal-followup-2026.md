## Source-quality caveat

Vendor-published and titled "data-backed". Its figures are attributed to named third
parties with stated sample sizes, which is better than most of the category, but **not one
of the attributions carries a year**, and every cited party is itself a vendor selling into
the same workflow (Proposify, Salesloft, Gong, PandaDoc). Treat every figure as a claim
attributed to a named vendor with an unknown vintage.

## Extracted content

### What silence at proposal stage means, quoted

Silence "doesn't mean a prospect dislikes the proposal or chose a competitor. Rather, it
often signals internal committee dynamics, overwhelm, internal politics, price shock, or
fear of delivering bad news."

### Time to silence

The article does not state a research-backed typical time before a prospect goes quiet. It
opens with a "four days" scenario as illustration only, and does not cite it.

## Claims this source supports

1. Silence at proposal stage is attributed to buyer-internal causes rather than to a
   decision against the seller. This agrees with the HubSpot source on mechanism.
2. **The strongest usable idea in this file is the Proposify claim that a large share of
   closed-won proposals close within 24 hours of first being opened.** If true even
   directionally, it means the value of the proposal-stage signal decays FAST, and a
   proposal sitting silent for two weeks is qualitatively different from a lead sitting
   silent for two weeks. That is the empirical basis for stage-weighted going-cold
   thresholds.
3. The Gong claim that "I never heard back" phrasing REDUCES meetings booked is a concrete,
   large-sample, negatively-signed finding, which is the kind least likely to be
   publication-biased. It constrains the wording of a next-action line.
