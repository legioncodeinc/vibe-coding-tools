# What Is a Good Sales Forecast Accuracy Rate? (Clari)

- **Title:** What Is a Good Sales Forecast Accuracy Rate?
- **URL:** https://www.clari.com/blog/sales-forecasting-accuracy/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Clari, a revenue-forecasting vendor)
- **Date on page:** May 27, 2026, by Jess Richter

## Source-quality caveat

Clari sells the product that replaces stage-weighted forecasting. Every claim below that
disparages stage-based forecasting is made by a party with a direct commercial interest in
that conclusion. The claims are recorded here as CLAIMS. Notably, the article's central
mechanism claim (rep optimism bias) is corroborated independently by the judgmental
forecasting literature in this archive, and its central numeric claims are not.

## Extracted content

### Accuracy benchmarks, attributed to Eagle Rock CFO 2026 benchmarking research

"quarterly forecasts across most B2B industries land within 8% to 15% of actuals, with top
performers reaching 95%+ accuracy"

### Method comparison, no primary source cited

- Stage-based forecasting accuracy "tops out" in the range of roughly 60 to 75% where CRM
  field population is inconsistent.
- Deal-level machine learning is claimed to reach roughly 75 to 90% accuracy, described as
  an improvement of roughly 20 to 30 percentage points over stage-weighted approaches.

### CRM completeness, attributed only indirectly

"only 60 to 70% of CRM fields are consistently populated across B2B organizations". The
article points at growth-onomics.com rather than a formal study.

### On rep behaviour, quoted, no source cited

Reps "mark deals as healthy in the CRM long after buyer engagement signals have gone cold,
push close dates to protect their pipeline"

"Rep optimism bias is the most persistent driver of forecast inaccuracy in most revenue
organizations"

## Claims this source supports

1. The self-reported stage is claimed to lag observable buyer engagement, with the CRM
   record staying healthy after the signal has gone cold. This is the exact failure mode a
   capture-derived board is positioned to catch, because capture sees the last touch and
   the CRM sees the last edit.
2. Stage-weighted forecasting degrades specifically as a function of INCOMPLETE FIELDS,
   which is the maximal case for an operator with no CRM at all.
3. Rep optimism bias is asserted as the dominant error direction: forecasts are wrong
   OPTIMISTICALLY more often than pessimistically.

## Conflict to carry forward

This source claims stage-based forecasting is 60 to 75% accurate. The archive contains no
independent confirmation of that figure and it comes from a vendor selling the
alternative. Do not restate it as a fact.
