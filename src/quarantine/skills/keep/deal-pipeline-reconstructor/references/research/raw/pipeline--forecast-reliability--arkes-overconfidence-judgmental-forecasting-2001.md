## Window note

This source is far outside the 6-month default research window, by design. It is
foundational literature on the exact cognitive failure that makes a self-reported deal
stage unreliable. No newer general treatment of forecaster overconfidence surfaced in the
sweep that was not vendor-published. Recorded here with its age stated plainly.

## Extracted content

Overconfidence is described as "a common finding in the forecasting research literature".

Three stated consequences of forecaster overconfidence:

1. People "neglect decision aids"
2. People "make predictions contrary to the base rate"
3. People "succumb to 'groupthink'"

Six counteracting principles proposed in the chapter, as summarised in the accessible
abstract:

- Consider alternatives explicitly
- List reasons the forecast might be wrong
- Appoint a devil's advocate in group settings
- Make explicit predictions and obtain feedback
- Treat feedback as valuable information rather than as criticism
- Run experiments to test prediction strategies

## Claims this source supports

1. Forecaster overconfidence is an established finding in the peer-reviewed forecasting
   literature, not a vendor talking point. This independently corroborates the direction
   (though not the magnitude) of the optimism-bias claim made by forecasting vendors.
2. The documented remedies are procedural and are directly transferable to a pipeline
   board: force the alternative reading, state why the placement might be wrong, and make
   the prediction explicit so it can be checked later. A board that shows its stage
   reasoning is applying remedies 1, 2 and 4.
3. Overconfident forecasters "make predictions contrary to the base rate", which is the
   mechanism by which a rep places a deal one stage further along than the evidence
   supports.
