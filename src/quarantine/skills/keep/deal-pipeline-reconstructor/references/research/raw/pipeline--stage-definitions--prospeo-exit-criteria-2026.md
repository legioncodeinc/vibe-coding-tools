## Source-quality caveat

Vendor-published aggregation. It cites named third parties for most figures, which is
better than most of the category, but the underlying datasets were not independently
verified and several attributions carry no year. Treat every number below as a CLAIM
attributed to a named party, not as an established fact. The exit criteria themselves are
editorial, with no cited source.

## Extracted content

### Exit criteria per stage (editorial, no source cited on the page)

| Stage | Stated exit criteria |
|---|---|
| Prospecting | "ICP fit confirmed, verified contact data in hand, initial engagement signal" |
| Qualification | "Budget path identified, decision-making authority confirmed, timeline established, success criteria defined, prospect agrees to next steps" |
| Discovery | "Confirmed business pain articulated by prospect in their own words, buying roles mapped, specific next step agreed" |
| Demo / Presentation | "Prospect confirms solution fit, agrees to decision process and timeline, key stakeholders identified" |
| Proposal | "Budget confirmed in writing, stakeholders aligned on solution, decision timeline agreed, pricing reviewed" |
| Negotiation | "Legal or procurement review initiated, commercial terms largely agreed, clear path to signature" |
| Closing and post-sale | "Signed agreement received, handoff to customer success initiated, first value milestone scheduled" |

### Stage definition, quoted

Sales stages are "the high-level phases a deal passes through from first contact to
closed-won", used for "forecasting, coaching, and figuring out where deals are stalling."

### Conversion benchmarks (attributed, conflicting)

- Attributed to a FirstPageSage dataset covering 2017 to 2025, B2B SaaS: Lead to MQL 39%,
  MQL to SQL 38%, SQL to Opportunity 42%, SQL to Closed 37%.
- Attributed to a Digital Bloom SaaS analysis: MQL to SQL "typical performance running
  just 15-21%".
- **These two conflict directly.** One says MQL to SQL is 38%, the other says roughly 15
  to 21%. The page presents both without resolving them.

### Cycle length benchmarks (attributed to Osserva)

- "The average B2B lead-to-close cycle runs 102 days"
- By industry: Software 90 days, Technology 121 days, Healthcare 125 days
- By annual contract value: under 1K USD 25 days; 50K to 100K USD 120 days; 250K to 500K
  USD 220 days
- By lead source: SEO inbound 28 days; cold calling 110 days

### Stale-deal guidance, quoted (no source cited)

"Set a stale-deal threshold at 2x the average stage duration and enforce it."

### Win-rate decay, attributed to Outreach

"Deals closed within 50 days have a 47% win rate. After that threshold, win rates drop to
roughly 20% or lower."

### Lead-decay claims, attributed

- Attributed to Outreach: "Sellers have roughly 14 days to engage a new lead before
  interest decays" and "The most successful reps reach out at least 9 times" within that
  window.
- Attributed to Nutshell: "Contacting leads within 24 hours increases conversion 5x".

## Claims this source supports

1. Exit criteria, where anyone bothers to define them, are almost entirely stated as
   BUYER-SIDE OBSERVABLE EVENTS (agreed a next step, reviewed pricing, initiated legal
   review). Those events are exactly what a capture record can evidence.
2. A defensible stale threshold is RELATIVE to typical stage duration rather than a single
   global day count.
3. Published stage conversion rates conflict badly between vendors, so a skill must not
   quote a conversion rate as if it were settled.
4. Deal age correlates with reduced win rate. The magnitude is contested, the direction is
   consistent across the archive.
