## Extracted content

The review's reference apparatus surfaces the canonical works of the field, including:

- Kahneman and Lovallo (1993), "Timid Choices and Bold Forecasts", cited in the context of
  forecasters exhibiting systematic biases in confidence relative to actual accuracy.
- Goodwin (2000), "Correct or combine? Mechanically integrating judgmental forecasts with
  statistical methods", cited in the context of integrating judgment with statistical
  method.

Themes the review covers, per the listing: overconfidence and optimistic bias in
judgmental forecasts; judgmental adjustment of statistical forecasts and whether such
adjustment improves or harms accuracy; and the effect of feedback on forecaster
performance over time.

The review emphasises integration approaches: combining judgmental forecasts with
statistical methods can improve accuracy over either approach used alone.

## Claims this source supports

1. There is a mature peer-reviewed literature on judgmental forecasting bias. Assertions
   that "rep-reported stages are unreliable" have real academic grounding and are not only
   a vendor pitch, even where the vendor's specific numbers are unsupported.
2. The literature's recurring prescription is COMBINATION rather than replacement: neither
   pure human judgment nor pure mechanical inference wins outright. This directly justifies
   the design in which the skill proposes an evidence-derived stage and the human confirms
   or overrides it, rather than either party deciding alone.
3. Feedback matters. A board that is re-run weekly and compared against its own prior
   placements is the feedback loop the literature calls for.
