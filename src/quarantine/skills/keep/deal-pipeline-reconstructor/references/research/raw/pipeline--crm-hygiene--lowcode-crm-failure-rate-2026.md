## Source-quality caveat, read this before using any number below

This is the weakest-sourced file in the archive and it is retained deliberately, as an
example of the category. The publisher sells an alternative to off-the-shelf CRM, so it
has a commercial interest in a high failure rate. **Thirteen statistics were extracted.
Eleven of them carry NO attribution at all.** Two carry an attribution (Forrester, year
unspecified; Cyntexa 2026). The title promises "real numbers" and then supplies mostly
unsourced ones.

Nothing from this file is used as a load-bearing claim in the distillation. It is cited
only to characterise the state of the evidence, and it is the archive's exhibit for why
vendor-published statistics in this domain must be treated as claims.

### Internal inconsistency

The page states the failure range three different ways in one article: 30 to 63 percent,
30 to 55 percent, and 30 percent outright with 55 percent partial. These are not
reconciled anywhere on the page.

## Claims this source supports

1. **The evidence base for CRM failure rates is bad.** The most confidently titled article
   in the sweep is mostly unsourced and internally inconsistent. Any skill quoting a CRM
   failure rate to a user is repeating an unverifiable number.
2. Where a mechanism is named in this category, it is consistently PEOPLE and ADOPTION
   rather than software. That direction is echoed across the archive, so the direction is
   usable while the magnitudes are not.
3. The one figure claiming SMB implementations fail LESS often than enterprise ones is
   unattributed and runs against the intuitive reading. Recorded, not relied upon.
