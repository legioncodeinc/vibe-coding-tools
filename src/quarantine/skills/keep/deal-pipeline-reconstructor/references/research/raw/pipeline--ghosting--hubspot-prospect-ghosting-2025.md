## Source-quality caveat

Practitioner-expert commentary, explicitly framed as such by its own subtitle. **It
contains no statistics at all.** That is unusual for the category and, for once, honest:
the piece does not dress opinion as data. It is used here as evidence of what practitioners
believe the CAUSES of silence are, not as evidence of frequency.

## Extracted content

### The six stated reasons a prospect goes silent

1. Overwhelmed with choices, leading to decision paralysis
2. Overwhelmed with competing priorities
3. Lack of perceived value or urgency
4. Unclear how the solution fits into their current workflow
5. Incomplete discovery, so the process missed their core needs
6. Information overload from technical jargon

### Recommended response, quoted

"don't take it personally. Instead, send a follow-up email or message that's friendly and
non-pushy."

Reset approach, quoted:

"I've been reflecting on our conversations and realize I may have missed understanding
your core challenges. Could we schedule a quick 15-minute no-commitment call where I ask
just three questions about what success actually looks like for you?"

On timing, quoted:

"sometimes it's best to let them be for a while, then check back in after a few weeks or
months."

Value-delivery tactic: share case studies or industry insight addressing the prospect's
specific concern rather than escalating follow-up frequency.

## Claims this source supports

1. **Not one of the six named causes is "they decided against you".** Every stated cause is
   about the buyer's own bandwidth, clarity, or internal situation. This is the strongest
   available argument for why a going-cold list must be a WORK QUEUE rather than a
   write-off list.
2. Cause 5, incomplete discovery, means silence can be diagnostic of a defect in an
   EARLIER stage. A deal that went quiet after a proposal may actually be a deal that was
   never properly qualified, which argues for showing the whole evidence trail per deal
   rather than only the last touch.
3. The recommended action for a long silence is a slow, low-pressure re-approach, not
   escalation. A next-action line for a cold deal should reflect that.
