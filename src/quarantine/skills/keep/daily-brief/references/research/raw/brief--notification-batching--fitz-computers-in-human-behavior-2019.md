# Batching smartphone notifications can improve well-being

- **URL:** https://static1.squarespace.com/static/57a40c19414fb54f51f8095f/t/685daca461a93c25f5b3dabe/1750969509917/2019+Fitz+Batching.pdf
- **Also indexed at:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563219302596
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Fitz et al., Computers in Human Behavior, 2019)

## Design

Randomized controlled field experiment. Custom Android app. Three weeks: week one
baseline, weeks two and three experimental. N = 237, recruited via Amazon Mechanical Turk
in India. Mean age 30.3, 19% female, 85% employed.

Four randomly assigned arms:

1. Control, notifications delivered normally at variable times.
2. Batched three times per day, at 09:00, 15:00 and 21:00.
3. Batched hourly, on the hour.
4. No notifications at all.

## Results

**Three times per day was the winning arm.** Participants "felt more attentive,
productive, in a better mood, and in greater control".

| Measure | Effect (Cohen's d) |
|---|---|
| Inattention | -0.65 |
| Concentration | +0.54 |
| Phone unlocks | -0.60 |
| Sense of phone control | +0.58 |
| Perceived productivity | +0.57 |
| Stress | -0.56 |
| Mood | +0.49 |

**Hourly batching produced minimal benefit.** It "did not differ from the control" except
for reducing the feeling of being interrupted.

**The no-notification arm produced harm, not benefit.** Participants "experienced higher
levels of anxiety and 'fear of missing out' (FoMO)": anxiety d = 0.56, phone-related FoMO
d = 0.59. There were no improvements in inattention or concentration. Participants "felt
no longer able to be as responsive as expected".

## Mechanisms reported

Inattention "fully mediated" the stress and productivity benefits of the three-times-daily
arm. Phone-related FoMO "fully explained" the anxiety observed in the no-notification arm.

## What this source does not establish

The study tested notification delivery frequency for a phone's whole notification stream.
It did not test the content quality of a digest, did not test a once-daily interval, and
did not measure long-run retention or abandonment of a digest product. The best measured
arm was three times daily, which is more frequent than a daily brief. Any argument from
this source to a once-daily cadence is an extrapolation and must be labelled as one.
