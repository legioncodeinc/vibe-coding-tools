# What I Learned Writing for the President's Daily Brief

- **URL:** https://www.thecipherbrief.com/column_article/what-i-learned-writing-for-the-presidents-daily-brief
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** practitioner (Becky Root, former PDB writer, first-hand account)

## Format and cadence

- Length: "typically just one page".
- Cadence: "seven days a week, 365 days a year".
- Audience: "the President and his key staff".
- Content: "articles on the top national security issues facing the United States".

## Structure

The PDB uses a Bottom Line Up Front structure: "the what, the why, and what's next/outlook.
This information is contained within the first sentence of your document, not buried later
in your piece."

## Selection discipline

The writer must "identify the most important information for your client". On padding:
"your client is paying for your analysis, they do not need to see all of your homework and
examples."

On the reader's constraint: "The President and his staff are busy, and intelligence
professionals strive to provide the most information they can, in the least amount of real
estate on a page."

## Credibility

Multi-round editing. "minor grammatical errors and formatting mistakes can put your
credibility at risk." Evidence-based argumentation is required.

## What is absent from this source

No word-count limits, no sentence-length rules, no measured claim about how much of the
PDB is actually read. The transferable content is the discipline, not a number: one page,
daily, bottom line in the first sentence, analysis rather than working material.
