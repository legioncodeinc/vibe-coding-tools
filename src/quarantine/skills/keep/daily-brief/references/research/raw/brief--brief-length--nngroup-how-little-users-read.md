# How Little Do Users Read?

- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** industry-research (Nielsen Norman Group, reanalysis of Weinreich et al.
  eyetracking and logging data)

## The measured findings

**Ceiling on reading.** "On the average Web page, users have time to read at most 28% of
the words during an average visit; 20% is more likely."

**The regression shape.** Visit duration decomposes into "a fixed time of about 25
seconds, plus an additional 4.4 seconds per 100 words".

**Marginal return on added words.** Users can read roughly 18 words in 4.4 seconds,
therefore when content is added to a page, assume "customers will read 18%" of the
addition. The other 82% is not read.

**The threshold that matters most for brief design.** "users read half the information
only on those pages with 111 words or less".

**Average page.** On the study's average page of 593 words, users "have time to read 28%"
if fully focused, realistically "read about 20% of the text".

## Direct design consequence

There are two distinct budgets, not one. A block of 111 words or fewer is read at roughly
half. Everything past that point is read at roughly 18% of the marginal words. A brief
that wants a specific sentence actually read must put it inside the first 111 words. Word
count past that point is not free, and it is not neutral either: it dilutes the share of
attention the earlier words receive.

## Scope caveat

This is web page reading behavior, not email or in-app report reading, and it predates
current AI-generated report formats. The 111-word threshold is a measured artifact of that
dataset. Treat it as a well-grounded order of magnitude for a scanning reader, not as a
constant of nature.
