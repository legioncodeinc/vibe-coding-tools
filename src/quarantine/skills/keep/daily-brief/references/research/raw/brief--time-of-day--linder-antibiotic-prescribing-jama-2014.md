# Time of Day and the Decision to Prescribe Antibiotics

- **URL:** https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1910546
- **Also indexed at:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25286067/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Linder et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, 2014)

## Design and sample

Retrospective analysis of "21 867 ARI visits to 204 clinicians in 23 practices" in one
health system, May 2011 through September 2012. ARI means acute respiratory infection, the
canonical setting where antibiotics are frequently prescribed inappropriately.

## Result

Overall, "44% resulted in antibiotic prescriptions."

Adjusted odds ratios for prescribing, relative to the first hour of the clinic session:

| Hour of session | Adjusted OR |
|---|---|
| First | 1.00 (reference) |
| Second | 1.01 |
| Third | 1.14 |
| Fourth | 1.26 |

P less than .001 for the linear trend. For the diagnoses where antibiotics are never
indicated, the fourth-hour increase was statistically significant, P less than .002.

## Authors' conclusion, quoted

The authors "found that primary care clinicians' likelihood of prescribing antibiotics for
ARIs increased during clinic sessions, consistent with the hypothesis that decision fatigue
progressively impairs clinicians' ability to resist ordering inappropriate treatments."

Suggested remedies: "time-dependent decision support, modified schedules, shorter sessions,
mandatory breaks, or snacks."

## Why this source is the load-bearing one on time of day

The magnitude is modest and honest. An odds ratio of 1.26 in the fourth hour is a real,
measured, within-session degradation that survives adjustment, and it is a fraction of the
size of the contested parole result. It supports a weak preference for surfacing a hard
decision early in a session. It does not support a claim that decisions later in the day
are unreliable.

## Caveats

Observational, single health system, one clinical decision type. Session position is not
the same as clock time, since sessions start at different hours. The authors themselves
frame it as "consistent with the hypothesis", not as proof of the mechanism.
