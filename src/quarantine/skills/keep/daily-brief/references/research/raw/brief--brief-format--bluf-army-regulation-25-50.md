# BLUF (communication)

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLUF_(communication)
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (encyclopedia entry summarizing Army Regulation 25-50)

## Definition

"Bottom Line Up Front, or BLUF, is the practice of beginning a message with its key
information (the 'bottom line')." Distinguished from an abstract by being "simpler and more
concise".

## Origin and standard

From "Army Regulation 25-50: Information Management: Records Management: Preparing and
Managing Correspondence", a roughly 100-page document setting military writing standards.
The regulation mandates "putting the main point at the beginning of the correspondence
(bottom line up front)".

Army writing must be "concise, organized, and to the point". Two core requirements: main
point first, and active voice. Target: "precise and lucid".

## Formatting rules

**Subject-line keywords in all caps signal what the reader must do:**

| Keyword | Meaning |
|---|---|
| INFO | Informational only |
| REQUEST | Seeks approval |
| ACTION | Recipient must respond |

**First paragraph rule.** The BLUF itself must answer who, what, when, where and why in
"clear, concise, and straightforward language".

**Length.** The entry says BLUF "is short and concise" and specifies no word count.

## Claim recorded but not accepted

The entry cites "47% greater return to shareholders over five years" for organizations
with effective communication. That figure is not about BLUF, has no methodology given
here, and is not usable. Recorded so it is not accidentally repeated as a BLUF result.

## Transferable rule

The ACTION / REQUEST / INFO label is the useful mechanic: the reader learns what is
demanded of them before reading the item, which lets them skip cleanly rather than read
defensively.
