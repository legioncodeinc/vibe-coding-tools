# What Is Eat the Frog? A Simple System for Productivity Minimalists

- **URL:** https://www.todoist.com/productivity-methods/eat-the-frog
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Todoist, task manager vendor, presenting the case FOR the
  method)

## The rule as stated

"If it's your job to eat a frog, it's best to do it first thing in the morning." Identify
the most important task and complete it before other work.

## How the frog is chosen

The frog is the Most Important Task, sitting in the "important but not urgent" quadrant.
It is "the task that makes you feel a sense of dread or reluctance because of its
complexity or significance."

## Size bound, which is the most useful specific here

"Your frog should take 1-4 hours or half a day of work (at most)."

## On the count of frogs per day

The page hedges: "Of course, you can always experiment to find out the exact number of
tasks you can complete in a day." It notes that Todoist's founder completes 15 to 25 tasks
daily but "aims to complete at least one high-impact task daily."

## Mechanisms invoked

- Planning fallacy: "Accurately forecasting tasks into the future is nearly impossible."
- Procrastination: "When we feel bad, we're more likely to avoid what's making us feel
  bad."
- Momentum: "When you experience a win first thing in the morning, you're more likely to
  build momentum."

## Stated limitation

"When tasks take too long or the goal isn't clear enough, you can feel overwhelmed and
fall into procrastination."

## Evidence quality

None of the three mechanisms is cited to a study on this page. This is a vendor account of
a popular-business-book method (Brian Tracy). It is archived as the canonical statement of
the pro position, not as evidence that the method works. The one durable, specific,
transferable item is the 1 to 4 hour size bound.
