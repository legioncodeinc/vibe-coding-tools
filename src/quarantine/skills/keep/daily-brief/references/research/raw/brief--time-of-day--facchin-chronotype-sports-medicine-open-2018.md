# The Effects of Time of Day and Chronotype on Cognitive and Physical Performance in Healthy Volunteers

- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-018-0162-z
- **Also indexed at:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6200828/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Sports Medicine Open, 2018)

## Design

Within-subjects repeated measures. 56 healthy participants: 25 early chronotypes (ECT), 31
late chronotypes (LCT). Three test points: 14:00, 20:00, and 08:00 the following morning.
Chronotype classified with the Munich ChronoType Questionnaire plus circadian phase markers
and actigraphy.

Mean age 21.8 (SD 3.8), 33 female, 21 male. ECT mean mid-sleep on free days 02:38 (SE
00:07); LCT 06:59 (SE 00:12). No group differences in age, height, weight or sleep
duration.

## Cognitive measures

1. Psychomotor Vigilance Task, 2 minutes, attentional vigilance.
2. Executive Function via the Memory and Attention Test, roughly 10 minutes, covering
   working memory, problem solving and decision making under increasingly complex rules.

## Results

**Vigilance (PVT).** Significant time-of-day by chronotype interaction, F(2,106) = 5.7,
p = 0.004. LCTs showed significant diurnal variation, morning significantly worse than
evening, p = 0.001. Diurnal change was 3.5% for ECTs versus 9.1% for LCTs. At 08:00,
"ECTs performed 8.4% better than LCTs", p = 0.004.

**Executive function.** Significant interaction, F(2,108) = 5.5, p = 0.005. ECTs were
significantly better in the morning than in the afternoon (p = 0.002) and evening
(p = 0.03). Diurnal variation was 7.1% for ECTs versus 2.6% for LCTs. At 08:00, "ECTs
performed 5.9% better than LCTs", p = 0.006.

## Conclusion, quoted

"LCTs were significantly impaired in all measures in the morning compared to ECTs."

## The reading that matters for scheduling

There is no universal cognitive peak hour. The interaction term is the finding: time of day
matters, but its direction is set by the individual's chronotype. Any product that hard
codes an early clock time as the high-performance window is right for one group and wrong
for the other.

## Caveats

Young sample, mean age 21.8, mostly students. Effects are single-digit percentages, not
transformative. Generalization to a working adult's decision quality over a full workday is
an extrapolation.
