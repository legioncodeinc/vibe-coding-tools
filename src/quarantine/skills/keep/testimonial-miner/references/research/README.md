# Research archive for testimonial-miner

Domain: the regulation of endorsements and testimonials in US advertising, the FTC's Rule on the
Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials and its enforcement record, permission and release
practice for using a customer's words, the ethics and mechanics of editing a quote, and what
makes a testimonial credible rather than generic. Swept 2026-08-17 with web search and direct
page fetches.

**This is not legal advice.** Nothing in this folder is. It is a research summary from public
sources assembled on one day, and advertising law varies by jurisdiction and changes.

## How to use this folder

Read `distilled-testimonial-practice.md` first. It is the only file the skill's guides cite
directly, and every claim in it ends in a bracketed pointer to a file in `raw/`. If a domain
claim appears anywhere in this skill without a trail through the distillation to a raw file, it
is a defect.

## Source count

Fifteen raw sources. Eight are FTC or eCFR primary documents, one is a state statute from the New
York State Senate's official legislation site, one is law-firm analysis, one is an academic study
summarized for practitioners, one is a user-research organization, and three are vendor or
practitioner blogs. The contract minimum is five and the assignment minimum is six, with a
stated preference for FTC primary sources over marketing blogs. Nine of fifteen are primary.

## Window

Default window was the last six months. Sources outside it, each flagged in its own header:

- `ftc--endorsement-guides-text--ecfr-part255.md`, current version dated July 26, 2023. It is the

## The honest headline about this archive

The regulatory half is strong. Eight FTC and eCFR primary documents, a state statute, and a
law-firm mapping of the actual 2026 enforcement record with named cases and real numbers. Claims
in the skill about what the law requires rest on the law's own text.

The persuasion half is weak. One genuine study, on retail product reviews rather than services.
One decade-old usability study. One practitioner blog with a good taxonomy and no evidence. The
2026 marketing content the sweep surfaced asserted conversion lift with no method disclosed, so
none of it was archived. **This archive cannot tell you what a testimonial is worth.** It can
tell you what makes one credible and what makes one legally dangerous. Sections 9 and 11 of the
distillation say so directly.

Section 12 of the distillation lists nine named gaps, including the two the FTC's own 119-question
FAQ does not answer: using a private DM as a testimonial, and editing a testimonial for length.
The skill's rules on both are house standards built on top of the primary sources, and are
labeled as house standards rather than as legal requirements.
