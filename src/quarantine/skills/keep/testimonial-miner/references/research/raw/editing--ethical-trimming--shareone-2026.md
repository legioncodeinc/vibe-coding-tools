## The organizing distinction

Two categories: **clarity edits** and **meaning edits**. Clarity edits are permissible. Meaning
edits are not. The stated principle: "edit for clarity, not for persuasion."

## Clarity edits, listed as acceptable

- Removing filler words such as "um", "like", "you know"
- Tightening a rambling sentence
- Cutting an off-topic tangent
- Adding captions that match the spoken words
- Minor reordering where meaning is unchanged

## Meaning edits, listed as prohibited

- Changing the core claim or outcome
- Upgrading an adjective, the example given being "somewhat" becoming "dramatically"
- Removing a timeframe, a condition, or a qualifier
- Splicing separate answers together to create implied causation
- Altering numbers or results
- Framing a personal experience as a universal guarantee
- Using b-roll that implies a stronger outcome than the words stated

## The nine rules as enumerated

1. "Never modify the core result, outcome, or experience"
2. "Context is part of the claim", meaning timeframes and conditions are preserved
3. Do not splice to create new meaning
4. Maintain accurate timeframes
5. Avoid guarantee framing
6. Use plain-English disclaimers where results vary
7. Obtain written consent documenting the scope of usage
8. Offer the customer approval of the final edit
9. Archive the raw files and transcripts

## The qualifier argument, quoted

"'It worked after we changed our process' is different than 'It worked'"

The source's reasoning is that removing such context lets a viewer "mentally fill in the
blank ... with the best possible outcome." That is the mechanism by which a truthful trim becomes
a false impression, and it is the reason a trimming rule has to protect conditions and
timeframes specifically rather than just protecting word count.

## The compliance framing

"preserving the original meaning of a customer's words is a compliance requirement, not just an
ethical preference"

Unsourced on the page, but it is consistent with 16 CFR 255.1's honest-opinion requirement and
with the FTC FAQ's treatment of altered reviews as a reportable complaint
(`ftc--endorsement-guides-text--ecfr-part255.md`,
`ftc--endorsement-faq--what-people-are-asking.md`).

## The approval workflow recommended

- Disclosure before the interview
- A written usage agreement specifying the channels
- Optional final customer approval of the edit
- File naming and archival documentation

## Gap this source does not fill

It does not define an ellipsis convention, does not say whether bracketed insertions are
acceptable, and does not address combining two quotes from the same person on different dates.
No source in this archive does. The skill's trimming rules therefore have to be authored as a
house standard built on top of the clarity-versus-meaning distinction, and must be labeled as a
house standard rather than as a legal requirement.
