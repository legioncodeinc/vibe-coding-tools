# New York Civil Rights Law section 51, action for injunction and for damages

- **Title:** New York Consolidated Laws, Civil Rights Law, section 51
- **URL:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVR/51
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (state statutory text, via the New York State Senate's official
  legislation site)

## The operative language

"Any person whose name, portrait, picture, likeness or voice is used within this state for
advertising purposes or for the purposes of trade without the written consent first obtained as
above provided may maintain an equitable action"

## Remedies

- Injunctive relief "to prevent and restrain the use thereof"
- The right to "sue and recover damages for any injuries sustained by reason of such use"
- Exemplary damages: "if the defendant shall have knowingly used such person's name, portrait,
  picture, likeness or voice in such manner as is forbidden or declared to be unlawful by section
  fifty of this article, the jury, in its discretion, may award exemplary damages."

## Exceptions noted in the text

Transfer of materials containing a likeness to authorized users; exhibition of photographs by
professional photographers unless objected to in writing; and use of a manufacturer's or artist's
name or likeness in connection with goods or creative works they have sold.

Section 50 establishes the unlawful uses that section 51 supplies the remedy for. Its full text
was not returned in this fetch. Recorded as a gap.

## Why this is in the archive

Nothing in the FTC Endorsement Guides or in 16 CFR Part 465 requires a business to obtain a
person's permission before quoting them. **The permission requirement comes from somewhere else
entirely: state right of publicity and privacy statutes.** New York is archived here as one
concrete, quotable example of the standard, and the standard it sets is high: *written* consent,
obtained *first*, before use for advertising purposes or purposes of trade.

This is a state-by-state area of law. New York's rule is not automatically the rule where any
given user or customer sits. A second source in this archive,
`permission--testimonial-consent-practice--testimonialdonut-2025.md`, names California Civil Code
3344 as a parallel provision, also requiring prior written consent. This archive contains no
survey of all fifty states and does not attempt one. Named gap.

Note the word "knowingly" attached to exemplary damages. It parallels the "knowing violation"
standard for FTC civil penalties. In both regimes, the aggravating factor is knowing that you did
not have the right and using it anyway. That is precisely what a permission tier prevents.
