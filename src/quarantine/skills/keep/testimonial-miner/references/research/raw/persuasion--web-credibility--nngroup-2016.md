# Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors

- **Title:** Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors (Nielsen Norman Group)
- **Author:** Aurora Harley
- **Published:** May 8, 2016
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** user-research organization
- **WINDOW EXCEPTION:** published 2016, far outside the six-month default window. Retained
  because it is the only source found in this sweep that reports observed user reactions to
  on-site testimonials rather than asserting their effectiveness. The 2026 searches returned
  only marketing-vendor content restating conversion claims with no method behind them. Its age

## Method as reported

A usability study run during a UX conference in Singapore. The article does not state a
participant count. Findings below are qualitative observations from that study.

## The four credibility factors

1. **Design quality.** "Typos, broken links, and other mistakes quickly degrade credibility."
2. **Upfront disclosure.** Pricing, fees, shipping, contact information, return policies,
   delivery timeframes. Users distrust sites that hide these.
3. **Comprehensive, correct and current content.** Thorough, relevant information that
   demonstrates expertise, covering the full range of what is offered.
4. **Connected to the rest of the web.** External validation through social media, review sites
   and news outlets. Isolated sites "appear to either have something to hide or not be a fully
   established, stable company."

## The finding that matters most here

Quoted: "People have learned to trust these external sources more than company-sponsored
content."

Every participant said they would read reviews before hiring a service.

**On testimonials specifically:** testimonials displayed on a company's own website drew
skepticism. Participants observed that "the website would of course include only positive
reviews." External review sites carried greater credibility than site testimonials.

## Applied reading

This is the strongest available argument for why the permission tier is a product feature and not
just a compliance chore. A quote that can be pointed back to a public, checkable source, a real
review page, a real public post, carries the external-validation property users said they trust.
A quote that exists only inside the company's own marketing page is the exact artifact
participants discounted.

It also argues against over-polishing. A trimmed, smoothed, uniform-sounding wall of praise on an
owned page reads as company-sponsored content, which is the category users reported trusting
least.
