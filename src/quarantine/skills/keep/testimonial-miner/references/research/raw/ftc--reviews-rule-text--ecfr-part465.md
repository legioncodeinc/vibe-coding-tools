## Extracted definitions (465.1)

- **Consumer review:** "a consumer's evaluation ... of a product, service, or business ...
  published to a website or platform"
- **Consumer testimonial:** "an advertising or promotional message ... that consumers are
  likely to believe reflects the opinions, beliefs, or experiences of a consumer"
- **Celebrity testimonial:** the same construction, but from "a well-known individual"
- **Clear and conspicuous:** a disclosure must be "easily noticeable ... and easily
  understandable by ordinary consumers"

The consumer review definition is tied to publication on a website or platform dedicated to
receiving and displaying evaluations. The consumer testimonial definition is not: it turns on
whether the message is an advertising or promotional message consumers are likely to believe
reflects a real consumer's experience. **A private DM praising a business is neither, until the
business turns it into an advertising or promotional message, at which point it becomes a
consumer testimonial.**

## Extracted prohibitions

**465.2, fake or false.** Prohibits writing, creating, selling, purchasing or disseminating
reviews or testimonials that misrepresent that the reviewer exists, or that the reviewer "used
or otherwise had experience with" the subject product or service. Covers purchasing or
disseminating material a business "knew or should have known" contained material
misrepresentations. Also reaches reviews procured from officers, managers, employees or their
immediate relatives that carry material misrepresentations. Carve-out for generalized
solicitations and for merely hosting consumer reviews.

**465.4, buying sentiment.** Prohibits providing "compensation or other incentives in exchange
for ... consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative."

**465.5, insiders.** Officers and managers must disclose a "material relationship to the
business" when posting reviews or testimonials. Officers and managers may not solicit insider
reviews "without a disclosure of the reviewer's material relationship," and may not solicit
them while encouraging non-disclosure.

**465.6, controlled review sites.** Prohibits materially misrepresenting that a controlled
entity provides "independent reviews or opinions."

**465.7, suppression.** Prohibits legal threats, physical threats, or a "public false
accusation" used to prevent or remove a review. Also prohibits misrepresenting that displayed
reviews "represent most or all the reviews submitted" when negative reviews were suppressed by
rating or sentiment.

**465.8, fake influence indicators.** Prohibits selling, distributing or procuring "fake
indicators of social media influence ... that materially misrepresent their influence or
importance for a commercial purpose."

**465.9, severability.** Provisions are "separate and severable from one another."

## What this source does NOT say

It does not prohibit selecting only the positive quotes a business already holds and putting
them in marketing. That specific practice is addressed in the FTC's own Q and A document, and
the answer there is that Part 465 does not cover it while the FTC Act separately might. See
`ftc--reviews-rule-qa--ftc-2024.md`.

It contains no permission requirement. Nothing in Part 465 requires a business to obtain a
person's consent before quoting them. The consent requirement comes from state right of
publicity law, recorded in `publicity--written-consent--ny-cvr-51.md`.
