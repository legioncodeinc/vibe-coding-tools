# Research: What Consumers Find Persuasive in Online Reviews

- **Title:** Research: What Consumers Find Persuasive in Online Reviews (Harvard Business Review)
- **Authors:** Jha, Biswas, and Ravula
- **Published:** February 18, 2025
- **URL:** https://hbr.org/2025/02/research-what-consumers-find-persuasive-in-online-reviews
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic research summarized for a practitioner audience. The underlying
  papers were not retrieved in this sweep, so effect sizes beyond what the summary states are
  not available here.

## Method as reported

Analysis of 111,728 purchase reviews, plus experiments with more than 2,000 participants.

## The finding

Persuasiveness depends on purchase frequency, and the two competing signals are a
**recommendation** ("I recommend this") and a **repurchase intention** ("I would buy this
again").

Quoted: "For items that are purchased only rarely, recommendations seem to be the most
persuasive. But when items are likely to be bought frequently, for example, cosmetics or bottles
of wine, it's most persuasive when reviewers indicate they would buy that item again."

Mechanism, quoted: "Recommendations are viewed as more credible than repurchase intentions for
infrequently bought items, while the opposite is true for frequently purchased goods or
services."

Context figure quoted from the article: "As many as 98% report that they rely on reviews to
inform purchase decisions." No primary attribution for that 98 percent is given in the summary,
so it is recorded as a second-hand statistic.

## Applied reading for a services business

Professional services, agency retainers and consulting engagements are infrequent purchases. On
this finding, the highest-value quote form for that kind of business is an explicit
recommendation, not a statement of intent to buy again. That gives the skill a concrete sorting
signal: a quote containing a recommendation ranks above a quote containing only satisfaction.

**Caveat that must travel with this claim.** The study is about product reviews on a retail
platform. Extending it to B2B services quotes is an inference, not a finding. Stated as such
wherever it is used.
