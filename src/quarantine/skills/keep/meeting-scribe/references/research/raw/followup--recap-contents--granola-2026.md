# Follow-up email after meeting: templates and examples that work

- **Title:** Follow-up email after meeting: Templates and examples that work
- **Publisher:** Granola (AI meeting notetaker vendor)
- **URL:** https://www.granola.ai/blog/follow-up-email-after-meeting-template
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Published:** 2026-05-07
- **Source type:** vendor-blog. Commercial interest in meeting-notes tooling. Treat
  prescriptive numbers as house style, not as measured findings, because the piece cites
  no study for its own word count.

## What the piece says a follow-up must contain

Four required elements, quoted:

1. **Specific meeting references.** "References something said or decided in the meeting,
   not just the meeting topic"
2. **Clear action items.** Documented with named owners and deadlines.
3. **Supporting materials.** "If you agreed to send a document, a deck, or a Notion link,
   it goes in this email"
4. **A single ask.** "One clear call to action. Multiple asks create friction and reduce
   response rates"

## Length

"Keep it under 150 words" with "Short paragraphs of two to three sentences" and bullets
for action items.

No citation is given for the 150 word figure.

## Timing

Send "within 24 hours of the conversation."

Stated rationale: "The Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve shows people lose a significant portion
of new information within a day without reinforcement."

That is a loose invocation of a 19th century nonsense-syllable memory study. The piece
does not cite the original, and the forgetting curve was not measured on business
conversations. Treat the 24 hour window as a practice norm with a decorative
justification, not as evidence.

## Subject lines

Specificity over generic phrasing. Instead of "Following up from our call," the piece
recommends a form like "Series A follow-up: NRR data + next steps."

Claim quoted: "Personalized subject lines increase open rates by 26% compared to generic
ones." No source is given for the 26 percent figure. Do not repeat this number.

## Tone

Precision and directness. Reference "the exact words someone used in conversation" to
signal genuine attention.

## What the piece says to leave out

- Generic openings without specific detail
- Vague next steps such as "let me know if you have any questions"
- Unfilled template placeholders
- "Unnecessary information unrelated to the meeting's actual discussion"

## Direct implication for the skill

The instruction to reference the exact words someone used is the load-bearing one, and it
is exactly what a Littlebird transcript can supply and a generic template cannot. The last
exclusion, unnecessary information unrelated to the discussion, is the seed of the
confidentiality filter: a recap built by pasting a full internal summary violates it by
construction.
