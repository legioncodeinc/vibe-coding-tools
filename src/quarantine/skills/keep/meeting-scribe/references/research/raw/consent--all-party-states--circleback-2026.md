# Recording consent for AI meeting notes

- **Title:** Recording Consent for AI Meeting Notes: What You Need to Know
- **Publisher:** Circleback (AI meeting notetaker vendor)
- **URL:** https://circleback.ai/blog/recording-consent-for-ai-meeting-notes
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog. A notetaker vendor writing about the legality of
  notetakers has an obvious interest. Treat the practice guidance as usable and the
  jurisdiction list as a claim to be checked, especially where it conflicts with the
  legal-reference source in this archive.

## The all-party consent list this source gives

Eleven states named: "California, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Maryland,
Massachusetts, Michigan, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, and Washington."

**This list conflicts with the Recording Law source in this archive.** See
`consent--state-law-map--recordinglaw-2026.md`. The conflict is unresolved here and must
be reported as a conflict, never smoothed.

## Multi-state calls

Quoted: "When participants sit in different states, the prevailing legal guidance is to
follow the most restrictive state's requirements."

## The three practice norms

1. **Disclose before recording.** Quoted: "Disclose before recording. Many teams tell
   participants the meeting will be recorded and that AI will process the audio."
2. **Get acknowledgment.** In all-party consent states and under GDPR where consent is the
   lawful basis, the source states that affirmative agreement, rather than mere absence of
   objection, is generally expected.
3. **Keep a policy.** Organizations should "maintain a recording policy that specifies
   what is recorded, how recordings are stored and retained, who can access them, and how
   participants can request deletion."

## The bot-in-the-room finding

Quoted: "No jurisdiction currently treats the visible presence of a recording bot in a
meeting's participant list as legally sufficient notice or consent."

This is the single most load-bearing line in the archive for this skill. The user's meeting
was recorded. A visible notetaker in the participant list did not, by itself, tell anyone
anything that counts.

## On objections

Quoted: "If any participant objects to recording, the safest and most professional course
of action is to stop recording or not begin."

## Direct implication for the skill

A recap that quotes a call verbatim announces, to everyone who reads it, that the call was
recorded and transcribed. Where the attendees were never told, that announcement lands as
a surprise. The skill therefore flags verbatim quoting to the user as a disclosure event
before the draft is written, rather than after the recipient reads it.
