# Designing organizational memory: preserving intellectual assets in a knowledge economy

- **Title:** Designing Organizational Memory: Preserving Intellectual Assets in a
  Knowledge Economy
- **Author:** Jeff Conklin, CogNexus Institute
- **URL:** https://cognexus.org/dom.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Published:** 1997
- **Source type:** academic-grade monograph from a research institute. Peer-adjacent
  rather than peer-reviewed, and the author is the originator of the IBIS and dialogue
  mapping methods it recommends, so the enthusiasm is not disinterested.

## Note on window

1997, far outside the window. Retained because it is the foundational statement of the
decision-rationale-loss problem, it is not superseded, and it is the only source in this
archive that offers a quantified return on decision capture.

## Organizational forgetting

Quoted: organizations routinely "forget" what they have done in the past and why they have
done it (1997, p. 3). Conklin's argument is that this cripples organizational learning
because the critical parts of the knowledge were never representable in the artifacts the
organization kept.

## What has to be captured

Quoted: organizations must learn to "capture the decision, the rationale behind it, the
open questions related to it, the assumptions behind it, and any related supporting
information" (1997, p. 33).

Decision rationale is named as "a critically important part" of informal knowledge that
organizations systematically fail to preserve.

## The specific indictment of meeting minutes

Quoted: "meeting minutes are sketchy, represent only one person's point of view, and
usually lack the energy and context of the conversations they were meant to capture"
(1997, p. 16).

## The one quantified claim

A software team using IBIS-structured meeting records reviewed those records and "found 11
errors in the software and its specification," and calculated they "saved between three
and six times the cost of documenting their design thinking in IBIS" (1997, p. 34).

Caveats that must travel with this number: single team, single project, self-calculated
return, reported by the method's own author, 1997. It is an existence proof that capture
can pay for itself, not a general effect size.

## Direct implication for the skill

Three things. First, the open questions belong in the captured record alongside the
decision, which is the research basis for treating unresolved items as a first-class
output rather than an afterthought. Second, the "only one person's point of view"
criticism is the case for anchoring decision entries in verbatim quotes with timestamps
rather than in the scribe's reading. Third, this is the earliest clear statement that the
rationale, not the outcome, is the thing that goes missing.
