# Parking lots in UX meetings and workshops

- **Title:** Parking Lots in UX Meetings and Workshops
- **Author:** Sarah Gibbons, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/parking-lots/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Published:** 2019-09-08, last reviewed 2024-12-12
- **Source type:** practitioner research organization. NN/g publishes method guidance
  grounded in its own usability research practice. Not a controlled study.

## Note on window

Published 2019, reviewed by the publisher in December 2024. The publisher's own review
date is inside a reasonable currency window even though first publication is not.

## Definition

Quoted: "A parking lot is a place to capture comments, topics, or questions that are not
related to the meeting agenda."

## Why items get parked

To hold focus on the agenda and prevent scope creep during a working session.

## The failure mode, which is the reason this source is here

Quoted: "Parking lots should offer outlines for future discussion, research, or meetings.
If they do not inform future action, they become only a way to kindly tell someone that
their contribution is worthless."

This is the clearest available statement that a deferred item is a live obligation, and
that deferral without a return path is a social cost rather than a neutral act.

## Recommended handling

- Include parking lot items in the meeting follow-up
- Assign owner and timeline using the W.W.W. frame: who does what, when
- Group related items by affinity before assigning
- Either reserve the final 20 to 30 minutes of the session for them, or schedule a
  dedicated follow-up one to two days later
- Always carry parking lot to-dos into the meeting notes to maintain accountability

## Scope limitation

This addresses items EXPLICITLY parked by a facilitator. It does not address the harder
case: a question that was asked, not acknowledged, and talked over, which nobody parked
because nobody registered it. The literature sweep found no source on that case. Named
gap.

## Direct implication for the skill

The parked item and the talked-over question are the same object with different
visibility. NN/g supplies the handling rule for both: it goes into the follow-up, it gets
an owner and a date, and an item with neither is reported as unowned rather than dropped.
