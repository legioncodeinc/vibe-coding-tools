# How to use a decision log for optimal results for your project

- **Title:** How to Use a Decision Log for Optimal Results for Your Project
- **Author:** Peter Landau
- **Publisher:** ProjectManager.com (project management software vendor)
- **URL:** https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/project-decision-log
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Published:** 2025-05-27
- **Source type:** vendor-blog. Commercial interest in project management tooling. The
  field list is conventional PMI-style practice and is the usable part; the reasoning is
  asserted rather than evidenced.

## Fields in a decision log

- Project name
- Project manager
- Identification number
- Decision title
- Date
- Area
- Description
- Rationale
- Alternatives
- Expected impact
- Contributors
- Approval signature

## Why teams keep one

Stated purposes:

- **Communication.** Decision logs "deliver supporting information to stakeholders."
- **Defensibility.** Quoted: "a decision log is a way to communicate to them and also
  support the decision" when stakeholders question a choice. It becomes essential "when
  you are put in a position to defend your decision."
- **Preventing re-litigation.** The log "can merely be a reminder of the course of action"
  and helps teams avoid "unnecessary conflict."
- **Reuse.** Logs provide "historic data necessary to plan future projects."

## When entries are written

"whenever there's a decision made in the project or when running a business," with the
guidance emphasizing decisions of meaningful impact rather than minor choices.

## What the piece does not establish

No study, no measurement, no evidence that keeping a log changes outcomes. The
defensibility claim in particular is the plausible-sounding assertion that the whole
category rests on, and this source does not test it.

## Direct implication for the skill

Two fields matter more than the rest for a meeting-derived log and are not in the
Littlebird summary's own Decisions block: **Alternatives** (what was considered and
rejected) and **Contributors** (who was in the room). The defensibility framing is the
honest statement of what a decision log is for, and it is precisely why a decision entry
needs the quote and the timestamp behind it rather than a paraphrase.
