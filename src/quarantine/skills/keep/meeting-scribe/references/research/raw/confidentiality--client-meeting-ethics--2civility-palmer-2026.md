# When AI notetakers enter your client meetings

- **Title:** When AI Notetakers Enter Your Client Meetings: Ethical Duties and Risks for
  Lawyers
- **Author:** Mark C. Palmer
- **Publisher:** 2Civility, Illinois Supreme Court Commission on Professionalism
- **URL:** https://www.2civility.org/when-ai-notetakers-enter-your-client-meetings-ethical-duties-and-risks-for-lawyers/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Published:** 2026-05-15
- **Source type:** professional regulator commentary. The most institutionally weighty
  source in this archive on disclosure norms, though its duty framework is specific to
  lawyers.

## Undisclosed recording

Quoted: "An attorney should not secretly activate an AI notetaker during a client
conversation."

Rationale quoted: "Undisclosed recording is inconsistent with the candor and honesty
lawyers owe clients and runs afoul of the trust at the heart of the attorney-client
relationship."

The duty is professional, but the mechanism is general: undisclosed recording is a trust
event independent of whether it is a legal one.

## The verification duty

Quoted: "An AI summary of a client meeting should not be relied upon until the
participating lawyer has reviewed and verified it."

This is the strongest statement in the archive that the AI summary is a draft input and
not a finding. It comes from a regulator, not a vendor.

## Diligence on the tool

Before enabling transcription, the practitioner must investigate "where the data goes, how
long it is retained, whether the vendor can use the content to train its models."

## When the other side brings their own tool

Quoted: "When clients use tools unfamiliar to the lawyer, the lawyer may never see the
recordings, transcripts, or summaries, and has no ability to verify their accuracy."

Recommended response: engagement letters should state that "any recordings, transcripts,
or summaries generated by client-selected AI tools are not dispositive or binding on the
lawyer unless promptly shared."

## The permanence point

A privileged conversation can become "a permanent, searchable record" held by a cloud
vendor, with or without either party's consent.

## Direct implication for the skill

Two rules. The user reviews the actual final text before it leaves, because a regulator
says relying on an unverified AI summary is a lapse and the same logic covers sending one.
And where the other party ran their own notetaker, the user's recap is now one of two
competing written records of the same call, which raises the value of quoting exactly and
lowers the tolerance for paraphrase.
