## Why this source is outside the 6-month window and kept anyway

The default window for this archive is the last 6 months. This is from 2015. It is kept
because it contains the single most useful operational rule located anywhere in the
sweep for restraining an over-eager editing pass, and because copyediting practice on
this point has not changed. Nothing in the recent window states it as plainly.

## Findings

### The restraint rule

The rule, in the author's own words:

> "If the only reason I can muster for revising text is 'I would never write it like
> that,' I leave the sentence alone."

And the boundary it draws: avoid revising text when the only problem is that the author
has written something in a way that the copyeditor would not have.

This is the anti-overcorrection principle in one sentence, and it is directly
transferable. An automated voice pass has exactly the failure mode this rule guards
against, amplified, because the model has a stronger house style than any human
copyeditor and no hesitation about applying it.

### What non-grammatical revision IS licensed

Evans permits revision of grammatically sound text only where it is **repetitive,
awkward, or unnecessarily wordy**. Three named conditions. Nothing else.

### Querying versus silent change

Editors who are prone to inserting their own voice should err on the side of querying the
author, even when they are certain they have not altered the intended meaning. Querying
every minor change would be burdensome, so querying is reserved for genuine uncertainty
about the revision approach.

### The honest admission

Evans states directly that there is really no way to provide a how-to lesson on
preserving voice, and describes experienced copyeditors as simply knowing when a sentence
does not fit. She is explicit that the skill is learned by experience rather than by
formula.

## Claims this source supports

1. The operative test for whether an edit is legitimate is **whether a reason other than
   personal preference can be stated**. "I would not have written it that way" is not a
   reason.
2. Revision of grammatically sound prose is licensed on three conditions only:
   repetitive, awkward, unnecessarily wordy.
3. An editor prone to imposing their own voice should query rather than change.
4. Professional practice does not claim voice preservation is reducible to a rule set.
   A skill that claims to have reduced it to one is overclaiming.
