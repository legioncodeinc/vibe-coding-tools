# For Style's Sake: Balancing Author Voice and Publisher Style in Scientific Manuscripts

- **Title:** For Style's Sake: Balancing Author Voice and Publisher Style in Scientific
  Manuscripts
- **Author:** Kevin Gladish
- **Publisher:** Science Editor (Council of Science Editors)
- **Published:** 14 August 2025
- **URL:** https://www.csescienceeditor.org/article/balancing-author-voice-and-publisher-style/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (professional association publication, practitioner
  interviews)

## Why this source matters here

It is the most recent located treatment of the specific conflict this skill creates on
every run: a house rule says one thing and the writer's own habit says another. The
finding is that the profession does NOT resolve this with a rule, which is itself the
result worth recording.

## Findings

### What the article does and does not give

It gives principles from practitioner interviews. It does **not** supply an explicit
rule set separating required style changes from voice-altering ones, and it does not
categorize edits as legitimate versus overreach.

That absence is recorded here as a finding, not a retrieval failure. Two professional
sources in this archive, this one and the Cell Mentor piece, both decline to reduce
voice preservation to a procedure.

### Principles stated

- **From the editor side (LaPointe):** on some points, the author knows best. Editors
  should respect an author's expertise in deciding whether to stand by a style rule or
  to be flexible.
- **From the author side (Dos Santos):** authors should advocate for something changed
  in the editing process, particularly with technical terms, when the change alters the
  meaning of the work.

### The framing used instead of categories

- **Legitimate function of style editing:** creating consistency, clarity and accuracy,
  and improving reader comprehension.
- **Overreach:** style edits that compromise accuracy, or that confuse the target
  audience.

The emphasis throughout is on communication, flexibility and transparency about editorial
decisions, with author input retained on technical terminology.

## Claims this source supports

1. Current professional practice does not resolve voice-versus-house-style with a rule.
   It resolves it with a conversation between two named parties.
2. The stated legitimate purposes of style editing are consistency, clarity, accuracy and
   comprehension. Anything outside those four is not defended in this source.
3. Overreach is defined by its EFFECT, compromising accuracy or confusing the audience,
   not by the size of the change.
4. Where a style change alters meaning, the author is the one who decides.

## Limits of this source for our purpose

- Scientific publishing. House style there is a published standard with an institution
  behind it. A personal voice skill is neither.
- Interview-based practitioner commentary. No measurement.
- The conflict-resolution mechanism it describes, two humans talking, is unavailable to
  an automated pass, which is precisely why an automated pass must hand conflicts back
  to the user rather than deciding them.
