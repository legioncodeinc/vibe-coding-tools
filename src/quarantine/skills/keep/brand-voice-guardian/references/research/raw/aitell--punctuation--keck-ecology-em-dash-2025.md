# The Rise of the Em Dash in Ecology Abstracts

- **Title:** The Rise of the Em Dash in Ecology Abstracts
- **Author:** Francois Keck
- **Publication:** Piece of K (personal research blog, independent analysis, code published)
- **Published:** 8 July 2025
- **URL:** https://www.pieceofk.fr/the-rise-of-the-em-dash-in-ecology-abstracts/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (independent measured analysis, not peer-reviewed, code public)

## Why this source matters here

The em dash is the single most talked-about AI tell and almost all of the talk is
anecdote. This is one of the few places where somebody actually counted, on a real
corpus, with a stated method and published code. It is not peer-reviewed, so it is
archived as a measurement rather than as an authority.

## Method

- Corpus: 10,000 English-language ecology abstracts from OpenAlex.
- Filter: moderate length only, 600 to 3,500 characters.
- Comparison: 2021 (pre-ChatGPT) versus 2025.
- Measure: frequency of a range of punctuation and special characters (commas, colons,
  asterisks, ampersands, question marks, em dashes and others), normalized by total
  character count.
- Code made publicly available.

## Findings

- Em dash frequency **more than doubled** across the four-year period.
- **No other punctuation mark showed a comparable increase.** The em dash is singled out
  by contrast with the rest of the punctuation inventory, which is what makes the result
  interesting rather than a general formatting drift.
- The author reads this as a very strong indication that LLMs may have already left a
  mark on the literature.

## Caveats the author raises himself

- The finding is explicitly **not proof of direct causality**, only correlation.
- The author flags uncertainty about punctuation marks that DECLINED over the same
  window, such as the semicolon, and questions whether an alternative LLM-related
  mechanism explains those rather than direct AI authorship.

## Claims this source supports

1. Em dash frequency in a scientific corpus more than doubled between 2021 and 2025.
2. The increase is specific to the em dash among punctuation marks in that corpus.
3. The author of the measurement declines to claim causality, and any skill citing this
   should decline too.

## Limits of this source for our purpose

- Ecology abstracts. A formal academic register with its own conventions. Not a social
  post and not an email.
- Corpus-level frequency, no per-document classification. Says nothing about whether any
  individual abstract was LLM-assisted.
- Blog publication, not peer review. Method and code are stated, which is why it is
  archived, but it has had no external scrutiny.
