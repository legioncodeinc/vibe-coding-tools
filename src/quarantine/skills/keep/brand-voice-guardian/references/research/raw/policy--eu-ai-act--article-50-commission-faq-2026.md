# Transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act

- **Title:** Transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act (FAQ)
- **Publisher:** European Commission, Shaping Europe's Digital Future
- **URL:** https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
- **Related:** https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (regulator, first-party)

## Why this source matters here

This is the only located legal instrument that speaks directly to AI-generated TEXT and
to what human editing does to the obligation. It matters to a voice QA skill for a reason
that is easy to miss: the exemption is defined by WHO TOOK RESPONSIBILITY, not by how
human the text sounds. A skill whose whole job is making machine text sound human has to
be clear that sounding human is not the legal test.

## Findings

### Deployer obligation for text

Deployers must clearly label AI-generated or manipulated text published for the purpose
of informing the public on matters of public interest.

Three criteria apply cumulatively. The text must be:

1. **published**,
2. **informative to the public**, and
3. concerned with **a matter of public interest**.

Public interest is enumerated as including politics, public administration, justice,
fundamental rights, public security, health, environmental protection, consumer safety,
and economic, financial, scientific or cultural developments.

### The human review and editorial control exemption

Content that has undergone human review or editorial control does not require labeling.
The FAQ defines the terms:

- **Human review**: deliberate examination of the substance of the content by one or more
  natural persons possessing relevant knowledge and professional judgement.
- **Editorial control**: control exercised in practice by a responsible editorial entity
  with authority to approve, alter or reject the substance.
- **Editorial responsibility**: a person must hold ultimate legal responsibility over the
  publication of the content, including the human review or editorial control.

**The exclusion that bites:** superficial, solely formal, or procedural checks, given as
examples spell-checking and grammatical correction, **do not qualify**.

### Provider obligation

Providers of AI systems generating synthetic content must ensure outputs are marked in a
machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.

### Dates

Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026** for most obligations. A limited grace period
runs to **2 December 2026** for marking obligations on systems placed on the market
before 2 August 2026.

## Claims this source supports

1. There exists a live legal regime, applicable from 2 August 2026, requiring disclosure
   of AI-generated published text on matters of public interest.
2. The exemption from labeling turns on human review by a person with relevant knowledge
   who holds ultimate legal responsibility, and on control over the SUBSTANCE.
3. **Formal or procedural passes, explicitly including spell-check and grammar
   correction, do not qualify as human review.** A tone-only rewrite that does not touch
   substance and carries no named responsible person is closer to the excluded category
   than the exempting one.
4. Providers must mark synthetic output machine-readably. That marking is a separate
   channel from stylistic detection, and stylistic editing does not address it.

## Limits of this source for our purpose

- EU jurisdiction. Says nothing about obligations elsewhere.
- Applies to published text on matters of public interest. An ordinary marketing post or
  a client email will frequently fall outside all three criteria.
- An FAQ interpreting a regulation is guidance, not the regulation, and not case law.
  Nobody should take legal advice from a skill.
- This archive did NOT determine whether a voice-matching rewrite counts as human review.
  It records the definitions and the explicit exclusion, and stops there.
