the only first-party statement in the archive. It is a corporate announcement, so it says
what LinkedIn WANTS to be true about its feed, not how the ranker actually weights

## Extracted content

**Ranking changes.** LinkedIn says it is "rolling out more advanced models that better
understand what posts are about" and is deploying "Generative Recommenders, augmented with
large language models (LLMs)."

**Enforcement.** The company states it is taking "action to stop behaviors that promote
inauthentic engagement, including comment automation, engagement pods, and unauthorized
third-party tools."

**More of.** "more posts that offer genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful
perspectives."

**Less of.** "repetitive, low-substance posts and engagement bait, such as 'comment to
agree' prompts or videos that don't match the text."

**Not stated.** The announcement makes no claim about original versus reposted content, no
claim about knowledge-and-advice content as a named category, and gives no numbers.

## Claims this source supports

1. The one first-party statement available says the platform is optimizing toward
   substance and away from formulaic engagement bait. A skill that turns real spoken
   expertise into posts is aligned with the stated direction.
2. Repetition is explicitly named as demoted. That is the strongest available argument for
   this skill's de-duplication rule, since the same strong opinion recurs across many
   calls and would otherwise be posted repeatedly.
3. Engagement-bait openers ("comment to agree") are named by the platform as suppressed.
   Do not draft them.
4. Ranking is now LLM-driven and understands what a post is ABOUT. Structural gaming
   tactics from vendor blogs are a weaker bet than substance.
5. This is a stated intention, not a measured ranking description. Every use of it must be
   framed as "LinkedIn says", never as "the algorithm rewards".
