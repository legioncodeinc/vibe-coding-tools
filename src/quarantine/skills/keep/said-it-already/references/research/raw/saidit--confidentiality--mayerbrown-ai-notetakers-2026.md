## Scope note

This source addresses organizational governance of AI notetakers, not personal content
publishing. It is archived because it establishes what categories of meeting content carry
legal exposure, which is exactly what a confidentiality screen has to detect. It is not
legal advice and this skill does not give legal advice.

## Extracted content

**The persistence problem, which is the core insight for this skill.** AI notetakers
"transform conversations that would otherwise fade from memory into searchable, reusable
records capable of circulating well beyond their original context."

**Consent law.** US federal law requires one-party consent, but California, Florida,
Illinois, Pennsylvania, and Washington require all-party consent. The California Invasion
of Privacy Act "separately prohibits unauthorized third parties from 'reading, attempting
to read, or learning the contents'" of communications without consent. The recommended
approach is "jurisdiction mapping to identify applicable consent standards based on
participant locations, applying the strictest consent standard."

**Privilege waiver.** Citing United States v. Heppner (2026), a court declined to extend
attorney-client privilege to materials prepared using consumer AI platforms, holding that
"no such relationship exists between an AI user and a platform" and that the user "could
have had no 'reasonable expectation of confidentiality.'" The firm's guidance: "Inputting
privileged information into a third-party AI tool may waive privilege."

**Discovery.** AI transcripts create "discoverable records" that may lack context.
Meetings involving ADA reasonable accommodation or the interactive process "may capture
protected medical information that employers are required to maintain as confidential."

**Transcription error and bias.** "If inaccurate or systematically less accurate
transcripts are relied upon in employment decisions, investigations, performance
management, or disciplinary processes, potential allegations of discrimination or
disparate impact under Title VII may arise."

**Tiered classification.** The firm recommends a "tiered meeting classification model"
distinguishing routine administrative meetings from those involving legal advice, HR
investigations, or trade secrets.

**Named sensitive meeting categories.** Legal advice, HR investigations, trade secrets,
ADA and accommodation discussions, performance management and discipline.

**Access limitation.** "Limit access to the privileged output of an AI notetaker, as broad
access beyond need-to-know personnel can undermine claims of confidentiality."

## Claims this source supports

1. A meeting transcript is a durable, searchable, circulate-able artifact, and its
   original context does not travel with it. Publishing a line from one is a context
   transfer, and that is the whole risk in one sentence.
2. A tiered meeting classification is professional practice. This skill's confidentiality
   screen is a personal-scale version of the same idea.
3. There is a named list of meeting categories that carry legal exposure, and it maps
   directly onto a hard do-not-publish list: legal advice, HR matters, trade secrets,
   accommodation and medical, performance and discipline.
4. Transcription accuracy is uneven across speakers and cannot be relied on for
   consequential decisions. That is an independent, legally framed argument for this
   skill's attribution rule.
5. All-party consent states exist and matter. Content pulled from a call is content pulled
   from a conversation other people participated in, which is a reason to screen for other
   people's words even when the legal exposure is low.
