## Source-quality caveat

Vendor content with no cited research behind it. Its value is that it is an explicit,

## Extracted content

**Fix: obvious transcription errors.** "Fix spelling, capitalization, and clear keyboard
errors."

**Fix: stumble repeats, keep emphasis repeats.**

- Before: "I, I, I went..."
- After: "I went..."
- But "very, very cold" stays, because that repetition is intentional.

**Fix: run-on chains.** Add minimal punctuation, break into units.

- Before: "and then we got there it was dark already my mom said don't go in"
- After: "And then we got there. It was dark already, and my mom said, 'Don't go in.'"

**Fix: paragraph breaks.** Break at topic shifts (new time period, new location, new
event) to improve readability "without changing content."

**Leave: dialect and nonstandard grammar.** "We was poor but we didn't know it" stays.
"Nonstandard grammar can be part of identity, region, or era."

**Leave: word choice.** Never substitute synonyms. Swapping "scared" for "concerned"
changes emotional weight.

**Leave: self-corrections and hedges.** "It was 1959, no, 1960, when he left" preserves
the speaker's memory process.

**Fillers.** Removed only in "intelligent verbatim" style, and only "when they do not add
meaning."

**Uncertainty brackets.** A consistent marker set: `[inaudible]` for unheard audio,
`[unclear]` for partially heard, `[name?]` for a best guess with doubt.

**Editorial note.** Put a note at the top stating the editing level, the punctuation
approach, the uncertainty markers, and confirming that "grammar, dialect, and word choice
were not rewritten."

## Claims this source supports

1. There is an established line between mechanical cleanup (repeats, punctuation,
   paragraphing) and rewriting (word choice, grammar, dialect). Cleanup preserves the
   speaker. Rewriting replaces them.
2. Not all repetition is noise. Stumble repeats go, emphasis repeats stay. A naive filler
   strip removes the speaker's emphasis along with the stumbles.
3. Hedges and self-corrections can be signal, not noise. They show a mind working.
4. Uncertainty in a transcript should be marked explicitly rather than silently guessed,
   which is directly transferable to this skill's uncertain-attribution handling.
5. Declaring the editing level up front is standard practice, which supports this skill
   showing the user the verbatim and the rebuild side by side.
