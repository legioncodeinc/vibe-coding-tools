## Source-quality caveat

The source itself is explicit that it is experiential: no external citations, all claims
drawn from the author's practice and client case studies. The numbers below are the
author's own reported experience, not measurements.

## Extracted content

**Planning horizon.** "Plan in 90-day blocks rather than a full year at once." Four
quarterly sessions, roughly an hour each. The stated reason: "most detailed 12-month
calendars are abandoned within the first quarter anyway."

**The order of operations, which is the important part.** The article puts building an
INPUT SYSTEM before building the calendar. The recommended input is capture from real
conversations: "three things clients asked that week. That's it. Three voice notes,
ninety seconds each." These feed the calendar automatically.

**Pillars.** Three to four content pillars, set annually, revisited quarterly.

**Batching cadence.** Two to three hours weekly producing content for two to three weeks
ahead. The author reports the batching yield rising from four or five usable pieces per
hour to "ten to twelve pieces of usable content" per hour once the input system matured.

**Review.** Fifteen minutes monthly reviewing performance and adjusting.

**Client case reported.** 34 posts from three months of voice notes, with a 51% email open
rate against a previous 22% average. No methodology given.

## Claims this source supports

1. The bottleneck in personal content is INPUT, not scheduling. An independent
   practitioner arrives at the same conclusion this skill is built on, and solves it with
   deliberate voice-note capture. This skill solves it with capture that already happened.
2. Batching output improves substantially once the input system exists. The author's own
   reported ratio roughly doubles.
3. Ten to twelve usable pieces per batching session is one practitioner's reported working
   volume. This is the closest thing in the archive to a defensible weekly bank size, and
   it is a single unsourced report, so it is a starting point rather than a target.
4. Planning horizons past 90 days are reported as abandoned. A weekly bank with a quarterly
   theme frame fits that reported behavior better than a long calendar.
5. Content pillars, three to four of them, give the bank a retrieval structure beyond seed
   type.
