## Window note

2015, outside the default window. Archived because it collects direct quotes from working
editors about what a first line has to do, which is the exact craft problem of turning a
spoken line into a post opener. No recent non-vendor equivalent surfaced.

## Extracted content

**On urgency.** William Zinsser: "Your lead must capture the reader immediately and force
him to keep reading."

**On function.** Chip Scanlan (Poynter): "A good lead beckons and invites. It informs,
attracts, and entices."

**On the promise.** John McPhee: "It's got to deliver on what you promise. It should
shine like a flashlight down through the piece."

**What editors actually look for.**

| Editor | Publication | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| Lisa Grossman | New Scientist | "an accurate sense of what the story is about", news "nearly always in the first sentence", humor that "first makes you smile, and then makes you keep reading" |
| David Grimm | Science | "an attention-grabbing first sentence; a clear, concise, and user-friendly summary of the new finding; and a sense of why the average reader should care", built "lean and mean" with "short, snappy sentences" |
| Karen Schrock Simring | Scientific American Mind | "instantly relatable", an "instant scene", avoiding telling words like "tragic, staggering, or amazing" |
| Tim De Chant | NOVA | "a hook, a story, and some familiarity, but ultimately something unsettled and unfinished about it" |

**On structure.** Rachel Nuwer builds ledes as "mini-anecdotes" that "introduce a
character... include the news... and, crucially, leave readers wanting to know more". They
should "present some form of tension, mystery, surprise, or challenge."

**On the two jobs.** David Quammen concluded a lede must "do two things, and do them very
quickly: offer a promise and establish tension."

**On standalone shape.** De Chant notes the best ledes "stand alone as their own tiny
story" with "a beginning, middle, and end."

## Claims this source supports

1. A first line has two jobs: promise something and create tension. This is the working
   test for a drafted hook.
2. Telling words ("staggering", "amazing") are a named failure. Show the thing instead.
   This is the same instinct that bans hype language in this repo's voice skills.
3. Short and lean beats ornate in an opener.
4. The best openers are self-contained micro-stories, which is precisely what a quotable
   spoken moment already is before it gets padded.
