# The Curse of Knowledge in Technical Writing

- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Earthly engineering blog, technical writing topic)
- **Why archived:** Names the cognitive bias behind the "no implied context" principle and

## Definition

The curse of knowledge is "the cognitive bias in which an individual assumes that their
audience possesses the prior knowledge necessary to understand a concept, leading to
ineffective communication."

## Original research

Introduced by economists Colin Camerer, George Loewenstein, and Martin Weber in research
on the economic implications of the bias. Their finding: "better-informed parties are
unable to ignore their better information."

## Failure modes in technical writing

- **Unexplained jargon.** Technical terms and acronyms used without definition.
- **Missing prerequisites.** Content assumes foundational knowledge the reader lacks.
- **Skipped explanatory steps.** Complex concepts presented abstractly rather than broken
  into manageable pieces.
- **Lack of context.** Instructions given without explaining why, so readers copy and
  paste without understanding the underlying logic.

## Countermeasures

- **User testing with the target audience.** Share drafts with intended readers to find
  comprehension gaps.
- **Deliberate self-editing.** Review after several days to spot overlooked assumptions.
- **Simplify language.** Break complex concepts into understandable explanations.
- **Prioritize empathy.** Write from the reader's position.
