# Guidance for Preparing Standard Operating Procedures (EPA QA/G-6)

- **URL:** https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-06/documents/g6-final.pdf
- **Landing page:** https://www.epa.gov/quality/guidance-preparing-standard-operating-procedures
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (US Environmental Protection Agency, EPA/600/B-07/001)
- **Why archived:** The most authoritative published SOP standard in the archive. Supplies
  the required-elements list, the "someone with limited experience" detail test, the
  independent-execution validation rule, and the document control requirements that make
  an SOP auditable.

## Definition

An SOP is "a set of written instructions that document a routine or repetitive activity"
performed by an organization. Purpose: give individuals the information to perform a job
properly, and ensure consistency in the quality and integrity of the work.

## Required text elements

**Title page.** Clear activity title, SOP identification number, issue or revision date,
applicable agency / division / branch, and dated signatures from preparer and approver.

**Table of contents.** Recommended for longer SOPs, for quick reference and to track
modifications.

**Procedures section.** Briefly describes the purpose of the work, applicable regulatory
standards, and scope. Organize into logical steps covering, as applicable:

- Scope and applicability
- Method summary
- Definitions
- Health and safety warnings
- Cautions
- Interferences
- Personnel qualifications
- Equipment and supplies
- Detailed procedures
- Data management requirements

**Quality assurance / quality control section.** QC procedures, acceptance criteria,
monitoring frequency, and corrective action when QC limits are exceeded.

**References section.** Full citation of interfacing documents, related SOPs, and
published methods.

## Writing style and detail level

SOPs should be written "in a concise, step-by-step, easy-to-read format" using "active
voice and present verb tense." Information must be "unambiguous and not overly
complicated."

The load-bearing detail test, quoted: procedures must contain "sufficient detail so that
someone with limited experience ... but with a basic understanding, can successfully
reproduce the procedure when unsupervised."

Also quoted: "information should be conveyed clearly and explicitly to remove any doubt as
to what is required."

## Review, testing, and revision

- Review by individuals with "appropriate training and experience" with the process.
- Quoted: "draft SOPs are actually tested by individuals other than the original writer"
  before finalization. Independent execution is the validation mechanism.
- Approval authority typically includes the immediate supervisor and the QA officer.
- Quoted: conduct "systematic review on a periodic basis, e.g. every 1-2 years, to ensure
  that the policies and procedures remain current and appropriate."
- Whenever procedures change, the SOP is updated and re-approved.

## Document control

- Each organization develops a numbering system to identify and label SOPs.
- Each page generally carries control notation: "short title and identification number,"
  "revision number and date," and "page X of Y." Quoted: this notation "is critical when
  the need for evidentiary records is involved."
- Maintain a master SOP list with number, version, issuance date, title, author, status,
  organizational division, and history of previous versions.
- The Quality Management Plan designates who ensures only current versions stay in active
  use and who archives superseded versions.
