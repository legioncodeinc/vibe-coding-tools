# SOP Formats Explained: Types, Use Cases and Examples

- **URL:** https://scribe.com/library/sop-format
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Scribe, a process documentation vendor)
- **Why archived:** Vendor-side taxonomy of SOP formats aimed at knowledge-work and
  software workflows rather than physical plant procedures, which is the closer match for
  a screen-capture-derived SOP. Also carries the vendor's own effectiveness claims, which
  are worth recording as vendor claims rather than facts.

## The four formats

### Step-by-step SOP

Quoted definition: "Step-by-step guides lay out instructions in linear order, starting at
the beginning and walking through the steps chronologically."

Use cases named: updating status on company tools, downloading documents, basic
spreadsheet functions, scheduling messages, employee onboarding.

### Checklist SOP

Documents with checkboxes so employees can mark items complete. Flexible because tasks do
not have to run in sequence.

Use cases named: reviewing written content, guiding users through a new tool,
troubleshooting simple IT issues, preparing resources for a new employee.

Quoted guidance: "When creating checklist guides, always specify how to use your SOP for
clarity." Some checklists require chronological order, others do not, and the document has
to say which.

### Flowchart SOP

Quoted definition: "Flowchart SOPs organize processes on a diagram, showcasing different
steps in boxes connected by lines."

Use cases named: answering customer inquiries, troubleshooting complex IT issues,
processing payments, approving employee leave, handling project delays.

Stated advantage: "Flowcharts let employees quickly follow the path they need" when
processes have multiple outcomes.

Stated challenge: complexity and design requirements make them time-intensive to create.

### Hierarchical SOP

Quoted definition: "Hierarchical SOPs structure processes under various large headers,
with subtasks beneath each one."

Use cases named: developing new products, creating business plans, hosting formal
conferences, implementing new technology, performing safety and maintenance checks.

Stated requirement: needs "in-depth knowledge of the process," and the vendor recommends
collaborating with employees who regularly perform the task.

## Selection guidance

Straightforward tasks take step-by-step. Processes with flexible ordering take checklists.
Multi-outcome procedures take flowcharts. Complex, lengthy processes take hierarchical.
