# An Easier Method for Extracting Tacit Knowledge

- **URL:** https://commoncog.com/an-easier-method-for-extracting-tacit-knowledge/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (practitioner blog summarizing published CTA research)
- **Why archived:** Practitioner-level restatement of ACTA with the six knowledge-audit
  probes written out. These probes are directly reusable as the confirmation questions a
  capture-derived SOP asks its author.

## Named method

Applied Cognitive Task Analysis (ACTA), published by Laura Militello and Robert Hutton in
1998 as a simplified technique for extracting expert mental models and tacit knowledge.

## The problem statement

Experts often cannot articulate their expertise. Typical responses to direct questioning:
"I just know what to do" or "I just do what feels right." The article does not quantify
how much knowledge is omitted; it argues that substantial tacit knowledge is inaccessible
through direct questioning.

## ACTA's four techniques

1. **Task diagram.** Break the task into 3 to 6 steps, identifying the cognitively
   demanding ones.
2. **Knowledge audit.** Structured interview probes to surface expertise patterns.
3. **Simulation interview.** Present a scenario to elicit cognitive process.
4. **Cognitive demands table.** Synthesize findings across multiple experts.

## The six knowledge-audit probes

- Past and future thinking
- Big picture understanding
- Noticing patterns
- Job smarts (efficiency)
- Opportunities and improvising
- Self-monitoring

Optional probes: anomalies, equipment difficulties.

## Source lineage

Builds on the Recognition-Primed Decision Making (RPD) model and references the
Critical Decision Method (CDM), which the article acknowledges as more rigorous but
impractical for ordinary practitioners.

## Critique of self-reported process

The whole method is an implicit critique of direct questioning: experts cannot reliably
describe their own processes. ACTA compensates with contextual probing and
scenario-based elicitation rather than introspective accounts.
