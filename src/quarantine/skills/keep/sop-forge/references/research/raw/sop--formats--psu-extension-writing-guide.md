# Standard Operating Procedures: A Writing Guide

- **URL:** https://extension.psu.edu/standard-operating-procedures-a-writing-guide
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (university extension publication, Penn State Extension)
- **Why archived:** Primary authority on SOP format selection and the decision matrix for
  choosing a format. Also the clearest published statement of the draft-by-observation
  workflow, which is exactly what a capture-derived SOP skill automates.

## Format types

Four formats, each matched to a different procedural shape.

**Simple steps.** Routine, short procedures requiring few decisions. Example step given:
"Dry-wipe dirt and debris from the first cow's udder."

**Hierarchical steps.** Recommended when consistency is critical. Lets experienced users
read only the main steps while beginners open the detailed substeps. Example: main step
"Wipe dirt and debris from the first cow's udder" branches into substeps about gloved
hands and paper towels.

**Graphic procedures.** Best when a long activity can be broken into shorter
subprocesses. Quoted: "Workers can learn several short subprocesses more easily than one
long procedure." Uses photographs and diagrams to complement text.

**Flowcharts.** For procedures with many decisions. Quoted: "Procedures that require many
decisions should be presented as a flowchart."

## Format selection matrix

| Condition | Format |
|---|---|
| 10 steps or fewer, few decisions | Simple steps |
| More than 10 steps, few decisions | Hierarchical steps or graphic |
| Many decisions, any step count | Flowchart |

## Required structural elements

- Clear, descriptive title
- Author or responsible party name
- Effective date
- Logical numbering scheme when multiple SOPs exist
- Materials or tools list where applicable
- Safety precautions for hazardous procedures

## Writing style

**Imperative, action verb first.** Contrast given in the source:

- Poor: "The weight of feed refusals should be recorded"
- Strong: "Record the weight of feed refusals in the feeder notebook"

**Conciseness.** Contrast given:

- Poor: "Make sure that you clean out all of the old grain from the calf pails before you
  put new grain in them"
- Strong: "Empty all old grain from calf pails before feeding new grain"

**Acronyms.** Quoted: "Use acronyms only when they are commonly understood, not just to
shorten your writing." BVD is fine because it is recognized; ATO (automatic take-off)
creates confusion.

## Detail level

Quoted: "Procedures definitely should include all steps that are essential, and that
should be performed the same way by all workers." Also quoted: "Highly detailed
procedures cannot take the place of training."

The worked example of insufficient detail: "Predip all four teats with the green dip cup"
is too vague. Adding "Squeeze dip up from bottom reservoir so that teat chamber is 3/4
full" supplies the precision that removes variation.

## The seven-step implementation cycle

1. **Plan for results.** Establish business goals and measurable outcomes before writing.
2. **First draft.** Observe the actual procedure being performed and document all steps in
   order.
3. **Internal review.** Workers review the draft and suggest changes. Quoted: "People are
   much more likely to accept and use the SOP if they feel a sense of ownership."
4. **External review.** Technical advisers check it.
5. **Testing.** An unfamiliar person follows the procedure exactly. Quoted: "Any steps
   that cause confusion or hesitation for the test worker should be revised."
6. **Post.** Display in the workplace, laminated, enlarged text.
7. **Train.** Explain the why alongside the how.

## Audience assumptions

The guide assumes workers may lack literacy or technical background and recommends visual
formats for that reason. Quoted: "Pictures truly are worth a thousand words, and they are
helpful regardless of the literacy level or native language of a worker."

Training section: "When training workers, share the reasons why procedures must be
performed correctly, not just what to do or how to do it."
