# How to Use the 6 Levels of Delegation to Scale Your Business

- **URL:** https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/how-to-delegate-effectively
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (business media article, interview-based)
- **Why archived:** Supplies the delegation-level vocabulary that lets an SOP state the
  authority a task is being handed off with, not just the steps. Also archived as a
  documented coverage gap: this widely cited delegation framework says nothing about
  documenting a process before handing it off, which is the gap the skill fills.

## The six levels

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do as I say | Specific instructions, no judgment. Example given: "Buy me a bath mat on Amazon." |
| 2 | Look into this for me | Research task. "It's almost like a human Google." Assistant gathers, you decide. |
| 3 | Give me your advice, and I'll decide | Assistant researches and narrows options; you choose. |
| 4 | Explore, decide, and check back with me | Assistant decides but reports before executing. |
| 5 | Explore and decide, within these limits | Bounded authority, e.g. decisions under $100 need no approval. |
| 6 | Just get it done | Full trust, no check-ins. |

## Prioritization exercise

Not a formal framework. The practical exercise given:

1. List 10 tasks you dislike or should not be handling.
2. Assign each to one of the six levels.
3. Identify which resources or providers you need to hire.

Stated judgment rule: weigh "the balance between the gravity of the project at hand and
the expertise of the person" before delegating critical decisions.

## Documented gaps in this source

- **No SOP guidance.** The article contains no guidance on documenting a process before
  delegating it. The framework tells you what authority to grant and is silent on how the
  person learns the task.
- **No statistics.** No quantitative data linking delegation to business growth or founder
  time recovered. The only evidence offered is one person's anecdotal health improvement.

Both gaps are recorded deliberately. The delegation literature in this archive assumes the
process is already documented, which for small operators it usually is not.
