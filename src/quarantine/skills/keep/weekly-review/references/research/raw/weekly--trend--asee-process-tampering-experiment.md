# Impact of Process Tampering on Variation

- **URL:** https://peer.asee.org/impact-of-process-tampering-on-variation.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic conference paper (ASEE, American Society for Engineering
  Education, paper ID 23451). Engineering-education demonstration study.

## What the funnel experiment shows

Deming's funnel experiment was designed to "visually show how variation in outcome is
impacted when the intention is to improve the performance." Attempts to correct random
fluctuation make the outcome worse.

## Tampering, defined

Tampering is "reacting to random or common-cause variation as if it is a result of some
assignable or special cause." Deming estimated that "94% of issues are the result of
misinterpreting common cause variation".

## The measured result

The paper's catapult experiment:

| Scenario | Process spread |
|---|---|
| No adjustments | 11.1 inches |
| Chasing the target through adjustments | 28.1 inches |

Variation increased by a factor of about 2.5 when operators adjusted the process to hit
targets.

## Reading for skill design

This is the quantified version of the warning in the run chart literature
[weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md]. Reacting to the most recent point does not
merely fail to help. In a measured demonstration it made the spread 2.5 times worse.

Applied to a weekly review, the tampering behavior is: read this week's commitment closure
rate, conclude the approach is broken, change tactics, repeat next week from a new baseline.
The series never stabilizes and the person never learns whether anything worked.

The countermeasure the skill implements: a change in approach is recommended only on a
trend or a shift under the run chart rules, or on a named carried item at three weeks, never
on a single week's movement.

## Caveats

- The 94 percent figure is Deming's estimate, widely quoted and not derived in this paper.
  Do not restate it as a measurement.
- A catapult is a stable mechanical process. A person's work week is not. The direction of
  the finding transfers; the magnitude does not, and the distillation says so.
