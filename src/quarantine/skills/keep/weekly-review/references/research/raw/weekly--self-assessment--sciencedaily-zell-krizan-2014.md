# Why we think we're good at something when we're not (press coverage of Zell and Krizan 2014)

- **URL:** https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/04/140402095056.htm
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** press coverage of academic work (ScienceDaily, 2014). Archived for the
  author quotes, which do not appear in the abstract. Lower rank than the journal record;
  treat quotes as author commentary, not as findings.

## Scale as reported here

Data from more than 330,000 individuals, across what the article calls "nearly two dozen
meta-analyses". The correlation coefficient is not stated in this piece; take r = .29 from
the journal record instead.

## Author quotes

On feedback quality:

> "This is one reason why we have barriers to self-insight, because oftentimes, even if we
> get feedback, it's not accurate."

On the self-esteem trade:

> "As a society we make the wrong trade-off by thinking that boosting self-esteem is going
> to boost performance."

On what improves self-insight:

> "If people are evaluating themselves in terms of very specific criteria, they're going to
> have better self-insight."

## Moderators as summarized here

- **Task definition.** Specific measurable skills yield better self-insight than vaguely
  defined abilities.
- **Task complexity.** Complex multi-skill tasks produce less accurate self-assessment.
- **Selective attention.** People "tend to emphasize strengths while downplaying
  weaknesses", which reduces accuracy.

## Reading for skill design

The third moderator is the named failure mode of a self-review that grades itself: strengths
get emphasized, weaknesses get downplayed, and the result reads positive. A weekly review
that always finds a win is that bias, automated.

The self-esteem quote is the clearest available statement of a position on the neutral
versus motivating question, and it points away from motivating.
