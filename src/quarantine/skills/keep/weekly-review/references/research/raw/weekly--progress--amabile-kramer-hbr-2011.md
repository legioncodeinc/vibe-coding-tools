# The Power of Small Wins

- **URL:** https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** practitioner and industry research (Amabile and Kramer, Harvard Business
  Review, May 2011, reporting the diary study behind The Progress Principle)

## The progress principle

Making headway on meaningful work contributed more to positive inner work life, meaning
emotions, motivations and perceptions, than any other factor examined.

## Quotes

> "Even small wins can boost inner work life tremendously."

> "On the flip side, small losses or setbacks can have an extremely negative effect."

> "If a person is motivated and happy at the end of the workday, it's a good bet that he or
> she achieved something, however small."

## The asymmetry

The article states that setbacks produce disproportionately stronger negative effects than
equivalent progress produces positive effects. **The magnitude of that asymmetry is not
quantified in the HBR article text retrieved.** Do not restate it as a multiple.

## Reading for skill design

Two pulls in opposite directions, and the skill has to hold both.

1. Real progress is worth recording. It is the top driver in this dataset, so a scorecard
   that only lists failures throws away the strongest available signal.
2. Setbacks land harder than wins lift. That is an argument for stating a bad week plainly
   and briefly rather than dwelling, and an argument against padding a bad week with
   invented wins, because a manufactured win next to a real setback does not neutralize it,
   it just makes the report untrustworthy.

The resolution used by the skill: record progress where it is real and countable, never
manufacture it, and keep both the wins and the losses at the level of the work.
