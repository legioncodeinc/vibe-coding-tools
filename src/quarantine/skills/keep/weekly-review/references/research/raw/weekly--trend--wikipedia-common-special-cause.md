## Definitions

**Common cause variation.** "Usual, historical, quantifiable variation in a system".
Constantly active within the system, predictable in a probabilistic sense, and individual
values are not individually significant. The inherent noise of the process.

**Special cause variation.** "Unusual, not previously observed, non-quantifiable variation".
Arises from new phenomena outside the historical experience base. Arrives as a surprise
signal indicating the system itself changed.

## Tampering

The article illustrates Deming's concern via Harry Alpert's prison riot example: officials
investigated the riot as unique when its causes were common across many prisons. Alpert's
observation is that there is "a temptation to react to an extreme outcome and to see it as
significant, even where its causes are common." Reactive intervention of that kind often
increases variation and worsens outcomes rather than improving the process.

## Reading for skill design

The distinction gives the weekly review its central honesty test: a bad week is common cause
until the series says otherwise. One low commitment-closure week is noise. Six consecutive
weeks below the median is a signal.

The tampering warning applies twice over to a scorecard that also sets next week's top three.
If the top three is rewritten every week in reaction to the most recent point, the review is
tampering with the person's own process. That is the mechanism behind the rule that a carried
item is escalated or dropped rather than silently rewritten.
