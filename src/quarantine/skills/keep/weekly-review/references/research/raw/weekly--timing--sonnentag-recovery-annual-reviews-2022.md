# Recovery from Work: Advancing the Field Toward the Future

- **URL:** https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-012420-091355
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Sonnentag and colleagues, Annual Review of Organizational
  Psychology and Organizational Behavior). A review article, so the findings below are
  summaries of primary studies it cites.

## The five core findings, as tabulated by the review

1. Evening recovery activities and experiences relate to improved well-being and
   motivational benefits during the day, with physical activity and psychological
   detachment from work particularly effective.
2. Evening recovery depends on momentary affective state, job stressors, and workday
   performance.
3. **Long-term benefits of recovery are weaker than short-term gains** and depend on
   additional factors.
4. Microbreaks during the workday sustain energy better than work-related strategies.
5. Recovery processes, "particularly psychological detachment from work on evenings and
   weekends, can be improved through intervention programs."

## Psychological detachment, defined

Refraining from job-related activities and thoughts during nonwork time. Mentally
disengaging from the job while away from it.

## The recovery paradox

Sonnentag (2018), as described in this review: job stressors call for recovery and at the
same time increase high-arousal negative affect and depletion, which are the states that
make recovery less likely. Recovery is least available exactly when it is most needed.

Related finding, same direction: "perceiving that one has performed well at work is
associated with higher levels of psychological detachment and relaxation in the evening".
The inverse follows. A week perceived as poor predicts worse detachment.

## Intervention effect sizes, from Karabinski et al. 2021 as cited here

| Intervention type | Effect on detachment |
|---|---|
| All detachment interventions, 34 studies | d = 0.36 |
| Boundary-management interventions | d = 0.65 |
| Interventions without a boundary-management component | d = 0.25 |
| Mindfulness interventions | d = 0.46 |

Also from the same meta-analysis: detachment outside of work improves under intervention,
detachment during work breaks does not.

## The counter-finding that must not be dropped

Not detaching predicts positive affect under specific conditions: when the thinking about
work is positive (Sonnentag and Niessen 2020), or when it takes the form of problem-solving
rumination (Firoozabadi et al. 2018). And cross-sectional work suggests "very high levels of
detachment may undermine performance" (Fritz et al. 2010).

So this literature does not say never think about work at the weekend. It says the *kind* of
work thinking matters, and that unstructured negative rumination is the harmful form.

## Reading for skill design, the Sunday-versus-Friday question

This is the only evidentiary anchor the archive has for the timing decision, and it does not
resolve cleanly. Both readings are real.

**Reading that argues against Sunday evening.** Boundary management is the single most
effective lever on weekend detachment (d = 0.65 versus d = 0.25 without it). A scheduled
Sunday-evening work scorecard is a boundary-management intervention run in reverse: it
installs a recurring, notified work stimulus at the end of the recovery window. The recovery
paradox sharpens this: an honest scorecard that must sometimes report a bad week will deliver
its worst news to a reader whose capacity to detach afterward is already lowest, because
perceived poor performance predicts worse detachment.

**Reading that argues Sunday evening is survivable.** Problem-solving rumination is not the
harmful kind of work thinking, and a bounded, structured review that ends in three concrete
decisions is closer to problem-solving than to rumination. Very high detachment may itself
undermine performance.

**Position taken by the skill:** default to Friday late afternoon, offer Sunday evening as a
supported alternative with the tradeoff stated to the user, and in either case bound the
report hard so it cannot become an open-ended rumination prompt. Justified in
`references/honest-scorekeeping.md`.

## Caveat

None of this literature studied a weekly review artifact. Every application above is a
