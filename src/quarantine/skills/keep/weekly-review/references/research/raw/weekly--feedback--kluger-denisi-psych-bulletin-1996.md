# The Effects of Feedback Interventions on Performance: A Historical Review, a Meta-Analysis, and a Preliminary Feedback Intervention Theory

- **URL:** https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Marking%20and%20Feedback/The%20effects%20of%20feedback%20interventions.pdf
- **Canonical record:** https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Kluger and DeNisi, Psychological Bulletin, 1996, 119, 254 to 284)

## Headline result, verbatim from the abstract

"FIs improved performance on average (d = .41) but that over 1/3 of the FIs decreased
performance."

## The negative share

Over 38 percent of the 607 effects were negative. Feedback made performance worse in more
than a third of the studied cases.

## Feedback Intervention Theory, the core claim

Effectiveness depends on where the feedback directs attention within a hierarchy running
from task details up to the self.

- "FI effects on performance are attenuated by cues that direct attention to meta-task
  processes" involving the self.
- Effects improve when attention stays at the task-motivation or task-learning level.

The critical variable is not the content of the feedback but what the recipient attends to
when receiving it. Identical feedback produces opposite outcomes depending on that locus.

## Reading for skill design

This is the source that dissolves the neutral-versus-motivating question rather than
answering it. Both a praising review and a harsh review can direct attention to the self,
and the theory predicts both degrade performance when they do. What separates a useful
review from a harmful one is whether it talks about the work or about the person.

Direct consequences for a weekly scorecard:

- Report what happened to the commitments, the leads, the money and the content. Do not
  report what the week says about the reader.
- Avoid both "strong week" and "weak week" as verdicts on the person. Those are self-level
  cues by construction.
- Avoid praise attached to identity, and avoid grading language.

## Caveats

- 1996 meta-analysis, pre-registration era. The one-third-negative figure is widely cited
  and widely rounded. Use "over a third of the measured effects" rather than a precise share.
- The studies are overwhelmingly externally delivered feedback, not self-generated review.
  Whether the same attention mechanism operates when the reader and the subject are the
  same person is an assumption. Named as a gap.
