# Does Monitoring Goal Progress Promote Goal Attainment? A Meta-Analysis of the Experimental Evidence

- **URL:** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/
- **Also indexed at:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/ (direct fetch blocked), https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/bul-bul0000025.pdf (direct fetch blocked by Incapsula)
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Harkin, Webb, Chang, Prestwich, Conner, Kellar, Benn, Sheeran; Psychological Bulletin, 2016)

## Scale

"A systematic literature search identified 138 studies (N = 19,951)" examining progress
monitoring interventions.

## Effect sizes

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| Interventions increasing monitoring frequency | d+ = 1.98, 95% CI 1.71 to 2.24 |
| Effect on goal attainment | d+ = 0.40, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.48 |

## Mediation

Changes in monitoring frequency mediated intervention effects on goal attainment. The
mechanism is the monitoring itself, not the intervention's other content.

## Moderators, which are the design-relevant part

Progress monitoring produced "larger effects on goal attainment when the outcomes were
reported or made public, and when the information was physically recorded."

Both moderators are directly buildable. A routine report is a physical record by
construction, and it persists, which is a weaker but real form of "reported".

## Conclusion, verbatim

"monitoring goal progress is an effective self-regulation strategy" and interventions
enhancing monitoring frequency "are likely to promote behavior change."

## Reading for skill design

This is the strongest evidence in the archive that a weekly scorecard is worth building at
all. The effect is moderate, not large: d+ = 0.40. It is not a claim that reviewing your
week transforms output. It is a claim that a recorded, persistent measure of progress
moves goal attainment by a moderate, replicated amount.

The moderators say the artifact should be a durable written record rather than an
ephemeral read, and that a small amount of external visibility helps.

## Caveats

- The literature is dominated by health behavior goals (diet, physical activity, glycemic
  control). Transfer to knowledge work goals is an assumption, not a finding.
- The abstract does not report how monitoring effects decay over time, so nothing here
  supports a claim about sustained use past the trial windows.
- Only the abstract and summary were retrieved. Full moderator tables not read.
