# Why Balanced Scorecards Fail: The Problem with KPI-Centric Implementation

- **URL:** https://bscdesigner.com/balanced-scorecard-challenges.htm
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** practitioner and vendor (BSC Designer). Archived for the failure taxonomy
  of the best-known scorecard framework there is.

## The central claim

> "What is often implemented isn't the Balanced Scorecard, but a limited performance
> measurement framework"

The failure is measuring instead of deciding. Organizations build the KPI layer and skip
the part where the measures connect to what anyone does next.

## Named contributing failures

- Using unsuitable tools, spreadsheets rather than purpose-built software
- Skipping strategy alignment guidelines
- Administrative pressure that produces employee disengagement
- Treating cascading as KPI distribution rather than decomposition of objectives

## Statistics

**None.** The article references research literature but gives no failure or abandonment
percentages. This archive contains no measured scorecard failure rate.

## Named consequences of a measurement-only implementation

- Fails to clarify anything or improve accountability
- Creates confusion and resistance
- Reinforces a "measurement bias" that undermines engagement
- Drives managers to abandon the framework entirely for OKR or Hoshin Kanri

## Recommendations

Strategy first: decompose into objectives before KPIs, cascade objectives rather than
metrics, establish cause and effect between perspectives.

## Reading for skill design

The failure mode named here is precisely the one a weekly scorecard is most likely to fall
into: it becomes a set of counts that nobody acts on, and then it stops being read.

Two rules follow.

1. **Every number in the scorecard has to earn its place by changing what happens next.**
   A metric that would not alter the top three under any plausible value is decoration and
   should be cut.
2. **The trend and the top three are the product; the counts are the supporting material.**
   A report that leads with this week's absolute figures is the KPI-centric failure in
   personal form.

"Measurement bias undermines engagement" is also an argument against grading the person,
which the feedback literature supports from a stronger evidentiary base
[weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].
