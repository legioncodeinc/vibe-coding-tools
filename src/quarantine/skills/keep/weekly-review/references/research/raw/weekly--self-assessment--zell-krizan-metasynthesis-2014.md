# Do People Have Insight Into Their Abilities? A Metasynthesis

- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691613518075
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Zell and Krizan, Perspectives on Psychological Science, 2014)

## Scale

22 meta-analyses synthesized.

## Headline result

"the mean correlation between ability self-evaluations and performance outcomes across
meta-analyses was moderate (M = .29, SD = .11)"

## Conclusion, verbatim

People "have only moderate insight into their abilities but also underscore the contextual
factors that enable accurate self-perception of ability."

## When self-assessment gets more accurate

Accuracy improved when:

1. Evaluations were **domain specific** rather than broad.
2. The performance task was **objective**.
3. The task was **familiar**.
4. The task was **low in complexity**.

## Reading for skill design

r = .29 is the number that justifies building a scorecard out of retrieval rather than out
of asking the user how the week went. Unaided self-assessment of a week explains under 10
percent of the variance in what actually happened.

The four moderators are a specification, not background. They say: count specific,
objective, familiar, simple things. Hours in meetings from a calendar is all four.
"Momentum on the business" is none of them, and a scorecard field like that will be as
inaccurate as an unaided guess while looking like a measurement.

## Caveat

This is about insight into abilities, not insight into what happened last week. Recall of
events is a different literature and this archive did not sweep it. The transfer is
plausible and is labelled as a transfer wherever the distillation uses it.
