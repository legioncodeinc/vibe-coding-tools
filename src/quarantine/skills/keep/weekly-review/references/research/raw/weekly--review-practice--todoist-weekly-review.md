# The Weekly Review: A Productivity Ritual to Get More Done

- **URL:** https://www.todoist.com/productivity-methods/weekly-review
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Todoist). Archived as the clearest statement of the
  practice and its origin. It is a task-manager vendor describing a ritual that sells task
  managers, so every efficacy claim here is a position, not evidence.

## Origin

The weekly review is a core component of Getting Things Done, created by David Allen. Allen
structures it in three phases: Get Clear, Get Current, Get Creative.

## Allen quotes, as reproduced by this source

> "The Weekly Review will sharpen your intuitive focus on your important projects as you
> deal with the flood of new input and potential distractions coming at you the rest of the
> week."

> "the Weekly Review is the critical success factor for marrying your larger commitments to
> your day-to-day activities."

## Steps

- **Get Clear.** Process all loose ends.
- **Get Current.** Update every item to reflect current status.
- **Get Creative.** Generate new ideas.

Plus reviewing checklists, previous and upcoming calendar items, project lists, and the
Someday/Maybe list.

## Timing, and this is the source of the design question

Three options are offered, with no evidence attached to any of them:

| Slot | Rationale given |
|---|---|
| Friday afternoon | Capitalize on the productivity dip before the weekend |
| Sunday evening | Prepare for the week ahead |
| Monday morning | Ease into the week with a fresh perspective |

The article states that consistency matters more than which day is chosen.

## Duration

One hour per week, minimum.

## On abandonment

Not addressed directly. The one relevant line:

> "the longer and more arduous your review is, the less likely you'll be to maintain the
> habit"

## Reading for skill design

Three things this source establishes and one it does not.

Establishes: the practice has a named origin and a canonical structure; the canonical
version costs an hour; the timing question has three named candidates and the canonical
literature does not resolve it.

Does not establish: that the weekly review works. This archive found **no controlled
evidence that the GTD weekly review improves any outcome.** That is a named gap, and it is
why the skill anchors on the self-monitoring meta-analysis instead of on GTD.

The one-hour figure is the strongest argument for automating the retrieval half. An hour a
week is the cost that gets abandoned first.
