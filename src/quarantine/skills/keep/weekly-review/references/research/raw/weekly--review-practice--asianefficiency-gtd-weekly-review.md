# GTD Weekly Review: Step-by-Step Guide and Checklist

- **URL:** https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/gtd-weekly-review/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community and practitioner blog (Asian Efficiency, a productivity training
  business). Archived for the adherence observations, which no stronger source supplies.
  Every efficacy claim here is a practitioner assertion.

## The checklist

**Get Clear:** collect loose papers and materials, get the inbox to zero, empty your head.

**Get Current:** previous calendar, upcoming calendar, project lists, next actions, the
waiting-for list, relevant checklists, pending items.

**Get Creative:** review someday/maybe, be creative and courageous about new ideas.

## Duration, which is the number that matters here

> "Plan for about 60-90 minutes when you are first starting out. Once you have done it
> consistently for a few weeks, most people can get through the full process in 30-45
> minutes."

Note the disagreement with the other practice source, which says one hour minimum
[weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md]. The honest range across the archive is
30 to 90 minutes of human time, unassisted.

## On adherence

> "The reason some people do not complete the weekly review is because they do not schedule
> it."

> "If you try to squeeze it in between meetings, you will rush through it and miss things."

> "Start smaller. Instead of the full 60-minute process, commit to a 15-minute 'mini review.'"

## Reading for skill design

Three transfers.

1. **Scheduling is the named adherence mechanism.** That is exactly what a Littlebird
   routine is. The routine removes the failure this source names as primary.
2. **The 30 to 90 minute cost is the thing to attack.** A skill that produces the retrieval
   and the counts turns most of that hour into reading time. This is the clearest
   justification for the whole skill existing.
3. **The mini review is a real fallback shape.** It supports the skill having a short form
   for a quiet week rather than always producing a full scorecard.

## Caveat

Practitioner blog with commercial interest in the practice. The adherence claim is an
observation from teaching, not a measurement. This archive contains **no measured adherence
or abandonment rate for the weekly review**, from any source. Named as a gap.
