# What's the Retrospective Prime Directive?

- **URL:** https://www.parabol.co/blog/retrospective-prime-directive/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Parabol, a retrospective tool vendor). Archived for the
  verbatim Prime Directive text and its provenance.

## The Prime Directive, verbatim (Norm Kerth)

> "Regardless of what we discover, we must understand and truly believe that everyone did
> the best job he or she could, given what was known at the time, his or her skills and
> abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand."

## Provenance

Formalized by Norm Kerth in *Project Retrospectives: A Handbook for Team Reviews*. The
name is borrowed from Star Trek. This source does not give a publication date.

## What this source does NOT contain

- **No criticisms** of the Prime Directive. It is presented entirely as beneficial.
- **No guidance on applying it to an individual** rather than a team. The article is
  exclusively about team retrospectives and group psychological safety.

Both absences are load-bearing for the skill, because the individual adaptation is exactly
what a solo weekly review needs and no archived source supplies it.

## Reading for skill design

The Prime Directive exists to make it safe to say true things in front of colleagues. Its
mechanism is removing the fear of blame so the facts can surface.

Ported to a solo review with no colleagues present, the fear of blame is not the binding
constraint and the directive loses its job. Worse, its wording is a ready-made excuse
generator: "everyone did the best job he or she could, given what was known at the time" is
one short step from reframing a dropped commitment as a learning, which is the specific
failure this skill is built to prevent.

The position the skill takes, and it is a synthesis rather than a sourced claim: keep the
Prime Directive's blamelessness about the **person**, refuse it about the **record**. That
synthesis is supported from a different direction by the feedback literature, which says
performance improves when attention stays on the task and degrades when it moves to the
self [weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].

## Gap

No archived source describes a validated individual retrospective format. Every claim the
skill makes about solo retrospectives is a transfer from team practice and is labelled as
one.
