# The CFPB's final collections rule: contact frequency limitations and limited content messages

- URL: https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2020/11/05/the-cfpbs-final-collections-rule-contact-frequency-limitations-and-limited-content-messages/
- Fetched: 2026-08-17
- Source type: legal-analysis (Consumer Finance Monitor, Ballard Spahr LLP)
- Published: November 5, 2020

## What it says

Verbatim on the primary call limit: "a debt collector may not place more than seven
telephone calls to a person within seven consecutive days" (section 1006.14(b)(1)).

Verbatim on the post-conversation restriction: "within a period of seven consecutive days
after having had a telephone conversation with the person in connection with the
collection of such debt" (section 1006.14(b)(1)).

Applies to debt collectors engaged in debt collection activities.

On what counts as a call: voicemails, including ringless voicemails, and limited content
messages count toward the seven-call limit, even though limited content messages are not
treated as collection communications under the FDCPA.

On other media: the proposal would have allowed limited content messages by text, but the
final rule "confined limited content messages to voicemail only" (section 1006.2(j)).

Key framing, as extracted: the restrictions operate as a rebuttable presumption rather
than a bright line. Exceeding the limit creates a presumed violation, and staying under it
does not guarantee protection against a harassment claim.

## Why it matters here

The rebuttable-presumption framing is the useful part for a business that is not covered
by the rule at all. Even a covered collector cannot treat seven calls as automatically
safe, which makes anything approaching that frequency a poor ceiling to design toward.
