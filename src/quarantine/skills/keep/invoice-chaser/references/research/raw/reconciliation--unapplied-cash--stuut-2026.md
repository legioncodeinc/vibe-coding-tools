# Unapplied Cash: Why It Happens, How Much It Costs, and How to Eliminate It

- URL: https://www.stuut.ai/blog/unapplied-cash-problem
- Fetched: 2026-08-17
- Source type: vendor (AR automation software blog)

## What it says

On the relationship damage of chasing a paid invoice, verbatim: "Calling a customer to
demand payment on an invoice they already paid three weeks ago is one of the most damaging
interactions in the AR function."

On the downstream effect, verbatim: "the customer's AP contact becomes less responsive to
future outreach because they now associate the AR team with errors rather than
professional account management."

On why payments go unmatched, verbatim: "ACH and wire transfers move money electronically
but often travel separately from the remittance advice that identifies which invoices the
payment covers."

On how common that is, verbatim: "Missing remittance is not an edge case: It is standard
behavior for companies paying by ACH across high invoice volumes."

On the character of the failure, verbatim: unapplied cash is "a systemic AR failure" that
accumulates when "rules-based matching breaks when remittance data is unstructured,
payments span multiple entities, or payment volume exceeds the team's capacity to clear
exceptions manually."

On cost, citing Payra's analysis: manual AR work "consumes 1,050 to 1,450 hours annually
at a total labor cost of roughly $78,000," of which 300 to 400 annual hours are attributed
to "cash application and payment matching."

On treasury effect: "cash sitting in a suspense account is cash treasury cannot include in
cash positioning models."

## Limitations noted at fetch time

The page gives no rate for how often payments go unapplied, and the hours-and-dollars
figures come from a second vendor (Payra) and describe mid-market AR teams, not solo
operators or small agencies. The relationship-damage claim is asserted from practitioner
experience, not measured.
