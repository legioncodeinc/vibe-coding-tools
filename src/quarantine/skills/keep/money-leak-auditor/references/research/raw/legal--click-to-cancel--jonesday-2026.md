## Status timeline

- 2025: The Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 Click-to-Cancel Rule in full, holding that
  the FTC had not completed the preliminary regulatory analysis required for
  economically significant regulations.
- 2026-03-05: FTC Bureau of Consumer Protection Director Christopher Mufarridge gave a
  speech emphasizing commitment to addressing deceptive negative option subscriptions.
- 2026-03-10 approximately: FTC announced an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking,
  reopening the process.
- 2026-04-13: ANPRM comment deadline, roughly 100 submissions received.

## What the vacated rule required

That "terminating a subscription be no more difficult than initiating one", meaning the
cancellation mechanism must be as simple as the enrollment mechanism.

## Enforcement without an active rule

The FTC continues to act under Section 5 of the FTC Act and under ROSCA (2010).
Settlements cited: Care.com at $8.5 million in 2024, and Amazon at $2.5 billion.

## State law

Roughly 30 states have automatic-renewal or negative-option statutes. California's
Automatic Renewal Law requires annual renewal reminders that disclose pricing and
available cancellation mechanisms.

## Notes for the auditor

Practical consequence for a 2026 audit: there is no federal rule in force requiring
one-click cancellation. A user should expect friction, phone-only cancellation, and
retention gauntlets to be legal in most B2B contexts. Budget time per cancellation and
do not promise the user a fast path.

The California ARL annual reminder requirement is a useful capture target: those
reminder emails are exactly the kind of artifact that appears in screen capture and
carries both a price and a renewal date.
