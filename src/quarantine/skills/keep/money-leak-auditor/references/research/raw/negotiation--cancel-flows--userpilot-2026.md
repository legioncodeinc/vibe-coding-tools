## Save offers presented in cancellation flows

- Discounts and plan downgrades, personalized to account usage patterns
- Pause options, temporary suspension instead of cancellation
- Extended trials
- Direct escalation to a customer success human for high-value accounts

## Recovery rates

- "an effective cancellation flow recovers 10-34% of users who explicitly try to
  cancel", with Churnkey's platform averaging 34%.
- Illustrative: roughly $6,000 monthly recovered at a $500K MRR business with 3.5%
  monthly churn.

## Pause

"Subscription businesses that enable pausing see 25% of would-be churners pause instead
of cancel, generating over $200 million in re-subscription revenue industry-wide."

## Typical flow structure

1. Exit survey asking for cancellation reasons
2. Segmentation based on the response
3. Personalized retention offer matching the stated reason
4. Compliance with single-offer limits under California law

Key finding as stated: surveys must precede offers so the offer can be personalized.

## Notes for the auditor

Read from the buyer's side, this source is a map of what is obtainable by starting a
cancellation. The offer is triggered by the stated reason, which means the reason a
buyer gives determines the offer they see. Give a price reason and the flow routes to a
discount; give a usage reason and it routes to a downgrade or a pause.

The 25% pause statistic matters for seasonal tooling: pause is a real third option
alongside cancel and downgrade, and it is frequently not advertised until cancellation
is initiated.

Caution: this source is about consumer and prosumer subscription flows. Enterprise
contracts with a signed order form do not have a self-serve cancel flow at all, and the
equivalent path is a renewal conversation with an account manager.
