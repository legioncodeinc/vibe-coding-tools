# Meta Community Standards: Spam

- **Title:** Community Standards, Spam
- **URL:** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (Meta Platforms Transparency Center)

## Why this source matters here

This is the policy under which a keyword-comment campaign itself can get penalized, not
just the outreach that follows it. "Comment KEYWORD and I will send it to you" is close
to the engagement-gating language Meta calls out by name, so the skill has to warn users
about the campaign design, not only the follow-up.

## Extracted content

**Policy rationale**

Spam is prohibited because it "is designed to deceive, mislead, or overwhelm users in
order to artificially increase viewership."

**Prohibited: bulk and automated activity**

- "Posting, sharing, engaging with content or creating accounts, Groups, Pages, Events or
  other assets...at very high frequencies"
- Restrictions may apply even at lower frequencies when combined with other spam
  indicators.

**Prohibited: engagement manipulation**

- Trading engagement (likes, shares, views, follows, clicks, hashtags) for monetary value.
- Operating giveaways where cash prizes are offered "in exchange for engagement".
- Requiring users to engage with content before accessing promised material.

**Prohibited: asset and privilege trading**

- Buying, selling, or exchanging accounts, groups, pages, or similar platform assets.
- Trading admin or moderator roles for compensation.

**Prohibited: deceptive distribution**

- Cloaking (presenting different content to systems versus users).
- Misleading links delivering substantially different content than promised.
- Deceptive redirects changing domains after user action.
- Like-gating or share-gating that restricts access to exclusive content.
- Domain impersonation using "typos, misspellings or other means".
- Clickjacking and popup tactics trapping users on landing pages.

## Claims this source supports

1. Meta prohibits "requiring users to engage with content before accessing promised
   material." A campaign phrased as "you must comment to get the guide" sits directly on
   that line. A campaign phrased as "comment and I will also DM you the link, which is
   also in my bio" does not gate access on engagement.
2. Cash-prize giveaways in exchange for engagement are prohibited outright.
3. Frequency alone can trigger enforcement, and lower frequency plus other spam signals
   can too. Volume discipline is not sufficient by itself.
4. The link delivered must match what was promised in the post, or it is a misleading
   link.
