## Source-quality caveat

LinkedIn's own User Agreement and Help Center are blocked to automated fetching by
robots.txt, so the numeric limits below could not be obtained from an official document.
This is a named research gap. The vendor number is corroborated independently in
`leadharvest--platform-rules--linkedin-crackdown-anybiz-2026.md`, and the vendor itself
concedes the numbers are not official.

## Extracted content

**LinkedIn does not publish limits**

> "LinkedIn doesn't publish an official LinkedIn connection request limit or public
> message limits. These ranges come from observed usage patterns."

**Connection requests**

| Account age | Daily | Weekly |
|---|---|---|
| New or reactivated, first 90 days | 10 to 15 | 40 to 60 |
| Aged, 90 days or more | 15 to 25 | 60 to 100 |

**Direct messages to first-degree connections**

| Account age | Daily |
|---|---|
| New | 20 to 40 |
| Aged | 40 to 80 |

**Profile views**

| Account age | Daily | Weekly |
|---|---|---|
| New | 100 to 200 | not stated |
| Aged | 150 to 300 | 1,000 to 2,000 |

**Supporting actions, daily**

- Likes: 30 to 100
- Comments: 10 to 30
- Follows: 20 to 50

**Enforcement escalation, three levels**

1. Soft warnings: "Unusual activity banners, filters kicking in, or frequent captchas"
2. Temporary restrictions: "Weekly invitation limit reached" notices, account
   restrictions lasting 3 to 7 days
3. Permanent bans: "Ignoring early warnings can eventually lead to permanent bans on your
   LinkedIn account"

InMail limits and the commercial use limit are not covered by this article.

## Claims this source supports

1. There is no official published LinkedIn limit. Any number a tool quotes is inferred
   from observation, including this one.
2. The practical ceiling for connection requests is roughly 100 per week on an aged
   account, lower on a new one.
3. Enforcement is graduated and starts with signals a human can notice (captchas, unusual
   activity banners) before it becomes a restriction.
