# Can CAN-SPAM Apply to Social Media? Yes It Can.

## Source-quality caveat

The underlying decision is from 2011, well outside a 6-month research window. It is
archived deliberately because it is the controlling authority on a question with no
newer answer in this archive, and because its age is itself relevant: this is a
district-court holding, not settled appellate law. Flagged as an old source on purpose.

## Extracted content

**Case**

Facebook, Inc. v. MAXBOUNTY, Inc., Case No. CV-10-4712-JF (N.D. Cal., March 28, 2011).

**Holding**

The court determined that communications posted on Facebook qualify as "commercial
electronic mail message[s]" under the CAN-SPAM Act, rejecting MAXBOUNTY's argument that
the statute applies only to traditional email.

**Reasoning**

The court read "electronic mail address" expansively to cover social media
communications, defining it as a "destination...to which an electronic mail message can
be sent or delivered," relying on the statute's "broad legislative purpose" to combat
misleading commercial communications that burden internet infrastructure.

**Caveats stated in the source**

- The court declined to dismiss the CAN-SPAM claims but dismissed a conspiracy claim.
- The decision addressed only whether the Act applies to social media. The court had not
  ruled on the merits of the substantive allegations.
- The holding concerns advertising messages sent to individual users through social media
  platforms.

## Claims this source supports

1. The clean line "CAN-SPAM is for email, platform rules are for DMs" is not reliable. At
   least one federal district court has held that CAN-SPAM can reach commercial messages
   delivered through a social platform.
2. The practical division still holds for most operators: platform DM rules are
   CONTRACTUAL, enforced by the platform through account restriction and termination
   under its user agreement, while CAN-SPAM is STATUTORY, enforced by the FTC with
   per-message civil penalties. The MaxBounty holding shows the statutory route is not
   categorically closed for social messages.
3. Consequence for design: the enforcement a keyword-campaign operator will actually
   encounter is contractual (a restricted or banned account), and it arrives faster and
   hurts more immediately than a regulatory action would. Both risks are real; the
   contractual one is the near-term one.
