# Appropriateness of Alerts and Physicians' Responses With a Medication-Related Clinical Decision Support System

- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9579928/
  (also published at https://medinform.jmir.org/2022/10/e40511)
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (JMIR Medical Informatics, peer reviewed, retrospective
  observational study)
- **Why archived:** The single hardest number in the archive on what a low-precision alert
  stream does to the human receiving it. Archived deliberately outside the 12-month window
  because it is a primary measured result, not a news item, and nothing newer in the sweep
  supersedes its figures.

## Design

Retrospective observational study with stratified sampling of medication-related alerts in
an emergency department. Data from August 2019 to June 2020. 382 alert cases received
detailed medical record review by a panel of a physician, a pharmacist, and a nurse.

## Headline numbers

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Alerts overridden | 92.9% (355 of 382) |
| Alerts judged clinically appropriate | 7.3% (28 of 382) |
| Alerts judged inappropriate | 92.7% (354 of 382) |
| Physician responses judged appropriate | 92.4% (353 of 382) |
| Physician responses judged inappropriate | 6% (23 of 382) |

## Four-way classification of each alert plus response

| Category | Share |
|---|---|
| Successfully triggered (appropriate alert, appropriate response) | 3.4% (13 of 382) |
| Physician nonadherence (appropriate alert, inappropriate response) | 3.9% (15 of 382) |
| Unintended adverse consequence (inappropriate alert, inappropriate response) | 2.1% (8 of 382) |
| Justifiable override (inappropriate alert, appropriate override) | 89% (340 of 382) |

The dominant category, at 89%, is the system being wrong and the human being right to
ignore it.

## Conclusions

Quoted: "Alert fatigue is unavoidable when a large number of irrelevant alerts are
generated in response to a small number of useful alerts."

Cause identified: the rule-based algorithm "operates as a rule base without reflecting the
individual condition of the patient", generating excessive unnecessary alerts that drive
habitual override behavior.
