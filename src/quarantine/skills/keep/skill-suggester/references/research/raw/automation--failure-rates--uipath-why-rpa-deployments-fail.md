# Why Some RPA Deployments Fail. And What You Can Do About It.

- **URL:** https://www.uipath.com/blog/rpa/why-rpa-deployments-fail
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (RPA vendor, relaying a consultancy figure)
- **Why archived:** It carries the most-cited automation failure-rate figure and four named
  causes. Archived with an explicit provenance warning, because the headline number is a
  vendor blog quoting a consultancy rather than a measurement anyone can check.

## The headline figure, quoted

> "30 to 50 percent of RPA projects initially fail"

Attributed on the page to an Ernst and Young report.

**Provenance warning, and carry it forward.** This is a vendor page citing a consultancy
report, with no linked methodology, no sample size, and no definition of "initially fail".
It is a widely repeated number, which is not the same as a verified one. Any use of it must
be labelled as an external low-confidence claim with its chain named. Do not restate it as
"studies show".

A second view on the same page, from Genpact, argues that such failures lead to greater
learning for an enterprise's overall digital transformation journey. That is a vendor
reframing rather than a counter-measurement, and it is recorded here only so the page is
represented fairly.

## The four named causes of failure

1. **Forgetting business and IT collaboration.** Weak project governance follows from
   insufficient communication between business operations and IT, which prevents efficient
   resolution of integration issues.
2. **Relying on weak governance.** Companies underestimate post-implementation maintenance.
   Processes, interfaces and data formats change regularly, requiring continuous planning
   and testing.
3. **Automating in a non-efficient way.** Organizations automate the wrong processes, or
   non-optimized processes, or overly complex ones with high exception rates, stated on the
   page as exceeding 40 percent, rather than optimizing the process first.
4. **Building on bad design.** Rushed implementations rely on unstable, non-scalable
   frameworks with no roadmap for milestones, skills, process selection, budget and pilots.

The page notes these frequently occur simultaneously, producing what it calls a significant
multiplier effect.

## The two transferable causes

Causes 2 and 3 transfer directly to a skill proposal, and the other two do not, because a
single operator has no business-IT boundary and no governance body.

- **Maintenance is underestimated.** The thing being automated keeps changing underneath the
  automation. A proposal that does not name who maintains it is incomplete.
- **Exception rate is the disqualifier.** The 40 percent exception figure is a vendor
  heuristic rather than a measured threshold, but the direction is the point: high
  variability is the property that turns a plausible candidate into a failed one.
