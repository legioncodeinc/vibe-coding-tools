# 7 Criteria for Successful RPA Processes to Avoid Failed Automations

- **URL:** https://www.enterbridge.com/blog/how-to-avoid-failed-automations-7-criteria-for-successful-rpa
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (systems integrator practitioner content)
- **Why archived:** A compact, explicit candidate-assessment checklist, including the
  negative cases. The negative cases are the reason this was archived: most published
  criteria lists only say what a good candidate looks like, and the useful half of a
  suggester is knowing when to say no.

## The seven positive criteria

| # | Criterion | As stated |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Time-consuming | Processes requiring large amounts of time, framed as hours out of the day |
| 2 | High volume | Tasks that must be done over and over, happening multiple times daily or continuously, yield the highest return |
| 3 | Repetitive | Stable processes that do not change often and run at a regular cadence work best |
| 4 | Well defined | Requires a clear map where every situation is defined, with explicit instructions for all scenarios |
| 5 | Prone to error | Processes where human errors are common, or where the consequence of one is high |
| 6 | Involves multiple employees | Automating a workflow affecting several people increases return compared to a single-person task |
| 7 | Involves disparate systems | Processes requiring multiple systems suit an automation that bridges them |

## What makes a bad candidate

- **Too simple.** A process taking only 15 minutes weekly is difficult to justify
  automating.
- **Lacks rules-based structure.** Tasks requiring decision-making or adaptation are
  unsuitable.
- **Too complex.** Processes where most transactions need human intuition are not feasible,
  though partial automation may still work.

The page states that human judgment and nuance are the key limitation, and that automation
cannot handle unpredictable situations or edge cases without manual intervention.

## The calibration caveat, stated because it is not in the source

Criteria 1 and 2 and the too-simple exclusion are all calibrated to enterprise RPA, where
building a bot is a funded project with development, testing, governance and maintenance
cost. The bar for "worth automating" moves with the cost of building. A source that assumes
a project-sized build cost cannot be read as setting a threshold for an artifact that costs
one working session to produce. The criteria transfer; the volume thresholds do not.
