# A systematic review and meta-analysis of discrepancies between logged and self-reported digital media use

- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01117-5
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Nature Human Behaviour, 2021)
- **Why archived:** The modern, aggregated version of the same finding. It is the source
  that makes the self-report problem a general property rather than a quirk of one study,
  and it is recent enough to sit inside the research window.

## Scale

The meta-analysis included **106 effect sizes** across the reviewed studies comparing
self-reported and objectively logged measurement of digital media use.

## Headline finding, quoted

> "Self-reported media use correlates only moderately with logged measurements, that
> self-reports were rarely an accurate reflection of logged media use."

## Direction of bias

The analysis includes a forest plot of the ratio of means between self-reported and logged
measures, indicating systematic directional bias across studies. The fetched material did
not resolve a single uniform direction across all study types.

## Authors' conclusion, quoted

> "findings raise concerns about the validity of findings relying solely on self-reported
> measures of media use"

with a recommendation of caution when self-reports are used to test associations between
media use and other outcomes. The review additionally reports that measures of problematic
use show an even weaker association with usage logs than measures of ordinary use.

## Named gap in what was retrieved

The specific pooled correlation coefficient was not present in the accessible portion of the
page at fetch time. The distillation therefore cites this source for the qualitative claim
that the correlation is moderate and self-reports are rarely accurate, and cites the two

## Domain caveat

The domain is digital media use, not work tasks. What transfers is the measurement claim:
when a person's estimate of their own screen behavior is checked against a log, the estimate
does not hold up. It does not transfer as a claim about how people estimate work
specifically. The two primary studies in this archive cover that case.
