# Speed to lead: what it is and how fast you need to be (Lead Source)

- **Title:** Speed to lead: what it is and how fast you need to be
- **URL:** https://leadsource.co/blog/speed-to-lead-evidence
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Lead Source, presented as an evidence review)

## Why this source is in the archive

It is the only page found in this sweep that inventories the speed-to-lead evidence base
and criticizes it, rather than repeating the headline numbers. The publisher sells lead
response tooling, so it is not disinterested, but its criticisms cut AGAINST its own
commercial interest, which raises their credibility.

## Extracted content

**The studies it inventories**

| Study | Sample | Headline |
|---|---|---|
| 2007 Lead Response Management, Oldroyd with InsideSales.com | "3 years, 6 companies, 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ calls" | "5 minutes vs 30 minutes: 100x contact odds, 21x qualify odds" |
| 2011 Harvard Business Review study | "2,241 US companies, test-lead audit" | "Average response 42 hours; 23% never responded" |
| 2011 HBR lead-life dataset | "1.25M leads, 29 B2C and 13 B2B firms" | "Within 1 hour: nearly 7x more likely to qualify than 1 hour later" |
| 2024 RevenueHero mystery shop | 1,000 B2B SaaS companies | "Average response 1 day 5 hours; 63.5% never replied" |
| 2026 Workato audit | 114 B2B companies | "More than 99% failed to respond within five minutes" |

**The criticism, quoted**

> "the evidence is strong on direction and rough magnitude, and weaker on exact figures"

Specific weaknesses it names:

- The 2007 study "used only six companies and never measured revenue".
- Harvard's data "predate mobile-first buying, modern chat, and instant calendar booking".

**Channel coverage.** The page does not state whether the studies isolate phone follow-up
of form-fill leads from other channels.

## Claims this source supports

1. The speed-to-lead literature is a small number of studies, heavily recycled, with the
   two most-cited from 2007 and 2011.
2. The best available summary of its strength is: direction strong, magnitude rough, exact
   figures weak.
3. The 2007 study never measured revenue. So "faster contact makes more money" is an
   inference layered on top of "faster contact reaches and qualifies more people".
4. Independent audits from 2024 and 2026 confirm that most companies are still slow. The
   size of the opportunity is corroborated even where the conversion multipliers are not.
