## Extracted content

**Definition, Article 6(1)(f).** Legitimate interest "allows an organization to use
someone's personal data without asking for consent if it has a real and reasonable reason
to do so", applying only where "that reason is not more important than the person's privacy
rights and freedoms".

**The three-part assessment**

| Test | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The interest is "real, specific, and lawful". Vague justifications such as "improving our services" fail. |
| Necessity | Processing is "genuinely necessary to achieve that purpose". Necessary means no reasonably available alternative with a smaller privacy footprint, not merely useful. |
| Balancing | Weigh the interest against impact on the individual: data sensitivity, reasonable expectations, potential harm, the existing relationship, and available safeguards. |

**Where legitimate interest cannot be used**

- Tracking cookies and online tracking, where the ePrivacy Directive requires consent.
- Special category data under Article 9.
- Selling personal data to third parties.
- Processing children's data for marketing.
- Fully automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, Article 22.

**Direct marketing and Recital 47.** Recital 47 "specifically identifies" direct marketing
to EXISTING CUSTOMERS as capable of qualifying. An "easy opt-out must always be available"
and objections must be honored immediately.

**Right to object.** "Individuals can object at any time to processing based on legitimate
interest", and the organization must stop unless it demonstrates "compelling overriding
grounds".

**Public sources and social media.** Not addressed by this document.

## Claims this source supports

1. Legitimate interest is not a checkbox. It is a documented three-part assessment, and the
   balancing test turns on reasonable expectations, which is the same test the ICO applies
   to social sourced data.
2. Recital 47's direct marketing carve-out is framed around EXISTING CUSTOMERS. A commenter
   who has never bought anything is not that, so the carve-out does not obviously reach
   them.
3. An easy opt-out is mandatory wherever legitimate interest is relied on, which means the
   CRM record needs a working unsubscribe and do-not-contact path from day one.
4. Objection is absolute for direct marketing and takes effect immediately.
