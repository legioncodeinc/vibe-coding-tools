# Collect information and generate leads (ICO Direct Marketing Guidance)

- **Title:** Collect information and generate leads
- **URL:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/collect-information-and-generate-leads/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (Information Commissioner's Office, UK data protection regulator)

## Why this is the most important source in this archive

It is the only regulator document found in this sweep that speaks DIRECTLY to the question
the skill has to answer: someone commented publicly on my post, may I put them in my
marketing CRM and message them.

## Extracted content

**Publicly available data does not license direct marketing.** The ICO states that data
protection law and PECR

> "don't necessarily prevent you from doing this but there may be restrictions."

And the decisive passage:

> "Because someone's social media page has not been made private or they are seeking a
> large audience for their social media post doesn't mean that you are free to use their
> personal information for direct marketing purposes."

The test the ICO applies is whether the person would EXPECT to be contacted this way.

**Article 14 privacy information, when data is not collected from the individual**

> "You must give people your privacy information within a reasonable period and at the
> latest within a month of obtaining their information."

And for direct marketing specifically:

> "The latest point at which you must provide your privacy information is when you first
> communicate with that person or disclose their information to someone else. But the one
> month time limit still applies, so it is a case of whichever is sooner."

Required disclosures include "the categories of their information you hold" and "the
source of their information".

**Due diligence on indirectly obtained data**

> "It is not enough to simply accept a third party's assurances that the information they
> are supplying to you is compliant."

Due diligence should verify the source, the collection method, consent records, accuracy
checks, and how the seller handles data subject rights.

## Claims this source supports

1. "They engaged publicly, so it is fine" is explicitly rejected by the UK regulator. Public
   visibility of a comment is not permission to market to the commenter.
2. The governing test is reasonable expectation. A person who commented a keyword asking
   for a specific resource plausibly expects to receive that resource. That same person did
   not thereby expect to enter a nurture sequence.
3. Recording the SOURCE of the information is a regulatory requirement, not just good CRM
   hygiene. The receipt, the surface, and the campaign belong on the record.
4. Privacy information must reach the person by the first communication at the latest, and
   within one month regardless.
5. The reasonable-expectation test is the reason the skill must separate the direct reply
   to a hand-raiser from enrolment in ongoing marketing. They are different processing
   purposes with different expectations attached.
