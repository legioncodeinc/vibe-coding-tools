# Lead Response Management Study, executive summary (Oldroyd / InsideSales.com)

- **Title:** Lead Response Management Study, Executive Summary (a 7 page summary of the original 35 page study)
- **URL:** https://www.onecavo.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/MIT-InsideSales.com_Lead-Response-Management.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic-industry hybrid (primary study, PDF, hosted by a third party)

## Age disclosure

This study is from 2007 and falls far outside the default 6 month research window. It is
archived on purpose, because it is the ORIGINAL source of nearly every speed-to-lead
number in circulation in 2026. Every vendor page in this archive traces back to it. You
cannot honestly assess the evidence base without reading the primary document.

## Extracted content

**Authors and institutions**

> "Dave Elkington, CEO InsideSales.com" and "James Oldroyd, PhD Professor MIT"

Research originally conducted at Northwestern University's Kellogg School of Management.

**Dates.** Survey conducted June to September 2007. Presented 2007-10-16 at MarketingSherpa's
summit.

**Sample**

| Component | Size |
|---|---|
| Kellogg survey | 495 company responses, 40 plus industries |
| MIT / InsideSales dataset | "six companies...over fifteen thousand leads and over one hundred thousand call attempts" across 3 years |

**Headline findings**

- Odds of contacting a lead "decrease by over 10 times in the 1st hour".
- Odds of qualifying a lead "decrease by over 6 times in the 1st hour".
- Comparing a 5 minute response to a 30 minute response: odds of contacting "drop 100
  times" and odds of qualifying "drop 21 times".

**Channel, stated explicitly**

> "Leads that were captured through a web form, and attempted or called at least one time"

The response mechanism studied is a PHONE CALL. The lead origin studied is a WEB FORM.

**Timing findings.** Wednesday and Thursday are the best days. 4pm to 6pm and 8am to 9am
are the best calling windows.

**Definition of "qualify".** Not defined in the document.

**Stated limitations.** None. The document does not state methodology limitations.

## Claims this source supports

1. The foundational speed-to-lead dataset is six companies, web-form leads, worked by
   outbound phone call, in 2007. Not social comments. Not direct messages.
2. The famous "100x" and "21x" figures are ODDS RATIOS comparing 5 minutes to 30 minutes,
   not conversion rates and not revenue.
3. "Qualify" is never defined in the executive summary, so the 21x figure has no anchored
   denominator.
4. The direction of the effect is measured on a large call volume and is credible. The
   magnitude is anchored to a channel and an era that do not match a Facebook commenter in
   2026.
