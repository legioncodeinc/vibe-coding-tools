## Extracted content

**2026 benchmark figures**

Optifai, Q2 2025 to Q1 2026, 939 B2B SaaS companies:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Close rate, response under 5 minutes | 32% |
| Close rate, response 24 hours or more | 12% |
| Companies responding within 5 minutes | 23% |
| Companies taking more than 24 hours | 42% |

Blazeo, February 2026, 573 companies across 6 industries:

- 54.9% hit a 15 minute SLA when a formal SLA existed, versus 29.5% without one.
- 62.5% of AI and automation users met an under-15-minute standard, versus 39.1% manual.
- 81.2% of slow responders (over 1 hour) lose leads, versus 46.6% of fast responders.

RevenueHero, 2024, 1,000 B2B SaaS companies: "63.5%" never replied to demo requests;
average response time "1d 5h 17m".

**SLA ladder recommended**

| Tier | Target |
|---|---|
| Hot MQL, high fit and high intent | under 5 minutes |
| Warm MQL, high fit | under 15 minutes |
| Warm MQL, low fit | under 1 hour |
| Cold MQL | same business day |

**Channel coverage gap, important.** The article names leads arriving "via a form, a chat
widget, a calendar link, a marketplace listing, or a social DM" but provides NO benchmark
data segmenting response time or conversion by channel. Nothing here distinguishes social
or DM performance from web-form phone follow-up.

**Caveats the article states about its own sources**

1. "The 2007 MIT/InsideSales study" is "vendor-platform data drawn from InsideSales'
   customer base, not a randomised controlled trial."
2. The Drift 2018 figures are "now only available through secondary aggregators because
   the original report URL has gone offline, treat them as directional."
3. "Vendor-commissioned entries: InsideSales, Velocifi, and Blazeo all sell lead-response
   software, so their data is credible but not disinterested."
4. The widely repeated "80% quality drop" claim "appears to be an editorial extrapolation
   of HBR's decay curves rather than a directly stated figure."

## Claims this source supports

1. Recent 2025 and 2026 datasets reproduce the direction of the effect on much larger
   company samples than the 2007 original.
2. The magnitude in modern data is far more modest than the legendary figures: 32% versus
   12% close rate, roughly a 2.7x difference, not 21x or 100x.
3. No published source in this archive segments speed-to-lead outcomes by channel. Applying
   any of these numbers to a social commenter is an assumption, and it must be labeled one.
4. A named, written SLA is itself associated with better response performance, independent
   of tooling. That is a finding a daily drip skill can act on: pick a target and write it
   down.
5. At least one widely repeated speed-to-lead statistic appears to be an extrapolation
   rather than a published finding.
