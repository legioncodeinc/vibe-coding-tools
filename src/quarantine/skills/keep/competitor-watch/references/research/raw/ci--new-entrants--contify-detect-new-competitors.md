## Eight detection methods, as listed

1. **Sales calls.** "Transcripts of sales calls can help you find company competitors"
   already in prospect consideration sets.
2. **Analyst reports.** IDC, Gartner, Nielsen categorize emerging players by tier and
   competitive positioning.
3. **Industry events and conferences.** Direct observation through demos and speaker
   panels.
4. **Social media.** LinkedIn updates, Twitter discussions, industry group mentions.
5. **Press releases and news.** "product launches, funding rounds, and go-to-market
   signals."
6. **Customer reviews.** G2, TrustPilot, Gartner Peer Insights reveal customer
   comparisons and alternatives.
7. **Patent and trademark filings.** USPTO and EPO searches expose "innovation pipelines"
   and adjacent category entrants.
8. **Non-English sources.** Local news and forums in regional languages identify emerging
   competitors before global expansion.

## Signals of market entry, as listed

- Unfamiliar competitor names mentioned in sales conversations
- Funding announcements and capital raises
- Executive or leadership changes
- Product launch announcements
- Management transitions
- Customer preference shifts

## Tiering

The article references Tier 1 (primary) and Tier 2 (secondary or emerging) tracking based
on deal impact. It does not formally define direct, indirect, or replacement competitor
categories.

## Frequency

No re-scan interval is recommended. The guidance is continuous monitoring, stating that
competitor identification "is not a one-time exercise."

## Note on method ordering

Sales calls are listed first. This source puts a first-party conversational source ahead
of every public monitoring source for the specific job of finding names you do not
already track.
