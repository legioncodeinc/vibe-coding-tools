# When Competitive Intelligence Goes Too Far

- **URL:** https://www.kompyte.com/blog/when-competitive-intelligence-goes-too-far
- **Publisher:** Kompyte (competitive intelligence vendor)
- **Published:** 2023-11-20
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## Named trade secret cases

1. **Uber and Waymo (2016).** Anthony Levandowski "downloaded over 14,000 files" from
   Google's Waymo while accepting an Uber position, including classified autonomous
   vehicle technology. Outcome: 33 theft counts, 179 million dollars restitution, 18
   months prison, later pardoned.
2. **Tesla and XPeng.** Guangzhi Cao sent "300,000 files and directories" to personal
   iCloud, including Autopilot source code, after accepting a job with XPeng. Settled
   2021, amount undisclosed.
3. **Apple and Baidu (2017).** Weibao Wang accessed "sensitive proprietary and
   confidential information" on Apple self-driving vehicles while employed at a Baidu
   subsidiary. Faces a maximum of 10 years and 250,000 dollars in fines per count.
4. **X and Meta (Threads).** X accused Meta of intentionally hiring former X employees to
   build Threads.

## The line as this source draws it

**Acceptable:** information gathered through "public databases, industry reports, online
platforms, and transparent market research" and "publicly accessible sources" including
websites, review sites, social media, paid media, and job postings.

**Unacceptable:** using current or former employees to obtain trade secrets; operating
without transparency about your identity and employer; purchasing stolen confidential
information.

## Gap in this source

The article gives no guidance on information received inadvertently, offered unsolicited
by a customer or partner, or observed during a screen share. It also references "Busting
Competitor NDAs" without stating a handling rule. Every case it names involves a person
who took files they were not entitled to take. The passive-observation case is not
addressed.
