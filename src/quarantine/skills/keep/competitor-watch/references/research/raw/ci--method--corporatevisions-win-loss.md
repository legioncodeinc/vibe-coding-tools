## Definition

Win-loss analysis systematically collects buyer feedback on closed deals to expose
competitive dynamics, positioning, and vulnerabilities that do not appear in public
marketing material or in traditional competitive intelligence.

## The three layers of competitive information

- **Visible layer.** Public marketing messages and feature comparisons, what everyone
  sees.
- **Shadow layer.** Hidden competitive dynamics during actual deals: "positioning shifts,
  unofficial narratives, and real-time tactical adjustments".
- **Strategic layer.** Deeper patterns showing where competitors consistently win or
  lose.

## Why seller-sourced explanations fail

When deals close, "sellers default to the usual suspects: pricing was too high, features
were missing, or that old classic, 'the decision was made before we got there.'" Buyer
feedback reveals "a dramatically different story."

## Findings from 6,000 plus B2B deals

1. **The competition myth.** Only **25 percent** of enterprise deals are truly
   competitive, meaning buyers see comparable value among options. "competitors aren't
   winning with better features or lower prices, they're winning by preventing true
   competition from emerging in the first place."
2. **The stakeholder pattern.** Economic buyers appear more frequently in competitive
   deals. Where competitors engage end users alongside economic buyers early, deals are
   "significantly less likely to become price-focused negotiations."
3. **Value-price alignment.** In competitive deals, "buyers focus more on total cost and
   less on solution capabilities."

## The seller-buyer perception gap, worked example

At SPS Commerce, reps assumed they lost a "standard deal" on typical competitive factors.
Automated win-loss analysis revealed the competitor's "shadow strategy, one that never
appeared in their public marketing materials." That hidden positioning surfaced only
through buyer feedback, and the company re-engaged and won the deal.

## Cadence and method

- Automated feedback collection triggered after deals close.
- Dynamic battlecards update "with each win and loss" rather than remaining static.
- At Highspot, "reps are getting email notifications every single time a deal closes or
  feedback comes in".
- Cross-deal pattern analysis: "Only by analyzing feedback across many deals can you
  expose the competitive patterns that matter."
- On consistency: "One-off buyer interviews might reveal interesting insights, but only
  by analyzing feedback across many deals can you expose the competitive patterns that
  matter."
