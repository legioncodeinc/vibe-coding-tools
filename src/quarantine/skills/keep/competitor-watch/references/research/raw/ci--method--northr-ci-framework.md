## The four-step framework

1. **Collect.** Product intelligence (features, changes, reviews), pricing intelligence
   (costs, tiers, hidden fees), positioning intelligence (messaging, targeting, claims).
2. **Analyze.** Three questions: what changed and why does it matter, where are the
   strengths and weaknesses, what patterns emerge from sales conversations.
3. **Distribute.** Battle cards, competitive alerts, win-loss debriefs.
4. **Act.** Use intelligence before, during, and after calls to handle objections.

## Cadence recommendations, as stated

| Activity | Recommended cadence |
|---|---|
| Pricing verification | Before every competitive deal |
| Battle card refresh | Quarterly, or on significant competitor change |
| Monitoring | Weekly scans of competitor news and updates |
| Win-loss debriefs | Document after competitive deals, share learnings weekly |

## The three tracked categories

- Product intelligence: offerings, functionality, recent changes
- Pricing intelligence: charges, tier inclusions, costs
- Positioning intelligence: self-description, target audience, claims

## Guidance for small teams

"CI is a habit, not a tool." For startups, one person (founder or sales leader) typically
synthesizes and distributes, while "everyone contributes observations." The article
states: "You do not need enterprise monitoring tools to start. Google Alerts (free), G2
(free), monthly website checks" are sufficient initially. Start with one battle card for
the top competitor.
