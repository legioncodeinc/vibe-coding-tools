# Ethics of Researching Your Competitors

- **URL:** https://www.aqute.com/blog/is-competitive-intelligence-ethical
- **Publisher:** Aqute Intelligence (competitive intelligence agency)
- **Published:** page shows 9 October, year not stated
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## Core position

"Yes, competitor intelligence is an ethical business activity. In that sense, it is no
different from traditional market research."

## The decision rule offered

"if you find yourself asking whether a practice is legal, then you probably shouldn't be
doing it." The firm argues for avoiding gray areas entirely, asserting that ethical
methods do not compromise effectiveness.

## Prohibited

Theft, eavesdropping, hacking. "No corporate espionage. No industrial espionage. No trade
secrets. No confidential information."

## Third-party information

Gathering publicly available information and interviewing industry professionals is
endorsed as a "normal and natural part of trading", noting this happens routinely at
trade shows and similar venues.

## Confidentiality method

The firm uses "double blind research": researchers do not know the client's identity when
conducting interviews, and client details never reach interviewees. Stated purpose is
that this "encourages our interviewees to speak freely."

## Professional standards

References the SCIP code, specifically the requirement to "accurately disclose all
relevant information, including one's identity and organization, prior to all
interviews."
