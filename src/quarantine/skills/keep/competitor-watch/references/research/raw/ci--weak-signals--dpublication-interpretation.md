## Definitions of a weak signal

- **Ansoff (1975).** Weak signals are "bribes of information" corresponding to an early,
  vague knowledge state. Unstructured, fragmented, incomplete environmental data situated
  in "the hazy area at the edge of an organization's vision" (Day and Schoemaker, 2005).
- **Godet**, as cited by Ilmola and Kuusi (2006): "a factor of change hardly perceptible
  at present, but which will constitute a strong trend in the future."
- **Hiltunen's semiotic model (2008).** A weak signal has three dimensions: signal,
  question, and interpretation. Interpretation is inherently subjective and tied to the
  observer's context.

## Ansoff's states of knowledge, as presented in the paper's Figure 1

1. **Early stage.** Only the conviction exists that a rupture-marked event is beginning.
2. **Intermediate phases.** Progressive enrichment of knowledge through successive
   information waves.
3. **Final stage.** Sufficient information is available to plan a strategic response to
   the discontinuity.

Each phase corresponds to increasingly specific strategic response options, beginning
with low-impact preparatory actions.

## Weak signals versus noise

Noise is "the background mass of parasitic signs and irrelevant facts pointing in
inconsistent directions" (Mendonca et al., 2012; Day and Schoemaker, 2005).

Stated directly: "It is difficult to distinguish between noise and weak signals since
there is no practical formula for distinguishing and assessing intuition."

## Ansoff's three filters

1. **Observation filter.** Collects and selects environmental information.
2. **Cognitive filter.** Assesses value and relevance.
3. **Power filter.** Transmits knowledge to top management and decision makers.

## Hiltunen's subjective test (2008)

Information qualifies as a weak signal if it provokes particular expert reactions:

- Does it cause colleagues to laugh?
- Do colleagues deny it will happen?
- Does it stimulate thinking?
- Is it previously unheard?
- Is it taboo or unspoken?

## Hiltunen's weakness classification (2008)

- **Early warnings.** Low number and low visibility, for example a new invention.
- **First symptoms.** Numerous and visible signals, but difficult to interpret, for
  example behavioral changes.

## Interpretation and sense-making

- **Constructivist view (Rossel, 2011; Seidl, 2004).** Weak signals are "constructions or
  a particular way of organizing incoming information", requiring alignment with the
  observer's model of reality.
- **Cognitive determination.** Weak signals must be "conceptualized cognitively, as
  cognitive phenomena, determined by the structures of the cognitive system."
- **Organizational dilemma.** Tension between internal uncertainty-reduction patterns and
  peripheral weak-signal detection that should "shake the stability of these patterns."
- **Socio-cognitive construction.** Evolution from weak to strong signal involves
  "socio-cognitive construction of the reality which helps us to forge meaning and to act
  in a significant way."

## Why organizations miss weak signals

- **Philosophical limitations (Kuosa, 2010).** Three problems: conceptual (vague
  encompassing of any potential change), ontological (contradicts the understanding of
  "societal change being interconnected, non-linear, interpretable and multicausal"), and
  epistemological (observation approaches appear "very arbitrary").
- **Blind spots (Meissner, Brands, and Wulf, 2017).** Internal expert assessments
  significantly underestimate impact and uncertainty compared with external experts.
- **Collective challenge (Holopainen and Toivonen, 2012).** Requesting expert advice
  within organizations suppresses "radical people" who might offer outside-paradigm
  perspectives in official discourse.
- **Retrospective bias (Carbonell et al., 2015).** People retroactively "want to see
  warnings" in past events.
- **Strategic planning gap (Camillus and Datta, 1991).** Conventional planning systems
  lack "sensitivity to changing environments."

## Amplification and response

Ansoff's response escalation covers two categories that progress with signal strength:
external environment relationship responses, and internal firm dynamics and structural
responses. Low-impact responses to weak signals initiate "gradual development of the
firm's awareness about its external environment and internal reality", minimizing
response time when strong signals emerge.

## Weak signals versus wild cards

Wild cards are "discrete phenomena that cannot be recognized in advance" and "hit the
system as a one-time event", lacking detectable precursor signals. Weak signals, by
contrast, have precursors.
