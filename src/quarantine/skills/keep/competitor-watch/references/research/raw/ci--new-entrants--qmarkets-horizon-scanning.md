## Definition

Horizon scanning is "a structured method for identifying weak signals" and early-stage
developments. It operates proactively by surfacing emerging developments before
mainstream visibility, which distinguishes it from reactive trend monitoring.

## Relationship to weak signals

Weak signals are the core input. The process involves "gathering signals from a wide
range of sources" including patents, academic research, startup activity, venture
funding, and media analysis.

## Horizon scanning versus environmental scanning

Environmental scanning surveys present forces shaping the organization. Horizon scanning
"looks further out, picking up weak signals of developments that have yet to take shape."
Most mature programs run both simultaneously.

## The four process steps

1. **Define strategic scanning objectives.** Align scanning with growth goals and
   disruption areas.
2. **Collect and analyze weak signals.** Gather from diverse sources using filters and
   categorization.
3. **Prioritize, cluster, and share trends.** Group related signals, score by impact,
   timing, and relevance.
4. **Turn scanning insights into action.** Integrate findings into ideation pipelines.

## Frequency

"Most enterprise teams benefit from ongoing scanning supported by quarterly reviews."
Frequency should align with strategic planning cycles and industry evolution speed.

## Triage

Prioritization uses impact, timing, and strategic relevance scoring. The article covers
technological, economic, environmental, political, and societal domains but does not name
STEEP or PESTLE as frameworks.
