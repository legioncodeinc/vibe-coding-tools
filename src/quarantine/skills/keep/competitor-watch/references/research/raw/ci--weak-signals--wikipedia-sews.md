## Definition

A strategic early warning system is an organizational mechanism designed to "detect and
deal with new patterns, trends, or events at an early stage." Its function is identifying
weak signals that represent "important discontinuities in an organizational environment",
enabling proactive response.

## Process phases

- **Phase 1, information gathering.** Scanning organizational environments through media
  examination and content analysis; monitoring already-recognized trends and issues.
- **Phase 2, diagnosis.** In-depth analysis of phenomena examining core contexts and
  developmental potential; creative exploration of trend evolution; clustering multiple
  issues to understand mutual influences; prioritization of resource-relevant trends.
- **Phase 3, response.** Formulating strategic reactions to identified relevant trends.

## Limitations

Organizational blindspots are identified as a fundamental constraint, requiring formal
blindspot analysis inside the SEWS cycle. Resource limitations also restrict
comprehensive trend tracking.
