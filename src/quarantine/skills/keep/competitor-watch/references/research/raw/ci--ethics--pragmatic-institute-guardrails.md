## Legal guardrails, the statutes named

- Uniform Trade Secrets Act (UTSA)
- Economic Espionage Act of 1996 (EEA), addressing "theft or misappropriation of trade
  secrets"
- Antitrust regulation, which prohibits competitors from discussing market division or
  pricing
- Fraud statutes, covering misrepresentation of identity or purpose
- Local law: breaking and entering, trespassing, theft

## Vendor and contractor liability

"Your vendor must follow the same laws as your company." Companies remain liable where
vendors are pushed to violate law. The article recommends vetting a vendor's history for
"cease-and-desist orders or other related legal conflicts."

## Ethical guardrails

Two stated heuristics: "If it feels wrong, it probably is" and "If you don't want your
mother to see it in the news, you probably shouldn't do it."

The article directs companies to follow both internal corporate ethics policy and the
SCIP ethical guidelines.

## The misrepresentation prohibition

On protected information such as B2B contracts and pricing data: "CI ethics dictate that
you don't misrepresent yourself or your purpose to get it."

On trade shows: "It's OK to approach booth representatives to ask. It's not OK to pretend
to be someone else, like a customer, when you do it. Misrepresentation now can lead to
problems later."

On primary collection by direct communication: "you don't misrepresent your identity or
intent when collecting this information."

## Protected information categories

Named as "more protected than other information that's available in public documents":

- Pricing data
- Salesforce compensation
- Development and marketing timelines
- Bundling strategies

## NDA constraint on internally sourced intelligence

"employees hired from a competitor must honor any nondisclosure agreement signed with the
previous employer and should not be pressured to disclose information that would violate
that."

## Five collection approaches in ascending risk order

1. **Secondary CI (lowest risk).** Public and open-source material. Caveat: "when you
   request gated content that requires registration, ensure that you don't misrepresent
   yourself."
2. **Internal company sources (low risk).** Field and support employees contributing
   insights while adhering to legal and ethical guidelines.
3. **External informant network (moderate risk).** "Using a give-and-take approach (both
   seeking information from external sources when needed and sharing information with
   them)" rather than opportunistic collection.
4. **Trade show and event intelligence (moderate to high risk).** Asking booth
   representatives is permissible; impersonation is not.
5. **Primary intelligence via direct communication (highest risk).** Phone, email, face
   to face. "Expressing honest interest in the topic usually is enough to get more
   information."

## Prohibited practices as listed

- Lying, misrepresentation, or theft
- Pretending to be a customer at trade shows
- Misrepresenting identity or intent in direct communication
- Pressuring employees to violate NDAs
- Acting on pricing information while holding a dominant market position (antitrust
  exposure)

## Best practice on analysis

"conduct your analyses based on information triangulated from multiple sources, including
open-source, subscription, syndicated, and primary, to create meaningful insights."
