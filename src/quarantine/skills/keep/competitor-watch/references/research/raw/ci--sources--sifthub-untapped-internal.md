## The central argument

Internal data is the primary untapped resource. "Before you spend a dime on external
consultants or expensive scraping tools, look inside your own building." Organizations
hold "terabytes of proprietary competitive data" that stays "trapped in silos."

## Overlooked internal sources, as listed

- CRM deal notes in unstructured formats
- Call recordings from conversational intelligence platforms
- Internal Slack and Teams messages containing field intelligence
- Customer feedback and churn surveys

## Frontline intelligence versus public monitoring

Public sources (websites, press releases, reviews) represent controlled information.
Field intelligence, described as "rumors, screenshots, and emails" shared in internal
channels, provides unfiltered market reality.

## The recommended capture mechanism

"Create a dedicated #competitive-intel channel. Encourage 'scouts', reps, CSMs, and
support staff to dump rumors, screenshots, and emails there."

## Statistic

One figure appears, and it is illustrative rather than surveyed: "If 40% of lost deals
mention 'integrations,' and the notes specifically mention a competitor's new API, you
have found a product gap..."

## Capturing informal mentions

Systematic channel monitoring plus structured questioning during interviews. Suggested
question to prospects: "When you tested our reporting feature vs. [Competitor X], what
specifically made theirs easier to use?"
