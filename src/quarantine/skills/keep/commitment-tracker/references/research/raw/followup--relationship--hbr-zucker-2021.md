- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17

## Note on window

This piece is from January 2021, outside the default six-month research window. It is
included deliberately: the search for recent equivalents returned cold-outreach content

framing. Age is flagged rather than hidden.

## Core premise

"just because someone hasn't responded to your initial request, it doesn't mean their
answer is 'no.'"

## Extracted recommendations

1. **Subject line.** "Write a compelling subject line. Remember most emails are read on
   mobile devices, so keep it short."
2. **Tone.** "Be mindful of your tone. You want to come across as friendly and polite."
3. **Length.** "Keep your message brief and easily scannable."
4. **The ask.** "Make a clear ask, so the recipient knows exactly what you want."
5. **The out.** "Give the recipient an out. It will demonstrate humility and ease any
   potential discomfort."
6. **Persistence.** "Be persistent. You want to demonstrate assertiveness but also good
   judgment about when to move on."

## What the article does not cover

It does not give specific escalation tactics, channel-switching rules, or a timing
protocol. Those gaps are filled, imperfectly, by the cold-outreach cadence source.

## Direct implication for the skill

- "Non-response does not mean no" is the interpersonal version of the skill's core
  evidence rule: absence of evidence that a thing was done is not evidence that it was not
  done. The same discipline applies to the ledger status and to the nudge wording.
- "Give the recipient an out" is a concrete drafting requirement for every nudge the skill
  produces. A nudge that offers no graceful exit reads as a demand.
- "Make a clear ask" plus "keep it brief" means a drafted nudge names one item, not the
  whole aging bucket.
