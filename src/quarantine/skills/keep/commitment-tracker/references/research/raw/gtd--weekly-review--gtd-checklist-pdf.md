# GTD Weekly Review checklist

- **Title:** GTD Weekly Review (official checklist PDF)
- **URL:** https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Weekly_Review_Checklist.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (the David Allen Company)

## Extracted checklist

### GET CLEAR

- **Collect Loose Papers and Materials.** "Gather all accumulated business cards,
  receipts, and miscellaneous paper-based materials."
- **Get IN to Zero.** "Process completely all outstanding paper materials, journal and
  meeting notes, voicemails, dictation, and emails."
- **Empty Your Head.** Capture any uncaptured projects, action items, waiting-for items,
  and someday/maybe ideas.

### GET CURRENT

- **Review Action Lists.** Mark off completed actions and identify further steps needed.
- **Review Previous Calendar Data.** "Review past calendar in detail for remaining action
  items, reference data, etc., and transfer into the active system."
- **Review Upcoming Calendar.** Examine scheduled events and capture triggered actions.
- **Review Waiting For List.** "Record appropriate actions for any needed follow-up.
  Check off received ones."
- **Review Project Lists.** Evaluate the status of all projects and outcomes, making sure
  each has at least one active next action.
- **Review Relevant Checklists.** Use checklists to trigger new actions.

### GET CREATIVE

- **Review Someday Maybe List.** Identify projects ready to activate, remove items no
  longer relevant.
- **Be Creative and Courageous.** Add new ideas and possibilities to the system.

## The two lines that define this skill

Two checklist items are the entire premise of commitment-tracker, and they are the two items
knowledge workers most reliably skip:

1. "Review Previous Calendar Data ... for remaining action items ... and transfer into
   the active system." This is exactly what a Littlebird meeting archive makes automatable.
2. "Review Waiting For List. Record appropriate actions for any needed follow-up. Check
   off received ones." This is the owed-to-me column, and GTD names it as a first-class
   list rather than a footnote on the to-do list.

## Direct implication for the skill

The two-column ledger is not a new invention. It is the GTD Next Actions list and the GTD
Waiting For list, populated from meeting summaries instead of by hand. The weekly cadence
matches the method's own prescribed review frequency.
