# Meeting follow-ups: why 80% of action items get forgotten

- **Title:** Meeting Follow-Ups: Why 80% of Action Items Get Forgotten
- **URL:** https://www.meetingtoll.com/blog/meeting-follow-ups-action-items-forgotten
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community / content-marketing blog. **LOW TRUST. Archived as a negative
  finding, not as evidence.**

## Why this file exists

The forge required research into action-item completion rates. This page is representative
of what that search actually returns: a dense stack of confident percentages, none of them
sourced. It is archived so that the distillation can state, with a citation, that the
commonly circulated numbers do not survive checking.

## Claims made on the page, with their sourcing status

| Claim as written | Source cited on page |
|---|---|
| "Without follow-up, 70% of decisions are forgotten" | none |
| "24-hour follow-ups increase task recall by up to 80%" | none |
| "Teams implementing prompt, detailed follow-ups complete 36% more action items on time" | none |
| "Organizations using standardized follow-up protocols experience up to 50% reduction in miscommunication" | none |
| "The average one-hour meeting with eight participants costs approximately $400-$800" | none |

The one attributed idea is the **Ebbinghaus forgetting curve**, credited to Hermann
Ebbinghaus, used to support the claim that humans forget roughly 70 percent of new
information within 24 hours. That is a historical psychology result about memorizing
nonsense syllables, not an organizational study about meetings, and it is being stretched
well past what it measured.

## Verified finding

**None of the quantitative claims about meeting follow-ups on this page cite peer-reviewed
studies or original research.** The headline "80% of action items get forgotten" does not
appear in the body with a source attached.

## Direct implication for the skill

commitment-tracker must not open its report, its SKILL.md, or its guides with a statistic
about how many action items get forgotten. Every readily available number for that claim
traces back to content marketing. The skill justifies itself from the user's own ledger
instead: count the harvested items and the ones with no downstream evidence, from the
user's real meetings. That number is observed, has receipts, and is the only completion
rate the skill is entitled to report.
