# The Impact of Commitment, Accountability, and Written Goals on Goal Achievement

- **Title:** The Impact of Commitment, Accountability, and Written Goals on Goal Achievement (Gail Matthews, Dominican University of California)
- **URL:** https://scholar.dominican.edu/psychology-faculty-conference-presentations/3/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic, but conference presentation only, NOT peer-reviewed journal

## What the page actually contains

An abstract and a downloadable presentation. The landing page states the research
motivation: to test the widely repeated but unsupported "Yale study" claim about written
goals, and to examine how "writing goals, committing to goal-directed actions and being
accountable for those actions" influence goal achievement.

## What the page does NOT contain

The landing page does not give sample size, the experimental conditions, or the
per-condition achievement percentages. Those numbers circulate widely on productivity
blogs (commonly quoted as 43 percent for unwritten goals rising to 76 percent for written
goals plus weekly accountability reports) but they are NOT verifiable from this page.

## GAP, recorded explicitly

**This is a named research gap for commitment-tracker.** The commonly cited accountability
percentages could not be verified from the primary source at the level of detail needed.
The skill must not quote a specific percentage uplift from accountability reporting.

What IS safely supportable from this source: the author's stated framing that written
goals, action commitment, and accountability to another person are three separable
factors in goal achievement, and the author's own point that the popular "Yale study"
figure is a myth.

## Direct implication for the skill

Do not put a number on how much a ledger improves follow-through. Describe the mechanism,
cite the implementation-intentions evidence for the mechanism, and leave the magnitude
unquantified.
