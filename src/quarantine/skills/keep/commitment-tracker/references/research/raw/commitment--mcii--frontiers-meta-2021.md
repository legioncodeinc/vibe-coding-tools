# Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions: meta-analysis

- **Title:** Meta-analysis of Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions (MCII) and goal attainment
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Frontiers in Psychology, open access)

## Extracted claims

- Overall effect of MCII on goal achievement: **g = 0.336** (small to medium), pooled
  across **24 independent studies** and **15,907 participants**.
- Intervention delivery moderates the effect significantly:
  - Face to face experimenter delivered: **g = 0.465**
  - Document based, self administered: **g = 0.277**
  - The authors attribute the gap to interpersonal contact improving strategy quality and
    commitment.
- Named boundary conditions where the technique weakens or fails:
  1. **Culture.** Benefits appeared in individualist cultures and failed in collectivist
     cultures when the goal pursued was an individual one (citing Kizilcec and Cohen,
     2017).
  2. **Unsupervised self design.** "Low-quality strategies emerged when participants
     self-designed plans in unsupervised contexts."
  3. **Thin base.** Only 21 studies were usable for moderator analysis. The authors state
     "additional studies are needed to determine the role of moderator variables."

## Conflict with the Gollwitzer and Sheeran figure

This meta-analysis reports a notably smaller pooled effect (g = 0.336) than the 2006
meta-analysis of implementation intentions alone (d = .65). The constructs are not
identical: MCII bundles mental contrasting with the if-then plan, and this review is more
recent and includes larger, less controlled document-based interventions. Both readings
are archived. Neither should be presented as "planning doubles your completion rate."

## Direct implication for the skill

The document-based versus face-to-face split is the relevant one. A written ledger is a
document based intervention, which is the weaker arm (g = 0.277). That is an argument for
the skill producing something a human actually reads and reacts to, not a silent file, and
an argument against over claiming what the ledger will do.
