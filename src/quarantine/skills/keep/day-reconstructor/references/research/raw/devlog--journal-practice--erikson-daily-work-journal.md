# Coding Career Advice: Keeping a Daily Work Journal

Mark Erikson, blog.isquaredsoftware.com, 2020-09-21.

- **URL:** https://blog.isquaredsoftware.com/2020/09/coding-career-advice-daily-work-journal/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (personal blog of a working maintainer, Redux maintainer)
- **Why archived:** A long-running practitioner account of the same practice, with a
  concrete file structure and a concrete time cost. It also supplies the retrieval use
  case, going back to old notes to recover a solution, which is the single strongest
  argument for the problems-solved section.

## What is recorded

- "Tasks I worked on and what specific things I did for those"
- "Conversations with other developers on my team and what topics we covered"
- "Problems I ran into, and (hopefully) how I solved them"
- "Where I left off at the end of the day"
- "What tasks I need to prioritize the next day"
- "Useful code snippets"

## Format and cost

One folder per month, named like `2020-09 (September)`. One Markdown note per day, named
like `2020-09-21 (Monday)`. Time cost stated as **10 to 15 minutes** at the end of the day.

Practice started in 2013, so this is a decade-plus account by the time of writing rather
than a new-habit recommendation.

## Claimed benefits

1. **Resuming work.** "When I pick up work after a long weekend, it reminds me what I'm
   even supposed to be working on now."
2. **Recovering a past solution.** "There's been several times I dug back into my notes and
   found the solution I'd written down the last time" when the same problem recurred.
3. **Seeing growth.** Reading how problems were approached years earlier.
4. **Performance review.** Called out as the largest benefit: "it's *extremely* valuable
   come performance review season. I can go back through every single workday of the past
   year, see exactly what tasks I worked on."

## What this supplies to the skill

- A validated day-file structure and naming scheme.
- The 10 to 15 minute cost figure, which sets the bar the automated version must beat.
- The explicit statement that the recurring-problem lookup is a real retrieval event, not a
  hypothetical one.
- Confirmation that the day file is expected to be read again later, which is why this
  skill's routine must read its own past reports rather than emitting isolated days.
