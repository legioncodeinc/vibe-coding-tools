## Study design

Three phases:

- **Iterative development:** N=20 developers, driving the design of the tool through
  feedback.
- **Survey:** N=413 participants.
- **Field study:** N=43 software development professionals, over 3 weeks.

The probe tool was called WorkAnalytics. The resulting open-source self-monitoring tool is
PersonalAnalytics (HASEL-UZH/PersonalAnalytics on GitHub).

## What developers wanted from a retrospective

- "A large variety of different metrics to retrospect about work." Developers did not
  converge on one metric. They wanted several angles on the same day.
- Experience sampling during the day, to increase awareness of work and to generate deeper
  insight than logging alone.
- "Actionable insights, enriched with benchmarking data from co-workers." Comparative data
  from peers was valued as a driver of behaviour change and collaboration.

## The awareness premise

The framing of the work is that "one way to improve the productivity of knowledge workers
is to increase their self-awareness about productivity at work through self-monitoring."
The stated need for tool support to retrospect implies developers do not carry an accurate
picture of their own work patterns unaided. That is an implication of the paper's framing,
not a measured effect size, and is recorded here as such.

## Named gaps

- Neither retrieved page states what was logged in detail.
- Neither retrieved page carries the paper's findings on privacy concerns about workplace
  monitoring, which the full paper is understood to discuss. Not in this archive. Do not
  cite this file for a privacy claim.
- No quantified gap between recalled work and logged work appears in either page.

## Note on retrieval fidelity

Both fetches were of publication landing pages, through a summarizing reader, not the full
text. Quoted strings come from the abstract as reproduced on those pages. This file
supports design claims about what a retrospective summary should contain. It does not
support numeric claims.
