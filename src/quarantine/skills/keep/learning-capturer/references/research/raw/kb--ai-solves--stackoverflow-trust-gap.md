# Mind the gap: Closing the AI trust gap for developers

- **URL:** https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/02/18/closing-the-developer-ai-trust-gap/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Stack Overflow, reporting its own Developer Survey)
- **Why archived:** Second, independent data point on the AI-assisted solve. Establishes
  that adoption and trust are moving in opposite directions, which is the condition under
  which a user accepts a fix, ships it, and retains nothing.

## Numbers, from the Stack Overflow Developer Survey series

| Year | Metric | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | Developers who trust AI tools | 40% |
| 2025 | Developers who trust AI tools | 29% |
| 2025 | Developers who use or plan to use AI tools | 84% |

Trust fell 11 percentage points between 2023 and 2025 while usage stayed high.

## Quote on the cost of verification

"When every piece of AI-generated code requires verification, you can't just accept it and
move on. Instead, you have to read it carefully, understand what it's doing, test it
thoroughly, and check for edge cases."

## What this source does NOT supply

The article does not publish the sample size for the survey in the fetched content, does
not give a figure for the proportion shipping code they do not understand, and gives no
debugging-time attribution. For the "does not understand" figure use the Clutch survey
instead [kb--ai-solves--clutch-devs-dont-understand-ai-code.md].

## Conflict note

The trust numbers here (29% trust in 2025) and the Clutch figure (59% use code they do not
understand, June 2025) are compatible but measure different things: stated trust versus
reported behavior. The gap between them is itself the finding. Do not merge them into a
single claim.
