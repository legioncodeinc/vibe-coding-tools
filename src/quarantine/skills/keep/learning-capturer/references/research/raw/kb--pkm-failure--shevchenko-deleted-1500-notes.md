## The five named mistakes

1. **Neglecting theory.** Quoted: "I read a couple of articles on how to use Obsidian and
   maintained the knowledge base for a couple of years on instinct." After roughly 2,000
   notes the system became unmanageable.
2. **Over-collecting other people's ideas.** For the first 2,000 notes the author "greedily
   collected other people's ideas from books, movies, articles, courses" without adding
   personal insight, which made the base feel lifeless.
3. **Poor link strategy.** Grouping by topic produced messy connections. The repair was to
   ask instead: in what context might I refer to this note in the future?
4. **Tool obsession.** Dozens of plugins installed, time spent customizing rather than
   writing. The repair was to keep only the few that were load-bearing.
5. **Unreflective consumption.** Adding notes from everything consumed without asking why.
   The repair was to add a note only on an internal impulse.

## What transfers to a debugging knowledge base

Mistake 3 is the important one here. The author's repair, indexing by future retrieval
context rather than by topic, is the same rule the troubleshooting-article literature
states as "use the reader's language". Two independent sources, different domains, same
conclusion.

## Caveat

Single-person anecdote. The counts are real but they are one person's, and there is no
control. Cite as an existence proof of the failure shape, not as a rate.
