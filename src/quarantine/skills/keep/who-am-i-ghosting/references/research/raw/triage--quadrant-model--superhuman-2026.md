# The time management matrix applied to email triage

- **Title:** Prioritize email responses using the time management matrix
- **URL:** https://blog.superhuman.com/time-management-matrix/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (email client vendor). Practitioner guidance. Archived as
  the representative example of the dominant triage model, and as evidence about what that
  model does badly.

## The model as presented

An Eisenhower-style two by two over importance and urgency, applied to inbox messages.

| Quadrant | Rule as stated |
|---|---|
| Important and urgent | "Tasks in this quadrant should be your highest priority, and completing them will help boost your productivity." Approaching deadlines, important scheduled meetings. |
| Important, not urgent | Preparing for the future, "such as hiring for key roles or conducting strategy planning for upcoming quarters." Deserves "priority right below Quadrant 1" and is routinely deprioritized. |
| Urgent, not important | "These tasks can be quickly reviewed and then either delegated to a better person or delayed to a point when it makes more sense for you to handle them." |
| Neither | "Quadrant 4 tasks aren't urgent, nor are they important. All they are is a waste of time that should be avoided if your goal is productivity." |

## Handling low-value senders

The advice is to unsubscribe, with a product keystroke, and to "quickly archive or delete
past messages from the same sender."

## The one research claim on the page

The page states that a study found "only 40% of emails need to be seen by the end of the
day, and more than a third don't need to be seen at all." No citation, no author, no
venue. Treat as unsourced.

## Why this file is in the archive

Not as a model to copy. As the thing to argue against.

The importance-urgency quadrant is the default frame for inbox triage and it is a poor
fit for this skill for a specific reason: both of its axes are properties of the message
as seen by the reader. Neither axis encodes who is waiting, how directly they addressed
the reader, or what the relationship is worth. A message from an important long-term
client that is neither urgent nor tied to a deadline lands in quadrant 2 or 4 and gets
deprioritized indefinitely, which is exactly the failure this skill exists to catch.

The measured predictors of reply behavior found in the academic sources are different
variables entirely: explicit information request, recipient count, prior pairwise
interaction history, human versus system sender
[triage--reply-prediction--dabbish-kraut-chi-2005.md]
[triage--enterprise-reply-features--yang-avocado.md]
[triage--deferral-behavior--sarrafzadeh-wsdm-2019.md]. The ranking model in this skill
is built from those and not from the quadrant.
