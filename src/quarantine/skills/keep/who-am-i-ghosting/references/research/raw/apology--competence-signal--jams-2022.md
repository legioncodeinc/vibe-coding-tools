# Projecting lower competence to boost apology effectiveness

- **Title:** Projecting lower competence to boost apology effectiveness: Underlying
  mechanism and boundary conditions
- **Venue:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2022. DOI 10.1007/s11747-022-00903-5
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-022-00903-5
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer reviewed experiments)
- **Relevance rating:** WEAK. Archived deliberately as the closest thing found to research
  on whether apologizing helps, together with an explicit statement of why it does not
  settle the question.

## Findings as stated

- "projecting lower competence in a specific, non-core domain of activity provides a
  significant boost to apology effectiveness."
- Mechanism: "A projection of lower competence increases the perceived costliness of the
  apology because the organization accepts a symbolic cost," and that costliness
  "increases the perceived sincerity of the apology and leads to more favorable
  responses."
- Boundary conditions. The strategy works only when stakeholders "have no other reason to
  question their competence"; only "when the failure is not relevant to the core
  business"; and it "is not effective for consumers with low communal relationship
  orientation."
- The studies did not find changes in perceived warmth or in overall competence ratings
  across apology conditions. The effect runs through sincerity perceptions rather than
  broad trait judgments.

## Why this is weak evidence for this skill

The unit of analysis is an organization apologizing to consumers after a service failure.
It is not a person apologizing to a colleague for a slow reply. The transfer is
speculative in both directions: the costliness mechanism could argue that a delay apology
reads as sincere, and the boundary condition about failures "relevant to the core
business" could argue the opposite, because for a professional contact, replying is
arguably core.

## The honest conclusion this file supports

The direct question, does opening a re-engagement message with an apology for the delay
increase or decrease the chance of a reply, has no located empirical answer. This is
recorded as a named gap in the distillation. The skill's no-apology-opener rule therefore
rests on two other things, both of which are properly sourced: the practitioner guidance
against apologizing extensively
