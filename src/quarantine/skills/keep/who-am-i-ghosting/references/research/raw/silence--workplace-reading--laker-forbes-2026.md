## Claims on the page

- "Pretending not to see a message creates a temporary escape from obligation."
- Silence provides plausible deniability for delaying a decision or avoiding a difficult
  issue.
- "Silence preserves options. It lets the recipient delay without openly refusing."
- "Response time has become a subtle status marker."
- Delayed replies affect junior employees disproportionately more than senior staff.
- Unanswered messages come to be about standing rather than about information transfer.
- Both parties often know the message was seen and both maintain the pretense.

## Research cited

One mention of attribution theory, for the claim that "When people do not know why
something happened, they supply a reason." No author, no study, no venue is given for it.

## What this file is good for and what it is not

It is not evidence for any of the above. It is a recent-window record that the framing
exists in mainstream business press: that silence is read as a deliberate act with a
meaning, and that the meaning the waiting party supplies is often about status.

The one usable design idea, which the skill adopts and attributes as a framing rather than
a finding: the person waiting will invent a reason for the silence, and the reason they
invent is rarely "they were busy." A re-engagement message that supplies a plain,
non-defensive reason therefore does work that an apology does not.

The status point is directly contradicted in mechanism, though not in effect, by the
pre-registered finding that receivers overestimate senders' urgency expectations
[responsetime--urgency-bias--giurge-bohns-obhdp-2021.md]. Where they conflict, prefer
the pre-registered study.
