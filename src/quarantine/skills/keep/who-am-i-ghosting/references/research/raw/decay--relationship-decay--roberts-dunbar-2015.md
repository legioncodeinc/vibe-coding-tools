# Managing Relationship Decay: Network, Gender, and Contextual Effects

- **Title:** Managing Relationship Decay: Network, Gender, and Contextual Effects
- **Venue:** Human Nature (Springer), 2015. DOI 10.1007/s12110-015-9242-7. PubMed 26489745.
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-015-9242-7
  (the PMC mirror at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4626528/ was blocked by a

- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer reviewed, longitudinal panel)
- **Window note:** 2015, outside the default window. Retained as the only located
  longitudinal measurement of how a relationship decays when contact drops.

## Design and sample

Personal networks tracked across a major life transition (leaving school for university or
work) at three time points over 18 months. 25 participants completed the whole study, 12
of them female, covering 1,291 network members. Multilevel regression on emotional
closeness rated 1 to 10, contact frequency, and shared activity scores.

## Findings

**Direction of decay, kin versus friends.**

| Tie type | Change in emotional closeness over 18 months |
|---|---|
| Kin | significant increase, b = 0.27, p less than 0.001 |
| Friends | significant decrease, b = -0.62, p less than 0.001 |

**Movement out of the inner network layer at 18 months.**

| Tie type | Still in the inner layer |
|---|---|
| Close friendships | 48.6% |
| Kinship ties | 70.3% |

**What prevented the decline.** "The decline in friendship quality was mitigated by
increased effort invested in the relationship, but with a striking gender difference."
For females the protective factor was increased contact frequency. For males it was shared
activities rather than contact frequency.

**Competition for time.** Participants who added more new friends showed a greater decrease
in emotional closeness to old friends between months 9 and 18.

## Limits of this source for this skill

The sample is 25 young people going through a school-to-work transition. It is not a study
of professional contacts, and the effect sizes should not be transferred to a business
network as though they were measured there. What transfers is the qualitative shape:
non-kin ties decay measurably without deliberate effort, roughly half of formerly close
non-kin ties had left the inner layer inside 18 months, and effort is what arrests the
decay.

## Why this source matters here

It answers the assigned question about how long a professional relationship survives
without contact, and the honest answer is that no located study measures that directly.
The nearest evidence says non-kin closeness declines over an 18 month horizon absent
deliberate maintenance, and that half of close friendships fall out of the inner layer in
that period. Combined with the dormant ties result, the picture is that decay is real but
recoverable, which is the case for a write-off list built on relevance rather than on a
decay deadline.
