# Sales follow-up statistics, and why this skill does not quote any of them

- **Title:** Sales Follow-Up Statistics 2026: 17 Numbers That Explain Why 80% of Deals
  Require 5+ Touches
- **URL:** https://leadresponse.co/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community / vendor content marketing. LOW TRUST. Archived as a negative
  finding, not as evidence.

## What the sweep was looking for

An evidence-based answer to: after how many unanswered attempts should a person stop
following up, and does a written-off thread ever revive. This determines where the
write-off threshold sits.

## What the search returned

Every result on the first page of the query was a vendor blog or an outreach-tool
marketing page. This page is representative and was fetched in full.

Every named source is another sales-tool vendor's blog. Not one traces to a study, a
dataset, a methodology statement, or a sample size. The citation chain is blog to blog.

## Two further reasons not to use these numbers here

1. **Wrong population.** These are cold outreach to strangers. This skill deals with warm
   threads where a real prior exchange exists and the other party wrote first. The base
   rates are not transferable.
2. **Wrong direction.** They measure the persistence of a seller pursuing a buyer. This
   skill is mostly about a person who owes a reply, which is the opposite posture.

## Consequence for the skill

The write-off threshold is not a number this archive can supply. It has to be built from
judgment inputs the skill can actually observe: whether the ask is still live, whether the
person has moved on, whether the user has already sent unanswered follow-ups, and whether
the relationship has independent value. This is recorded as a named gap.
