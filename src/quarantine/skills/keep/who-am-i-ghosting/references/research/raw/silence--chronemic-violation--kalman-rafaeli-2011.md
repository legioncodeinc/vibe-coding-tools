# Online Pauses and Silence: chronemic expectancy violations

- **Title:** Online Pauses and Silence: Chronemic Expectancy Violations in Written
  Computer-Mediated Communication
- **Authors:** Yoram M. Kalman, Sheizaf Rafaeli
- **Venue:** Communication Research (SAGE), 2011
- **URL:** https://www.openu.ac.il/personal_sites/download/yoram-kalman/online-pauses-and-silence.pdf
  (author-hosted preprint). Publisher record:
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0093650210378229
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer reviewed experiment, small N)
- **Window note:** 2011, outside the default six month window. Retained because it is the
  only located experimental work on how a delayed reply is interpreted by the person
  waiting, which is the exact mechanism this skill is about.

## Design

Vignette experiment. Managers evaluated job candidates whose email response latency was
manipulated across three conditions: one day, two weeks, and silence over a month.
Crossed with candidate reward valence (high or low), giving six conditions.

N = 55 graduate MBA students. Average age 36. 31 percent female. All daily email users.

## Findings

- One day was the normative expectation in this population.
- A two week delay and outright silence both violated expectations, but only for high
  reward candidates.
- For high reward candidates, silence "significantly decreased the likelihood of
  recommending the applicant from 57% to 19%" and increased non-recommendation likelihood
  "from 43% to 81%" (chi square, p = .02).
- MANOVA: significant main effect of latency (Wilks lambda = .45, p = .003) and an
  interaction between latency and valence (Wilks lambda = .54, p = .03).
- For low reward candidates, longer pauses were "not perceived as expectancy violations."
  The interpretation offered is that participants had already lowered their expectations
  based on a prior negative impression.

## The silence threshold heuristic

The paper reports the working definition that "a pause longer than one order of magnitude
above the average response latency is online silence." Against the Kooti median of 47
minutes that would put the threshold at roughly eight hours, which is aggressive for
professional email. Treat the heuristic as directional, not as a setting.

## Why this source matters here

Two consequences. First, silence is read as a signal and it costs the silent party
concretely, so the skill is addressing a real harm rather than a manufactured anxiety.
Second, and more useful for ranking: the penalty landed only where the waiting party had
a live stake in the answer. Where the recipient had already written the sender off,
silence cost nothing. That is direct support for ranking on what the person was waiting
for and how much the relationship matters, rather than on elapsed days.
