# Dormant Ties: The Value of Reconnecting

- **Title:** Dormant Ties: The Value Of Reconnecting
- **Authors:** Daniel Z. Levin, Jorge Walter, J. Keith Murnighan
- **Venue:** Organization Science, 2011. JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/20868904.
  SSRN record: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1625543
- **URL:** https://business.gwu.edu/sites/g/files/zaxdzs5326/files/15_FP.SP_Walter.J_15levin_2011a.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer reviewed, two field studies with executives)
- **Window note:** 2011, outside the default window. Retained because it is the only
  located empirical work on what actually happens when a professional reaches back out
  after a long silence, which is the question the re-engagement drafting guide turns on.

## Design

Executive MBA students were instructed to reconnect with two dormant contacts, one from a
formerly strong relationship and one from a formerly weak one, about a real and important
work project. Surveys before reconnecting and again about a month after.

- Primary study: 129 executives completed both surveys, a 57.6 percent response rate, out
  of 224 initial participants across four Executive MBA classes.
- Second study, run as a check on the first: 95 executives, an 81.9 percent response rate, out of 116.

## Findings

**Efficiency.** Dormant strong ties took significantly less time to consult than current
strong ties (coefficient -0.022, p less than 0.001), and were "on par with current weak
ties" on time efficiency.

**Novelty.** Dormant strong ties scored 5.72 on novelty versus 5.07 for current strong
ties (p less than 0.001). Dormant weak ties scored 5.93 versus 4.94 for current weak ties.

**Trust.** Dormant strong ties scored 5.47 versus dormant weak ties at 4.50 (p less than
0.001). Trust in dormant strong ties "did decay somewhat" against current strong ties at
5.86, but remained far above weak ties at 4.17.

**Shared perspective.** Dormant strong 5.51 versus dormant weak 4.71 (p = 0.0014). "dormant
strong ties retained considerable shared perspective, more so than weak ties and just
about as much as current strong ties."

**Usefulness of knowledge received, raw means.** Dormant strong 5.70, dormant weak 5.59,
current strong 5.63, current weak 5.20.

## Qualitative findings

- Participants were "initially reluctant to reconnect" and then were "overwhelmingly
  positive about the effects of reconnecting."
- Executives reported the experience of reconnection as being "as if we had been talking
  regularly for the past seven years."
- The authors record a dark side case: one reconnection was emotionally positive but
  "ultimately unhelpful" because "the benefits were personal rather than relevant to his
  work project."
- The second study found "the pool of valuable dormant ties is deep," extending well
  past the two names chosen for the main study, and that a person's tenth choice of
  reconnection was "about as useful as his or her first choice."

## Why this source matters here

Three consequences for this skill.

1. The anticipated awkwardness of reaching back out is measurably worse than the actual
   experience. That is the empirical basis for telling the user that a cold thread is
   worth reopening and for not opening the draft with an apology that performs the
   awkwardness the other party is unlikely to feel.
2. Trust and shared perspective survive dormancy. A thread that went quiet three months
   ago is not a relationship that has to be rebuilt from zero, and the draft should not
   read as though it does.
3. The dark side case and the deep-pool finding together argue against ranking on days
   cold. Reconnection value is roughly flat across the candidate pool and depends on
   whether the contact is relevant to a live purpose. That is the case for a relevance
   weight and against a recency sort.
