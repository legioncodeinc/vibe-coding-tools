# You Don't Need to Answer Right Away: the email urgency bias

- **Title:** You don't need to answer right away! Receivers overestimate how quickly

- **Authors:** Laura M. Giurge, Vanessa K. Bohns
- **Venue:** Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, volume 167 (2021),

## Design and scale

- Eight pre-registered experimental studies.
- Total N = 4,004 participants.

## Findings as stated on the record

- "Receivers overestimate senders' response speed expectations to non-urgent emails sent
  outside normative work hours."
- The bias "creates stress disparities and reduces well-being through increased stress."
- "A simple sender-side intervention, adding a note clarifying response expectations,
  successfully mitigates the effect."

## Why this source matters here

It is the counterweight to the whole premise of a ghosting skill. The felt urgency of an
unanswered message is systematically higher in the receiver's head than in the sender's.
That is an argument for a short, high-precision list and against an anxiety-driven long
one, and it is an argument against opening a re-engagement message with an elaborate
apology for a delay the other person may not have experienced as a delay at all.
