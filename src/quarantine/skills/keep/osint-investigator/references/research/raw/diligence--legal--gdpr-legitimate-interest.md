# How to Use Legitimate Interest as a GDPR Legal Basis (and When Not To)

## The three-part test

**1. Purpose test.** The processing must serve a genuine, specific, lawful interest. "Vague justifications like 'improving our services' or 'business development' will not hold up." Commercial purposes qualify following the October 2024 CJEU ruling, provided the later steps are met.

**2. Necessity test.** Processing must be genuinely required for the stated purpose. "Necessary" does not mean "useful" or "more efficient". Less intrusive alternatives must be considered and rejected first.

**3. Balancing test.** Weigh the interest against the individual's rights, accounting for "the nature of the personal data" and "the potential consequences of the processing", plus reasonable expectations and applied safeguards.

## Where legitimate interest fits

Fraud prevention (Recital 47), IT and network security, direct marketing to existing customers with easy opt-out, B2B contact use where a prior relationship exists, aggregated non-identifiable analytics.

## Where it does not

Non-essential cookies and online tracking (ePrivacy requires consent regardless), **Article 9 special category data**, selling personal data to third parties, marketing to children, fully automated decisions with legal effects (Article 22 governs instead).

## The Legitimate Interest Assessment

A written record containing: a specific description of the processing, whose interest is involved, the necessity argument with alternatives considered, the balancing assessment using EDPB factors, the outcome, and a scheduled review date. It is "a living document".

## Data minimisation

The necessity step carries minimisation directly. The worked example: if two years of transaction data suffice for fraud detection, processing ten years "fails the necessity test".

## Special category data

Unambiguous: "Sensitive data categories are also outside the scope." Health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, and biometric data need explicit consent or a specific Article 9 exception. Legitimate interest cannot substitute.

## Relevance to a person dossier

The three-part test is a ready-made structure for the skill's purpose-binding step, and the necessity limb is the direct authority for refusing a "find everything" mode: breadth that is merely useful, rather than necessary to the stated purpose, fails the test. The Article 9 exclusion is the legal spine under the skill's sensitive-category exclusion list, and it applies by construction rather than by discretion.
