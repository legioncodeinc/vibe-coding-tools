# Startup founder fraud: What investors need to know

- **URL:** https://technical.ly/entrepreneurship/startup-founder-fraud-investor-diligence-ballard-spahr/
- **Publisher:** Technical.ly, interviewing Ballard Spahr attorneys Rushmi Bhaskaran and Kimberly Klayman

- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17

## The recurring fraud patterns

"inflated metrics or customer data", "misrepresentations to lenders or acquirers", and "hidden weaknesses in financial or operational systems", driven by a pervasive "culture of 'growth at all costs'". Cited case: a founder and chief growth officer fabricated millions of fake customer records in a $175 million fraud against a financial institution.

## The diligence failure

When fraud surfaces, the questions asked are "How did it get this far given the rounds of investment the company did?" and "Did investors ask the right questions?" The attorneys state that "investors are expected to have a baseline of skepticism, especially in transactions involving rapid growth, unconventional business models or unusually charismatic founders."

## Four red flags

1. **Suspicious metrics.** "Explosive customer growth, unheard-of conversion rates, or suspiciously high revenue per user".
2. **Resistance to transparency.** "Founders who resist sharing raw data - or who provide overly polished reports - may be managing perception".
3. **Weak controls.** Missing financial policies and informal processes signal broader cultural problems.
4. **Founder-dependent conviction.** When "investor conviction is based more on personality than on data, risk escalates quickly".

## Four verification steps

- Probe source data: request "logs, bank statements, and operational records, not just slide decks".
- Evaluate team incentives and whether processes are "respected or bypassed".
- Understand regulatory touchpoints and compliance gaps.
- **Document everything.** "Clear records of what was examined protect investors' reputations."

## Relevance to a person dossier

Two imports. First, the red flags are **claim shapes**, not person judgments, which is exactly the register a defensible dossier writes in: the skill records that a specific growth number was stated on a specific date and whether anything independent supports it, and does not characterize the person. Second, "document everything, clear records of what was examined" is the strongest available argument for the dossier's coverage disclosure: naming what was searched and found empty is itself part of the deliverable, because it is the record that shows what diligence was performed.
