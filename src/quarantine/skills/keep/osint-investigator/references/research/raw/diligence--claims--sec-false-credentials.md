# Investor Alert: Beware of False or Exaggerated Credentials

- **URL:** https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-55
- **Publisher:** US Securities and Exchange Commission, Office of Investor Education and Advocacy
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (US federal regulator)
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## Core warning

"Fraudsters may misrepresent their backgrounds and experience to lure investors into investment schemes." The alert's remedy is independent verification rather than reliance on the claim.

## Taxonomy of inflated credential claims

| Type | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Educational misrepresentation | Fabricated degrees or attendance. Cited example: claiming Harvard or Nyack College degrees while never enrolled. |
| Fake honors | Nonexistent awards, such as a fictitious "Top 25 Rising Business Star" designation attributed to a major publication. |
| False professional titles | Claiming certifications such as Certified Financial Planner without holding them. |
| Inflated experience | Overstating years in an industry, past investment success, or trading records. |
| Media manipulation | Using television appearances, social media, and press materials to manufacture legitimacy. |

## Verification resources named

1. IAPD (Investment Adviser Public Disclosure) for adviser background and disciplinary history.
2. FINRA BrokerCheck for broker licensing and conduct.
3. State securities regulators for licensing status.

## Standing guidance

"Independently verify claims with reliable sources". Be "particularly skeptical if you do not receive direct and specific answers."

## Relevance to a person dossier

Supplies the claim taxonomy for the "what they told you" section. The five categories are the specific claim types worth extracting from transcripts and threads, because they are the ones known to be inflated: education, honors, titles and certifications, years and track record, and press coverage. The media-manipulation category is the reason the dossier's external section reports press coverage as coverage and checks whether an outlet is independent or a placement, rather than treating any appearance as corroboration.
