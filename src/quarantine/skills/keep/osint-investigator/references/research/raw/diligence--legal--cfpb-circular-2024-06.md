## When a dossier becomes a consumer report

The circular restates the statutory definition: a consumer report is "any written, oral, or other communication of any information by a consumer reporting agency bearing on a consumer's credit worthiness, credit standing, credit capacity, character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living" used for employment evaluation.

The determinant is that the dossier comes from **a third party that "assembled" or "evaluated" consumer information specifically to furnish reports to employers**.

## Which uses trigger FCRA

"Employment purposes" means "a report used for the purpose of evaluating a consumer for employment, promotion, reassignment or retention as an employee". Obligations apply "both to information used for the purpose of evaluating a consumer for employment initially, and to information used for ongoing employment purposes".

## Algorithmic scores

A company that "collects consumer data in order to train an algorithm that produces scores or other assessments about workers for employers" can qualify as a consumer reporting agency. The transactions-or-experiences exception "by its own terms does not apply to a report containing information not about transactions or experiences between the report-maker and the consumer, such as when the report includes algorithmic scores."

## What counts as "assembling"

Examples the circular gives:

- Companies that "collect consumer information from third parties for dissemination to employers in background reports".
- Firms that "collect information from employers about workers' collective bargaining activity, or job performance, and then sell it to other employers".
- Developers that obtain "data from sources other than an employer receiving the report, including from other employer-customers or public data sources, to generate the scores".

Software providers performing ongoing maintenance, licensing, or active analysis of worker data can meet the assembling definition even when delivery happens through software.

## Relevance to a person dossier

This is the single strongest argument for a hard purpose gate. The regulator's own framing uses the word "dossier" and hangs coverage on **purpose plus third-party furnishing**. A business-relationship brief assembled by the user, about a counterparty, for the user's own reading, is outside the employment-purpose trigger. The same artifact assembled about a job candidate, or handed to an employer, is squarely inside it. The skill therefore has to know its purpose before it assembles anything, and has to refuse the employment-screening use rather than quietly produce it.
