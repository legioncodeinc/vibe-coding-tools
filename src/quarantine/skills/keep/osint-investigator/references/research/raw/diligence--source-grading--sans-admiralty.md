## Source reliability scale (A through F)

| Grade | Label | Definition as given |
|---|---|---|
| A | Completely reliable | "A source that consistently and over time has provided verifiable data, information, and intelligence." |
| B | Usually reliable | Reputable threat feeds or researchers whose assessments prove mostly accurate over time. |
| C | Fairly reliable | Open-source intelligence from generally credible researchers, though occasionally inaccurate. |
| D | Not usually reliable | Unverified sources with mixed track records of accuracy in past claims. |
| E | Unreliable | "Potentially compromised/deceptive sources with a history of making false claims." |
| F | Reliability cannot be judged | New or unverified sources lacking sufficient history for assessment. |

## Information credibility scale (1 through 6)

| Grade | Label | Definition as given |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Credible and verified | Multiple independent trusted sources confirm the intelligence. |
| 2 | Probably true | Consistent with known intelligence but lacks independent verification. |
| 3 | Possibly true | Somewhat consistent with existing intelligence; requires further investigation. |
| 4 | Doubtful | Not yet confirmed; plausible within the current landscape but needs verification. |
| 5 | Improbable | Claims contradict known actor tactics, techniques, and procedures. |
| 6 | Truth cannot be judged | Insufficient context or conflicting information prevents assessment. |

## Application guidance

- Independence requirement: "Source reliability and information credibility must be evaluated separately to avoid bias."
- Named common mistake: do not assume a reliable source automatically means credible information. Assess both axes independently.
- Priority threshold used in the article's CTI context: ratings of B2 or better warrant immediate action; lower ratings warrant monitoring rather than escalation.

## Relevance to a person dossier

The two-axis split maps cleanly onto a business dossier. The source axis grades WHERE a statement came from (a signed contract, a self-authored bio, an anonymous forum post, a Littlebird screen capture). The credibility axis grades WHETHER the statement holds up against everything else known. A person's own LinkedIn headline can be a grade-C source carrying a grade-2 claim, and that pairing is more informative than a single blended confidence number.
