# Critical review of the Admiralty Code

- **Publisher:** Blockint (intelligence analysis practitioner site, summarizing peer-reviewed critiques)
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (secondary summary of academic literature: Baker et al. 1968, Besombes and Revault d'Alonnes 2008, Irwin and Mandel 2019)

## The independence assumption does not survive contact with analysts

Baker et al. (1968) found that **87% of ratings fell along the diagonal (A1, B2, C3)**, which indicates the two scales are not being used independently in practice. Analysts allow their judgment of source reliability to contaminate their judgment of information credibility, which defeats the design intent of the two-axis system.

## The credibility axis is multidimensional and the reliability axis is not

Baker et al. noted that "the source reliability scale is unidimensional while the information credibility scale is multidimensional" (it combines consistency with prior knowledge AND intrinsic plausibility). Folding two dimensions into one letter grade complicates evaluation and invites misuse.

## Ordering across the two axes is undefined

Besombes and Revault d'Alonnes (2008) argued that two axes reduce clarity: it is not defined whether B3 information is more or less probable than C2 information. They also argued the plausibility criteria are too thin, and proposed adding source proficiency and event likelihood.

## Grade boundaries are interpreted inconsistently

Irwin and Mandel (2019) found that subjective interpretation of the boundaries varies substantially among users. The distinction between "completely reliable" (a complete history of reliability) and "usually reliable" carries no numeric anchor, which produces miscommunication even across NATO member states using the same published scale.

## Key terms are never operationalized

Irwin and Mandel identified that "authenticity," "competency," and "trustworthiness" appear in the scale definitions without formal definition, which increases subjectivity.

## Context insensitivity

The system treats source reliability as a constant property of the source rather than as varying by topic and context, and it does not distinguish subjective human sources from objective sensor sources.

## Relevance to a person dossier

This is the reason a dossier skill must not ship a raw Admiralty grade as if it were an objective measurement. The usable residue of the critique: keep the two axes, but (a) define the grade boundaries locally and concretely in terms of the actual evidence types the skill handles, (b) require the two grades to be justified with separate sentences so diagonal collapse is visible, and (c) treat "grade the source" as a per-claim judgment, not a per-person one.
