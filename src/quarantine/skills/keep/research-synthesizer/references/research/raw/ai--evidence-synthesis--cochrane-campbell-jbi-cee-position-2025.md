# Joint position statement on AI use in evidence synthesis

- **URL:** https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.ED000178/full
- **Publisher:** Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI, and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence

- **Published:** 11 November 2025
- **Authors:** Ella Flemyng, Anna Noel-Storr, Biljana Macura, Gerald Gartlehner, James Thomas, Joerg J. Meerpohl, Zoe Jordan, Jan Minx, Angelika Eisele-Metzger, Candyce Hamel, Pawel Jemiolo, Kylie Porritt, Matthew Grainger

## The six principles

1. "Evidence synthesists are ultimately responsible for their evidence synthesis, including
   the decision to use artificial intelligence (AI) and automation"
2. The organizations "support the aims of the Responsible use of AI in evidence SynthEsis
   (RAISE) recommendations"
3. AI use is acceptable "as long as they can demonstrate that it will not compromise the
   methodological rigour or integrity of their synthesis"
4. "AI and automation in evidence synthesis should be used with human oversight"
5. "Any use of AI or automation that makes or suggests judgements should be fully and
   transparently reported"
6. AI developers must "ensure their AI systems or tools adhere to the RAISE recommendations"

## Accountability

"An author is accountable for the content, methods and findings of their evidence
synthesis, including the decision to use AI, how it is used, and its impact."

Authors must remain "critical of [AI tool] evaluations" and confirm tools are
"methodologically sound" before use.

## Where disclosure is required

Disclosure is required wherever AI "makes or suggests judgements", specifically:

- Study eligibility decisions
- Risk of bias assessments
- Data extraction
- Evidence synthesis
- Certainty of evidence assessments
- Plain language summaries

Required disclosure contents: AI system names, versions and dates; purposes; justification
with performance evidence; limitations; potential biases; and financial interests.

"Generally, AI used to improve spelling, grammar or manuscript structure does not need to be
listed, but we recommend authors check the journal's specific policy."

**The dividing line is judgement, not text generation.** Language cleanup is exempt.
Anything that decides or suggests what counts as evidence is not.

## Verification and reproducibility

Authors should "describe any steps taken to verify AI-generated outputs" and "make the
inputs (e.g. prompt development), outputs, datasets and code publicly and freely available".

"depending on how certain an evidence synthesist is about an AI system or tool in their
synthesis, they may need to pilot (or calibrate) the AI system."

## Named risks

| Risk | Statement |
|---|---|
| Hallucination | AI is "at risk of fabricated outputs and hallucinations" |
| Algorithmic bias | "potentially embedded with algorithmic biases" and "susceptible to overfitting" |
| Opacity | "opaque decision making and black-box predictions" that "undermines the reliability and replicability of AI-driven outputs" |
| Amplification | "misuse could erode methodological standards by exacerbating existing biases" |

## The one quantified example given

Using AI for abstract screening in rapid reviews, where "single review author" screening
risks falsely excluding **13 percent** of relevant studies. AI acting as a second reviewer
is offered as a way to mitigate that risk.

This figure is the archive's only measured number on what single-pass human screening
misses, and it is the number that justifies treating any one-pass sweep as incomplete.

## Where AI use is acceptable

When methodological soundness is demonstrated, trustworthiness and reliability are
maintained, human oversight exists, use is fully transparent and reported, and validation
is context-specific.

## Where it is not

Where methodological rigour would be compromised, where integrity would be undermined,
where validation for that specific context is absent, and where risks cannot be adequately
reported and mitigated. Human judgement remains essential for high-stakes determinations
about evidence quality and synthesis conclusions.
