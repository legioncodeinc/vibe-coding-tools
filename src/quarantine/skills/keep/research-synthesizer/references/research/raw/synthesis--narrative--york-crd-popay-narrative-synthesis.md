## The four elements of the narrative synthesis process

1. "Developing a theory of how the intervention works, why and for whom"
2. "Developing a preliminary synthesis"
3. "Exploring relationships within and between studies"
4. "Assessing the robustness of the synthesis product"

## Tools and techniques named under each element

| Element | Techniques |
|---|---|
| Developing a theory | Idea webbing, conceptual mapping, textual descriptions |
| Preliminary synthesis | Tabulation, groupings and clusters, transforming data, vote-counting, validity assessment |
| Exploring relationships | Conceptual triangulation, reciprocal and refutational translation, investigator and methodological triangulation, moderator variables, qualitative case descriptions, visual representation |
| Assessing robustness | Validity assessment, best evidence synthesis, checking with primary study authors |

## Transparency as the stated goal

The guidance aims at "increasing the transparency and reproducibility of the process". The
authors state the framework "provides a useful framework for the conduct of narrative
synthesis, particularly for increasing transparency."

The implication carried by that emphasis: narrative synthesis without a stated method is
opaque, and opacity is the failure mode the framework exists to counter. The excerpt
retrieved does not contain an explicit statement that narrative synthesis is at risk of
reviewer bias, so that reading is an inference from the transparency framing rather than a
sourced claim.

## The two ideas this archive draws on

1. **Preliminary synthesis comes before exploring relationships.** Describe what each
   source says before you start reasoning across sources. The order is part of the method.
2. **Robustness is assessed as a separate step, after the synthesis exists.** The synthesis
   product is a thing you then evaluate, not a thing that arrives pre-validated.

## What this source does NOT say

It gives no rule for synthesizing across sources of different types, for example a peer
reviewed study against a vendor blog post against something the reader saw on screen. The
framework assumes a corpus of research studies.
