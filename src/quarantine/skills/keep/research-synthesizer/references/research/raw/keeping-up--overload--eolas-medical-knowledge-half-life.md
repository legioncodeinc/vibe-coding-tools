## Commercial interest, stated first

Eolas Medical sells clinical knowledge management software. The company has a direct
commercial interest in the conclusion that clinical knowledge goes stale quickly and needs a
managed system. This source is the weakest in the archive and is retained for one specific
claim, the half-life framing, which is not available elsewhere in the archive.

## Doubling time progression reported

| Year | Doubling interval |
|---|---|
| 1950 | roughly every 50 years |
| 1980 | every 7 years |
| 2010 | every 3.5 years |
| 2020 | projected at 0.2 years, that is 73 days |

**Attribution problem.** The article presents this progression **without naming a study or
author.** The same progression appears in the Fierce Healthcare source
(`keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md`) attributed to a 2011
study in Transactions of the American Clinical and Climatological Association. This archive
treats the Fierce Healthcare attribution as the better-sourced one and treats the numbers
themselves as a widely repeated estimate rather than a measured constant.

## Half-life framing

"the 'half-life' or time for half of medical understanding to evolve was around 18-24 months
as of 2017, and on track to shrink to weeks."

Attributed only to "Harvard Medical School commentators noted in 2017", with **no named
citation.** Recorded as an unattributed claim.

## The other figure carried

"82% said fewer than half of the research papers they read ended up influencing how they
treat patients", from a survey of internists. The survey is not named.

That figure, if directionally right, describes a specific and useful phenomenon: **most of
what a professional reads does not change what they do.** Exposure and behaviour change are
different things, and the gap between them is large.
