# Physicians overwhelmed by the amount of reading needed to stay current

- **URL:** https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/study-doximity-survey-found-physicians-overwhelmed-amount-reading-needed-stay-date
- **Publisher:** Fierce Healthcare, reporting a Doximity survey
- **Published:** 25 October 2022
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** journalism, reporting vendor-run survey research
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## Window and interest caveats, stated first

This source is outside the six-month window and the survey was run by Doximity, a company
whose product is a professional network and clinical news feed for physicians. Doximity has
a commercial interest in the finding that physicians are overwhelmed by information. The
figures below are used directionally, never as measured constants.

## Survey

600 physicians across oncology, neurology, cardiology, endocrinology and gastroenterology.

| Finding | Percent |
|---|---|
| Interested in learning about new trials, treatments or procedures | 95 |
| Felt overwhelmed by the amount of information | 70 |
| Wanted clinical trial information reflecting patient diversity | 70 |
| Wanted geographic-specific information | 60 |
| Interested in insurance coverage information | 66 |
| Wanted to review clinical guidelines | 95 |
| Interested in patient resources | 75 |

The shape of the result: near-universal appetite for the material, and a large majority
unable to consume it. Demand is not the constraint. Throughput is.

## The doubling time figures the article reports

Attributed to a 2011 study in Transactions of the American Clinical and Climatological
Association:

| Year | Doubling time of medical knowledge |
|---|---|
| 1950 | 50 years |
| 1980 | 7 years |
| 2010 | 3 years |
| 2020 (predicted) | 73 days |

## The time cost figure

A 2022 Journal of Internal Medicine study concluded that a primary care provider staying
current with the latest guidelines would need to practice "nearly 27 hours a day."

## Quote

Dr Amit Phull, Doximity: "When you are that much behind in terms of just feeling like you're
practicing up to date with the clinical science ... that creates a not-so-virtuous cycle."

## What this supports

The structural claim that keeping up in a fast-moving field is not a discipline problem. At
the reported volumes it is arithmetically impossible, and the professional's actual state is
partial exposure of unknown coverage. That is the correct default assumption about anyone's
knowledge of a moving topic, including their own.
