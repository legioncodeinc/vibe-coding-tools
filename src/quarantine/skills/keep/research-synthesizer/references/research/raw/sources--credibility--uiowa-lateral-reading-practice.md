## Definition

Lateral reading means doing "some research about the source BEFORE deciding whether you
should listen to anything the source has to say." The reader leaves the page and opens new
tabs rather than evaluating the source from its own presentation.

## The operational procedure

1. Research an unfamiliar source before accepting its claims.
2. "Try to determine a consensus about the source by researching it using Google and
   Wikipedia."
3. Search for the key components: "Publication (Usually best)," "Funding organization,"
   "Author," or "Content".
4. "Read a minimum of 3 to 5 new sources to see what they have to say about your original
   source."
5. "If you can't find 3 to 5 sources, that is information in itself. It means your original
   source doesn't have an established reputation."

## The rule worth extracting

Step 5 is the useful one and is rarely stated this plainly elsewhere: **failure to find
corroborating coverage of a source is itself a finding about that source**, not an
inconclusive result. An unlocatable publisher is a graded outcome.

Step 3 names funding organization as a first-class thing to look up, alongside publication
and author. That is the practical hook for detecting a commercial interest behind a source.
