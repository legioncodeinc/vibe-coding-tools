# Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group, interim guidance

- **URL:** https://methods.cochrane.org/rapidreviews/sites/methods.cochrane.org.rapidreviews/files/public/uploads/cochrane_rr_-_guidance-23mar2020-v1.pdf
- **Publisher:** Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group (RRMG)
- **Published:** 23 March 2020, standing interim guidance document
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## What this document is

Consensus interim guidance from the Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group on how to
abbreviate a systematic review when a decision maker needs an answer faster than a full
systematic review can deliver. Recommendations were set by consensus thresholds: 70
percent or above agreement counted as high agreement, 50 to 69 percent as moderate.

Stated timeline for a rapid review: "1 week up to 6 months, starting once protocol details
approved."

## Recommendations by stage

### Question development and topic refinement

- "Involve key stakeholders ... to set and refine the review question, eligibility criteria"
  and outcomes.
- Consult stakeholders on research fit and on protocol changes as the review progresses.

### Protocol

- Must state review questions, PICOS, and inclusion and exclusion criteria.
- Post-hoc changes are allowed with a documented rationale.

### Literature search

- "Involve an Information Specialist."
- "Always search Cochrane CENTRAL, MEDLINE ... and Embase (if available access)."
- Peer review at least one search strategy.
- "Limit literature searches to English language; add other languages only if justified."
- Restrict specialized databases to "1-2 additional sources, or omitted if time and
  resources limited."
- "Limit grey literature and supplemental searching."

### Study selection and screening

Title and abstract stage:

- Pilot test with 30 to 50 abstracts across the screening team.
- "Use two reviewers for dual screen of at least 20% (ideally more) of abstracts."
- A single reviewer screens the remainder; a second reviewer screens all excluded records.

Full text stage:

- Pilot test with 5 to 10 articles.
- "Use one reviewer to screen all included full text articles."
- A second reviewer screens all excluded full texts.

### Data extraction

- "Use a single reviewer to extract data using a piloted form."
- A second reviewer verifies correctness and completeness.
- "Limit data extraction to a minimal set of required data items."

### Risk of bias

- Use a valid tool for the included study designs.
- "Use a single reviewer to rate risk of bias, with full verification" by a second reviewer.
- Limit ratings to the most important outcomes.

### Synthesis

- "Synthesize evidence narratively."
- Meta-analysis only "if appropriate (i.e., studies are similar enough to pool)."
- A single reviewer grades certainty of evidence; a second reviewer verifies, with
  documented rationales.

## The shape of the whole method

Every abbreviation in this guidance follows one pattern: **one person does the work, a
second person verifies it, and the abbreviation is documented rather than hidden.** The
guidance never abbreviates by dropping the verification step. It abbreviates by dropping
duplication of the primary effort while keeping a check on the output.

## What this source does NOT say

It gives no explicit list of contraindications, that is, no named topics or question types
where rapid review methods should not be used at all. It is guidance on how to go faster,
not on when speed is inadmissible.
