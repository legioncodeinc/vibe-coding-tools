# Rapid reviews, University of Southern Denmark library guide

- **Publisher:** University of Southern Denmark library
- **Published:** standing library guide, undated on the page
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic
- **Note on quotes:** dashes inside quoted passages normalized to spaced hyphens.

## Definition

A rapid review is "a streamlined variation of the systematic review where individual steps
are omitted or completed in a shorter time to accommodate decision makers."

## Timeframe

Rapid review: 1 to 6 months. Systematic review: 6 months to 2 years.

## The streamlining strategies listed

| Step | Rapid review approach |
|---|---|
| Literature searching | Limited to 3 topic-specific databases (Medline, Embase, Cochrane) rather than comprehensive searching |
| Screening | "Two reviewers screen the first 20% together ... One reviewer screens the remaining, the second reviewer verifies" |
| Data extraction | One reviewer extracts, a second verifies |
| Quality assessment | Single reviewer with verification rather than dual independent review |
| Synthesis | Narrative synthesis preferred; meta-analysis only where appropriate |

## Stated limitation

The guide notes that Tricco and colleagues "recommend that two individual reviewers screen
and select studies to minimize selection bias." The single-reviewer approach therefore
carries a known selection-bias cost, and the guide presents it as a tradeoff accepted for
speed rather than as a method of equal quality.

## Sources the page cites

Garritty et al. (2020) and WHO / Tricco et al. (2017).

## Why this source is in the archive

It is the clearest short statement that the rapid review is a **named, legitimate, and
documented compromise** rather than a sloppy systematic review. That framing is what a
practitioner-facing synthesis needs: the shortcuts are declared, not concealed.
