# Thirty Years of Meeting Science: Lessons Learned and the Road Ahead

- **URL:** https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031925-091223
- **Authors:** Steven Rogelberg, Liana M. Kreamer, Jon Gray
- **Published:** 2026
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior)

## What it is

A three decade review of the meeting science literature, organizing it into five
thematic areas: meetings as stressors, meetings as communication technology, meetings as
platforms for voice and inclusion, meetings as stages for leadership, and meetings as
expressions of culture and identity.

## Extracted findings

Abstract, quoted:

> "This review examines the development and evolution of meeting science over the past
> three decades. Drawing on diverse disciplinary foundations, including the
> organizational sciences, communication studies, and computer science, we provide a
> thematic discussion of meetings research."

On pre-meeting activity, quoted:

> "linking pre-meeting communication to meeting effectiveness"

matters significantly for outcomes, attributed in the review to Allen et al., 2014b.

On meeting design, quoted:

> "Meeting design characteristics and attendee perceptions of staff/team meeting quality"

are directly connected, attributed to Cohen et al., 2011. Stand-up versus sit-down
formats produce different outcomes in meeting effectiveness, attributed to Bluedorn et
al., 1999.

On recurring meetings, the review reports that

> "effects of team meetings on team and organizational success"

vary based on meeting frequency and structure, attributed to Kauffeld and
Lehmann-Willenbrock, 2012.

On meeting load, the review reports relationships between

> "meeting time demands"

and

> "employee well-being",

attributed to Rogelberg et al., 2006.

## Why it matters for this skill

This is the strongest available academic anchor for the claim that what happens BEFORE a
meeting is causally linked to how the meeting goes. It also establishes that recurring
meetings behave differently from one off meetings, which supports differentiating brief
shapes by meeting type.

## Limits of this source

The review is a synthesis, not a primary study. It reports the existence of the
pre-meeting communication link without restating effect sizes. Treat it as evidence that
the link is established in the literature, not as a quantified effect.
