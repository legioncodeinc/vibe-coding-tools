# Effective decision making and the rule of 7

- **URL:** https://www.bain.com/insights/effective-decision-making-and-the-rule-of-7/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** consulting-research (Bain and Company, drawn from Decide and Deliver
  by Blenko, Mankins and Rogers)

## Core finding, quoted

> "Once you've got 7 people in a decision-making group, each additional member reduces
> decision effectiveness by 10%."

The article further states that a group of 17 or more members "rarely makes any
decisions."

## What the page does not contain

No guidance on RAPID role assignment, no operational guidance for running large
meetings, no methodology for the 10% figure. The page references the authors' book
Decide and Deliver for detail that is not reproduced on the page.

## Why it matters for this skill

This is the evidence base for treating a large multi attendee call as a structurally
different brief. At 26 attendees the Bain arithmetic says the call is not a decision
forum at all, so a brief that lists all 26 people and their history is both unreadable
and pointed at the wrong job. The correct large meeting brief names the few people who
matter to the user's own slice, states what the user personally owns, and drops the rest
to an appendix roster.

## Limits of this source

Short marketing summary of a book finding. The 10% per person figure is presented without
sample, method, or confidence interval. Treat it as a widely cited heuristic with a
consulting research provenance rather than a peer reviewed effect size.
