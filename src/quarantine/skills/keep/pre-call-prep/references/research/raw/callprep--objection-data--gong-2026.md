# Handling Sales Objections with AI

- **URL:** https://www.gong.io/blog/handling-sales-objections-with-ai
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Gong)

## What the page actually contains

This page was fetched specifically to find conversation intelligence data on objection
recurrence across calls. It does not contain that data. Recording the negative result
here because it defines a gap the distillation carries forward.

Qualitative claims made:

- "Top performers view objections as valuable insights" and "see objections as buying
  signals, not rejection"
- "CRM systems capture only one percent of customer interactions"
- The vendor claims its platform "captures 99 percent of customer interactions that CRM
  systems miss"

Case study figure: "Frontify achieved a 30 percent increase in lead conversion by using
AI to provide contextual signals that helped refine their objection handling and
messaging approach."

## What the page does NOT provide

- Frequency data on how often specific objections recur across calls with the same account
- Research on the timing impact of addressing objections early
- Comparative statistics on top performer objection technique
- Any data on how prior call objection information influences preparation for the next call

## Why it matters for this skill

Two uses. First, the "CRM captures only one percent of customer interactions" claim, even
as vendor marketing, is the argument for building a brief from a capture and transcript
record instead of from CRM fields. Second, the absence of published data on objection
recurrence is a real gap: the design decision to surface prior objections in the brief
rests on practitioner guidance (see the HubSpot pre-call planning file) and not on
measured evidence. Say so rather than implying otherwise.

## Limits of this source

Vendor marketing page. The one percent and 99 percent figures are self serving product
claims with no methodology. The Frontify figure is a single customer testimonial. Nothing
on this page is usable as an independent fact.
