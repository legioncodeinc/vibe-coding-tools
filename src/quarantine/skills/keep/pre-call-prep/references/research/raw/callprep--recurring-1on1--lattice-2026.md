# The Complete Guide to One-on-One Meetings: Benefits, Agendas, and Tips

- **URL:** https://lattice.com/articles/the-ultimate-managers-guide-to-leading-effective-one-on-ones
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Lattice)

## Cadence and length

> "78% of managers have daily or weekly check-ins with their direct reports. Of those,
> 97% said they feel somewhat or very connected to their reports."

Attributed to Lattice's 2025 State of People Strategy Report. The guide notes a
"biweekly or even monthly cadence" works for some employees.

Duration: "between 30 and 60 minutes", varying by employee, topics, and frequency.

## Agenda topics as published

- Productivity and projects: priorities, progress, blockers
- Feedback and coaching
- Career development
- Engagement and morale

## Continuity across instances

> "Taking notes helps both parties stay aligned and accountable."

The guide recommends tooling to "record action items, commitments, and key insights to
maintain continuity across meetings" and to "review what was discussed, assign
responsibilities, and set deadlines."

## The failure mode it names

Managers should not let one on ones become purely transactional. The guide warns against
"only covering tasks and to-dos", noting that "status updates shouldn't be the only thing
you discuss during your time together."

## Other statistics

Internal Lattice data: employees with regular one on ones are "three times more likely to
be engaged and 38% more likely to stay with their company."

## Why it matters for this skill

The named failure mode, a recurring 1:1 collapsing into a repeated status readout, is the
same failure mode a recurring brief has. It is the reason the recurring-instance brief
must lead with the delta since the last instance and the still-open commitments, not with
a fresh restatement of the relationship.

## Limits of this source

Vendor blog for a performance management product. The engagement and retention numbers
are the vendor's own internal data with no published methodology. The 78% and 97% figures
come from the vendor's own survey report. Use the qualitative guidance, not the numbers.
