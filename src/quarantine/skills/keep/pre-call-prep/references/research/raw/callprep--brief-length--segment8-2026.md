# Writing Executive Briefs That Actually Get Read

- **URL:** https://blog.segment8.com/posts/executive-brief-one-page/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (practitioner blog)

## Extracted guidance

**Length.** One page, roughly 250 words. The author describes rewriting an eight page
document into that format.

**Reading conditions and time budget.** Quoted:

> "Executives receive 50-100 emails per day. They're in back-to-back meetings."

An eight page brief requires

> "20-30 minutes of focused reading time"

but executives typically have only

> "3-minute gaps between meetings where they're scanning emails on their phone"

The stated target reading time for a one page brief is

> "90 seconds"

**Structure.** Four questions, with the author's own length budget per section:

1. "What's the situation? (2-3 sentences)"
2. "What's the problem? (1-2 sentences)"
3. "What are the options? (3-4 bullet points)"
4. "What do you recommend and why? (2-3 sentences)"

**What gets read versus skipped.** Read: content that

> "makes the decision obvious in 30 seconds"

Skipped: comprehensive background, lengthy contextual setup, hedged recommendations.

Core claim, quoted:

> "Executives don't read briefs, they scan them for decisions they need to make."

**Appendix strategy.** Supporting analysis moves to page 2 and beyond, so the reader can
"read page 1 in 90 seconds and make a decision."

## Why it matters for this skill

This is the primary source behind the one screen per meeting target and the appendix
split. The 90 second read budget and the phone-in-a-3-minute-gap reading context are the
design constraints for the brief format.

## Limits of this source

Practitioner blog, not research. The word counts and time budgets are the author's
working rules of thumb, not measured. Treat them as a design target that is directionally
supported rather than an empirical finding.
