# MEDDIC vs. SPIN: Which Sales Framework Wins in 2026?

- **Source type:** vendor-blog (Claap)

## MEDDIC, element definitions quoted

| Letter | Element | Definition as published |
|---|---|---|
| M | Metrics | "What are the measurable success indicators for the prospect?" |
| E | Economic Buyer | "Who holds the purse strings?" |
| D | Decision Criteria | "What specific requirements must be met to close the deal?" |
| D | Decision Process | "How does the decision get made, and who is involved?" |
| I | Identify Pain | "What is the problem the prospect is trying to solve?" |
| C | Champion | "Who within the organization will advocate for your solution?" |

Stated fit: complex, high value B2B sales with long cycles and multiple decision makers.

## SPIN, element definitions quoted

| Letter | Element | Definition as published |
|---|---|---|
| S | Situation | "What's the current situation of the prospect?" |
| P | Problem | "What specific problems are they facing?" |
| I | Implication | "What are the consequences if the problem isn't solved?" |
| N | Need-Payoff | "How would solving this problem help the prospect?" |

Stated fit: shorter sales cycles focused on relationships and lower value products.

## Statistics cited

- Companies following defined sales processes are "33% more likely to be high performers"
- Formal sales processes correlate with "18% more revenue growth"
- Companies providing ongoing training see "50% higher net sales per employee"

No win rate statistics were provided.

## Why it matters for this skill

MEDDIC gives a ready made checklist of the six facts a sales brief should try to fill
from the record, and which of the six are still blank is itself the most useful thing a
sales brief can say. SPIN gives the shape of a discovery-stage first meeting where the
record is empty and the goal is questions rather than answers.

## Limits of this source

Vendor blog. The article explicitly does not state what either framework requires a rep
to prepare BEFORE a call, so the mapping from framework element to brief section in this
skill is authored inference over the definitions, not a claim from the source. The three
statistics carry no attribution on the page and were not verified.
