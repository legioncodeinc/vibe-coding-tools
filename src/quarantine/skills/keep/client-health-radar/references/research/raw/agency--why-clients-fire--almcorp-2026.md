## Why this source matters for client-health-radar

The warning-sign list is the single most directly implementable thing found in the entire
agency-retention sweep, because almost every item on it is a behavior visible in meeting
transcripts, message threads, or a calendar. It is the backbone of the skill's behavioral

## Extracted claims

**Twelve reasons clients fire agencies, quoted verbatim:**

1. "The agency sold one thing and delivered another"
2. "Communication became a source of anxiety"
3. "The goals were never truly aligned"
4. "The agency became an order-taker"
5. "Reporting proved activity, not value"
6. "The strategy went stale"
7. "The client lost trust in the team, not just the performance"
8. "Scope, pricing, and delivery drifted out of balance"
9. "The agency failed to manage the client's internal politics"
10. "The agency did not adapt when performance changed"
11. "The client's business changed and the agency did not re-sell its value"
12. "Small issues accumulated into a trust deficit"

**The warning signs named, verbatim from the page:**

- Client stops asking strategic questions and focuses only on deliverables
- Fewer or more stakeholders suddenly attend calls
- Email tone becomes shorter, more formal, approval-heavy
- Reporting questions repeat monthly
- Client delays feedback, approvals, or payment
- Client requests documentation not previously needed
- Client seeks account access, exports, or asset inventories
- Renewal discussions get postponed
- Client compares work to internal alternatives or competitors
- A new executive joins and schedules a partner review
- Client says performance is "fine" but engagement drops sharply

**On cadence, no fixed frequency is prescribed:** "The cadence should match the speed of the
work and the importance of decisions. Clients should know when they will get updates, what
those updates will cover, and how urgent issues will be escalated."

## Direct implication for the skill

Read that warning list against what a Littlebird capture actually contains and most of it is
retrievable. Attendee lists per meeting come from the linked calendar event. Access and export
requests, documentation requests, and postponed renewals appear in transcripts and threads as
explicit asks. Competitor comparisons appear as named entities. Approval and payment delays
appear as unmet promises in the client's direction.

Two items on that list are the reason this skill exists in this shape. "Client says
performance is 'fine' but engagement drops sharply" is precisely the case where a text
sentiment score reads neutral-to-positive and the behavior reads alarming. And "Email tone
becomes shorter, more formal, approval-heavy" is a change in register over time, not a
polarity, which is why the skill tracks trajectory and quotes rather than scoring.
