# Agency Client Retention: Stop Month 6 Churn

- **Title:** Agency Client Retention: Why You're Losing Clients Before Month 6 (And How to Stop It), AgencyDashboard.io
- **URL:** https://agencydashboard.io/blog/agency-client-retention-month-six
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog. Statistics are second-hand and attributed to named studies
  without links. Treat every number as unverified at one remove.

## Why this source matters for client-health-radar

It supplies the timing argument: the decision to leave is made long before the cancellation,
which is the whole premise of a leading-indicator radar. It also supplies a ranked list of
stated departure reasons in which price is not the top item.

## Extracted claims

- **The lag between decision and cancellation.** "Clients who cancel at month six or later
  usually made their real decision back in month two or three", attributed on the page to
  OnboardMap research. No link.
- **Why month six specifically:** it is described as "the collision point between 'the
  client's patience is running out' and 'the first contract term is ending.'"
- **Concentration of churn early:** "roughly 43% of all client churn happens within the first
  90 days", attributed to GigRadar's 2026 retention study. No link.

- **Ranked stated reasons for leaving, quoted:**

| Reason | Percent citing |
|---|---|
| "a lack of proactive strategic guidance" | 68% |
| "poor communication" | 57% |
| "the agency could not clearly demonstrate its own value" | 53% |
| price (ranked sixth) | 37% |

  The page does not name the study behind this ranking or give a sample size.

- **Vertical numbers borrowed from elsewhere:** "SEO services carry a 38% annual churn rate,
  with typical contracts running just six to twelve months" and "28% of client departures
  happen within the first six months" for project-based agencies, both attributed to Focus
  Digital's 2026 report, which is archived separately here and discloses no methodology.

- **The cadence the article recommends:**
  - Week one: onboarding summary covering scope, goals, timeline
  - Every two weeks: proactive progress updates
  - Monthly: full reporting session with business context
  - Quarterly: milestone review connecting work to the original goals

- Onboarding recommendations: written scope documents, realistic timeline expectations
  (three to six months for SEO), a kickoff call within days, and defined thirty-day milestones.

## Direct implication for the skill

Three things transfer. The decision-precedes-cancellation lag is the argument for watching
leading behavior rather than waiting for a renewal conversation. The reason ranking, weak as
its sourcing is, points every top item at communication and proactivity rather than at output
quality, which is what the skill can actually observe from meetings and threads. And the
four-tier cadence is a usable default shape to compare an observed cadence against, on the
explicit understanding that the skill prefers the client's own observed baseline and only
falls back to a published shape when there is not enough history to derive one.
