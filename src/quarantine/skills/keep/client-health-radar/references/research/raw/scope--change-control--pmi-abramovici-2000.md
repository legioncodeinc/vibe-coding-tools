## Why this source matters for client-health-radar

Scope creep is the margin half of the skill. This is the canonical practitioner statement of
where it comes from and what stops it, from the body that owns the term.

## Extracted claims

- Framing: scope creep is described as "one of the most insidious problems commonly faced by
  project managers", involving uncontrolled changes to project requirements.

- **Causes, external to the delivery team:**
  - Customer requirement changes
  - Environment and platform changes
  - Poor initial understanding of the customer requirement
  - Vague specifications and statements of work

- **Causes, internal to the delivery team:**
  - Engineers' natural inclination to improve the product
  - Team desire to exceed the minimum requirement
  - Absence of a change control procedure
  - Undocumented modifications made without impact assessment

- **Controls recommended before the engagement starts:**
  - "Ensure the specification and SOW are reviewed requirement by requirement with the
    customer prior to signature"
  - Establish explicit ground rules for customer involvement
  - Define the scope-of-work process and get customer agreement to it

- **Controls recommended during the engagement:**
  - A "no freebies" discipline
  - Route improvement suggestions to a separate channel rather than into the current build
  - "Maintain separate cost accounts for out-of-scope work"
  - Use a change-of-scope proposal checklist

- **Sourcing quality, stated honestly:** the article cites no statistics and no empirical
  study. It argues from a hypothetical case study.

## Direct implication for the skill

Two of these transfer directly into a capture-based tool. The "separate cost accounts for
out-of-scope work" instruction is exactly what an accumulated out-of-scope estimate is: a
running tally kept separately from the contracted work. And the internal causes matter as much
as the external ones, which means the skill has to look for out-of-scope work the user
volunteered, not only for work the client demanded.
