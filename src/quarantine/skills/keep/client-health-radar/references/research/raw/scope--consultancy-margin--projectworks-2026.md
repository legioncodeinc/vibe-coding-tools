# A Consultant's Guide to Managing Scope Creep and Protecting Margins

- **Title:** A Consultant's Guide to Managing Scope Creep and Protecting Margins, Projectworks blog
- **URL:** https://www.projectworks.com/blog/scope-creep
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog (professional services automation vendor)

## Why this source matters for client-health-radar

The PMI material is written for project managers inside a product organization. This one is
written for the billable-hours case, which is the skill's actual audience: agencies,
consultancies and freelancers whose margin is the difference between hours contracted and
hours worked.

## Extracted claims

- **Definition used:** scope creep is "the gradual, uncontrolled expansion of a project's
  original requirements without a corresponding adjustment in budget, timeline, or resource
  allocation."
- **The professional-services-specific form:** unbilled "favor-based" work that erodes
  profitability.
- **Margin framing:** scope creep is called "the silent killer of consultancy profitability",
  and it turns "a high-margin engagement into a pro-bono nightmare" by inflating cost without
  a matching budget increase.
- **Mechanism of accumulation:** changes begin modestly, described as "a project sponsor
  asking for 'one more feature'", and small additions "quietly accumulate and disrupt a
  project's progress" because boundaries expand "slowly, often unnoticed."
- **One statistic, with weak sourcing:** "37% of project failures" are attributed to "a lack
  of clearly defined objectives and milestones." No study, sample or citation is given on the
  page for this figure. Treat as unverified.
- **The five-question triage the article recommends for any incoming change:**
  1. Does the change align with the original project goals?
  2. What is the impact on "the project's schedule, budget, and available resources"?
  3. Is the change necessary to achieve the project's core deliverables?
  4. Is there a formal change control process?
  5. Are alternative solutions available?
- **Prevention recommendations:** a structured change control process, a Work Breakdown
  Structure to "clearly define the tasks involved", and clear documentation of scope
  boundaries.

## Direct implication for the skill

The phrase "quietly accumulate" and "often unnoticed" is the whole reason a capture-based
detector has a job. The individual ask is small enough that nobody escalates it. The record
of every ask, dated and quoted, is the artifact nobody has. The five-question triage is a
usable shape for what the skill presents per detected item, minus question 4 which the skill
cannot answer from capture.
