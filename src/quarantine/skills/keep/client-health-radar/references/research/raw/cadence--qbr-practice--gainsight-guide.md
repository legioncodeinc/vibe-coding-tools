# The Essential Guide to Quarterly Business Reviews (Gainsight)

- **Title:** The Essential Guide to Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs), Gainsight
- **URL:** https://www.gainsight.com/essential-guide/quarterly-business-reviews-qbrs/
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-docs (customer success platform vendor, evergreen guide rather than
  a dated post). SaaS-oriented; the transfer to a project-based agency relationship is
  imperfect and the distillation says so.

## Why this source matters for client-health-radar

It is the standard statement of account cadence practice, and it is where the skill gets the
tiering idea: cadence should not be uniform across the roster, it should follow account tier
and account health.

## Extracted claims

- **Base frequency:** QBRs run quarterly, but the guide declines to prescribe one universal
  frequency: "not every customer requires the same cadence or depth of engagement."

- **Tiered cadence model:**
  - Strategic accounts: high-touch, in-person or live virtual review
  - Growth accounts: digital-first review by video or email recap
  - Emerging and SMB accounts: light-touch, mostly automated
  - At-risk accounts: targeted intervention with focused check-ins

- **The link to renewal, stated as a mechanism rather than measured:** "QBRs help reassure you
  that your customer will renew their contract or subscription once it expires by consistently
  demonstrating value", and "if the customer doesn't experience success with your product,
  there's a good chance that customer eventually will churn."

- **One statistic, attributed:** "B2B customers who have strong executive engagement are 2.5
  times more likely to renew", attributed to McKinsey. No link on the page.

- **Content elements the guide says belong in a review:** strategic performance against goals;
  ROI analysis with concrete metrics; progress on previous commitments; benchmarking data; a
  customer health score update; and clear action items with owners and deadlines.

## Direct implication for the skill

"Progress on previous commitments" is the promise ledger. "Clear action items with owners and
deadlines" is the Action Items block a Littlebird meeting summary already produces. The
tiering model justifies deriving a per-client expected cadence rather than applying one
silence threshold across a roster.

The executive-engagement figure is the closest published thing to the "who else did they bring
to the call" signal, and it points in the same direction: more senior people in the room is
associated with retention, so senior people disappearing from the room is worth flagging. That
is an inference across two sources, not a measured finding, and it is labeled as such
downstream.
