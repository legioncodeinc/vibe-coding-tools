# Task switching

- **Title:** Task switching. Stephen Monsell. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, volume 7, number 3, March 2003.
- **URL:** http://epa.psy.ntu.edu.tw/perception/pdf/Monsell_2003.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer-reviewed review article)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

This is the standard review of the laboratory switch-cost literature. It is the reason
"switching has a cost" is a defensible statement at all. It is also the reason the size of
that cost cannot be carried over to a workday: the effects it describes are measured in
milliseconds on trials that last under a second.

## Extracted claims, quoted

- Definition and typical magnitude: "Responses take longer to initiate on a 'switch trial'
  than on a 'non-switch' or task-repetition trial, often by a substantial amount (e.g. 200
  ms relative to a baseline of 500 ms). Also, the error rate is often higher after a task
  switch."
- Preparation does not remove the cost: "Preparation generally does not eliminate the
  switch cost. In the examples shown, the reduction in switch cost seems to have reached a
  substantial asymptote, the 'residual cost,' after roughly 600 ms of preparation."
- The residual cost is durable: "Substantial residual costs have been reported even when 5
  s or more is allowed for preparation."
- The applied caution the review offers is about interface design for operators monitoring
  multiple information sources and switching between activities under time pressure, air
  traffic control being the named example. The review does not extend the laboratory
  numbers to everyday office multitasking.

## What this source does NOT support

It does not support any statement of the form "each switch costs you N minutes". The
measured quantity is a response-latency difference on a single trial, on the order of
tenths of a second, in a task where the two tasks are known, cued, and trivially small.
The review contains no estimate of the cost of switching between real work contexts, and
it makes no claim about aggregate daily cost.

## Direct implication for the skill

Switching is a real cost with a real mechanism, and that justifies counting switches and
showing the user their distribution. It does not justify multiplying a switch count by any
per-switch penalty to produce hours. There is no published per-switch penalty in units of
workday minutes to multiply by. Any skill that performs that multiplication has invented
the multiplier.
