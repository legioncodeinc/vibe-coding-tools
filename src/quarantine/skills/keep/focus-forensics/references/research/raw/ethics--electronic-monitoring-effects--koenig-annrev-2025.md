# Electronic Monitoring at Work

- **Title:** Electronic Monitoring at Work. Cornelius J. Koenig. Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, volume 12, pages 321 to 342, January 2025. First published as a Review in Advance on 14 August 2024.
- **URL:** https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-110622-060758
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer-reviewed annual review)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

This skill produces exactly the artifact an employer would want: a per-hour record of how
a person's attention moved. The difference between a self-analysis tool and a surveillance
tool is who holds it and who chose to run it, not what it computes. This review is the
evidence on what happens when the same class of measurement is applied to someone by
someone else.

## Extracted claims

- Performance: "the overall impact of monitoring on performance appears neutral."
- Strain: the review reports "a small positive correlation...with strain."
- Attitudes: "a small negative correlation...with job attitudes", indicating lower job
  satisfaction.
- Trust: multiple studies find monitoring undermines trust in management and affects
  perceptions of organizational fairness.
- Why the headline effects look small: "modest effect sizes may stem from paradoxical
  effects that counterbalance each other, a phenomenon known as suppression." A neutral
  average is consistent with real harms and real gains cancelling, not with nothing
  happening.
- Moderators named: individual personality and locus of control, job characteristics and
  task complexity, national legal regulation and cultural context, and how the monitoring
  is implemented and communicated.
- Overall conclusion: "relationships are likely contingent upon various moderating
  factors". Context determines consequences.

## Direct implication for the skill

1. **The design note is not decoration.** The strongest moderator the review names that is
   under anyone's control is how the monitoring is implemented and communicated. A person
   voluntarily reading their own capture, on their own machine, with the output going only
   to them, is at the far end of that moderator. A manager running the same analysis on a
   report is at the other end, and the review's strain and trust findings apply there.
2. **Neutral average performance effects are not a defense of monitoring.** The
   suppression point means an aggregate null can hide a real strain cost. A skill that
   pointed this analysis at another person could not honestly claim it was harmless on the
   grounds that the measured average effect on performance is about zero.
3. **This is why the skill is hard-scoped to the user's own capture.** Littlebird captures
   one machine belonging to one person who installed it. The skill analyzes that person's
   own history for that person. It does not run against a named colleague, it does not
   produce a comparison between people, and it does not produce an artifact framed for
   anyone but the user.
