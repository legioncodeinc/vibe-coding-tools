# Effects of task interruptions caused by notifications from communication applications on strain and performance

- **Title:** Effects of task interruptions caused by notifications from communication applications on strain and performance. Sandra Ohly, Luca Bastin. Journal of Occupational Health, volume 65, article e12408, 6 June 2023.
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/joh/article/65/1/e12408/7479297
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer-reviewed journal article, randomized between-subjects field experiment)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

It is the archive's best evidence that a specific, adoptable, one-week-scale change to the
environment reduces interruption frequency and that the reduction carries through to
outcomes. It is also unusually candid about its own limits, and those limits are the same
limits this skill has.

## Method, quoted

- Final sample: "247 cases". "162 (65.6%) were female and 110 (44.5%) were employees. The
  mean age was 28.15 years (SD = 7.38)." "80.2% participants 30 years or younger" and
  "71.5% indicated having a bachelor's degree or higher."
- Design: between-subjects, randomized. "Participants were randomly assigned to one of two
  experimental conditions 1 day before the preselected day. In the experimental condition
  participants were asked to disable notifications as far as possible across all ICT
  devices."
- Manipulation: the experimental group received "detailed instructions on how to disable
  the automatic communication notifications across their devices (mobile phones,
  notebooks, and wearables)"; controls kept baseline settings.

## Findings, quoted

- Manipulation check: "the frequency of interruptions at t2 was lower in the experimental
  group than in the control group (F = 37.92, P under .001)."
- Performance: the mediated path was significant, supporting "fewer interruptions caused
  by notifications lead to a higher performance."
- Strain: the indirect effect on irritation was significant, "as evident by the
  bootstrapped confidence interval excluding zero".
- Moderation, fear of missing out: "for individuals with low and medium levels of FoMO,
  interruptions decrease performance, whereas for individuals high on FoMO, interruptions
  do not affect performance."
- Moderation, telepressure: "for individuals with medium and high levels of telepressure,
  interruptions decrease performance, whereas for individuals low on telepressure,
  interruptions do not affect performance."
- Non-result: "For internal interruptions as an outcome, the interaction of FoMO and
  frequency of interruptions was not significant."

## Limitations, quoted

1. "participants in the experimental group were not exposed to a complete notification ban
   but were left with the option to leave important notifications enabled." The authors
   note this "more lenient approach could be responsible for smaller effects."
2. "all variables were measured via self-reports that could entail the risk of
   distortions", and specifically "the frequency of interruptions measured using
   self-reports rather than objective behavioral data should be interpreted with cautions
   due to recall bias."
3. "the short treatment period of one working day also pose a threat to external validity,
   because participants may need time to adapt their behavior."
4. "the heterogeneous sample composition questions the generalizability of the results to
   older, less educated individuals."
5. "the assumed self-regulatory pathway was not supported in our study."

## Direct implication for the skill

1. **This is the best-supported nudge in the archive and it is still one working day, in a
   young and highly educated sample, measured by self-report.** Offer it as the default
   experiment when the pattern points at externally triggered switching, and say plainly
   that the evidence behind it is one day long.
2. **The moderation findings forbid a universal prescription.** The same intervention
   helped some people and did nothing measurable for others, and which group a given
   person is in was not predictable from their behavior alone. That is a direct argument
   for one suggested experiment with a stated check, rather than a recommendation
   presented as settled.
3. **The paper measured interruption frequency by asking people, and flagged that as a
   weakness.** This skill measures observed transitions instead, which avoids recall bias
   and introduces sampling limits instead. Neither instrument is clean. Say which one is
   in use.
