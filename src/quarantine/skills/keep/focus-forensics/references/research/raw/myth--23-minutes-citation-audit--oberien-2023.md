# Interruptions cost 23 minutes 15 seconds, right?

- **Title:** Interruptions cost 23 minutes 15 seconds, right? oberien's Blog, 5 November 2023.
- **URL:** https://blog.oberien.de/2023/11/05/23-minutes-15-seconds.html
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** community (independent citation audit by a named blogger)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

The assignment requires that the "23 minutes 15 seconds to refocus" figure be traced to
its actual source and characterized accurately rather than repeated. This post is the
primary tracing work. It is a blog, which is the weakest source class in this archive, but
it is a blog that did checkable work: it names the papers it opened and quotes what they
actually contain, so any claim it makes can be verified against the papers themselves, and
two of those papers are archived here separately.

## What the author did

Attempted to verify a citation for a colleague. Went to the paper most commonly cited as
the source. Quoted result: "Open the paper, Ctrl+F for '23', 'no results'." Then checked
the other papers routinely cited for the figure, and then surveyed the blog posts that
cite it.

## What the checked papers actually say

| Paper checked | What the audit found in it |
|---|---|
| The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress (Mark, Gudith, Klocke, CHI 2008) | The number 23 does not appear. The paper reports "the time spent on only the original task was in fact lower when interruptions were present (20.31 and 20.60 min) compared to no interruptions (22.77 min)". It does not address recovery time between the interruption and the return to the original work |
| A Diary Study of Task Switching and Interruptions | 50 task switches per week reported. No recovery time data |
| Disruption and Recovery of Computing Tasks (Iqbal and Horvitz, CHI 2007) | "takes 11 - 16 minutes to resolve an interruption" |
| If Not Now, When? | Mentions collecting resumption lag data but "never provides any value for that number" |
| No Task Left Behind? (Mark, Gonzalez, Harris, CHI 2005) | Examines task resumption probability, not a specific recovery time |

## The survey of secondary citation

Of 23 blog posts analyzed:

- 9 posts cited academic papers incorrectly.
- 2 posts correctly referenced actual paper results.
- 9 posts cited interviews with Gloria Mark in which the 23 minutes 15 seconds figure was
  spoken.
- 2 posts referenced the Wall Street Journal quoting Mark with the figure.

## The author's conclusion

No primary printed source for the 23 minutes 15 seconds figure was found. The figure
appears in interviews with Gloria Mark and in media citations of those interviews, and not
in a peer-reviewed publication.

## Direct implication for the skill

Do not print the number 23 minutes 15 seconds in any output of this skill, in any framing,
including a debunking framing that repeats it. The honest statement is: the published
field measurements of resumption in knowledge work land in a wide band, roughly 9 to 26
minutes depending on what is being measured and by whom, with standard deviations at or
above the means, and the tidy single figure in circulation has no paper behind it.

That is a specific instance of the general rule this skill is built on. A number that
sounds precise and travels well is exactly the kind of number that gets detached from its
method and repeated until it is unfalsifiable. This skill is in the business of not
producing more of them.
