## Why this source is in the archive

It is secondary coverage of the same citation audit archived at
`myth--23-minutes-citation-audit--oberien-2023.md`, and it adds one piece of information
the audit itself does not: the figure was still being written up and recirculated as news
years later, which is the behavior of a claim that has become folklore rather than a
finding. It is archived as evidence about how the number travels, not as independent
evidence about the number.

## Extracted claims

- Framing of the claim being examined: "a widely cited statistic claiming it takes exactly
  23 minutes and 15 seconds to refocus after an interruption has become gospel in
  productivity circles."
- Claimed origin: "the actual origin of the 23 minutes and 15 seconds figure traces back
  to a 2006 Gallup interview with researcher Gloria Mark." Spoken in an interview, never
  published as a formal finding.
- On peer review: "After searching through five academic papers commonly cited as sources
  for the 23-minute figure, none actually contained this specific number."
- On miscitation: "9 blog posts incorrectly referenced academic papers" and "1 post
  included fabricated quotes not found in referenced papers."
- Stated moral: this illustrates how "scientific information can become corrupted as it
  travels from research papers to popular media."

## Conflict with the primary audit, stated rather than smoothed

This piece says one surveyed post contained fabricated quotes. The primary audit as
retrieved reports the 9 incorrect citations, the 2 correct ones, the 9 interview citations
and the 2 Wall Street Journal citations, and does not give a fabricated-quote count. The
fabrication count is therefore reported here as appearing in the secondary coverage only,
and it is not used anywhere in the skill.

The Gallup interview attribution likewise appears in this secondary piece with a year
(2006) that the primary audit does not state in the text retrieved. Treat the specific
year as unconfirmed.

## Direct implication for the skill

Both readings agree on the load-bearing point and disagree only on details of the
provenance, so the skill takes only the agreed part: there is no peer-reviewed paper
containing the figure. Everything past that stays out.
