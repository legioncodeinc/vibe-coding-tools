# "A Pace Not Dictated by Electrons": An Empirical Study of Work Without Email

- **Title:** "A Pace Not Dictated by Electrons": An Empirical Study of Work Without Email. Gloria Mark, Stephen Voida, Armand Cardello. Proceedings of ACM CHI 2012.
- **URL:** https://ics.uci.edu/~gmark/Home_page/Publications_files/CHI%202012.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer-reviewed conference paper)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

It is the cleanest published demonstration that a single behavioral change moves switch
frequency and run length in the same direction at the same time, measured rather than
self-reported, in a within-subjects design where each person is their own control. That
design is exactly the design of this skill's week-over-week comparison, and this paper is
the evidence that the design detects something real.

## Method, quoted

- "13 information workers in an organization".
- "a three-day baseline data collection phase and a five-day experimental ('No Email')
  condition", using "a within-subjects design".
- Instrument: "A custom window activity logging application was designed to collect
  real-time data about our participants' multitasking behavior" recording "the frequency of
  switches between document or program windows."

## Findings, quoted

| Measure | Baseline | No Email | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Window switches per hour | M = 37.1, SD = 31.4 | M = 18.2, SD = 23.5 | "t(12) = 6.50, p under .0001" |
| Seconds per window | M = 75.5, SD = 394.3 | M = 131.9, SD = 568.1 | "t(12) = 5.00, p under .001" |
| Heart rate variability | 77.03 (SD = 16.17) | 80.39 (SD = 18.36) | "stress, as measured by heart rate variability, was lower without email" |

Authors' summary: "Without email, people multitasked less and had a longer task focus, as
measured by a lower frequency of shifting between windows and a longer duration of time
spent working in each computer window."

## Read the standard deviations before quoting the means

The window-duration standard deviations are roughly five and four times their own means.
The switch-rate standard deviations are close to their means. Thirteen participants. The
direction of the effect is strong and the significance tests are convincing; the point
estimates are not stable quantities to carry around, and no user should be told they
should be switching 18 times an hour.

## The intervention itself

Removing email entirely for five workdays is not a behavior change a person can adopt as a
weekly nudge. What generalizes is the mechanism: cutting off a single high-frequency
interrupt source moves both switch count and run length together. Realistic weekly
versions of the same mechanism, such as closing the mail client for a defined block, are
untested at this strength and should be offered as an experiment rather than as a
prescription.

## Direct implication for the skill

1. Switch frequency and run length are two views of the same underlying change and move
   together. A report that shows both is showing one phenomenon twice, which is honest as
   long as it says so and does not present them as two independent confirmations.
2. Within-subject comparison is the design the evidence supports. Every claim in this
   study is a comparison of a person against themselves under two conditions. That is
   precisely what the skill's week-over-week output is, and it is why that output is the
   skill's most defensible product.
3. The measured behavioral change came from removing an interrupt source, not from the
   worker trying harder. Behavioral nudges in this skill should target the environment,
   not the user's willpower.
