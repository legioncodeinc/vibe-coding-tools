# No Task Left Behind? Examining the Nature of Fragmented Work

- **Title:** No Task Left Behind? Examining the Nature of Fragmented Work. Gloria Mark, Victor M. Gonzalez, Justin Harris. Proceedings of ACM CHI 2005.
- **URL:** https://ics.uci.edu/~gmark/CHI2005.pdf
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** academic (peer-reviewed conference paper)

## Why this source matters for focus-forensics

This is the canonical observational study of work fragmentation in knowledge work, and it
is the paper most often miscited as the source of the "23 minutes" figure. It supplies
the real baseline numbers, and it supplies them with an explicit account of how the
observation was done, which is what makes the numbers checkable.

## Method, quoted

- "Twenty-four people in total were observed in detail: 7 managers, 9 analysts, and 8
  developers."
- Technique: "a combination of three main ethnographic techniques: observation, long
  interviews and shadowing of informants."
- Instrumentation: "The observer used a time watch and notepad to record details of the
  informants' actions. The observer noted the time (to the second) and other details of
  the event." Notes were "transcribed into an activity tracking log inspired by
  Minztberg's structured observation method."
- Dose: "three and a half days" per informant, "an average time of 25 hours, 42 minutes
  per person. Over 700 formal hours of observation were done."

Note what this cost: a human being with a stopwatch, physically following each worker for
three and a half days. That is the measurement effort behind the numbers below.

## Definitions, quoted

- Work fragmentation: "a break in continuous work activity" consisting of "two main
  aspects: the length of time people spend in a continuous activity, and interruptions of
  that activity."
- Working sphere: "a unit of work that has a unique time frame, persons involved in them,
  and use of particular tools and applications."

## Quantitative findings, quoted

| Finding | Value as printed |
|---|---|
| Time in a working sphere before switching or interruption | "11 min. 4 sec., (sd=18 min. 9 sec.)" |
| Share of working sphere segments interrupted | "57.1% of all informants' working sphere segments were interrupted, on the average" |
| Distinct working spheres per person | "an average of 11.7 (sd=2.4) different working spheres" |
| Interrupted work resumed the same day | "77.2% of interrupted work was resumed on the same day" |
| Working spheres visited before resuming | "an average of 2.26 (sd=2.79) working spheres" |
| Time before same-day resumption | "an average length of time of 25 min. 26 sec (sd=54 min. 48 sec.)" |
| Collocated workers, time before switch | "11 min. 56 sec., (sd=15 min. 33 sec.)" |
| Distributed workers, time before switch | "9 min. 56 sec., (sd=13 min. 29 sec.)" |

Read the standard deviations. Every one of them is larger than or close to its own mean.
The distribution of run lengths is heavily skewed, so a mean run length is a poor summary
statistic and a median plus a distribution is the honest one.

## Limitations the authors state

- "it is very difficult to code activities during ethnographic observation without a video
  record (recording was not permitted)."
- On error: "measurement errors, but we assume these are normally distributed."
- On generalization: "we can only generalize our results to companies with similar
  characteristics: high pressure firms where many different tools are integral to work."

## Direct implication for the skill

Three things.

1. The often-quoted resumption number in this paper is 25 minutes 26 seconds, not 23
   minutes 15 seconds, and it is the time to return to an interrupted working sphere on
   the same day, with a standard deviation more than twice the mean. Any skill quoting a
   resumption figure at all must carry that spread.
2. The published baseline for a continuous run is roughly 11 minutes, measured by a human
   observer with a stopwatch, in one class of firm, twenty years ago. It is a reference
   point, not a target and not a benchmark to score a user against.
3. Fragmentation is defined by the authors as run length plus interruptions. Both halves
   are countable structure. Neither half is a time ledger.
