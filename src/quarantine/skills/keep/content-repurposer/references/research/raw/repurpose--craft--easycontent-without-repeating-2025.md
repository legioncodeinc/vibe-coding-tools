## Extracted content

### The core framing

Repurposing as **translation** rather than duplication. The stated method is to change the
ENTRY POINT while preserving the core message.

### The angle taxonomy it offers, verbatim

Three named reframing approaches:

1. "A **pain point** angle" - leading with problems teams face.
2. "A **question** angle" - opening with curiosity-driven inquiries.
3. "A **story** angle" - introducing concepts through narrative.

### Adapting depth rather than meaning

Per-surface allocation as stated: LinkedIn gets the essence, blogs get the why and how,
newsletters get the story, videos get the emotion.

### Formats feeding each other

"Let one format feed another" by expanding the conversation rather than reusing content.

## Claims this source supports

1. The problem is recognized in practice and has a name. "Repurposing without repeating
   yourself" is a live concern, not an invented one.
2. Changing the ENTRY POINT into the same idea is the practitioner-level remedy.
3. Different surfaces carry different depth of the same idea, which is a real and separate
   constraint from character count.

## Where this source is insufficient, and why the skill goes further

The three angles offered are all OPENING devices. Pain point, question and story are three
ways to start a piece that then makes the same argument. Under the distinction in
[repurpose--variation--schumann-petty-clemons-jcr-1990.md] that is cosmetic variation with
a rewritten first line, not substantive variation. A reader following the same person on
three surfaces reaches the same claim three times through three different doors.

The angle taxonomy authored in this skill's `references/angle-taxonomy.md` is built to
change the CLAIM BEING MADE, not the door. It is an extension of this source's idea, and it
is authored rather than sourced. The skill states that.
