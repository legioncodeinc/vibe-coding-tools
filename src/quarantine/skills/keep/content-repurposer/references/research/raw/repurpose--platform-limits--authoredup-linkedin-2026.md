## Why this source matters

Two different things are on this page and they have different evidence quality. The limit
table is a restatement of platform behavior and matches other sources. The post-length
performance analysis is first-party data from a tool that instruments LinkedIn posts, with
a stated sample size and date range, which is rare in this domain. The vendor sells a
LinkedIn writing tool, so the finding that longer posts perform better is directionally
convenient for them. Report it as their measurement, attributed, not as a fact about
LinkedIn.

### The truncation points, which matter more than the ceiling

- **Feed post, desktop:** approximately 210 characters visible before "see more".
- **Feed post, mobile:** approximately 140 characters visible before "see more".
- **Headline in search results:** approximately 60 to 70 characters visible.
- **About section:** approximately 300 characters before "See more".

### Performance analysis

- **Sample:** 372,126 LinkedIn posts.
- **Window:** September 2025 to February 2026.
- **Finding as stated:** posts in the 1,301 to 2,500 character range showed "27% higher
  engagement" than posts under 400 characters, with a median engagement rate of 2.61% to
  2.67%.
- **Method:** not described beyond the sample size and window. No control for account size,
  posting frequency, topic, or follower count is stated.

## Claims this source supports

1. LinkedIn's real constraint for a repurposed post is the fold at roughly 140 characters
   on mobile, not the 3,000-character ceiling. The first two lines are the whole decision.
2. The stated ceiling and the effective limit are different numbers on this platform, which
   generalizes: every surface in the pack has a ceiling and a fold, and the fold is the one
   that governs the draft.
3. There is at least one large-sample vendor measurement suggesting longer LinkedIn posts
   engage better than very short ones. Attributed, uncontrolled, and commercially
   convenient to the publisher.

## Caveats

The 27% figure has no stated controls and comes from a company selling long-post tooling.
Do not put it in a deliverable as a reason to write long. It is in this archive so the
skill can say "one vendor dataset points this way and here is why it is weak".
