## Extracted content

### Tactics named

1. Long-form to short-form: webinars, white papers and podcasts into blog posts, short
   videos, carousels, infographics.
2. Video dismantling: record video first, extract the audio as a podcast, clip for social,
   gate the full video, use the transcript for written pieces.
3. Multi-channel deployment of one asset across email, social, direct mail, webpages, and
   sales conversations.
4. Format rotation: sales decks into blog series, ebooks into tip sheets and checklists,
   old transcripts into LinkedIn posts.
5. Bundling existing high-performing assets into kits.
6. Archival mining of existing video and audio libraries.

### The format-versus-angle question, which is the reason this file is in the archive

Quoted practitioners, verbatim:

- Ashley Baker: "Change the packaging, not the message."
- Pam Didner: "Different formats need their own hook."
- Andrew Davis: frames the work as "Reimagining your insights for each unique platform",
  and reframes the operating question from "Where else can we publish this?" to "How does
  this idea transform on different platforms?"
- Stated directly: "Each format needs its own hook. Don't just copy and paste... Spend time
  crafting the right opening for each channel."

## Claims this source supports

1. Professional practice explicitly names per-format hook rewriting as mandatory, and names
   copy-paste as the failure mode.
2. The dominant framing in professional practice is still "change the packaging, not the
   message", which is cosmetic variation in the vocabulary of
   [repurpose--variation--schumann-petty-clemons-jcr-1990.md]. **This is the tension at the
   center of this skill.** The best trade-press advice available stops at reformatting plus
   a new hook. The one experiment in the archive says that is the low-attention strategy.
3. Andrew Davis's reframing ("how does this idea transform") is the closest thing in the
   craft literature to the angle argument, but it is one sentence and it is not developed
   into a method.

## Gap this source exposes

No source in this archive, including this one, supplies a worked taxonomy of angles. The
angle taxonomy in this skill is authored, not sourced, and the skill says so.
