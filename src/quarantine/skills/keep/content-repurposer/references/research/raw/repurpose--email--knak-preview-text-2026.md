## Evidence quality

Practitioner guidance from an email tooling vendor. Cites Litmus for one recommendation but
gives no sample size or study. The client-by-client behavior described is checkable in
principle and matches common practice.

## Extracted content

### The overall recommendation

"Aim for 40 to 90 characters of preview text, with the key message in the first 40."

The cited Litmus recommendation is staying under 90 characters.

### Why a single number is impossible

What actually displays "ranges from zero to about five lines of text, roughly 278
characters at the top end, depending on the client and how the reader has configured their
inbox."

### Per-client behavior as stated

| Client | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Gmail | Subject line and preview text share one line of inbox space |
| Apple Mail on iPhone | Preview length is a reader setting, up to five lines |
| Outlook desktop | Message Preview displays one to three lines |
| Outlook on the web | Three lines per message |

No subject line character limits by client are given on this page.

## Claims this source supports

1. **Email has no single format constraint. It has a reader-configurable one.** Gmail
   shares one line between subject and preview, so a long subject eats the preview
   entirely. Apple Mail's preview length is set by the reader, not the sender.
2. The effective budget for the piece of an email a reader decides on is roughly 40
   characters, with a soft extension to 90. That is a tighter constraint than the LinkedIn
   mobile fold and much tighter than any post ceiling in the archive.
3. Preview text is a separate authored field, not a truncation of the body. An email
   derivative in a content pack that does not specify its preview text has left its most
   constrained surface unwritten.
