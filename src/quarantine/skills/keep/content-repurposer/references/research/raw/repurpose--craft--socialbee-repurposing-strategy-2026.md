## Extracted content

### Named failure modes of ordinary repurposing

1. **Platform mismatch.** Content not adapted to how each platform functions.
2. **Audience misalignment.** Different segments consume differently.
3. **Idea dilution.** Breaking content into too many fragments weakens each piece.
4. **No system.** Repurposing treated as one-off tasks.

Stated framing: "when you repurpose, you transform existing content into something that
actually fits how people consume content today."

### A three-way distinction worth keeping

| Term | Definition as given |
|---|---|
| **Repurposing** | Converting to new formats for specific goals |
| **Resharing** | Reusing exact content at different times |
| **Cross-posting** | Publishing identical content across platforms |

### The eight-step process

1. Collect all content centrally.
2. Identify high-value, evergreen, or proven-performing pieces.
3. Extract standalone components: quotes, statistics, frameworks.
4. Organize by content pillars with dedicated weekly schedules.
5. Adapt for each platform's native format.
6. Schedule in advance across platforms.
7. Target roughly 30% repurposed and 70% new content.
8. Analyze performance to refine future decisions.

Also stated: space repurposed content across time and vary formats to prevent repetition.

## Claims this source supports

1. **Idea dilution is a named failure mode in practice.** Cutting one source into too many
   pieces makes each piece weaker. This is a direct argument for capping the pack at 5 to 7
   short posts rather than maximizing count.
2. **Cross-posting is a distinct and worse thing than repurposing**, and the vocabulary
   distinguishing them exists. A pack of five identical posts on five surfaces is
   cross-posting wearing a repurposing label.
3. Selecting for "high-value, evergreen, or proven-performing" pieces before repurposing is
   standard practice. This is the source-selection step, named.
4. Spacing across time and varying format is the standard anti-repetition remedy in
   practice. Note that it is a SEQUENCING remedy, not an ANGLE remedy, which is exactly the
   limitation the angle taxonomy addresses.

## Caveats

The 30/70 repurposed-to-new ratio is unsourced and specific enough to look measured. It is
one practitioner's convention. Cite it as such or not at all.
