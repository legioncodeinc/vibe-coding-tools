# How Often Should You Post on LinkedIn in 2026? Data From 2 Million+ Posts

- **Title:** How Often Should You Post on LinkedIn in 2026? Data From 2 Million+ Posts
- **Author:** Tamilore Oladipo, Buffer
- **URL:** https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/
- **Published:** 2025-08-28
- **Fetched:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog, first-party dataset with a stated statistical method

## Why this source ranks near the top of the archive

It is the only cadence source found that states a sample size, names its statistical
methods, and reports a finding that cuts against its own commercial interest in one place
(it explicitly says LinkedIn does not punish frequent posting, which is a claim scheduling
tools usually benefit from making in the opposite direction). It is still vendor analysis
of a self-selected user base, not a controlled study.

## Extracted content

### Dataset and method

- **Sample:** over 2 million LinkedIn posts from more than 94,000 accounts.
- **Date range:** not stated in the article. This is a real weakness.
- **Methods, as stated:**
  - Z-score analysis comparing each account's high-frequency and low-frequency weeks
    against that account's own baseline.
  - Fixed effects regression controlling for account-level differences to isolate the
    frequency effect.

The within-account design matters. It compares an account to itself rather than comparing
big accounts to small ones, which removes the most obvious confound in this kind of
analysis.

### Findings

- **Headline recommendation:** "posting 2 to 5 times weekly on LinkedIn is the sweet spot
  for improving reach *and* engagement".
- 2 to 5 posts per week: plus 1,182 impressions per post, plus 0.23 percentage points of
  engagement.
- 6 to 10 posts per week: plus 5,001 impressions per post, plus 0.76 percentage points.
- 11 or more posts per week: "nearly 17,000 more impressions per post, 3x more engagements".
- On whether posting too often hurts: "no". Stated directly that "LinkedIn doesn't 'cap'
  your reach or punish you for posting often."
- Gains are described as incremental rather than exponential at higher frequencies.

## Claims this source supports

1. A weekly content pack of 5 to 7 short pieces plus a carousel, an email and a thread is
   inside the range this analysis calls the sweet spot for one surface, and does not exceed
   any cap it found.
2. There is no measured algorithmic penalty for posting frequently on LinkedIn in this
   dataset. The argument for restraint in a repurposed pack is a READER argument, not an
   algorithm argument. This is an important correction to the folk wisdom.
3. Cadence guidance in this domain is measurable and one credible measurement exists. It is
   platform-specific to LinkedIn and does not transfer to the other surfaces.

## What this source does NOT support

- **It does not measure repetitiveness.** The accounts posting 11 or more times per week
  were not assessed for whether their posts said the same thing. This analysis cannot
  distinguish a high-variety high-frequency account from a spammy one, so it cannot be used
  to argue that repetition is harmless.
- No equivalent measurement was found for Facebook, X, Instagram, or email in this sweep.
  Named gap.
