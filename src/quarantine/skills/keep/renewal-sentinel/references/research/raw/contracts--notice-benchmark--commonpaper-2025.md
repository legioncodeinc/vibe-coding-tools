# Common Paper: Auto-Renewal Clauses benchmark

- **Title:** Auto-Renewal Clauses in Contracts
- **URL:** https://commonpaper.com/clause/auto-renewal/
- **Fetch date:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** vendor-blog with a stated dataset (contract standards organization; figures drawn from its own corpus)
- **Publication date:** 2025-12-04

## Stated dataset

Analysis of more than 10,000 contracts, described as cloud service agreements and SaaS
contracts.

## Headline figures

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Cloud service agreements with automatic renewal | 85% |
| Auto-renewing agreements requiring 30 days notice of non-renewal | 84% |
| Agreements with an automatic fee increase on renewal | 21% |
| Most common automatic increase size | 5% to 8% |

## Framing the source gives

The 30-day notice window is "the date before which customers must let the vendor know if
they are churning", and it is "easy to overlook, especially in high-volume contract
environments". Renewal terms frequently carry embedded escalation clauses such as CPI plus a
percentage, which compound across terms.

## What the source does not give

Typical renewal term lengths. Not stated.

## Notes for the sentinel

This is the single most load-bearing statistic in the archive for the cancellation-window
feature. If 85% of these agreements auto-renew and 84% of those want 30 days notice, then
the default working assumption for an unknown annual SaaS contract is a 30-day notice
window, and the decision deadline is the renewal date minus 30 days.

The caveat that matters: this is a corpus of cloud service agreements, weighted toward
contracts written on standardized paper. A self-serve credit-card SaaS plan with no
negotiated agreement often has no notice requirement at all, and cancels effective at the
end of the current term. Do not apply the 30-day default to a self-serve plan without
checking.
