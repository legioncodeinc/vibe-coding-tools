## Procedural facts

- ANPRM announced 2026-03-11.
- Published in the Federal Register 2026-03-13.
- Comment deadline 2026-04-13.

An ANPRM is the stage **before** a notice of proposed rulemaking. There is no proposed rule
text yet, therefore no compliance date and nothing for a vendor to comply with.

## Scope of the rule that is actually in force

The existing rule, adopted more than fifty years ago, remains "limited to prenotification
plans, such as product-of-the-month clubs, in which consumers are charged unless they
affirmatively decline an offer."

That is a narrow scope. It does not reach an ordinary SaaS annual auto-renewal.

## Questions the FTC asked for comment on

- Enrollment and cancellation timeframes.
- The impact of retention or "save" offers, and consumer savings from them.
- Businesses complying without unfair practices.
- Benefits of the vacated rule to consumers.
- Current compliance costs across overlapping regulations.
- Prevalence of negative option programs.
- Practices impairing consumer understanding, consent, or cancellation.
- Alternative regulatory approaches.
- Compliance burdens.
- Industry-specific exemptions.

## Enforcement posture

The source describes the rulemaking as "a major priority for the Trump-Vance FTC" and states
the Commission's view that existing laws "do not adequately address consumer harms
associated with negative option plans."

## Notes for the sentinel

Two things a renewal skill must get right and this source settles:

1. The in-force federal Negative Option Rule covers prenotification plans, not SaaS
   subscriptions. Do not cite it as protection for a software renewal.
2. The replacement is at ANPRM stage. Any statement about what it "will require" is
   speculation, and the skill should not make one.
