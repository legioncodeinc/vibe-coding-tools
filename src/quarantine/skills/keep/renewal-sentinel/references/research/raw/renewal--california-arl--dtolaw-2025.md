## Statute and effective date

California Automatic Renewal Law as amended by AB 2863. Effective 2025-07-01.

## Renewal reminder

The law mandates annual reminders to subscribers. The reminder must identify the product or
service, the charge frequency and amount, and the means of cancellation.

The source does **not** state how many days before renewal the reminder must go out. This
archive records that gap rather than filling it.

## Price increase notice

Notice must be given "between 7 and 30 days before the fee change takes effect" and must be
"in a manner that is capable of being retained by the consumer."

## Cancellation mechanics

- Same-medium rule: a consumer must be able to cancel through the same medium they used to
  sign up, or the medium through which they typically interact with the business.
- Online cancellation must "simultaneously and prominently display a direct link or
  click-to-cancel button."
- Phone lines must be answered promptly during normal business hours.
- A cancellation left on voicemail must be processed within one business day.

## Penalties

Not stated in this source.

## Notes for the sentinel

California is the closest thing to a real click-to-cancel obligation currently in force in
the United States, and it is state law and consumer-facing. When a user asks why a vendor's
cancel flow is easy, the answer is usually California or the vendor's own policy, not the
FTC.

The seven-to-thirty-day price increase notice is a directly useful sentinel trigger: a
captured price-change email from a consumer-facing vendor implies a fee change landing
inside roughly one month.
