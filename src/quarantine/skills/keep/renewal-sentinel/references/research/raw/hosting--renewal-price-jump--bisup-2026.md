## The multiplier

Renewal rates run 2x to 4x the introductory rate. Examples given:

| Plan type | Intro | Renewal | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry shared | $2.99 per month | $11.99 per month | about 4x |
| Mid shared | $4.99 per month | $14.99 per month | about 3x |
| Entry managed WordPress | $6.99 per month | $24.99 per month | about 3.5x |

## Why the model exists, as stated

"The low price is real but temporary. It usually covers only your first billing cycle."
Providers rely on switching costs: once a site is live, migration feels burdensome, which
lets the renewal price rise without churn.

## Mitigations the source lists

1. Lock longer terms, two to three years, at the introductory rate.
2. Choose hosts that disclose the renewal price up front.
3. Set renewal date reminders.
4. Ask for a loyalty discount before renewal.
5. Migrate to a host offering free transfers if the increase is unacceptable.

## Reliability assessment

Vendor content from a host that sells the same promotional-term structure it is describing.
The multiplier range is consistent with publicly posted pricing across the shared hosting
category and is not a controversial claim. Treat the specific dollar figures as illustrative
of the category, not as a survey.

## Notes for the sentinel

This is the reason hosting cannot be projected from last year's charge. A first-term hosting
renewal is the one case in this entire skill where the prior-year amount is a systematically
bad predictor, because the prior amount was promotional by design and the renewal is
routinely two to four times larger.

The rule that follows: for a hosting item on its first renewal, the projected amount is
marked Low confidence and the note says the promotional term is ending. Do not carry the
promotional figure forward.
