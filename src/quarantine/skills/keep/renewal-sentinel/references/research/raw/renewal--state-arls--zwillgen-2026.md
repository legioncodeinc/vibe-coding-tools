## State automatic renewal laws, as tabulated

| State | Effective date | Requirement noted |
|---|---|---|
| California | 2025-07-01 (AB 2863) | Express affirmative consent; retention offers allowed only alongside a button that immediately effectuates cancellation on the same page; cancellation by the same medium used to sign up |
| Arkansas | 2025-08-03 | "Largely mirrors the California auto-renewal law in all respects" |
| Colorado | 2025-08-06 for cancellation; 2026-02-16 for the consumer definition | One-step online cancellation link |
| Massachusetts | 2025-09-02 | Point-of-sale disclosures; specific obligations for shorter subscription terms |
| New York | 2025-11-05 | Price increase consent, or a 14-day cancellation window; pre-billing disclosure |
| Maine | 2026-01-01 | Separate consent to the automatic renewal provision |
| Maryland | 2026-06-01 | Pre-trial and discount notices; alternative cancellation options |
| Connecticut | 2026-07-01 | Annual renewal reminders; strict phone cancellation requirements |

California's amendments also require notice 7 to 30 business days before a price increase.

## B2B scope

Colorado's amendments "broaden the existing definition of 'consumer' to arguably apply to
B2B subscriptions." The source words this as arguable, not settled.

## Notes for the sentinel

The practical consequence for a solo operator or small agency: most of these statutes are
consumer statutes. A business buying software on a business account frequently sits outside
them. The protection that actually applies to a business buyer is the contract's own notice
clause plus, in a few states, a B2B-specific statute. Do not tell a business user that a
consumer ARL protects their SaaS renewal without checking the scope.
