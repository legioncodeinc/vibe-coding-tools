# New York General Obligations Law Section 5-903 (official statute text)

- **Title:** New York General Obligations Law, GOB 5-903, Automatic renewal provision of contract for service, maintenance or repair unenforceable by contractor unless notice thereof given to recipient of service
- **URL:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/5-903
- **Fetch date:** 2026-08-17
- **Source type:** official-docs (the state legislature's own publication of the statute)
- **Publication date:** statute, current codification

## What it covers

Contracts for service, maintenance, or repair to real or personal property. This is a
business-to-business notice statute, not a consumer statute.

## The notice requirement

The provider must give written notice "at least fifteen days and not more than thirty days
previous to the time specified for serving such notice" before the automatic renewal takes
effect. The notice must be "served personally or by certified mail, calling the attention of
that person to the existence of such provision in the contract."

## Consequence of failure

The automatic renewal provision is "unenforceable against the person receiving the service,
maintenance or repair."

## Exception

Does not apply where "the automatic renewal period specified is one month or less."

## Notes for the sentinel

This is the strongest counter-lever available to a business buyer who missed a notice
window, and it is why the skill records where the vendor relationship sits. The mechanism:
the burden runs against the vendor. If the vendor did not serve the statutory notice in the
window, the auto-renewal clause is unenforceable against the buyer.

Two limits the skill must state:

1. The statute is written around service, maintenance, and repair of property. Whether it
   reaches a pure software subscription is a legal question this archive does not answer,
   and no source here settles it. Flag it as a question for a lawyer, never as a conclusion.
2. It is New York law. It governs where it governs. Read the contract's choice-of-law
   clause before relying on it.
