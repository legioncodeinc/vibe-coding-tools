# Guide 05: Market Insights and Battlecards

A feature-comparison table is the floor of a competitive research deliverable, not the ceiling. When a user asks for "insights," "more value," or pushes back that a first draft is "just bullets and tables," this is the guide to reach for. Full reasoning: `../references/research/distilled-competitive-research.md`, section 5.

## Compute statistics, do not just describe

Every market-pattern claim in the output should trace to a number derived from the workbook's own data, never an impression. Before writing any narrative, compute at minimum:
- Segment splits within the closest category (e.g. how many competitors share the subject's specific approach vs. a different one)
- Pricing transparency split (published price vs. gated/custom-quote), since this is very often a real, usable differentiator
- Any other split the data naturally supports (geographic/compliance positioning, a named emerging sub-segment, etc.)

State the number, then state the implication in the same sentence or the next one. "12 of 17 (71%) are company-only" is a fact; "that means most of this category is not who the subject actually loses deals to" is the insight. Both belong in the output.

## Write the market-pattern page in prose, not bullets

A dedicated page (or section) that reads like an analyst's memo: 2 to 4 short sections, each with a clear claim as its heading, 1 to 3 paragraphs of prose underneath stating the number and the "so what." Bullets are fine for the executive-summary "close first / protect and lead with" lists (short, scannable, action-oriented); the market-pattern page itself should read as connected analysis, since that is what distinguishes an insight from a fact dump.

## Battlecards: Fact, Impact, Act

For the 5 to 6 competitors closest to the subject on the dimension that actually decides deals (not the longest list, not just the best-known names), build a battlecard per the FIA structure [`../references/research/distilled-competitive-research.md`, section 5; raw source: `battlecards--klue-battlecard-101.md`]:

- **Fact**: their one-line pitch, in their own words or close to it, plus who they target
- **Impact**: where they genuinely win (be honest, a battlecard that only flatters the subject is not trustworthy) and where the subject wins back
- **Act**: a literal, sayable talk track, specific enough that a rep could say it on a call verbatim, not a generic "explain the differentiator"

Use directional, charged language over neutral description ("a fraction of their price" beats "cheaper"), and specific numbers over vague comparisons wherever the research supports it. Keep the set small; 5 to 6 well-built cards beat 15 shallow ones.

## Accuracy is part of the value, not separate from it

Date the report. Name where figures came from. Flag anything gated, custom-quoted, or unverified explicitly rather than presenting it with false precision. Stale or fabricated competitive intel destroys trust in the whole asset faster than a smaller, honestly-scoped one would.
