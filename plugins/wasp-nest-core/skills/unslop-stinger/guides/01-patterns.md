# The pattern catalog

Adapted from the unslop skill by Lauren Tan (MIT; source and license
archived at `../references/research/raw/`), with the colony's no-em-dashes
rule folded into pattern 13. The catalog is the tool; the loop in SKILL.md
is how you use it.

## Content

1. **Puffery.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape",
   "setting the stage for". Cut it and state what happened.
2. **Name-dropping.** Listing outlets without context. Pick one, say what
   was said.
3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...",
   "showcasing...". Delete or expand with real content.
4. **Promotional language.** "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking",
   "groundbreaking", "renowned". Neutral descriptions instead.
5. **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "some argue". Name the source
   or delete the claim.
6. **Formulaic resilience.** "Despite challenges, X continues to thrive."
   Replace with the specific fact.

## Language

7. **AI vocabulary.** additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance,
   foster, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal,
   showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant. Plain words instead.
8. **Fancy is.** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features" when the
   verb is "is" or "has".
9. **Not just X but Y.** State the point directly.
10. **Rule of three.** Forcing triads. Use the natural number.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Four names for one actor in a paragraph. Pick one
    and repeat it.
12. **False ranges.** "from X to Y" with no meaningful scale. List the
    topics.

## Style

13. **Em dash overuse.** Periods and commas only. No parentheses or en
    dashes as substitutes either: that trades one tell for another. If a
    thought needs separation, end the sentence or use a comma.
14. **Colon overuse.** Fine before a list; not as a mid-sentence connector.
15. **Boldface overuse.** Not every proper noun earns bold.
16. **Inline-header lists.** A bold label and colon that restate the line is
    a tell; convert to prose. A bold lead-in ending in a period plus
    genuinely new detail is fine.
17. **Title case headings.** Sentence case.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove from headings and bullets.
19. **Curly quotes.** Straight quotes.

## Communication artifacts

20. **Chatbot phrases.** "I hope this helps", "Let me know if...", "Of
    course!", "Certainly!". Remove.
21. **Cutoff disclaimers.** "While details are limited..." Find the source
    or remove.
22. **Sycophancy.** "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!" Respond
    directly.

## Filler

23. **Filler phrases.** "In order to" becomes "to". "Due to the fact that"
    becomes "because". "It is important to note that" is deleted.
24. **Excessive hedging.** "could potentially possibly be argued" becomes
    "may".
25. **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." Specific plans or
    facts instead.

## Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** substrate, wedge, vector, nexus, vantage,
    paradigm, north star, flywheel, gold-plating, scaffolding (as metaphor),
    surface (as in API surface). Pick the concrete word: "base" for
    substrate, "add" for wedge in, "way" for vector, "more than the job
    needs" for gold-plating.

## Plain speech

27. **Say what it does, not how it feels.** The fix names the mechanism or
    the number. If a sentence cannot be restated as a concrete instruction,
    fact, or number, cut it. If it could appear unchanged in another
    project's docs, it says nothing about this one.
28. **Shorten or split dense sentences.** One idea per sentence.
29. **Active voice.** Name the actor. Passive only when the actor is unknown
    or genuinely does not matter.
30. **Cut adverbs or use a stronger verb.** "runs quickly" becomes "is fast"
    or the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured delta.
31. **Prefer the plain word.** utilize to use, leverage to use, facilitate
    to help, numerous to many, "in the event that" to if.

## Adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job. Voiceless prose is its own tell:

- Have opinions. React to facts instead of neutrally listing pros and cons.
- Vary rhythm. Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time.
- Acknowledge complexity. "Impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats
  "impressive".
- Use "I" when it fits. First person is not unprofessional.
- Let some mess in. Perfect structure reads machine-made.
- Be specific. Not "this is concerning" but the concrete detail that is
  concerning.
