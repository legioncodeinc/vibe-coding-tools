Distilled research for the automated-plus-heuristic WCAG audit this Stinger runs in wave W5, feeding a 0-100% score and AA/AAA-style rating where every finding cites its success criterion. Coverage is thin: only two raw sources exist, both filed under the single research cluster "accessibility," one a vendor blog written by the developer of a competing accessibility-audit plugin and one an advocacy-organization enforcement-tracking article. Both sources concentrate specifically on the European Accessibility Act (EAA, Directive (EU) 2019/882) and its WCAG-version mapping, neither source documents WCAG success criteria testing methodology, specific automated-scanning-tool behavior, or non-EU accessibility regimes (US ADA/Section 508, etc.) at all. Any claim about testing procedure or non-EU legal frameworks is not researched here and should not be treated as grounded.

Research window: single sweep, 2026-08-18.

## 1. What WCAG version this audit should cite, and why

Neither Directive (EU) 2019/882 itself nor its Annex I names WCAG or any WCAG version directly, Annex I expresses functional accessibility requirements as outcomes (perceivable through more than one sensory channel, understandable, operable), not a checklist with a version number. [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] The presumption-of-conformity route runs through Article 15(1): conformity with a harmonised standard whose reference is published in the Official Journal creates a presumption of conformity, but only as far as that standard covers the requirement. [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] The relevant harmonised standard is EN 301 549 (the European ICT accessibility standard). Both raw sources agree on the version mapping and status as of mid-2026:

| EN 301 549 version | WCAG mapping | Status as of August 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| V3.2.1 (dated March 2021) | WCAG 2.1 Level AA | Currently the cited, presumption-granting version | [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |
| V4.1.1 | WCAG 2.2 (adds nine success criteria over 2.1, including 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured Minimum, 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication Minimum, all at AA) | Draft V4.1.0 went out for review November 2025; V4.1.1 expected to be cited in the Official Journal around October 2026, per the vendor blog; the advocacy-org source independently estimates referencing "within 18 months" of its May 2026 publish date, with a transition period | [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |

Audit implication: this Stinger should score against WCAG 2.1 AA as the version with a live presumption-of-conformity route, while noting WCAG 2.2 AA items as forward-looking/good-faith indicators rather than the compliance bar, since "French and German authorities are already treating WCAG 2.2 conformance as good-faith evidence" per the advocacy-org source, a nuance the vendor-blog source does not make. [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md]

## 2. Why claiming the newer WCAG version is not automatically the safer audit framing

The vendor-blog source argues, on engineering grounds, that claiming WCAG 2.2 AA conformance is broadly a superset of 2.1 AA, but on legal-claim grounds it is a worse choice for two reasons: it asserts something the EAA never required while not asserting conformity with the standard that actually grants the presumption, and it is a larger promise (nine additional success criteria, each "another thing that can be wrong on your site, in a claim you published, in writing, about yourself"). [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] The source cites the FTC's action against accessiBe as turning "on claims of exactly this shape, not on the state of anyone's site, but on what was said about it," and recommends a dated, specific compliance statement naming known outstanding issues and a contact channel over a blanket conformance claim. [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] This is a single-sourced framing (only the vendor blog makes this argument), but it is directly relevant to how this Stinger should word its own audit report's compliance-claim language, favor dated, specific, gap-disclosing findings over a flat "AA compliant" verdict.

## 3. EAA enforcement is live and penalties vary by roughly two orders of magnitude across the EU 27

The advocacy-org source, the higher-authority source of the two for enforcement data (it cites European Commission DG JUST implementation notes and national market-surveillance authority bulletins, versus the vendor blog's self-described developer opinion), reports the EAA became enforceable across the Union on 28 June 2025. As of its May 2026 publish date:

| Finding | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Transposition | Formally complete in 27/27 Member States, but operationally uneven, designated market-surveillance authority, published complaint mechanism, and penalty schedule in force lag in seven Member States | [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |
| Penalty range | Floors as low as 5,000 euros (Estonia, Slovenia); top per-violation ceilings around 75,000 to 100,000 euros (France, Germany, Netherlands); up to 1,000,000 euros in Spain (Ley 11/2023); Italy uses a turnover-percentage tier up to 5% of annual turnover instead of a fixed ceiling | [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |
| First enforcement actions | Three Member States, Germany (BAFA), Spain (Ministerio de Asuntos Economicos), and France (ARCOM and DGCCRF), issued the first sanctioning resolutions and formal-notice tranches during the 2025-26 window, concentrated on e-commerce checkout flows and self-service kiosks | [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |
| Automated-scan pass rate | Fewer than 50% of major EU e-commerce properties pass an automated WCAG 2.1 AA scan at mid-2026, up from 30-40% before the 28 June 2025 deadline; national coverage ranges roughly 30% (late-transposing states) to 70% (consumer-banking and regulated-transport segments) | [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] |

The consolidated automated-scan dataset behind the pass-rate figure covers approximately 4,000 EU-domain e-commerce properties as of mid-2026, per the source's own citation list, though the underlying scan methodology (which automated tool, which specific WCAG success criteria) is not described in the archived text. [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md]

## 4. The microenterprise exemption

Both sources independently name the same exemption with consistent detail: Article 4(5) of Directive (EU) 2019/882 exempts microenterprises, defined as fewer than 10 persons with turnover or balance-sheet total under 2,000,000 euros, providing services, from the accessibility requirements and from every obligation attached to them. [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] The advocacy-org source adds a distinction the vendor blog does not make: the exemption is services-side only, it "does not extend to products." [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] Audit implication: before scoring a site as EAA-relevant at all, confirm the operator is not a services-side microenterprise under this threshold, and confirm whether the target is a product or service context (products do not get the microenterprise carve-out). The vendor blog separately flags that "almost nobody selling EAA compliance tools mentions this," a pointed but single-sourced observation about the vendor market this Stinger operates adjacent to. [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md]

## 5. The disproportionate-burden defense is narrow

The advocacy-org source states Article 14's disproportionate-burden defense "carries the burden of proof and a five-year documentation requirement, and has not yet succeeded at the level of a whole-platform exemption" as of its publish date. [raw/www-disabilityworld-org-articles-eaa-first-year-enforcement-report.md] This is single-sourced (the vendor blog does not discuss Article 14), and should be reported as a narrow, burden-of-proof-heavy defense rather than a reliable exemption path.

## 6. Honest gap the vendor-blog source itself flags

The vendor-blog author explicitly states they "could not establish from secondary sources, to my own satisfaction, exactly which references currently stand cited in the Official Journal under Directive (EU) 2019/882 as opposed to under Directive (EU) 2016/2102" (the older public-sector accessibility directive), and warns that "sources disagree, and vendor pages state it with a confidence their citations do not support." [raw/groundedwp-com-blog-wcag-21-or-22-for-the-eaa.md] This Stinger should carry the same caveat rather than presenting the EN 301 549 version mapping in section 1 as settled beyond what these two sources support, the recommended per-audit check is the live Official Journal listing, not a cached claim, a step neither raw source substitutes for.
