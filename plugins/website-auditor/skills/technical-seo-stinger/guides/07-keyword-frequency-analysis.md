That is a binding acceptance criterion regardless of research coverage. The honest response to a real gap is not to skip the checkpoint, and not to invent a cited standard that does not exist - it is to build a clearly-labelled, defensible procedure and say plainly that it is one. ## Procedure

See `references/templates/keyword-frequency-worksheet.md` for the full worked method: per-location counting (title, meta description, H1, H2/H3, first-100-words, total body), a per-1000-words comparability ratio (explicitly NOT a density-optimality claim), and three flag patterns (zero-presence, title/H1-absent, suspiciously repetitive).

## What the adjacent Google guidance does establish (mythbusting only)

Google's generative-AI documentation states its systems "understand synonyms and general meaning" and do not require AI-specific long-tail keyword-variation rewriting (distillation Section 10, `[raw/www-semrush-com-blog-google-publishes-generative-ai-search-guide.md]`). That is a negative constraint worth carrying into this checkpoint's judgment: do not flag a page for lacking exact-phrase repetition of a keyword when a synonym or paraphrase clearly covers the same intent. It does not establish a positive frequency-scoring method, only that exhaustive exact-phrase coverage is not the bar.
