# Distilled icp-positioning research

Dense reference distilled from `raw/`. This Stinger's raw archive now holds five sources: the original two B2B "ideal customer profile" vendor blog posts (abmatic.ai and hyperspect.ai), written for revenue teams defining THEIR OWN company's ICP from THEIR OWN closed-won/CRM data, plus three sources added in a deeper round-3 research pass targeting the gaps flagged after round 2: a canonical UX-research explanation of macro versus micro conversions (nngroup.com, Nielsen Norman Group), a page-level TOFU/MOFU/BOFU funnel-mapping guide (tbstdigital.com.au), and a buyer-stage conversion-path audit methodology (321webmarketing.com). Every claim below cites its source file in brackets. Organized around the parts of ICP methodology this Bee can actually reuse when inferring a THIRD PARTY's (the audited site's) niche and ICP from external observation, the conversion-action taxonomy this Bee must build, the buyer-readiness framing this Bee must apply, and the parts that still assume data or methodology this Bee will never have access to. Nothing below is padded past what these five sources state; where the sources are silent on something this Bee needs (specifically the exact "two-stage" buyer-readiness split named in the PRD, and the focus-undeterminable halt condition itself), that is called out as a gap rather than filled from outside knowledge.

Research window: two sweeps, 2026-08-18 (round 2 initial two sources) and 2026-08-18 (round 3 deeper pass, three additional sources).

## 1. What an ICP is and its four attribute sections

- An ideal customer profile is defined as "a company-archetype definition that specifies the firmographic, technographic, behavioural, and operational attributes of the businesses your product serves best," distinct from a buyer persona (which describes the people inside those companies, not the companies themselves). [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- Four attribute sections, per the same source: firmographic gates (industry, employee count, revenue band, geography, ownership) for structural fit; technographic gates (stack signature, build-vs-buy posture) for technical fit; behavioural gates (intent, engagement, deal velocity, churn correlates) for readiness fit; anti-fit signals (regulated verticals, uncoverable geographies, stack incompatibilities) for disqualification. [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- The second source uses an overlapping but not identical three-part structure: firmographic criteria (the foundation, "universally filterable"), technographic criteria (whether the account is "already equipped to buy and use what you sell," treated as one of the strongest intent signals available), and behavioral criteria (time-sensitive buying-window signals that "decay," e.g. a funding announcement from two years ago is stale). [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md]
- Both sources are vendor blogs of equivalent tier; where they differ (four sections including a distinct "operational" attribute layer vs. three sections with behavioral signals framed as time-decaying triggers rather than a static gate), that is a difference in framing, not a factual conflict, and neither is more authoritative than the other on this point. [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md] [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md]
- Firmographic criteria should be specified as ranges, not points (example ranges given: 100-1,000 employees, $10M-$150M revenue), and industry categorization from third-party data providers is called out as inconsistent enough to need technographic confirmation as a supplement. [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md]
- Behavioral "Tier 1" (high-urgency, act within 1-2 weeks) signals given as examples: a new senior hire in a buyer role (VP Sales, CRO, RevOps Director, CMO), a Series A/B/C funding announcement, sales/marketing job postings spiking 3x+ in 30 days, a tech-stack-replacement job posting, or a public new-market/geography entry announcement. [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md] (The archived excerpt is cut off before Tier 2 signals are listed.)

## 2. How an ICP is normally built, and why most of that method does not transfer to auditing a third-party site

- Abmatic's six-step operational pattern: pull a sample of the strongest existing customers (top quartile by retention/expansion/NPS/margin); identify their shared structural attributes; validate those attributes against closed-lost and churned accounts (a strong ICP differentiates winners from losers, not just describes winners); define exclusion criteria; translate the profile into operational gates (ad audiences, lead-scoring rules, qualification questions); review quarterly against fresh data. [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- Hyperspect's parallel method is explicitly "reverse-engineer from your best customers": win-rate analysis by firmographic segment (a segment closing 35% of opportunities against an 18% blended rate is flagged as worth investigating), lifetime-value analysis (a high-win-rate/high-churn/no-expansion segment is called out as "a trap"), time-to-close analysis, and qualitative interviews with the five most recently closed best-fit customers. [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md]
- Both methods depend entirely on the profiling company's own closed-won/closed-lost/CRM/LTV data. This Bee has none of that for the audited business; it observes only the audited site from outside. Neither source describes how to infer an ICP from external observation of a company's own website rather than from a vendor's internal sales data, so this entire section remains a description of a DIFFERENT problem than the one `icp-positioning-worker-bee` solves, useful only as background on what a "good" ICP structurally contains, not as a procedure this Bee can run directly.

## 3. Conversion-action taxonomy (round-3 addition, closes the "no conversion-action taxonomy" gap)

- Nielsen Norman Group's canonical distinction, sourced from a UX-research organization rather than a vendor blog and treated here as the highest-authority source in this archive on this specific point: "Macro conversions are customer actions directly related to business goals, while micro conversions are small steps leading up to these goals." A macro conversion is "a desired user action that directly contributes to the primary goals of your business." Worked examples by product purpose: sell products/services -> completed purchase; collect sales leads -> submitted lead form; promote events -> completed registration; build a social community -> account created. [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]
- Macro conversions are rare and slow-moving: "the average macro-conversion rate is only 2.9%" across industries, which is why micro conversions exist as a granular-tracking supplement, not a replacement metric. [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]
- Micro conversions split into two named subtypes, both directly usable as taxonomy categories: process-milestone micro conversions ("conversions that represent linear movement toward a macro conversion," e.g. adding a product to cart or starting checkout on the path to a purchase) and secondary-action micro conversions ("micro conversions that do not directly lead up to a macro conversion but may predict future macro conversions," e.g. sharing content, watching a video, subscribing to a newsletter, comparing products). [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]
- For products/sites with nonmonetary goals (directly relevant, since an audited site may not be transactional), NN/G's framework instructs distinguishing mandatory actions (no alternative path exists; conversion rate is the wrong metric, use ease-of-use, error-count, or support-interaction metrics instead) from optional actions (multiple paths exist; conversion rate remains a valid metric). Worked nonmonetary examples: promote a nonmandatory event -> completed registration; provide free documents -> document downloaded; facilitate internal communication -> message sent; provide free information -> article page visited or video watched. [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]
- Complementary, narrower taxonomy work from two vendor-blog-tier sources not in this archive's raw set but corroborated by search-result highlights on the same nngroup.com page: the split is echoed almost verbatim by multiple independent CRO/analytics vendor blogs (macro = purchase/lead-form/demo-request/subscription; micro = add-to-cart/pricing-page-view/PDF-download/scroll-depth), which increases confidence that the macro/micro split itself is settled practitioner consensus, not a single source's idiosyncratic framing, even though only the NN/G primary source was fetched in full for this archive.
- Direct usability for this Bee: this taxonomy gives a ready-made two-tier classification scheme (macro conversion actions vs. the two micro-conversion subtypes) that this Bee can apply to whatever conversion actions it detects on the audited site (purchase, lead-form submit, phone call, newsletter signup, account creation, booking, per the PRD's own example list), labeling each detected action as macro, process-milestone-micro, or secondary-action-micro based on how directly it maps to the site's inferred primary business goal. [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]

## 4. Buyer-readiness / funnel-stage models (round-3 addition, partially closes the "no staged readiness model" gap)

- The clearest three-stage buyer-intent model in this archive comes from a conversion-path-audit methodology: awareness stage ("experiencing a problem and looking for information... not choosing a vendor"), consideration stage ("defined their problem and are now actively weighing different types of solutions... engaged and educated, but they aren't ready to buy your specific product yet"), and decision stage ("chosen a solution type and are now evaluating specific vendors... need direct, clear proof points and an easy way to engage with sales"). [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md]
- The same source ties each stage to a distinct conversion-offer type, directly composable with the conversion-action taxonomy in section 3: awareness-stage offers are low-commitment (newsletter sign-up, educational PDF/guide download, content-series subscription, simple assessment tool); consideration-stage offers are higher-value/comparative (comparison guides, in-depth webinars, ROI calculators, case studies); decision-stage offers are high-commitment (consultation requests, demo sign-ups, free trial activations, pricing-page interactions). [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md]
- The source also gives a four-step audit procedure directly analogous to a task this Bee performs: (1) map every existing conversion mechanism on the site to a buyer stage; (2) identify the highest-traffic pages; (3) cross-reference the two lists to find stage/traffic mismatches (its named example: "high traffic going to awareness-stage blog posts that have no relevant conversion path"); (4) validate with behavioral data (A/B testing, heatmaps, session recordings) where available. Step 3's finding, that a stage/offer mismatch is "the single largest opportunity for improving lead capture," is presented as this methodology's central diagnostic output. [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md]
- A parallel, page-level rather than action-level model comes from a TOFU/MOFU/BOFU (top/middle/bottom-of-funnel) framing applied directly to website page types, which is close to how this Bee would need to classify pages rather than individual conversion actions: TOFU pages (homepage, blog/articles, resource hub, campaign landing pages, high-level about page) "welcome new visitors and introduce your value proposition... without pushing for a sale"; MOFU pages (service/solution overviews, case studies, detailed about-us, downloadable resources, events/webinars) are "built for engagement and evaluation... help visitors understand how you compare to alternatives"; BOFU pages (pricing, contact us, book-a-demo, testimonials, FAQs) "support high-intent users... remove barriers, answer final questions, and prompt action." [raw/tbstdigital-com-au-structuring-website-tofu-mofu-bofu.md]
- Cross-source note: TOFU/MOFU/BOFU (three stages) and the awareness/consideration/decision model (also three stages) are effectively the same underlying three-stage funnel described in two different vocabularies by two independent sources, which corroborates the three-stage framing as the dominant practitioner convention. [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md] [raw/tbstdigital-com-au-structuring-website-tofu-mofu-bofu.md]
- Direct gap against the PRD: PRD-005 asks for "a two-stage buyer-readiness model (e.g. awareness-stage visitor vs. decision-stage visitor)." Neither of the two funnel sources in this archive describes a two-stage model; both independently converge on three stages (awareness/consideration/decision, or TOFU/MOFU/BOFU). This archive therefore does not directly validate a two-stage split; the two-stage framing in the PRD reads as a deliberate simplification of the three-stage industry-standard model (collapsing "consideration" into either the awareness or decision side, unstated which), not a variant this research found independently attested anywhere. Flagging this explicitly rather than fabricating a two-stage source: **if `icp-positioning-worker-bee`'s two-stage model needs firmer grounding, it should be built as an explicit collapse of the three-stage awareness/consideration/decision model documented here, with the collapse rule stated in the Bee's own guide, not sourced as if a two-stage model exists natively in the literature.**

## 5. Failure patterns relevant to the focus-undeterminable gate

Both original sources catalog ways an ICP definition fails to be usable; these map onto signals this Bee could use to judge whether a site's focus is determinable at all, though neither source frames it in exactly that way:

| Failure pattern | Description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wishful ICP | Gates copied from a board deck rather than derived from customer data | [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md] |
| Perpetual ICP | A definition never refreshed even as the company's revenue/motion changed materially | [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md] |
| Mono-attribute ICP | A single revenue or headcount band with no industry, geography, technographic, or behavioural overlay | [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md] |
| Describes the average, not the best | Averaging across all customers produces a profile that "matches everyone adequately and nobody precisely" | [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md] |
| Unmeasurable criteria | Terms like "growth-oriented" or "tech-forward" cannot be filtered/scored in any tool | [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md] |
| No exclusion criteria | "A profile without a negative ICP is a wishlist"; real qualification requires knowing who to walk away from | [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md] |
| Lives only in a slide deck | If not embedded in scoring/list-building/qualification workflows, it "does not exist in any operationally meaningful sense" | [raw/hyperspect-ai-blog-icp-definition-framework.md] |
| A counter-example: an ICP with no firmographic gates, no technographic gates, no exclusion criteria, described only as "B2B SaaS companies" | Named consequence: win rate under 8%, elevated churn, the ICP "fails to do any of the work an ICP exists to do" | [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md] |
| Single-mechanism website (round-3 addition) | "Most B2B websites are built with one primary way to convert: a high-commitment contact form, demo request, or sales call... This is lazy strategy, and it's the primary reason inbound programs fail to produce qualified leads" | [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md] |
| Stage/traffic mismatch with no relevant conversion path (round-3 addition) | "High traffic going to awareness-stage blog posts that have no relevant conversion path" is named as the single largest, most common audit finding | [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md] |

Read against this Bee's job: a site whose observable content only supports "unmeasurable" or "mono-attribute" characterizations (e.g. the only determinable fact is a broad vertical with no discernible segment, geography, or buyer signal) remains the closest analogue in this archive to a focus-undeterminable condition. The round-3 addition sharpens this only slightly: a site with a single generic conversion mechanism (e.g. one contact form, no stage-differentiated offers) is evidence of poor site strategy, not evidence that the site's focus/niche is undeterminable, and this Bee should not conflate the two. A "single-mechanism website" still has a determinable niche in most cases; genuine focus-undeterminability (the HARD GATE condition) is closer to "no vertical, no ICP-relevant signal, and no coherent conversion action of any kind is present or inferable," which none of the five sources in this archive names or defines directly.

## 6. Adjacent vocabulary useful for this Bee's terminology

- ICP gate: "a binary filter that an account must pass to enter the eligible pool... [gates] reject rather than discount," distinct from and stricter than a scoring rule. [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- ICP vs. segmentation: "An ICP defines who you serve at all; segmentation slices the in-ICP set into operating cohorts (verticals, sizes, motions)." [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- Anti-ICP signal: "an attribute that automatically disqualifies an account regardless of other fit." [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- ICP vs. TAM: total addressable market is the full category-buyer universe; the ICP narrows it, and "a clean ICP usually produces a serviceable addressable market that is 5 to 25 percent of the TAM." [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- Operational quality metrics for an existing ICP (coverage of closed-won, out-of-ICP win rate, out-of-ICP churn rate, pool size vs. capacity) are all defined against a company's own sales history and do not apply to an outside-in audit of a third party's site. [raw/abmatic-ai-blog-what-is-an-ideal-customer-profile.md]
- Macro conversion / micro conversion / process-milestone / secondary-action: see section 3; these four terms, sourced from NN/G, are the recommended vocabulary for this Bee's conversion-action taxonomy output. [raw/nngroup-com-macro-vs-micro-conversions.md]
- Awareness / consideration / decision stage, and the TOFU/MOFU/BOFU synonym set: see section 4; these are the recommended vocabulary for the three-stage funnel model this archive actually supports, pending the Bee's own explicit two-stage collapse rule. [raw/321webmarketing-com-audit-conversion-paths-buyer-stage.md] [raw/tbstdigital-com-au-structuring-website-tofu-mofu-bofu.md]

## 7. Coverage gaps in this archive

- Neither original source discussed a conversion-action taxonomy; this gap is now substantially closed by section 3 (NN/G's macro/micro/process-milestone/secondary-action framework).
- Neither original source described a staged buyer-readiness model; this gap is now substantially closed by section 4 for a three-stage model (awareness/consideration/decision, equivalently TOFU/MOFU/BOFU), but the PRD's specific "two-stage" framing remains unattested anywhere in this archive and should be treated as this Bee's own explicit simplification of the three-stage model, not as independently sourced.
- No source in this archive addresses inferring firmographic/technographic/behavioral ICP attributes from a website's own public content (copy, structure, integrations visible in the page source) rather than from a CRM; this Bee's actual working method for the firmographic/technographic/behavioral sections of an ICP (as opposed to the conversion-taxonomy and funnel-stage sections, now covered) is still not grounded in this archive.
- No source discusses a hard-stop/halt-and-ask design pattern for an undeterminable classification by name. Section 5's round-3 addition (single-mechanism website, stage/traffic mismatch) sharpens what "poor site strategy" looks like but does not define or name a "focus cannot be determined" condition; that condition remains this Bee's own construct, not sourced.
- Remaining thin-coverage caveat: even after the round-3 deeper pass, this Stinger's distillation draws on five raw sources, four of them vendor-blog tier and one (nngroup.com) an official UX-research-organization source treated as the archive's highest-authority citation. Two of the five (abmatic.ai, hyperspect.ai) are written for a company profiling its own customer base rather than for an outside audit of a third party, and their six-step/reverse-engineer methodologies (section 2) remain not directly usable by this Bee. Treat any inference beyond what is directly quoted or tabled above as unsupported by this archive.
