# Distilled audit-intake research

Dense reference distilled from `raw/`. This Stinger's raw archive now holds five sources: the original two generic agency "client discovery process" how-to blogs (agencypro.app and clariva.io), plus three sources added in a deeper round-3 research pass targeting the gaps flagged after round 2: a website-redesign intake-form template (revamp.dev), a 32-question web-design client questionnaire (ybug.io), and a Claude-skill README describing folder-scaffolding conventions for an AI-runnable agency workspace (github.com/krsnczky/agency-icm-builder). Every claim below cites its source file in brackets. Organized around what a first-Bee intake step actually needs: why a structured intake matters, what a workable intake form/question set covers, field taxonomies specific to website work, folder/workspace scaffolding conventions, stakeholder identity capture, and where an authorization/consent step would normally sit in a generic discovery framework, contrasted against this plugin's explicit design choice to omit that step. Nothing below is padded past what these five sources state; where the sources are silent on a topic relevant to `audit-intake-worker-bee`, that is called out as a gap rather than filled from outside knowledge.

Research window: two sweeps, 2026-08-18 (round 2 initial two sources) and 2026-08-18 (round 3 deeper pass, three additional sources).

## 1. Why a structured intake step matters

- Both original sources treat unstructured or skipped discovery as the primary driver of downstream engagement failure, not delivery quality. A post-mortem case in one source traced an eight-week misalignment (wrong stakeholder, wrong success criteria) to a 45-minute kickoff call run with no pre-work, no documented findings, and no stakeholder map. [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- Citing PMI's Pulse of the Profession and McKinsey research, the same source frames discovery investment as "the single highest-leverage intervention on engagement margin," and gives a rough ratio: "a 30-minute investment in discovery prevents 30 hours of rework downstream." [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- The second original source frames it as a completeness/exit-condition problem rather than a time-investment problem: discovery is "complete" only when three defined outputs exist (a complete brief covering all eight of its stated coverage dimensions, a documented risk map, and a signed scope), and is "ongoing" until then regardless of whether the agency treats it that way. [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- The round-3 questionnaire source frames the same problem in outcome terms specific to web design work: "A web project without a proper brief is a project without a map. Designers make assumptions. Clients have unstated expectations. The gap between them shows up in revision round three," and lists four concrete benefits of a completed questionnaire: an accurate proposal basis, better-informed client reviewers, protection against scope creep, and fewer revision rounds. [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md]
- Named discovery gaps and their downstream symptom, per one source's table: missing decision-maker in early conversations leads to late scope changes; no documented success criteria leads to "what we agreed to" disputes; unstated brand/compliance constraints lead to late-stage rework; vague timeline expectations lead to pressure to compress mid-project. [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- Named discovery-gap failure patterns from the second original source: an undefined budget surfacing only at invoice time, a launch timeline set without a matching content-delivery commitment, and an approval chain ("Marketing team") that omitted the actual final approver (a CEO), discovered only at final review. [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- Source-authority note: all four narrative sources here (agencypro.app, clariva.io, revamp.dev, ybug.io) are vendor or vendor/community blogs of roughly equivalent tier; none is more authoritative than another on the general "discovery matters" claim, and their framings are complementary (time-cost argument, completeness-gate argument, revision-round argument) rather than contradictory. [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md] [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md] [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md] [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md]

## 2. Multiple structured-discovery models (sources do not converge on one framework)

The sources describe different phase/stage breakdowns for the same underlying discipline. All are stated here rather than reconciled into one, since none is more authoritative per its source_type (vendor blog / vendor-community blog, essentially the same tier):

| Source | Framework | Stages | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| agencypro.app | Four-phase discovery framework | 1. Intake (pre-call form); 2. Stakeholder mapping; 3. Kickoff call (fixed 90-minute agenda); 4. Synthesis and confirmation (discovery document, scope draft, client sign-off) | [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md] |
| clariva.io | Four-stage discovery process | 1. Intake (viability check: budget, timeline, deliverables, stakeholders, technical constraints); 2. Questionnaire (deeper brand/competitive context, sent only after intake confirms viability); 3. Brief review (checked against an eight-dimension completeness standard); 4. Risk check and sign-off (every undocumented assumption flagged and resolved before both parties sign) | [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md] |
| revamp.dev | Seven-step intake-to-handoff framework, specific to website redesign | 1. Set the frame (before the form); 2. Copy/paste the intake form; 3. Validate the "unknowns" before doing design work; 4. Translate the intake into a scope draft; 5. Generate a redesign demo from the existing site; 6. Run a structured review; 7. Confirm implementation path | [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md] |

Both original sources agree that intake is phase/stage 1 and precedes any deeper conversation, and both treat an unresolved gap as something that must be surfaced explicitly rather than assumed away. The round-3 revamp.dev source adds a distinct principle relevant to a single-pass intake design: "If they can't answer parts of the form, turn those into explicit open questions. Decide what you'll assume vs what you'll wait on" [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md], which validates this Bee's simpler model (four required questions, no open-ended unknowns tolerated for those four) as one defensible design point on a spectrum, not the only one. Neither clariva.io's eight "coverage dimensions" nor the ybug.io questionnaire's full 32-question list were captured in full in the archived excerpts (both fetches were cut off), so those two enumerations cannot be distilled past what is quoted below. [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md] [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md]

## 3. What a workable intake form/question set covers

- One original source lists eight intake-form sections, completed by the client in 20-35 minutes: company basics (name, industry, size, revenue band, primary product/customer); project context (service needed, why now, primary goal, budget range, timeline); current state (what's being done today, tools/platforms in use); stakeholders (primary contact, decision-maker if different, other parties, approval process); previous attempts (prior agency experience); success definition (90-day/6-month/12-month metrics); access and prerequisites (which systems need access, confirmed lead time); communication preferences (channels, cadence, response expectations). [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- The stakeholders section is directly relevant to identifying an "audited-party contact name" and "audited-party business name": the source's stakeholder-mapping exercise is a four-role table (decision-maker, influencer, executor, end user), each with an authority description and a recommended engagement level at kickoff. One 14-person agency's adoption of this mapping found the contract signer was NOT the direction decision-maker in 30% of engagements. [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- The other original source's minimal viability-check intake fields are narrower: budget range, timeline, deliverables, stakeholders, and technical constraints, explicitly scoped to "confirm the project is viable" rather than to understand it fully. [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md]

### 3a. Website-specific field taxonomy (round-3 addition, closes the earlier "no website-audit-specific intake mechanics" gap for the field-content half, not the workspace-mechanics half)

- The revamp.dev intake template is organized into eleven lettered sections (A through K) specific to website work, giving the closest available grounding in this archive for a website-focused intake field taxonomy: (A) project basics, including company/org name, primary contact (name, role, email), other stakeholders, final approver, current website URL(s), and subdomains/separate properties that matter; (B) goals and priorities, including primary action visitors should take (the "main CTA") and secondary actions; (C) audience and context; (D) brand and creative inputs; (E) pages and information architecture; (F) content readiness; (G) functionality and integrations (forms, CRM, email marketing, scheduling, payments, search/localization/auth); (H) platform, access, and technical constraints, including who controls domain/DNS and which access types can be provided (CMS admin, hosting, domain/DNS, analytics, tag manager, search console, code repo); (I) SEO and measurement; (J) reviews, approvals, and timeline; (K) deliverables and handoff. [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md]
- Section (A)'s explicit fields, "Company / org name," "Primary contact (name, role, email)," "Who is the final approver / sign-off owner?," and "Current website URL(s)," are the tightest match in this archive to this Bee's exact four required fields (auditor name, audited-party contact name, audited-party business name, website URL): every one of this Bee's four fields corresponds to a named field in this template's project-basics section, which increases confidence that the four-question minimal design is a genuine subset of an established field taxonomy rather than an arbitrary simplification. [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md]
- The ybug.io questionnaire (32 questions across business/goals, audience/competitors, design/brand, and, per its own table of contents though not captured in the archived excerpt, technical/content and project-logistics sections) opens with the same "primary goal of this website" and "who has final approval authority" questions as revamp.dev's template, corroborating those two fields as the two most load-bearing pieces of intake data across independent sources. [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md] The article's own key takeaway states this directly: "Always ask who has final approval authority. The answer determines whether your review cycles take days or weeks." [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md]
- Gap, still open: none of the five sources is written for a single-session, four-question, no-open-questions-tolerated intake model. All five describe multi-field, multi-session intake forms or questionnaires for a full agency engagement (ranging from revamp.dev's ~11-section form to ybug.io's 32 questions). The mapping in this section is the closest available grounding for the fields that matter (contact identity, business identity, URL, final-approver identity), not a source that validates a minimal four-question design as sufficient on its own; it does, however, confirm that all four of this Bee's fields map onto fields that independently-authored, more elaborate intake templates also treat as essential.

## 4. Workspace/folder scaffolding conventions for agency deliverables (round-3 addition, closes the "no website-audit-specific... workspace scaffolding" gap in part)

- The github.com/krsnczky/agency-icm-builder README describes a Claude-skill-packaged convention for scaffolding an "AI-runnable workspace for a client-service agency," where "folder structure does the orchestration." The core claim: "for sequential, human-reviewed client work, the filesystem is the framework," attributed to the term ICM (Interpretable Context Methodology) from an arXiv paper (arXiv:2603.16021) the skill's author credits but did not independently reproduce beyond the citation. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- The skeleton it scaffolds has a top-level routing file that "holds no content itself" (`CLAUDE.md`), a `clients/` directory with a `_template/` subfolder that new clients are created by copying, stable "departments" and "workflows" directories, and a `system/` directory holding the load-order contract and capture rules. Each client folder further splits into a `wiki/` (current-state notes plus historical log), a `memory/` (distilled learnings), and a `work/` (deliverables) subfolder. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- Four stated "core guarantees" of this convention, useful as design principles for any AI-agent-run workspace scaffold even though this source describes a different (ongoing multi-client agency) context than a single-engagement website audit: clients never mix (uncertain attribution goes to a quarantine inbox rather than a guessed folder); load per task, not per session (an explicit contract states which files each task type needs, and the agent stops after loading those); sessions end in capture (a log entry and state update are written back automatically at session end); state is inspectable by a human without any dashboard or vendor tool. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- Relevance and limits: this source validates the general pattern this Bee already follows (copy a template/scaffold structure at engagement start, keep a manifest/ledger file a human or later Bee can inspect, keep per-engagement state isolated) as an established convention in adjacent AI-agent-workspace tooling, not merely an invention specific to this plugin. It does not, however, describe website-audit-specific folder naming (e.g. a `www.<domain>-audit/` convention) or template-hydration mechanics (populating placeholder tokens in downstream report/XLSX templates at scaffold time); those remain ungrounded in this archive. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- Source-authority note: this is a community post (a single-maintainer, 3-star GitHub repository), the weakest-tier source type in this archive; treat its "core claim" and "core guarantees" as one practitioner's documented convention, not an industry standard, and weight it accordingly against the four vendor/vendor-community blog sources above.

## 5. Authorization/consent: not a step this Bee performs, by design

Per PRD-002 non-goals (explicit user instruction), `audit-intake-worker-bee` never records or verifies authorization to audit the target site. Checking what the raw sources actually say about consent/authorization-adjacent steps in a generic discovery process:

- Neither original source describes anything resembling "verify authorization to audit/scan the target's website." The closest analogues in the raw archive are about SCOPE sign-off, not audit-permission sign-off: agencypro's phase 4 output includes "client sign-off" on the discovery document/scope draft [raw/agencypro-app-blog-client-discovery-process.md], and clariva's stage 4 ("Risk check and sign-off") describes getting "sign-off from both parties on the brief as written," which is what "makes the scope enforceable." [raw/www-clariva-io-blog-client-discovery-process.md]
- The round-3 sources add nothing new on this point. revamp.dev's step 3 ("Validate the 'unknowns' before doing any design work") and step 7 ("Confirm implementation path") are scope-and-feasibility checks, not audit-authorization checks. [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md] ybug.io's questionnaire likewise asks about final approval authority for design decisions, not permission to analyze the site. [raw/ybug-io-blog-web-design-client-questionnaire.md] The agency-icm-builder workspace-scaffolding source does not address authorization at all; it is a folder-convention document, not a discovery-process document. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- Both of the original sign-off steps concern agreeing to a scope of paid work between an agency and its own client, not permission to run an external audit against a third party's live website. None of the five sources' frameworks maps cleanly onto a security/scanning-authorization concept.
- Given that, this distilled research does not surface any generic-discovery "authorization step" that PRD-002 is deliberately cutting; the nearest analogue (scope/brief/approval sign-off) is a different concern than site-audit authorization. This plugin's explicit design choice, per PRD-002, is that `audit-intake-worker-bee` does not add an authorization-capture step even though a fuller discovery framework (per all five sources) would end its intake/kickoff phases with some form of mutual sign-off or approval. That omission is intentional and should not be read as an oversight to fix.

## 6. Coverage gaps in this archive

- No source in this archive discusses URL validation mechanics (e.g. normalizing a submitted URL to a canonical domain, handling redirects at intake time) or template-hydration mechanics (writing intake answers into placeholder tokens inside downstream report/XLSX templates at scaffold time). The revamp.dev source treats the URL as a given input to an external tool, not as a field with its own validation rules; the agency-icm-builder source describes copying a folder template but not hydrating placeholder tokens within files. [raw/revamp-dev-website-redesign-client-intake-form-template.md] [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- No source discusses resuming an interrupted intake against an already-scaffolded workspace, which is one of this Bee's stated invocation triggers. The agency-icm-builder source's "sessions end in capture" principle is adjacent (it implies a workspace should always be in an inspectable, resumable state) but does not describe a resume-detection mechanism comparable to this Bee's `run-ledger.json` check. [raw/github-krsnczky-agency-icm-builder.md]
- No source discusses a `www.<domain>-audit/` style engagement-folder naming convention specifically, or a single-engagement (as opposed to ongoing multi-client) workspace shape. The agency-icm-builder scaffold is built for an agency running many concurrent, ongoing client relationships, not a single bounded audit run; its `clients/<slug>/` shape is the closest available analogue to this Bee's `www.<domain>-audit/` shape, but the mapping is approximate, not a direct precedent.
- Neither clariva.io's eight "coverage dimensions" nor the full content of ybug.io's technical/content and project-logistics questionnaire sections were captured in the archived fetches (both were cut off mid-article); treat any claim about the specific content of those unfetched sections as unsupported by this archive.
- Remaining thin-coverage caveat: even after the round-3 deeper pass, this Stinger's distillation draws on five raw sources of source-type tier vendor blog, vendor/community blog, or community post, none of which is an official/primary standard for either discovery process design or AI-agent workspace conventions. Treat any inference beyond what is directly quoted or tabled above as unsupported by this archive.
