# Distilled audit-reporting research

Dense reference distilled from `raw/`. Every claim below cites its source file in brackets. Organized around this Bee's actual job: rendering the scored findings register (produced upstream by `audit-scoring-worker-bee`) into a customer-facing and auditor-facing report, Markdown plus styled HTML, in wave W8, the final step of every audit run. This is a rendering step, not an analysis step: nothing here licenses inventing a finding, a number, or a severity that is not already in the findings register.

This is the round-3 rewrite of this file. Coverage was flagged thin after round 2 (2 vendor-blog sources only). This pass adds 3 sources: one professional-standards-body PDF (official docs, from The Institute of Internal Auditors), one detailed practitioner blog on security-audit-report structure, and one open-source tool's own documentation for Markdown-to-branded-HTML rendering. 5 raw sources now exist total; still no primary source specific to THIS plugin's exact deliverable shape (a website/SEO/security audit rendered as both Markdown and styled HTML with subtle agency branding, in one pass). Gaps that remain after this pass are stated explicitly in section 9 rather than filled by invention.

Research window: round 1/2 sweep, 2026-08-18. Round 3 deeper pass, also 2026-08-18 (same day, later sweep).

## 1. The two (or more) audiences every audit report must serve

- Three independent sources, two vendor blogs and one practitioner/community blog, converge on the same split: a decision-maker/budget-holder audience that will only ever read the front matter, and a technical implementer audience that needs precise, unambiguous detail to act. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md] [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md] [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- Framing for the technical audience: findings need exact identifiers and explicit fix instructions, not vague description. The cited contrast: "some pages have canonical issues" is not actionable, versus naming the exact count of affected pages, the specific misconfiguration, example URLs, and the corrected format. [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md] ForensicSpot's version of the same point, phrased for the finding's issue statement specifically: open with a declarative sentence naming the gap without hedging ("Multi-factor authentication is not enforced on the organisation's VPN gateway") rather than a passive, hedged formulation ("It was noted that multi-factor authentication may not be in use") that obscures accountability and urgency. [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- Framing for the non-technical audience: a finding is only useful once translated into business cost. The cited example: a client does not care that an X-Frame-Options header is missing, they care that a competitor could embed the site in a fake page and steal customer data. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md] ForensicSpot frames the same requirement from the report-writer's side: business readers need to understand the business consequence "without decoding technical specifics," and a report written for only one audience fails the other. [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- A third, more formal framing of the same split comes from The Institute of Internal Auditors' (IIA) own practice-guide-derived toolkit: internal audit Standard 2420 ("Quality of Communications") requires content and detail to be "determined by the needs of the audience," and it may be "deemed appropriate to use different formats/versions that are customized for the audience" - explicitly naming the practice of producing a condensed, executive-summary-only version for board members alongside the full detailed report for others. [raw/theiia-org-globalassets-site-auditing-report-writing-toolkit.md] This IIA source is written for internal-audit engagement reports (financial/operational/compliance audits inside an organization), not website/technical audits, so its numbered Standards citations (2420, 2430, 2440, 2410.A1) are internal-audit-profession-specific and should not be read as generally applicable; the dual-audience/condensed-version framing itself is the transferable part.
- Practical device for bridging the two audiences in one document, corroborated by all sources that address it: put a short, skimmable front section for leadership and a full technical appendix/detail section for the implementer in the SAME document, so neither audience needs a separate deliverable. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md] [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md] [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]

## 2. Recommended report structure

Four sources independently propose a front-to-back structure that moves from summary to technical detail. They are not identical in section naming (general "present a technical report" advice, SEO-audit-specific naming, security-audit-specific naming, and internal-audit-standard naming), but they agree on the shape: executive summary first, categorized/risk-grouped findings in the middle, a prioritized action list, and a technical appendix last.

| Section | sitegrade version | sitemapfixer (SEO-audit-specific) version | ForensicSpot (security-audit-specific) version | IIA (internal-audit-standard) version |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive summary | 1 page: overall site health score, top three priorities, estimated cost of inaction. "The only page most decision-makers will read." | Roughly 300-400 words plus one visual: traffic trend, overall health score, top three critical issues, estimated traffic impact, recommended priority order. | Max 2 pages: audit period and scope in one sentence, overall posture in 2-3 sentences, critical/high findings by title only, most important required action. Written last, placed first. | Introduction/objectives/scope/results, conclusions, summary of significant observations, concerns encountered with management. No technical jargon or methodology detail. |
| Risk / findings body | Risk overview (1-2 pages): color-coded findings, red/yellow/green, grouped by business area (security, speed, legal compliance) rather than by technical category. | Split by domain: Technical SEO Findings, On-Page SEO Findings, Off-Page and Authority Findings, each following the same per-issue format (describe issue, explain impact, list affected URLs/scope, state fix). | "Summary of findings" (all findings with severity + status, for management/technical leads) then "Detailed findings" (per-finding: issue, evidence, rating, impact, recommendation, for remediation owners). Findings framed as the unit of communication between auditor and auditee. | Observations/findings listed in order of significance, grouped by activity if applicable: title and reference, criticality rating, statement of facts (condition/criteria/cause/effect), recommendation, management's action plan (owner + target date). |
| Prioritized action | Recommendations (1 page): prioritized action items with estimated cost and timeline, quick wins first, then medium-term, then long-term. | Priority Matrix: all findings bucketed P1 through P4 with owner assignments, effort estimates, business-impact notes. | Folded into "Management responses": owner, acceptance/rejection, remediation date per finding - creates accountability and a follow-up basis. | Management's action plan per observation (corrective action, activity owner, target date), plus an overall "engagement rating" (satisfactory/marginal/unsatisfactory/pass/fail). |
| Technical detail | Technical appendix: headers, configurations, code snippets. Client will never read it, their IT person will. | Data Appendix: full URL lists, crawl exports, screenshot evidence, raw data tables. Needs no narrative explanation, serves the technical team only. | Appendices: evidence artefacts, tool outputs, control mapping tables, for technical teams and follow-up auditors. Also has a distinct "Scope and methodology" section (systems in scope, test dates, frameworks, constraints) that the other three sources fold into the executive summary or omit. | Distribution list as its own report component; "Background" (synopsis of the audited activity/process) as its own section, separate from scope. |

All four sources agree on the underlying shape (summary front, detail back) but none carries more authority than another for THIS plugin's specific deliverable: sitegrade and sitemapfixer are vendor blogs, ForensicSpot is a vendor/community educational blog, and the IIA toolkit, while an official professional-standards-body document, is written for a different audit discipline (internal/financial audit) than this plugin's website audits. Where they differ on section naming or granularity, all readings are given above rather than picking one as canonical. ForensicSpot's addition of a distinct "Scope and methodology" section and a formal "Management responses" section (rather than folding action items only into a priority matrix) is a structural point the round-2 sources did not surface, and is worth considering as an addition to this Bee's rendered structure if the upstream findings register carries scope/methodology metadata to render.

## 3. Executive summary: what it must contain

- sitemapfixer gives the most granular executive-summary checklist found in this research: (1) a traffic trend with a benchmark comparison, year-over-year or against industry average, ideally sourced from Google Search Console; (2) an overall health score on a simple 0-100 scale or letter grade, with a one-line footnote on methodology so it does not look arbitrary, and consistent methodology run-over-run so progress is measurable; (3) the top three critical issues only, each with a one-sentence reason it matters and a link to its full section, explicitly capped at three because "longer lists dilute urgency"; (4) an estimated traffic/business impact, which does not need to be a precise forecast, a directional range statement is described as sufficient and more persuasive than a bare list of technical errors; (5) a recommended priority order referencing the same P1 to P4 tiers used in the full priority matrix. [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md]
- sitegrade's version of the same section is coarser (score, top three priorities, cost of inaction) but agrees on the one-page constraint and on "this is the only page most decision-makers read" as the reason it has to carry the full argument on its own. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md]
- ForensicSpot adds two constraints not present in the round-2 sources: the executive summary must be self-contained ("a reader who reads only the summary should understand the key risks and what must be done") and it is written LAST, after all findings are graded and verified, but placed FIRST in the final document - an explicit statement of drafting order versus presentation order that the other sources leave implicit. [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- The IIA toolkit adds a content constraint the vendor-blog sources do not state as explicitly: the executive summary "should not contain technical jargon and internal audit methodologies" at all - not just "translate" jargon (sitegrade's framing) but exclude it outright, deferring anything requiring technical explanation to the detailed report. It also names an item none of the other sources mention: management concerns, specifically situations where "management has accepted a level of risk that may be unacceptable to the organization," and repeat/carried-forward observations from a previous audit with a note on whether prior action plans were completed - directly relevant to this plugin's re-audit-over-time use case (see the health-score consistency point below). [raw/theiia-org-globalassets-site-auditing-report-writing-toolkit.md]
- Consistency point that applies directly to this Bee: since this plugin re-runs audits on the same site over time, three separate sources now converge on a FIXED, repeatable scoring methodology (so scores/ratings are comparable audit over audit) as a structural requirement, not just a nicety: sitemapfixer's health-score consistency point, ForensicSpot's severity-rating-derived-from-a-matrix framing (Critical/High/Medium/Low/Informational, justified not merely asserted), and the IIA toolkit's explicit instruction to show "the trend (positive or negative) compared to prior audits of the same activity" and flag repeat observations. [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md] [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md] [raw/theiia-org-globalassets-site-auditing-report-writing-toolkit.md]

## 4. Per-finding format for the technical body

- sitemapfixer's (SEO-audit-specific) format: describe the issue, explain its impact, list the specific affected URLs or scope, and state the recommended fix with enough detail that an implementer does not need a follow-up consultation. [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md]
- ForensicSpot's (security-audit-specific) format is more granular and names five required parts per finding rather than four: an issue statement (declarative, unhedged), evidence (specific, reproducible artefacts - example given: a screenshot), a risk rating (Critical/High/Medium/Low/Informational, derived from a likelihood-by-impact matrix and justified in the finding, not merely asserted), an impact description, and a recommendation. It also introduces vocabulary this Bee's findings register may want to distinguish: a "finding" (evidence-backed, violates a specific control requirement) versus an "observation" (a noted issue or improvement opportunity that lacks sufficient evidence or does not violate a specific requirement, and which carries less remediation weight, typically placed in an appendix or separate section rather than the main findings body). [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- The IIA toolkit's format for an "observation" entry, closest to a finding-write-up template of any source here: title and reference; criticality rating; statement of facts using the classic condition/criteria/cause/effect structure; recommendation; and management's action plan (owner + target date) as part of the SAME entry rather than a separate section - differing from ForensicSpot's convention of a separate "management responses" section keyed by finding. Both conventions (inline owner/date vs. separate response section) are legitimate per their respective sources; this Bee should pick whichever the findings register's own data shape supports, since neither source is more authoritative than the other for a website-audit context. [raw/theiia-org-globalassets-site-auditing-report-writing-toolkit.md] [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- Framework-specific report VARIANTS exist and are named explicitly by ForensicSpot as producing different finding vocabulary for the same underlying communication problem: an ISO 27001 report uses "nonconformity" (major/minor); a SOC 2 Type II report is an opinion letter describing "exception items"; a GDPR Article 28 processor audit uses a compliance-checklist-plus-narrative shape; a penetration-test report adds proof-of-concept steps. None of these vocabularies apply directly to this plugin's website-audit output, but the underlying principle they all serve, translating technical/procedural observations into justified, evidence-backed, prioritized statements that drive action, is the transferable point. [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- sitemapfixer's technical-findings taxonomy (crawl coverage/indexation, Core Web Vitals, crawl errors, redirect chains, canonical configuration, hreflang, sitemap health for the "Technical" subsection; title tags, meta descriptions, H1 usage, internal linking, structured data, image optimization for "On-Page"; domain rating, referring domains, backlink quality, competitor authority gap for "Off-Page") is specific to SEO audits and is included here only as an illustration of the level of granularity a per-finding writeup should reach, not as this plugin's own finding taxonomy, since this Bee renders whatever categories the upstream findings register already defines. [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md]

## 5. Translating findings into business language

- The recommended device is analogy: "your front door is unlocked" for missing security headers, "you are sending customer mail on postcards instead of sealed envelopes" for no SSL certificate, "your shop has a revolving door that takes 8 seconds to open" for slow load times. Framed explicitly as translation of expertise into business-decision language, not condescension. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md]
- Cost framing is not optional decoration: findings without a price attached are called "just a wish list." The recommendation is to give at least a range per recommendation, and to separate quick wins (an example threshold given is under 500 EUR) from major projects (an example threshold given is 5,000+ EUR), which the source frames as turning the report into a proposal rather than a report. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md] This is one source's specific pricing convention (a single vendor blog, not corroborated by any other source in this cluster, including the three added in this pass), so treat the exact EUR thresholds as illustrative of the pattern (cheap/quick versus expensive/major) rather than as a number to reproduce literally.
- ForensicSpot's version of the same requirement, from a security-audit angle: the executive summary should "translate findings into business risk terms (regulatory exposure, financial loss, reputational damage)... without requiring the reader to understand the underlying technical mechanism," and should prioritize by severity rather than by discovery order. This corroborates sitegrade's core point (cost/consequence framing, not raw technical severity, drives executive attention) from an independent source, though ForensicSpot does not give pricing conventions. [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]

## 6. Delivery format and length

- Total document length guidance: keep the executive summary to one page (sitegrade) or roughly 300-400 words (sitemapfixer) or max 2 pages (ForensicSpot); the full report including technical appendix can run 10-20 pages (sitegrade) or 10-30 pages for internal engagements per the IIA-adjacent CLIMB-style convention referenced inside the IIA toolkit's broader practice guide, provided it is structured so a client only needs to read the first few sections. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md] [raw/sitemapfixer-com-learn-seo-audit-report.md] [raw/forensicspot-com-topics-information-security-audit-and-compliance-audit-report-structure-and-findings.md]
- Presentation mode: a live walkthrough (screen-share) is described as beating a PDF sent by email, walking the executive summary, showing two or three critical findings with visual evidence, and ending on the action plan, in about twenty minutes, with the full written report left as a reference document. This is a delivery-format recommendation from a single vendor source (sitegrade) and is not addressed by any of the other four sources. [raw/sitegrade-io-en-quick-answers-how-to-present-technical-report-non-technical-client.md]
- Distribution and confidentiality: the IIA toolkit is the only source in this cluster to address WHO a report should be sent to and under what constraint. Internal audit reports "may be considered confidential documents" with distribution "on a need-to-know basis"; different formats may be created per recipient (e.g. board members receiving only the executive summary rather than the full report); and before releasing results to parties OUTSIDE the organization, the responsible party must assess risk, consult senior management/legal counsel, and control dissemination. This maps loosely onto this plugin's customer-facing vs. auditor-facing split (two audiences, two documents, differing detail level per audience) but the IIA's specific external-disclosure control process is internal-audit-governance-specific and not directly applicable to a website-audit deliverable produced for a paying client who IS the intended external recipient. [raw/theiia-org-globalassets-site-auditing-report-writing-toolkit.md]

## 7. Markdown-to-branded-HTML rendering: templating approaches

This section is new in this pass; round 2 found no source addressing this at all. One source now covers it directly, an open-source npm/MCP tool ("mcp-md-html-pdf") whose own documentation was archived as the closest available example of a working Markdown-to-branded-HTML-document pipeline.

- Mechanism: a brand definition (`brand.json`: name, tagline, web/email, logo path, icon path, a small fixed color palette of `primary`/`accent`/`muted`/`surface`, a heading font stack and a body font stack, a named style preset, and footer options) is merged with Markdown/HTML content to produce a single self-contained HTML file (roughly 10 KB, described as opening on any device, sendable via WhatsApp/email/Slack, and printable to A4 with proper margins/page breaks). The logo is base64-embedded so the output file has no external dependencies. [raw/npmjs-com-package-mcp-md-html-pdf.md]
- The brand-override layering model is directly relevant to this plugin's "one brand, many reports" situation: overrides can be set at four levels, from highest to lowest priority: inline per-call overrides, a path to a different brand file, an environment-variable default brand, and a built-in generic fallback. This lets a single default brand (Legion Code Inc.'s own subtle branding) apply automatically to every report while still allowing a specific field (e.g. accent color) to be overridden per client/document if ever needed. [raw/npmjs-com-package-mcp-md-html-pdf.md]
- Style presets are described as changing "layout personality" (typography, spacing, density) while never overriding the brand's own colors or fonts - a separation of concerns (brand identity vs. document layout mood) worth carrying into this Bee's own template design even if this exact tool is not adopted. The preset named "technical" ("data-dense, monospace accents... Analyses, audits, API docs") and "executive" ("authoritative, accent lines... Board reports, C-level") map closely onto this Bee's two report types (auditor-facing technical vs. customer-facing executive). [raw/npmjs-com-package-mcp-md-html-pdf.md]
- This is a single, low-adoption (4 weekly npm downloads at fetch time) open-source tool's own documentation, not an industry-standard framework or a widely-used library; the specific tool need not be adopted, but its brand.json shape (a small named palette, a footer note field, a header logo, two font stacks, a style preset) is a reasonable, minimal reference shape for what "a brand definition" needs to contain for this kind of document, and no other source in this cluster offers a comparably concrete shape. Treat it as one workable pattern, not a spec.

## 8. Subtle agency-branding conventions in client deliverables

- The one source that speaks to branded-document mechanics at all (mcp-md-html-pdf) provides the KNOBS for branding (logo, a 4-color palette, two font stacks, a footer with an optional contact line and an optional short note such as "Confidential") but does not provide GUIDANCE on subtlety or restraint - it is a configuration reference, not a design-taste document. Its footer.note example given is "Confidential," not a credit line or watermark example, so it does not directly demonstrate the "footer credit line" or "watermarking" conventions this research pass was tasked to find. [raw/npmjs-com-package-mcp-md-html-pdf.md]
- No source in this cluster (5 raw files, across two research passes) provides guidance specifically on how to brand a client-facing deliverable SUBTLY, i.e., conventions for a footer credit line ("Prepared by Legion Code Inc." or similar), watermarking, or styling that signals the originating agency without the document reading as self-promotional or "garish." This is stated as an explicit, still-open gap rather than filled by invention: none of the five sources discuss restraint, subtlety, or the tension between agency-attribution and client-ownership of a deliverable. If this Bee needs a concrete subtle-branding pattern, the closest usable material is the mcp-md-html-pdf brand.json shape's `footer.showContact` (boolean toggle for whether web/email appear at all) and `footer.note` (a single short line) fields, used minimally, e.g. a footer line reading only the agency name and no logo/color emphasis beyond what the rest of the document already carries, rather than a dedicated brand section, but this is an inference from the tool's field shape, not a sourced recommendation.
