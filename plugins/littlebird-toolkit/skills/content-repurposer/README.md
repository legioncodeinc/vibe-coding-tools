# content-repurposer

Turns one long piece into a week of derivatives that each take a different angle, so the person following you on two surfaces does not read the same idea five times.

## What it does

You have one good artifact: a long post, a webinar, a call that went deep. There is a week of content in it and you do not want to spend the week digging it out. The skill produces a content pack: 5 to 7 short posts that each stand alone, plus a carousel, an email, a thread, a sequencing plan and a do-not-publish list.

The craft problem it solves is that most repurposing is one idea restated five times. To anyone following you on two surfaces that reads as spam, and that reader is the one you most want to keep. The fix is the angle taxonomy: seven angles, assigned before drafting and never chosen from format. The claim, the counter-example, the how, the story behind it, the objection, the specific number, the contrarian read.

Two tests make the variety auditable rather than asserted. The claim test: write each piece's claim as one flat sentence, and if two are paraphrases, one gets re-angled. The disagreement test: could a reader agree with one piece and disagree with another? If not, they are one piece in two costumes.

## When to use it

- The webinar is done and you want a week out of it.
- You wrote something that landed and you want more surfaces from it.

Just ask for it. Trigger phrases include "repurpose this post", "turn this into a week of content", "make content from this call", "break this down for LinkedIn", "I need a content pack".

## Run it on a routine, or on demand

| Mode | Cadence | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| On demand | Whenever you have a source | The full pack: screened, angled, drafted, sequenced |
| Nomination routine | Weekly, Monday 09:00 local | Names the artifact from your last 7 days most worth expanding, counts the angles it supports |

On demand is primary. The routine only nominates, because a pack needs a confidentiality screen, an angle audit and approval on the text, and a routine cannot hold an approval gate open. The skill sets it up: it shows you the prompt and schedule, you approve, it creates it.

## What you get

One file, `content-pack-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, dated to the first send day. The do-not-publish list is section two. Then the angle assignment table, which makes the variety checkable: piece, surface, angle, claim sentence, and the piece it passes the disagreement test against.

Then the drafts. Each carries its angle, full text, character count against the ceiling, and its exact first 140 characters, because on LinkedIn the ceiling is 3,000 and the fold is around 140. The email ships with subject and preview text as separate lines. Then the sequencing plan, one reason per slot, with a slot held for an objection from the pack's comments.

## What it needs

- The Littlebird MCP on a Power or Pro plan. Paste the source in and it runs without retrieval.
- A personal voice skill, optional but load-bearing, since this publishes under your name. Without one the skill says so before drafting.
- A source that honestly supports four of the seven angles.

## Limits worth knowing

If the source supports only three angles, it says so and offers a smaller pack or a second source. Two or fewer and it will not proceed. It does not pad, because an invented counter-example ships under your name.

No source in the archive measures whether repurposed content outperforms originals, so the skill states no lift, no reach claim and no traffic multiple, and never says the algorithm rewards anything. Captured engagement is reported as partial, and it never writes "your best-performing post".

It drafts and holds. Nothing is posted, scheduled or written into a third-party tool without you approving the exact text.
