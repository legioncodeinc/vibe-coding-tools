# Distilled: what the evidence says about a weekly self-review

Written from a fresh read of `raw/`. Every claim ends in a bracketed citation to the raw file
it came from. Where sources conflict, both readings are stated and the preferred one is
named. Where the archive is thin, section 9 says so rather than padding.

Sweep run 2026-08-17. Seventeen sources. Ranking applied throughout: peer-reviewed academic
outranks academic conference paper outranks industry or practitioner research outranks
press coverage outranks vendor blog outranks community blog.

---

## 1. Does periodic self-review work? Yes, moderately, and not for the reason people think

The strongest result in the archive is not about weekly reviews. It is about monitoring.

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies | 138, N = 19,951 [raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md] |
| Effect of interventions on monitoring frequency | d+ = 1.98, CI 1.71 to 2.24 [raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md] |
| Effect on goal attainment | d+ = 0.40, CI 0.32 to 0.48 [raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md] |

Monitoring frequency mediated the effect, meaning the monitoring itself is the active
ingredient [raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md].

**Two moderators are directly buildable.** Effects were larger "when the outcomes were
reported or made public, and when the information was physically recorded"
[raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md]. A persistent routine report is
a physical record by construction. That is a design instruction, not a nice property: the
value is in the durable artifact, not in the moment of reading it.

**What this does not say.** d+ = 0.40 is moderate. It is not a claim that reviewing your week
transforms output. The literature is dominated by health behavior goals, so transfer to
knowledge work is an assumption
[raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md].

**And the weekly review itself has no evidence in this archive.** The practice has a named
origin in Getting Things Done and a canonical three-phase structure, Get Clear, Get Current,
Get Creative [raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md]. Allen calls it "the
critical success factor for marrying your larger commitments to your day-to-day activities"
[raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md]. That is a position from the method's
author, published by a task-manager vendor. Nothing in this archive tests it. **Anchor the
skill on the monitoring meta-analysis, not on GTD.**

## 2. The cost, which is what actually gets abandoned

| Source | Stated human time |
|---|---|
| Todoist | one hour per week minimum [raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md] |
| Asian Efficiency | 60 to 90 minutes at first, 30 to 45 once practiced [raw/weekly--review-practice--asianefficiency-gtd-weekly-review.md] |

The sources disagree on the floor. The honest range is 30 to 90 minutes of unassisted human
time.

Both sources name the same failure. "the longer and more arduous your review is, the less
likely you'll be to maintain the habit"
[raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md]. And: "The reason some people do not
complete the weekly review is because they do not schedule it"
[raw/weekly--review-practice--asianefficiency-gtd-weekly-review.md].

**Reading.** A scheduled routine removes the failure named as primary, and automating the
retrieval removes most of the cost named as secondary. That is the case for this skill in one
paragraph. The recommended fallback when even the short form is too much is the mini review,
15 minutes [raw/weekly--review-practice--asianefficiency-gtd-weekly-review.md], which is the
practice-level ancestor of the skill's quiet-week short form.

## 3. Self-assessment is bad enough to justify retrieval instead of recall

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Meta-analyses synthesized | 22 [raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md] |
| Participants across the synthesized work | over 330,000 [raw/weekly--self-assessment--sciencedaily-zell-krizan-2014.md] |
| Mean correlation, self-evaluation to performance | M = .29, SD = .11 [raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md] |

People "have only moderate insight into their abilities"
[raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md].

**The four accuracy moderators are a field specification.** Accuracy improved when
evaluations were domain specific, and when tasks were objective, familiar, and low in
complexity [raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md]. Restated by an
author: "If people are evaluating themselves in terms of very specific criteria, they're
going to have better self-insight"
[raw/weekly--self-assessment--sciencedaily-zell-krizan-2014.md].

Hours in meetings from a calendar is specific, objective, familiar and simple. A field like
"momentum" is none of the four and would carry the accuracy of an unaided guess while
wearing the costume of a measurement.

**The bias has a direction.** People "tend to emphasize strengths while downplaying
weaknesses" [raw/weekly--self-assessment--sciencedaily-zell-krizan-2014.md]. A self-review
that grades itself and always finds a win is that bias, automated.

**Caveat carried forward.** This literature is about insight into abilities, not recall of
last week's events. The transfer is plausible and is labelled a transfer everywhere it is
used [raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md].

## 4. Neutral or motivating? The archive says the question is wrong

The best evidence available on delivering performance information to a person:

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean effect of feedback interventions | d = .41 [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md] |
| Share of effects that were negative | over 38 percent of 607 effects [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md] |

Verbatim: "FIs improved performance on average (d = .41) but that over 1/3 of the FIs
decreased performance" [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].

The mechanism is where attention lands. "FI effects on performance are attenuated by cues
that direct attention to meta-task processes" involving the self, and improve when attention
stays at the task level [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md]. The
critical variable is not the content of the feedback but what the recipient attends to
[raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].

**The position this supports.** Neither neutral-cold nor motivating-warm. Both praise and
criticism can be self-level cues, and the theory predicts both degrade performance when they
are. The axis that matters is task versus self, not harsh versus kind. A weekly review should
report what happened to the work and never issue a verdict on the person.

Supported from a second direction: an author of the self-assessment synthesis states "As a
society we make the wrong trade-off by thinking that boosting self-esteem is going to boost
performance" [raw/weekly--self-assessment--sciencedaily-zell-krizan-2014.md]. And from a
third: scorecard practice reports that "measurement bias" undermines engagement when the
system becomes an administrative judgment
[raw/weekly--scorecard--bscdesigner-balanced-scorecard-failure.md].

**Conflict, stated.** The progress literature pulls the other way. Progress on meaningful work
contributed more to positive inner work life than any other factor examined, and "Even small
wins can boost inner work life tremendously"
[raw/weekly--progress--amabile-kramer-hbr-2011.md]. That is an argument for recording wins.
It is not an argument for manufacturing them, and the two sources are compatible once the
distinction is task-level versus self-level: recording that a thing shipped is task-level,
telling someone they had a great week is self-level.

**Caveat.** Kluger and DeNisi studied externally delivered feedback. Whether the same
attention mechanism operates when reader and subject are the same person is untested here
[raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].

## 5. Setbacks land harder than wins lift, and the magnitude is unknown

Progress is the top driver of inner work life, from 238 participants across 26 teams in 7
organizations and over 12,000 diary entries
[raw/weekly--progress--mindtools-progress-theory.md]. "On the flip side, small losses or
setbacks can have an extremely negative effect"
[raw/weekly--progress--amabile-kramer-hbr-2011.md].

**Two independent sources describing this work decline to quantify the asymmetry**
[raw/weekly--progress--amabile-kramer-hbr-2011.md,
raw/weekly--progress--mindtools-progress-theory.md]. Any specific multiple attached to this
finding elsewhere is not supported by anything in this archive. Treat the asymmetry as
directional only.

**Reading.** State a bad week plainly and briefly. Do not dwell, and do not pad it with an
invented win, because a manufactured win beside a real setback does not neutralize the
setback, it just costs the report its credibility.

## 6. The trend is the product: exact thresholds, and a live conflict

A weekly series is a run chart with a one-week interval, so the run chart rules apply.

| Rule | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Shift | "Six or more consecutive points either all above or all below the median" [raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md] |
| Trend | "Five or more consecutive points all going up or all going down" [raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md] |
| Runs | Too few or too many crossings of the median, against tabulated critical values [raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md] |
| Astronomical point | A point "obviously, even blatantly, different from the rest" [raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md] |

**How few points is usable.** "the run chart is useful with just three or four data points in
order to get an early indication of central tendency and trend"
[raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md]. Keep the source's own hedge. Early
indication, not a trend.

**The conflict.** Perla and Provost signal a shift at six points. Carey recommends seven or
eight. Anhoj and Olesen's simulation study concludes their shift rule "takes at least 8 data
points to signal a shift if one has between 12 and 22 data points in total", holding the
false signal rate near 5 percent
[raw/weekly--trend--anhoj-olesen-run-chart-simulation-2014.md].

**Preferred reading: the conservative one.** Where Perla would allow "shift" at six, wait.
The cost of a false trend claim in a personal scorecard is that the reader changes tactics
in response to noise, which is worse than saying nothing. Twelve weeks of history is the
depth the skill reads, because 12 sits inside the 12 to 22 band where the stricter rule is
defined [raw/weekly--trend--anhoj-olesen-run-chart-simulation-2014.md].

**Why any of this matters, verbatim.** "When improvement data are presented in healthcare
(eg, clinical reports, dashboards, project updates, and board reports), people will often
over- or under-react to a single or most recent data point (and begin tampering, possibly
making things worse)" [raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md].

**Tampering is measured, not theoretical.** Reacting to common cause variation as if it were
special cause [raw/weekly--trend--wikipedia-common-special-cause.md] produced, in a catapult
demonstration, a process spread of 28.1 inches against 11.1 inches with no adjustments, about
2.5 times worse [raw/weekly--trend--asee-process-tampering-experiment.md]. There is "a
temptation to react to an extreme outcome and to see it as significant, even where its causes
are common" [raw/weekly--trend--wikipedia-common-special-cause.md].

**Caveats.** These rules assume points drawn from a stable process
[raw/weekly--trend--perla-run-chart-bmj-2011.md]. A person's work week is not one. A catapult
is [raw/weekly--trend--asee-process-tampering-experiment.md]. The direction of both findings
transfers; the magnitudes do not, and the rules are used here as a discipline against
overclaiming rather than as a hypothesis test. Deming's "94% of issues are the result of
misinterpreting common cause variation" is his estimate, not a measurement in that paper
[raw/weekly--trend--asee-process-tampering-experiment.md].

## 7. Why scorecards get abandoned

No measured abandonment rate exists anywhere in this archive
[raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md,
raw/weekly--scorecard--bscdesigner-balanced-scorecard-failure.md]. What exists is a failure
taxonomy from two vendor and practitioner sources.

| Failure | Source wording |
|---|---|
| No decision hook | "The dashboard shows data but doesn't tell the viewer what to do when a number looks wrong" [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] |
| No alerts | "Users have to remember to check. Out of sight means out of routine" [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] |
| Trust gap | One mismatched figure undermines confidence in every other number [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] |
| Access friction | Slow loads, clicks, logins [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] |
| Wrong audience, no owner | KPIs misaligned to role; "everyone means no one" [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] |
| Measurement instead of decision | "What is often implemented isn't the Balanced Scorecard, but a limited performance measurement framework" [raw/weekly--scorecard--bscdesigner-balanced-scorecard-failure.md] |

Named design recommendations: conditional formatting for at-a-glance status, a last-updated
timestamp on every view to remove freshness doubt, plain-language normal ranges, **one
primary decision per audience segment**, alerts named by decision outcome rather than by
metric, and embedding into an existing recurring workflow
[raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md].

The scorecard-specific version of the same failure: measurement-only implementations "fail to
clarify strategy or improve accountability", create resistance, and drive people to abandon
the framework entirely [raw/weekly--scorecard--bscdesigner-balanced-scorecard-failure.md].

**Reading.** The decision hook is the top three. The counts are supporting material. A weekly
review that leads with this week's absolute figures is the measurement-instead-of-decision
failure in personal form. The last-updated recommendation is the direct ancestor of the rule
that a stale or paused sibling routine is named rather than silently omitted, because a
missing section reads as a zero and the trust gap says one wrong number poisons the rest
[raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md].

**Note against the skill's own spec.** "One primary decision per audience segment"
[raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md] argues for one item, not three. The
archive contains no evidence for three specifically. Section 9 names this.

## 8. The top three cannot be the three loudest items

"people are more likely to perform unimportant tasks (i.e., tasks with objectively lower
payoffs) over important tasks (i.e., tasks with objectively better payoffs), when the
unimportant tasks are characterized merely by spurious urgency"
[raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md]. Five experiments, with the
normative reasons for choosing the urgent task controlled for
[raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md]. The behavior violates dominance,
and "pursuing an urgent task has its own appeal, independent of its objective consequence"
[raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].

**Three consequences for a selection method.**

1. Urgency is discounted rather than weighted equally, because the reader already
   over-weights it and an equal-weight scorer just reproduces the bias with arithmetic
   attached [raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].
2. Spurious urgency is filtered before scoring. Urgency counts only from a real date
   attached to a real source, because sender adjectives are the exact stimulus the paper
   manipulated [raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].
3. Consequence is stated in the shown reasoning. Reasoning that names only a deadline hands
   the bias back to the reader [raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].

**Caveat.** Abstract-level retrieval only, consumer-behavior laboratory tasks, and the
perceived-self-importance moderator that secondary coverage attributes to this paper is not
confirmed in the abstract and is not cited
[raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].

## 9. Sunday evening versus Friday afternoon

The practice literature offers three slots with no evidence attached to any of them: Friday
afternoon to use the pre-weekend productivity dip, Sunday evening to prepare for the week,
Monday morning for a fresh perspective. It then says consistency matters more than the choice
[raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md].

The only evidentiary anchor is the recovery literature, and it does not resolve cleanly.

**Against Sunday evening.** Psychological detachment from work during nonwork time is a core
recovery experience, and interventions can improve it, "particularly psychological detachment
from work on evenings and weekends"
[raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md]. Boundary management is by
far the most effective lever: d = 0.65 with a boundary-management component against d = 0.25
without, from a meta-analysis of 34 interventions with an overall d = 0.36
[raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md]. A scheduled, notified
Sunday-evening work scorecard is boundary management run backwards.

The recovery paradox sharpens it: job stressors call for recovery while producing the states
that make recovery less likely, and "perceiving that one has performed well at work is
associated with higher levels of psychological detachment and relaxation in the evening"
[raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md]. An honest scorecard must
sometimes report a bad week, and it would deliver that news to the reader least able to
detach afterwards.

**For Sunday evening.** Not detaching predicts positive affect when the work thinking is
positive or takes the form of problem-solving rumination, and very high detachment may
undermine performance [raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md]. A
bounded review ending in three concrete decisions is closer to problem solving than to
rumination.

**Position preferred here: Friday late afternoon as the default, Sunday evening offered with
the tradeoff stated.** The boundary-management effect size is the largest single number in
this part of the archive and it points one way
[raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md]. The Friday objection, that
the week is not finished and Friday judgment is tired, is weakened by the fact that the
generator is a routine rather than the person: the machine has no Friday afternoon.

**Caveat, and it is large.** None of this literature studied a weekly review artifact. Every
application above is a transfer, and long-term benefits of recovery are weaker than
short-term gains in this literature anyway
[raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md].

## 10. Retrospective practice ported to one person

The Prime Directive, verbatim: "Regardless of what we discover, we must understand and truly
believe that everyone did the best job he or she could, given what was known at the time, his
or her skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand"
[raw/weekly--retrospective--parabol-prime-directive.md]. Formalized by Norm Kerth in *Project
Retrospectives: A Handbook for Team Reviews*
[raw/weekly--retrospective--parabol-prime-directive.md].

**Two absences in the source are load-bearing.** It contains no criticism of the directive,
and no guidance on applying it to an individual rather than a team
[raw/weekly--retrospective--parabol-prime-directive.md].

**The problem with a naive port.** The directive's mechanism is removing fear of blame so
facts can surface in front of colleagues
[raw/weekly--retrospective--parabol-prime-directive.md]. In a solo review there are no
colleagues, the mechanism has no job, and the wording becomes an excuse generator: "everyone
did the best job he or she could, given what was known at the time" is one step from
reframing a dropped commitment as a learning.

**Synthesis, and it is a synthesis rather than a sourced claim.** Keep the blamelessness
about the person, refuse it about the record. This is supported from a different direction
by the finding that performance improves when attention stays on the task and degrades when
it moves to the self [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md].

| Number | Why it is fenced |

| "94% of issues are common cause" | Deming's estimate, not derived in the paper that quotes it [raw/weekly--trend--asee-process-tampering-experiment.md] |
| "One third of feedback makes things worse" as a precise share | The measured figure is over 38 percent of 607 effects; say "over a third of the measured effects" [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md] |

## 12. Named gaps

1. **No controlled evidence that the GTD weekly review works.** The archive has the method's
   author asserting it through a vendor blog and nothing else
   [raw/weekly--review-practice--todoist-weekly-review.md]. The skill anchors on monitoring
   instead [raw/weekly--self-monitoring--harkin-psych-bulletin-2016.md].
2. **No measured adherence or abandonment rate** for weekly reviews, dashboards, or
   scorecards [raw/weekly--review-practice--asianefficiency-gtd-weekly-review.md,
   raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md].
3. **No validated individual retrospective format.** Every solo-retrospective claim is a
   transfer from team practice [raw/weekly--retrospective--parabol-prime-directive.md].
4. **No evidence for three as the right size of a priority list.** The nearest thing in the
   archive argues for one [raw/weekly--scorecard--clicdata-dashboard-adoption.md]. Three is a
   product decision, not a finding, and the skill says so.
5. **No study of any weekly review artifact under any timing condition.** The Friday-versus-
   Sunday position is built entirely by transfer from recovery research
   [raw/weekly--timing--sonnentag-recovery-annual-reviews-2022.md].
6. **No evidence on self-review specifically.** The feedback literature is external feedback
   [raw/weekly--feedback--kluger-denisi-psych-bulletin-1996.md]; the self-assessment
   literature is ability judgment
   [raw/weekly--self-assessment--zell-krizan-metasynthesis-2014.md]. Nothing measures a
   person reading a machine-generated report about their own week.
7. **Two retrievals were degraded.** The run chart simulation study was read through a search
   index extract after a CAPTCHA block
   [raw/weekly--trend--anhoj-olesen-run-chart-simulation-2014.md], and the mere urgency effect
   was read at abstract level only
   [raw/weekly--top-three--zhu-yang-hsee-mere-urgency-2018.md].
