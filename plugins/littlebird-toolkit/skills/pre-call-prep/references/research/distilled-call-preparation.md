# Distilled: meeting and sales call preparation

Written from a fresh read of `raw/` on 2026-08-17. Twelve sources archived. Every claim
below ends in a bracketed citation to the raw file it came from. Anything not cited here
is not a fact this skill is allowed to assert.

Research window: sources published or last updated within the last 12 months, except the
Bain rule of 7, which restates a 2010 finding that is still the standard citation for
meeting size and is flagged as such [raw/callprep--meeting-size--bain-rule-of-7.md].

## 1. Does preparation actually change meeting outcomes

This is the first question and the sources disagree. Both readings are stated.

| Reading | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-meeting activity is linked to meeting effectiveness | Three decade review of meeting science reports the literature "linking pre-meeting communication to meeting effectiveness", attributed to Allen et al., 2014b [raw/callprep--meeting-science--rogelberg-annualreviews-2026.md] | Peer reviewed review article, but reports the link without restating an effect size |
| A thin pre-meeting prompt does nothing | Preregistered field experiment, 361 participants, 7,196 meetings, two weeks. A goal-setting survey delivered 5 minutes before each meeting produced no statistically significant effect on perceived meeting effectiveness [raw/callprep--pre-meeting-nudge--arxiv-2602-16939.md] | Strongest single study in the archive by design quality |

**Preferred reading, and why.** These do not actually contradict each other once you look
at what was manipulated. The field experiment tested a prompt that added no information:
it asked the attendee to pick a goal from a closed list of ten
[raw/callprep--pre-meeting-nudge--arxiv-2602-16939.md]. The review is about pre-meeting
communication, meaning content moving between people before the meeting
[raw/callprep--meeting-science--rogelberg-annualreviews-2026.md]. The defensible
synthesis: **prompting someone to think harder before a call does nothing measurable;
giving them information they did not already have is the part that could matter, and the
size of that effect is not established in this archive.**

**Design consequence.** A brief that restates the agenda and tells the user to set an
objective is, on the best available evidence, worthless. Every line of a brief has to
carry a fact the user did not already have in their head. This is the single most
important finding in the archive and it constrains everything downstream.

**Named gap.** No source in this archive measures the effect of an information-carrying
pre-call brief on call outcomes. The vendor claims that gesture at this are not evidence
[raw/callprep--ai-prep-tools--ampup-2026.md]. Do not assert that briefs improve outcomes.

## 2. How long the brief can be

| Constraint | Value | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Target reading time | 90 seconds | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |
| Target length | One page, roughly 250 words | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |
| Actual reading conditions | Scanning on a phone in a "3-minute gap" between meetings | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |
| An eight page document | Needs "20-30 minutes of focused reading time", which the reader does not have | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |
| Reading behavior | "Executives don't read briefs, they scan them for decisions they need to make." | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |
| Depth handling | Supporting analysis moves to page 2 and beyond so page 1 stays at 90 seconds | [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md] |

**Design consequence.** One screen per meeting, appendix for depth. This is a single
practitioner source and it is not research, so the numbers are a design target rather
than a measured threshold [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md]. It is
nonetheless the only source in the archive that addresses length at all, which is itself
a named gap.

**Reinforcing evidence from a different direction.** Manual pre-call account research is
described as roughly a 20 minute task [raw/callprep--ai-prep-tools--ampup-2026.md], and a
published pre-call checklist budgets 15 to 20 minutes across six tasks
[raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md]. The brief's job is to compress a 15
to 20 minute research task into a 90 second read. That ratio is the product.

## 3. What goes in the brief

Two independent published pre-call frameworks agree on most of the content. Merged:

| Element | HubSpot seven step | Sendspark four step |
|---|---|---|
| Company landscape and industry | Step 1 "Conduct in-depth research of your prospect's business landscape" [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Step 1 company background, website, LinkedIn, press releases, earnings summaries [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md] |
| The individual person | Step 2 "Know who you're going to be talking to", role, tenure, career progression, published content [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Step 2 stakeholder mapping across the buying committee [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md] |
| What changed recently | Not a separate step | Step 3 trigger events: funding, leadership shifts, product launches [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md] |
| Prior interaction history | "previous interaction key points and agreements", which topics generated interest, repeated objections, decision timeline [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Step 4 prior engagement history [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md] |
| Objections | Step 7 "Anticipate potential objections and queries" [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Not a separate step |
| Call objective | Step 4 "Establish firm goals for the call", specific and measurable [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | 3 to 5 objectives and 8 to 12 questions, 4 minutes [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md] |
| Mutual connections | Step 3 [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Not a separate step |
| Support materials | Step 5 [raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md] | Not a separate step |

**The strongest single claim for prior-history review:** "67% of lost deals have prior
engagement history that was never reviewed", attributed to Salesforce
[raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md]. This is a secondhand vendor
attribution with no report title or URL and was not independently verified
[raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md]. It is the closest published
statement of the problem and it is weak evidence. Do not restate it as a fact.

**Timing.** Deep research 24 to 48 hours out, final review 15 minutes before dialing
[raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md]. That two phase split maps exactly
onto a routine that runs the evening before plus an on-demand mode for the single next
meeting.

## 4. Why specificity is the whole game

| Statistic | Attributed to | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| "73% actively avoid suppliers that send irrelevant outreach" | Gartner, 2025 | [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md] |
| "71% of buyers describe their experience with supplier reps as 'frustrating'" | SBI, 2024 | [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md] |
| Buyers "mostly or fully define their purchase requirements 83% of the time before speaking with sales" | 6Sense, 2025 | [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md] |
| "81% of buyers choose vendors before sales contact" | 6Sense, 2024 | [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md] |
| "61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience" | Gartner, 2025 | [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md] |

All five come through an aggregator; the underlying reports are gated and were not
independently verified [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md].

**What the pattern says.** The buyer arrives already informed
[raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md]. Generic content is actively
penalized [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md]. The remaining
edge is knowing this specific person and this specific history, which is exactly what a
record based brief supplies and what a generic template does not.

## 5. Recurring meetings degrade differently

The meeting science review reports that the effects of team meetings vary by frequency
and structure, attributed to Kauffeld and Lehmann-Willenbrock, 2012
[raw/callprep--meeting-science--rogelberg-annualreviews-2026.md]. Practitioner guidance
names the specific failure mode: a recurring 1:1 collapsing into "only covering tasks and
to-dos", with the warning that "status updates shouldn't be the only thing you discuss"
[raw/callprep--recurring-1on1--lattice-2026.md]. The stated remedy is continuity:
"record action items, commitments, and key insights to maintain continuity across
meetings" [raw/callprep--recurring-1on1--lattice-2026.md].

**Design consequence.** A recurring meeting brief that reprints the same relationship
summary every week is the document version of the same failure. The recurring brief leads
with the delta since the last instance and the commitments still open, not with a fresh
restatement of who the person is.

## 6. Meeting size changes what a brief is for

> "Once you've got 7 people in a decision-making group, each additional member reduces
> decision effectiveness by 10%."
> [raw/callprep--meeting-size--bain-rule-of-7.md]

A group of 17 or more "rarely makes any decisions"
[raw/callprep--meeting-size--bain-rule-of-7.md]. The figure is presented without sample,
method, or confidence interval and is a consulting heuristic rather than a peer reviewed
effect size [raw/callprep--meeting-size--bain-rule-of-7.md].

**Design consequence.** Above roughly seven attendees the call is not a decision forum,
so a per-person history dossier is aimed at the wrong job and cannot fit on one screen
anyway. The large meeting brief names the user's own slice, the two or three people who
matter to it, and moves the full roster to the appendix.

Separately, B2B deals are stated to involve "an average of 6-10 decision-makers"
attributed to Gartner [raw/callprep--pre-call-research--sendspark-2026.md], so a
multi-stakeholder sales call sits right at the Bain threshold by construction.

## 7. Qualification frameworks as brief checklists

MEDDIC elements, definitions as published
[raw/callprep--qualification-frameworks--claap-meddic-spin-2026.md]:

| Letter | Element | Published definition |
|---|---|---|
| M | Metrics | "What are the measurable success indicators for the prospect?" |
| E | Economic Buyer | "Who holds the purse strings?" |
| D | Decision Criteria | "What specific requirements must be met to close the deal?" |
| D | Decision Process | "How does the decision get made, and who is involved?" |
| I | Identify Pain | "What is the problem the prospect is trying to solve?" |
| C | Champion | "Who within the organization will advocate for your solution?" |

Stated fit: complex, high value B2B sales, long cycles, multiple decision makers
[raw/callprep--qualification-frameworks--claap-meddic-spin-2026.md].

SPIN elements: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-Payoff, with the published
definitions being question forms rather than facts to look up
[raw/callprep--qualification-frameworks--claap-meddic-spin-2026.md]. Stated fit: shorter
cycles, relationship focused, lower value
[raw/callprep--qualification-frameworks--claap-meddic-spin-2026.md].

**Honest labelling.** The source explicitly does not say what either framework requires a
rep to prepare before a call
[raw/callprep--qualification-frameworks--claap-meddic-spin-2026.md]. Using MEDDIC's six
elements as a slot list for a sales brief, and reporting which slots the record cannot
fill, is an authored design decision over those definitions, not a claim the source
makes. It is labelled as such in the guides.

## 8. What existing AI prep tools do and where they fail

Named gaps in the current tool category
[raw/callprep--ai-prep-tools--ampup-2026.md]:

| Gap as named | What it means here |
|---|---|
| "No structured prior-call synthesis": tools fail to tie "prior call history, deal stage, and stakeholder context into a single page" | The open loops and last-conversation sections exist to close this |
| "Generic output without learning": generators produce one off summaries rather than learning | The routine must read its own past reports so week two is not a copy of week one |

## 9. Objections: what the archive supports and what it does not

**Supported.** Practitioner guidance says to review prior conversations for repeated
objections and to anticipate objections before the call
[raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md]. Qualitatively, top performers are
described as treating objections as buying signals
[raw/callprep--objection-data--gong-2026.md].

**Named gap, searched twice, not found.** No source in this archive gives frequency data
on how often a specific objection recurs across calls with the same account, nor any
measured effect of preparing for a known prior objection. The Gong page does not contain
it [raw/callprep--objection-data--gong-2026.md]. A statistics aggregator page dedicated to
objection handling does not contain it either
[raw/callprep--objection-stats--careertrainer-2026.md]. The design decision to surface
prior objections in the brief therefore rests on practitioner guidance alone
[raw/callprep--pre-call-planning--hubspot-2026.md], and this skill says so rather than
implying an evidence base it does not have.

**Weak supporting figure for continuing-relationship default.** "80% of sales require 5
follow-up calls after the meeting" [raw/callprep--objection-stats--careertrainer-2026.md],
from a low trust aggregator with self referential attribution
[raw/callprep--objection-stats--careertrainer-2026.md]. Directionally consistent with the
continuing-relationship brief being the common case, not evidence for it.

## 11. Coverage gaps in this archive

1. **No measured effect of an information-carrying pre-call brief on call outcomes.** The
   one high quality experiment tested a contentless nudge
   [raw/callprep--pre-meeting-nudge--arxiv-2602-16939.md].
2. **Brief length has exactly one source and it is a practitioner blog**
   [raw/callprep--brief-length--segment8-2026.md]. The 90 second and 250 word targets are
   working rules of thumb, not measured thresholds.
3. **No objection recurrence data**, searched across two dedicated sources
   [raw/callprep--objection-data--gong-2026.md]
   [raw/callprep--objection-stats--careertrainer-2026.md].
4. **Almost all commercial statistics are secondhand vendor attributions.** The primary
   Gartner, Forrester, 6Sense and SBI reports are gated
   [raw/callprep--buyer-expectations--corporatevisions-2026.md].
5. **Nothing on non-sales meeting types.** No source in the archive addresses briefing for
   a partner sync, a client review, or an internal standup specifically. Those brief
   shapes are authored by analogy from the recurring meeting and meeting size findings
   [raw/callprep--recurring-1on1--lattice-2026.md]
   [raw/callprep--meeting-size--bain-rule-of-7.md] and are labelled as design decisions
   rather than research findings.
