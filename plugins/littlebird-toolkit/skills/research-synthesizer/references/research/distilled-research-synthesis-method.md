## 1. What kind of synthesis a practitioner topic answer actually is

A scoping review provides "an overview of the available research evidence" rather than
answering a specific question, and is used to map the scope of research on a topic,
summarize existing evidence, and identify research gaps
[raw/synthesis--scoping-review--asu-libguide-scoping-review.md].

Scoping reviews do not aim "to answer a specific question to guide decision-making", do
"not typically include a risk of bias assessment", and "often prioritize breadth over
depth" [raw/synthesis--scoping-review--asu-libguide-scoping-review.md].

Quoting the PRISMA extension for scoping reviews, they "are generally conducted to provide
an overview of the existing evidence regardless of methodological quality or risk of bias"
[raw/synthesis--scoping-review--asu-libguide-scoping-review.md].

**Consequence for this skill.** A one-shot topic answer is a scoping exercise, not a
systematic review. Breadth over depth, and no quality appraisal is built into the method.
Since the output is going to be acted on, quality has to be handled explicitly and
separately, because the review type does not supply it
[raw/synthesis--scoping-review--asu-libguide-scoping-review.md].

## 2. The rapid review: what may be abbreviated, and the one thing that never is

A rapid review is "a streamlined variation of the systematic review where individual steps
are omitted or completed in a shorter time to accommodate decision makers", taking 1 to 6
months against 6 months to 2 years for a systematic review
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--sdu-libguide-rapid-vs-systematic.md].

The Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group interim guidance sets the abbreviations by stage
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--cochrane-rrmg-interim-guidance-2020.md]:

| Stage | Abbreviation permitted |
|---|---|
| Search | Always search CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase. Restrict specialized databases to "1-2 additional sources, or omitted if time and resources limited". "Limit literature searches to English language; add other languages only if justified". "Limit grey literature and supplemental searching" |
| Title and abstract screening | "Use two reviewers for dual screen of at least 20% (ideally more) of abstracts", single reviewer for the rest, second reviewer screens all exclusions |
| Full text screening | "Use one reviewer to screen all included full text articles", second reviewer screens all excluded |
| Data extraction | "Use a single reviewer to extract data using a piloted form", second reviewer verifies. "Limit data extraction to a minimal set of required data items" |
| Risk of bias | "Use a single reviewer to rate risk of bias, with full verification". Limit to the most important outcomes |
| Synthesis | "Synthesize evidence narratively". Meta-analysis only "if appropriate (i.e., studies are similar enough to pool)" |

**The pattern.** Every abbreviation in that guidance is the same move: one person does the
primary work, a second verifies the output, and the abbreviation is documented rather than
hidden. The guidance never abbreviates by removing the check
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--cochrane-rrmg-interim-guidance-2020.md].

The cost is named. Tricco and colleagues "recommend that two individual reviewers screen and
select studies to minimize selection bias", so the single-reviewer approach carries a known
selection-bias cost accepted for speed
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--sdu-libguide-rapid-vs-systematic.md].

That cost has been quantified once in this archive: single review author screening risks
falsely excluding **13 percent** of relevant studies
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

**Consequence for this skill.** Any single-pass sweep should be assumed to have missed
something on the order of one item in eight, and should say so rather than presenting itself
as complete [raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

The Cochrane guidance gives no list of question types where rapid methods are inadmissible.
It is guidance on going faster, not on when speed is disqualifying
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--cochrane-rrmg-interim-guidance-2020.md].

## 3. Separating findings from interpretation

Narrative synthesis proceeds in four elements, in order: "Developing a theory of how the
intervention works, why and for whom", "Developing a preliminary synthesis", "Exploring
relationships within and between studies", and "Assessing the robustness of the synthesis
product" [raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md].

The preliminary synthesis uses tabulation, groupings and clusters, transforming data, and
vote-counting. Exploring relationships uses conceptual triangulation, reciprocal and
refutational translation, moderator variables, and visual representation
[raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md].

The guidance aims at "increasing the transparency and reproducibility of the process" and
the framework is described as useful "particularly for increasing transparency"
[raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md].

**Two rules extracted.** Describe what each source says before reasoning across sources; the
order is part of the method. And robustness is assessed as a separate step after the
synthesis exists, so a synthesis is a thing you then evaluate rather than a thing that
arrives pre-validated [raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md].

**Gap.** The framework gives no rule for synthesizing across sources of different types, for
example a peer reviewed study against a vendor blog post against something the reader saw on
screen. It assumes a corpus of research studies
[raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md].

## 4. Grading a source: credibility is determined off the page

In the founding study, 10 professional fact checkers, 10 PhD historians, and 25 Stanford
undergraduates were compared on the same credibility tasks. Fact checkers were "fastest and
most accurate"; historians and students were readily deceived
[raw/sources--credibility--stanford-fact-checkers-lateral-reading.md].

The mechanism: fact checkers scanned a site briefly, then left it and opened other tabs to
find out what the rest of the web said about that source before evaluating its claims.
Historians and students stayed on the page and were misled by professional appearance,
logos, and nonprofit status
[raw/sources--credibility--stanford-fact-checkers-lateral-reading.md].

Wineburg: "Very intelligent people were bamboozled by the ruses that are part of the toolkit
of digital deception" [raw/sources--credibility--stanford-fact-checkers-lateral-reading.md].

**Domain expertise did not substitute for the technique.** The historians were subject
experts and still lost
[raw/sources--credibility--stanford-fact-checkers-lateral-reading.md].

The operational procedure: research the source before accepting its claims, determine a
consensus about the source by searching for the publication ("Usually best"), funding
organization, author, or content, and "Read a minimum of 3 to 5 new sources to see what they
have to say about your original source"
[raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md].

And the rule that is rarely stated this plainly: "If you can't find 3 to 5 sources, that is
information in itself. It means your original source doesn't have an established reputation"
[raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md].

**Failure to corroborate a source is a finding about that source, not an inconclusive
result** [raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md].

There is no single evaluation framework. A university study skills guide names six (CRAAP,
5 Ws, lateral reading, SIFT, PROMPT, RAVEN) and matches each to a source type, separating
the framework for academic sources from the one for "news, websites, or AI-generated
content" and from the one for "academic and grey literature"
[raw/sources--credibility--abertay-evaluation-frameworks.md].

**Gap.** That source did not return the expansion of any acronym, so no claim about how
CRAAP, PROMPT, RAVEN, SIFT or the 5 Ws actually work appears anywhere in this skill
[raw/sources--credibility--abertay-evaluation-frameworks.md]. SIFT specifically is not
represented by any primary source in this archive
[raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md].

## 5. Sources with a commercial interest

Industry sponsorship bias is "A tendency for the methods and results of a study to support
the interests of the funding organisation"
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

The Cochrane methodology review by Lundh and colleagues found
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md]:

| Measure | Risk ratio |
|---|---|
| Efficacy results favouring the sponsor's product | 1.27 (95% CI 1.17 to 1.37) |
| Authors' conclusions favouring the sponsor's product | 1.34 (95% CI 1.19 to 1.51) |
| Effect on harm estimates | none found, evidence quality very low |

**The conclusions figure exceeds the results figure.** Sponsored work is skewed more in what
it concludes than in what it measures
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

The mechanisms are enumerable and therefore checkable: designing questions to yield
misleading answers, unrepresentative populations, suboptimal comparator dosing, questionable
analytical choices, suppressing nonsignificant findings, selective outcome reporting, and
republishing positive results multiple times
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

Non-publication of unfavourable results is documented behaviour, not suspicion. Of 38
FDA-positive pre-market antidepressant trials, "37 of 38 (97%)" were published. Of 36 trials
with negative or questionable results, "22 (61%) were not published, 11 (31%) were published
with narrative 'spin', and only 3 (8%) were accurately represented as negative", inflating
published effect sizes by roughly 30 percent
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

Affiliation predicts conclusion strongly. Among second-hand smoke reviews, "Of authors with
no ties to industry, 87% (65/75) found negative health effects; whereas, only 6% (2/31) of
authors with industry ties drew similar conclusions"
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

**Transfer caveat.** All of this evidence is clinical and pharmaceutical. Applying the
magnitude of these effects to vendor marketing content in a non-clinical field would be an
unsupported extrapolation. What transfers is the direction and the mechanism
[raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md].

Funding organization is named as one of the four things to look up when checking a source
laterally, alongside publication, author, and content
[raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md].

## 6. Keeping up: partial exposure is the normal state

A Doximity survey of 600 physicians found 95 percent interested in learning about new
trials, treatments or procedures, 95 percent wanting to review clinical guidelines, and 70
percent feeling overwhelmed by the amount of information
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md].

Demand is not the constraint. Throughput is
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md].

A 2022 Journal of Internal Medicine study concluded that a primary care provider staying
current with the latest guidelines would need to practise "nearly 27 hours a day"
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md].

Doubling time of medical knowledge, attributed to a 2011 study in Transactions of the
American Clinical and Climatological Association: 50 years in 1950, 7 years in 1980, 3 years
in 2010, and a predicted 73 days by 2020
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md]. The same
progression appears in vendor content with no named citation at all, which is why the better
attributed version is the one used here
[raw/keeping-up--overload--eolas-medical-knowledge-half-life.md].

An unattributed vendor figure worth noting but not relying on: "82% said fewer than half of
the research papers they read ended up influencing how they treat patients", from an unnamed
survey of internists [raw/keeping-up--overload--eolas-medical-knowledge-half-life.md]. If
directionally right it describes a real gap between exposure and behaviour change.

**Consequence for this skill, and it is the central one.** Keeping up in a fast-moving field
is not a discipline problem. At the reported volumes it is arithmetically impossible, and
the professional's actual state is partial exposure of unknown coverage
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md]. That is the
correct default assumption about anyone's knowledge of a moving topic.

**Interest and window caveats.** The Doximity survey is vendor-run by a company selling a
clinical news feed and is from 2022
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md]. The half-life
source is vendor content with no named citation for any of its three headline claims and is
rated low reliability [raw/keeping-up--overload--eolas-medical-knowledge-half-life.md].

## 7. The governing standard for AI-assisted synthesis

The joint position statement from Cochrane, the Campbell Collaboration, JBI and the
Collaboration for Environmental Evidence, published 11 November 2025, sets six principles
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md]:

1. "Evidence synthesists are ultimately responsible for their evidence synthesis, including
   the decision to use artificial intelligence (AI) and automation"
2. Support for the RAISE recommendations
3. AI use is acceptable "as long as they can demonstrate that it will not compromise the
   methodological rigour or integrity of their synthesis"
4. "AI and automation in evidence synthesis should be used with human oversight"
5. "Any use of AI or automation that makes or suggests judgements should be fully and
   transparently reported"
6. Developers must ensure their tools adhere to RAISE

"An author is accountable for the content, methods and findings of their evidence synthesis,
including the decision to use AI, how it is used, and its impact"
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

**The dividing line for disclosure is judgement, not text generation.** Disclosure is
required wherever AI makes or suggests judgements about eligibility, risk of bias, data
extraction, synthesis, certainty of evidence, or plain language summaries. AI used to improve
spelling, grammar or structure generally need not be listed
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

Required disclosure contents: system names, versions and dates, purposes, justification with
performance evidence, limitations, potential biases, and financial interests
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

Authors should "describe any steps taken to verify AI-generated outputs"
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

The Cochrane Rapid Reviews Methods Group position statement adds the operational form:
specify "which tools will be used, the tasks they will perform, and how their outputs will
be verified", and for a generative model "document the model version and prompts applied"
[raw/ai--rapid-reviews--cochrane-rrmg-ai-position-2025.md].

"AI is a support tool, but authors remain fully accountable for the accuracy,
interpretation, and overall validity of the rapid review"
[raw/ai--rapid-reviews--cochrane-rrmg-ai-position-2025.md].

And the sentence that should govern the tone of any AI-produced synthesis: "sustained human
oversight, despite its own fallibility, must remain the core principle of any AI-supported
evidence synthesis effort" [raw/ai--rapid-reviews--cochrane-rrmg-ai-position-2025.md]. The
qualifier is doing real work. The statement does not claim human review is reliable, only
that it is the accountable check.

**Four things must be recorded for an AI-assisted synthesis to be auditable: which tool,
which task, which model version and prompts, and how the output was verified**
[raw/ai--rapid-reviews--cochrane-rrmg-ai-position-2025.md].

A library guide states the same principle as "AI tools should be used as a companion to
human expertise, not a replacement", against four standards: rigor, transparency,
reproducibility, human oversight
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md].

Tasks that guide says AI may support with oversight: "Screening support after reviewer
calibration", "De-duplicating citations", "Extracting structured data fields for human
verification", "Drafting summaries that are reviewed and edited by subject experts", with
the requirement that "All AI-generated outputs must be reviewed, corrected, and approved by
the review team" [raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md].

Tasks it says to avoid: literature searching, inclusion and exclusion decisions without
human review, critical appraisal without human review, automated synthesis or meta-analysis,
and final manuscript production without verification
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md].

**The documented failure mode for search:** "AI-generated searches miss a large proportion of
relevant studies and lack transparency and reproducibility"
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md]. The consequence is not that
results are wrong but that **coverage is unknown and the search is not reproducible**
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md].

## 8. The measured failure modes

### Fabricated citations, at scale, and growing

An audit of over 2.5 million papers published between January 2023 and February 2026 in the
PubMed Central Open Access dataset, covering more than 125 million references verified
against PubMed, Crossref, OpenAlex and Google Scholar, found slightly over 4,000 fabricated
citations across 2,800 papers
[raw/ai--citations--forbes-lancet-fabricated-citations-2026.md].

| Period | Rate |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 1 in 2,828 papers |
| 2025 | 1 in 458 papers |
| First 7 weeks of 2026 | 1 in 277 papers |

Roughly a 12-fold increase over three years, beginning after widespread LLM adoption in late
2022 and 2023 [raw/ai--citations--forbes-lancet-fabricated-citations-2026.md].

The harm named by the study's lead author: "A medical professional or clinical guideline
developer has no way of knowing that the evidence they are relying on does not exist"
[raw/ai--citations--forbes-lancet-fabricated-citations-2026.md].

**Precision caveat.** Even at the 2026 figure the absolute rate is roughly 0.36 percent of
papers. The alarming property is the slope, not the level, and reporting the slope alone
would overstate it [raw/ai--citations--forbes-lancet-fabricated-citations-2026.md].

### Citation accuracy in AI search, and confidence without grounds

The Tow Center tested eight generative search tools across 1,600 queries (20 publishers, 10
articles each, 8 tools), giving each tool a direct excerpt and asking for headline,
publisher, date and URL [raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

The tools collectively delivered incorrect answers to "more than 60 percent of queries".
Perplexity was best at 37 percent incorrect; Grok-3 was 94 percent incorrect; DeepSeek
misattributed 115 of 200; ChatGPT Search misidentified 134 of 200
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

**The finding that matters most.** ChatGPT "incorrectly identified 134 articles, but signaled
a lack of confidence just fifteen times out of its two hundred responses, and never declined
to provide an answer" [raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].
The error rate is a tooling problem. The absence of hedging is a trust problem, because it
removes the reader's only cue that a claim needs checking.

Paid tiers "answered more prompts correctly than their corresponding free equivalents" while
demonstrating "higher error rates" through "definitive, but wrong, answers", because the free
versions declined or hedged more
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

"More than half of responses from Gemini and Grok 3 cited fabricated or broken URLs", with
154 of Grok-3's 200 citations leading to error pages
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

Content was frequently credited to a syndicated republisher rather than the original outlet,
including where a licensing partnership existed
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

And the trust-transfer effect, quoted by the authors from a BBC report: "when AI assistants
cite trusted brands like the BBC as a source, audiences are more likely to trust the answer
- even if it's incorrect."
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md]

**Scope caveat.** That study measures consumer AI search products citing news articles. It
does not measure an agent that fetches a page and quotes it. Two findings transfer
structurally: a URL that has not been opened may not exist, and a confident tone is not
evidence of a checked source
[raw/ai--citations--cjr-tow-center-ai-search-citation-problem-2025.md].

## 9. Conflicts in the archive

**Conflict 1. Is any task off limits for AI?**

A library guide places literature searching, critical appraisal without human review, and
automated synthesis on an avoid list
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md]. The joint position statement
from four evidence synthesis organizations prohibits no specific task and instead conditions
all use on demonstrated soundness, oversight and disclosure
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

**Preferred reading: the position statement, with the library guide's search warning kept as
a live caution.** The position statement carries four institutional publishers and a
consensus process behind it, while the library guide is one institution's operational
policy. But the guide's specific claim about search, that AI-generated searches miss a large
proportion of relevant studies and are not reproducible, is a stated failure mode and not
merely a policy preference, so it survives as a caution about coverage
[raw/ai--tooling--ohsu-libguide-ai-in-systematic-reviews.md].

**Conflict 2. Is a single-reviewer pass acceptable?**

Cochrane rapid review guidance endorses single-reviewer screening with verification
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--cochrane-rrmg-interim-guidance-2020.md]. Tricco and colleagues
recommend two independent reviewers to minimize selection bias
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--sdu-libguide-rapid-vs-systematic.md]. The joint position
statement quantifies the cost at 13 percent of relevant studies falsely excluded
[raw/ai--evidence-synthesis--cochrane-campbell-jbi-cee-position-2025.md].

**Preferred reading: both are right and they are answering different questions.** Single-pass
is acceptable when the abbreviation is declared and the miss rate is stated. It is not
acceptable when the output is presented as complete. That is a reporting rule, not a method
rule [raw/synthesis--rapid-review--cochrane-rrmg-interim-guidance-2020.md]
[raw/synthesis--rapid-review--sdu-libguide-rapid-vs-systematic.md].

**Conflict 3. The doubling-time numbers.**

The same progression of doubling intervals appears with a named 2011 study attached
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md] and with no
attribution at all [raw/keeping-up--overload--eolas-medical-knowledge-half-life.md].

**Preferred reading: treat the progression as a widely repeated estimate, not a measured
constant**, and cite the attributed version if it is cited at all
[raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md].

## 10. Named gaps in this archive

1. **Everything quantitative about keeping up, staleness, and sponsorship bias comes from
   clinical medicine.** No source in this archive measures those effects in software,
   finance, law, marketing, or any other fast-moving practitioner field
   [raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md]
   [raw/sources--commercial-interest--catalogofbias-industry-sponsorship.md]. Any transfer to
   another domain is an inference and is labelled as one wherever it appears.

2. **No source gives a method for synthesizing across heterogeneous source types.** The
   narrative synthesis framework assumes a corpus of research studies and gives no rule for
   weighing a peer reviewed paper against a vendor blog post against something the reader saw
   on screen [raw/synthesis--narrative--york-crd-popay-narrative-synthesis.md]. The
   internal-versus-external weighting used by this skill is therefore constructed, not
   sourced.

3. **No source in this archive addresses prior exposure at all.** Nothing found describes a
   method for separating what a reader has already encountered from what is new to them.
   Every evidence synthesis method in the archive starts from zero. The delta structure that
   is the point of this skill has no methodological literature behind it in this archive, and
   is presented as a design choice rather than a sourced practice.

4. **SIFT is named but not documented.** One source lists it as a framework for digital
   content [raw/sources--credibility--abertay-evaluation-frameworks.md] and the page that
   ranked for it does not describe it
   [raw/sources--credibility--uiowa-lateral-reading-practice.md]. No SIFT claim appears
   anywhere in this skill.

5. **Two sources are vendor content with a direct interest in their own findings**
   [raw/keeping-up--overload--fiercehealthcare-doximity-physician-survey.md]
   [raw/keeping-up--overload--eolas-medical-knowledge-half-life.md]. Both are labelled at the
   point of use.

6. **No measured evidence on whether delta-first presentation actually helps a reader.** The
   claim that leading with what is new saves the reader time is untested here.
