# Owed-response detection

The gate that decides whether a conversation is even a candidate. Nothing is scored, drafted, or shown to the user until it passes every test on this page.

This gate exists because the failure mode of this skill is a long list of false positives that trains the user to stop reading it. In production, a general safety-net routine flagged the same blocked contact for 16 consecutive days without changing its approach, and the user never acted on it once. A list nobody acts on is worse than no list, because it also costs the user the seconds spent dismissing it.

**A short high-confidence list beats a long complete one.** Say that to the user in the output. Then act like it here.

## Why a gate rather than a score

The base rates make it necessary. 92.30 percent of enterprise emails never receive a reply, and roughly half of all email traffic goes to more than one recipient besides the sender (`research/distilled-responsiveness-and-reengagement.md`, section 1). People reply to about a third of their inbox at low load and to under 5 percent of it at a load near 100 messages a day (same section). Non-reply is the ordinary operation of the medium.

If unanswered were the criterion, the output would be the length of the inbox. The debt has to be established positively, item by item, and anything that cannot be established is dropped rather than downgraded.

## Gate 0: participation

**The user must be a participant in the conversation.**

This is a privacy rule, not a relevance rule, and it is absolute. Littlebird captures whatever pixels were on screen. That includes other people's inboxes during a screen share, a colleague's Slack visible over a shoulder, a support agent's queue, a demo account, a thread someone scrolled past in a meeting.

The test. Include a conversation only where at least one of these holds:

- The user appears as the sender of at least one message in the thread, tagged as theirs by the capture rather than inferred from context.
- The user is named as an addressed recipient in the visible thread header or the message body.
- The thread is a one-to-one channel where the other party is the only other participant and the app context establishes the user as the account owner.

If none holds, the conversation is excluded entirely. Do not score it, do not count it in the suppressed tally with any identifying detail, do not mention its subject, do not mention the people in it. It does not exist for the purposes of this skill.

Two specific traps:

- **A screen share in a meeting.** Another person's inbox displayed on a Zoom or Meet share is captured exactly the way the user's own is. It looks identical in retrieval. If the surrounding capture indicates a meeting was in progress and the app is a conferencing tool, treat every message thread visible in that window as somebody else's until the user is proven a participant.
- **Shared or team inboxes.** A support or sales queue the user has access to is not automatically a conversation the user owes a reply to. Apply the direct-address test in gate 2 before treating anything in it as owed.

Attribution is guilty until proven innocent (`evidence-standards.md`, rule 4).

## Gate 1: the other party is a human who can be replied to

Excluded by construction, never scored, never surfaced, never counted as a near miss:

| Category | Recognition signals |
|---|---|
| No-reply senders | `noreply@`, `no-reply@`, `donotreply@`, `notifications@`, `bounces@`, `mailer-daemon@`, and any address whose local part contains `noreply` in any casing |
| Newsletters | An unsubscribe footer, a "view in browser" link, a campaign or list header, a masthead, a regular publication cadence with identical formatting |
| Platform notifications | "X commented on", "Y liked your", "new sign-in from", "your invoice is ready", "build failed", "N new followers", digest and roundup mails |
| Automated transactional mail | Receipts, shipping updates, calendar invitations generated by a system, password resets, verification codes, billing notices |
| Bots and assistants in chat | App-tagged senders in Slack or Teams, webhook posters, integration accounts, workflow runners |
| Marketing sequences | A cold outbound sequence the user never answered. The user owes a stranger nothing. |
| Broadcast and announcement channels | Any channel where posting is restricted and replies are not expected |

The deferral logs support this cut directly: deferred messages were more likely to come from human senders than non-deferred ones, 0.849 against 0.744 (`research/distilled-responsiveness-and-reengagement.md`, section 4). System mail is not what people postpone with a guilty conscience.

Edge case worth getting right. A human message that arrives through a platform notification wrapper, such as a LinkedIn message alert or a marketplace inquiry forwarded by the platform, is a **human** message. Classify on who wrote it, not on what carried it.

## Gate 2: the user is the party who owes

The message must be addressed to the user in a way that puts the response obligation on them specifically.

### The direct-address ladder

| Level | Situation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | One to one. A DM, a text, a one-to-one email where the user is the sole recipient. | Owed |
| **Named** | A group thread where the user is named or at-mentioned and the ask attaches to that mention. "@user can you confirm the date?" | Owed |
| **Small addressed group** | Two or three people in To, an ask that is not assigned to anyone, and no other participant has answered it. | Owed, with a note that it is shared |
| **Answered by another** | Same as above, but another named participant already responded substantively. | Not owed |
| **Unnamed in a group** | A group thread of four or more where the user is not named and the ask is general. | Not owed |
| **CC** | The user is in CC and someone else is in To. | Not owed, regardless of content |
| **BCC or list** | The user received it as part of a list. | Not owed |

The CC rule has no exceptions. Being copied is being informed, not being asked. If the user actually owes something that arrived by CC, it will also exist as a direct ask somewhere, and that direct ask is the item.

Both the recipient-count effects point the same way and both are measured: more recipients cut reply probability by 18 percent and rated importance by 10 percent (`research/distilled-responsiveness-and-reengagement.md`, section 3), and deferred messages averaged 3.9 recipients against 7.0 for non-deferred (same file, section 4). A crowded thread is not where personal response debt lives.

### The ask test

A message with no ask in it is not owed a reply, however warm the relationship.

Present, in the last message from the other party:

- A direct question, marked by a question mark or an interrogative construction
- An explicit request: "can you", "could you", "would you mind", "please send", "let me know", "I need"
- A proposal awaiting acceptance or rejection: a time, a price, a scope, a name
- An attachment or document sent for review
- A stated dependency: "once you confirm", "waiting on your", "blocked until"
- A second attempt at any of the above: "bumping this", "following up", "did you get a chance", "circling back", "any update"

Absent, and the message is an FYI, a thank-you, a reaction, or a closing pleasantry, the thread is not owed. Send it to `natural-close-detection.md` rather than dropping it silently, because some of those are natural closes and the distinction matters for the write-off list.

An explicit information request was the single largest positive predictor of a reply in the measured model, at plus 22 percent, and an action request raised rated importance by 20 percent (`research/distilled-responsiveness-and-reengagement.md`, section 3). The presence of an ask is the strongest available signal that the thread was live when it stopped.

### The last-speaker test

The other party spoke last, and what they said was one of the asks above, and the user has said nothing in that thread since.

If the user spoke last, the item belongs in the ball-in-their-court section, not here. See the next page.

Watch two ways this goes wrong in capture:

- **A reply sent from a channel Littlebird did not see.** The user may have answered by phone, in person, in an app that was never on screen, or from a device Littlebird does not run on. Treat every item as provisional and say so in the output. Confirm before encoding (`evidence-standards.md`, rule 6).
- **Collection time is not send time.** Message items carry a collection timestamp and per-message send timestamps and they are different values (`littlebird-mcp-reference.md`). Days cold is computed from the **send time** of the other party's last message. Using collection time will systematically understate the age of an old thread that was recaptured recently.

## Gate 3: not naturally closed

Run `natural-close-detection.md` in full. A thread that ended because it was finished is not ghosting, and treating it as ghosting is the most embarrassing false positive this skill can produce, because the user remembers that conversation ending well.

## Gate 4: still actionable

Drop the item if any of these hold:

- The ask has been overtaken by events. The deadline passed, the event happened, the deal closed, the role was filled, the launch shipped.
- The answer was delivered somewhere else. The user answered the same question in a meeting, in another channel, or through a third party. Check meeting summaries with `LB_INTERNAL_SEARCH_MEETINGS` before concluding an item is open.
- The person is unreachable. They left the company, the account is closed, the number is disconnected, the user blocked them or was blocked.
- The user has already decided. If a previous run of this skill or a previous routine report shows the user marked this item held, closed, or written off, it does not come back. Ever. This is what `LB_INTERNAL_GET_ROUTINE_REPORTS` is read for at the start of every run.

## The mirror: ball in their court

The same gates, with the last-speaker test inverted. The user spoke last, the user's last message contained an ask, and the other party has not replied.

This section is separated out in the output for a reason that is not cosmetic. Roughly half of what a person experiences as "I am ghosting people" turns out on inspection to be the reverse, and conflating the two produces two distinct harms: false guilt, and wasted messages sent to people who owe the user rather than the other way round. Keeping them in one list means the user cannot tell which is which at a glance, and the guilt attaches to all of it.

The bar for this section is lower and the treatment is different. Do not draft an apology-adjacent nudge for a person who is the one who went quiet. Draft a plain restatement of the open question with a date attached, or recommend dropping it.

The urgency-bias finding cuts here too. Receivers systematically overestimate how quickly senders expected a response (`research/distilled-responsiveness-and-reengagement.md`, section 6). Applied in the mirror: the user's own unanswered outbound message is probably registering as less urgent to its recipient than it feels to the user.

## What happens to everything that fails a gate

Counted, not named. The output carries a suppression tally by reason:

```
Suppressed: 41 automated or no-reply senders, 12 group threads with no direct
address, 7 CC-only, 9 naturally closed, 4 overtaken by events, 6 previously
closed by you.
```

Two rules about this tally.

1. It carries counts and reasons only. No names, no subjects, no content. This is what keeps a large suppression number from becoming a back door around the participation gate.
2. It is evidence of work, and it is the number that earns the user's trust in the short list above it. A run that suppressed 79 items and surfaced 5 is a run that did its job.

## The cap

Maximum **7** items in the owed section. Maximum 7 in the ball-in-their-court section. Maximum 3 in the ambiguous bucket.

If more than 7 items clear the gates and score above threshold, take the top 7 by score and report the count of the remainder in one line, without names. Do not extend the list because it was a heavy week. The cap is the mechanism that keeps the list actionable, and a cap that bends is not a cap.
