# Lifecycle email research archive

This archive supports the `lifecycle-email-stinger` and its paired Bee.

## Capture scope

- Topic: hot and warm lead follow-up email strategy, drafting, cadence, suppression, and measurement
- Requested research window: 2026-06-04 through 2026-09-04 inclusive
- Capture date: 2026-09-04
- Search method: fresh web search followed by direct page review
- Source priority: official requirements and product documentation, measured vendor studies, current practitioner guidance, then community anecdotes
- Outside-window exception: Google sender requirements and FTC CAN-SPAM guidance are retained as evergreen constraints and are labeled outside-window in their raw files and in the distillation

The exact publication or update date had to appear on the source page, in current page metadata, or in the dated search result for a source to count as in-window. Refreshed older pages are labeled as in-window modifications rather than new publications. A current page with no in-window publication or modification date is not presented as in-window research.

## Known limits

- The Belkins and Hunter quantitative findings concern B2B outreach, much of it cold. They support sequence mechanics but are not direct conversion benchmarks for opted-in hot or warm leads.
- Belkins' in-window page date is a modification date, while the proprietary outcome data covers 2025. It is current analysis of older campaign data, not a June through September 2026 outcome dataset.
- SparrowCRM guidance is current and directly about lead follow-up, but it is vendor-authored advice, not an independent controlled study.
- Reddit evidence is anecdotal, self-selected, and sometimes promotional. It identifies failure modes and language patterns to test, not universal performance claims.
- No source establishes one universal hot-lead or warm-lead cadence across industries, jurisdictions, price points, and buying cycles. The cadence reference therefore supplies starting hypotheses that must be adapted and measured.
- Open and click events can be useful diagnostics, but the archive does not support treating them as proof of human intent.
