# Sales Battlecards 101: Guide + Battlecard Templates

## Notes

Recommends the **Fact, Impact, Act (FIA)** structure for every battlecard entry:
- **Fact**: the competitive context or insight (what the competitor actually does, prices, or claims)
- **Impact**: why it matters and when a rep should reach for it (the "so what?")
- **Act**: the specific action, a talk track, an objection-handling line, a next step

Three traits separate effective battlecards from a feature-comparison table:
1. **Context.** "Don't just state facts about a competitor in isolation; always provide the 'so what?'" A fact with no impact is trivia, not enablement.
2. **Charge.** Use directional language ("expedites," "eliminates," "locks you in") instead of neutral description. Neutral lists read as endorsements as often as warnings.
3. **Specificity.** Concrete metrics beat vague comparisons ("$10k/yr floor" beats "expensive").

Accuracy discipline: "Keeping your content up to date is the most, most, most, MOST important thing to do." Recommended practice: verify older intel before reusing it, source refreshes from recent win/loss interviews, and set an explicit refresh cadence. Stale battlecards actively destroy team trust faster than having no battlecard at all.
